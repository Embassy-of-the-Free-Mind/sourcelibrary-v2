The Philosophers' Library

Fifteen philosophers, each summoned from pages of their own books, read, write, lecture and argue here. Three daemons watch and keep score. You keep the hall: convene them, call the verdicts, and follow them back to the page.

The hall is quiet. Pick a problem, choose a room, and set them to work.
▼

How the hall counts

Souls. Each philosopher speaks from a handful of pages of their own books held in Source Library. A line that rests on a page links to it. Where nothing a philosopher wrote survives, the soul is built from testimony and says so.

Verdicts. Agreed: both speakers ended on a claim that at least one of them rejected at the start. Objection: one raised a point the other could not answer in that exchange. Impasse: neither. A later debate can answer an objection or reopen an agreement, so both counts can fall.

Daemons. After each session a second, separate call to Claude scores it from 1 to 5: Elenchus for rigor, Mneme for fidelity to the books, Thauma for wonder. An insight is marked only when wonder is 4 or 5 and rigor at least 3. Two trials survived temper an insight; one unanswered attack breaks it.

Limits. Claude writes every part and Claude judges. This is a record of a simulation, not a verdict on the philosophers, and the scores are one model's opinion of its own work.

Every line spoken here is written by Claude from positions and pages each philosopher left behind. None of it is quotation, except the pages shown in the Stacks and under Souls, which are Source Library's own text. To read the books, go to Source Library.
