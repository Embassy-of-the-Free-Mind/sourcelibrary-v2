2. ORIGINAL SOURCE TEXTS (Public Domain, with jurisdictional caveats)
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The historical texts digitized by Source Library are, in most
jurisdictions, in the public domain by virtue of their age. These
include works published before 1928 (US), works whose authors died more
than 70 years ago (EU), and similar thresholds worldwide.

HOWEVER, public domain status is not universal:

  • The Vatican (Vatican City State) asserts perpetual copyright over
    certain ecclesiastical texts and official documents. Works published
    by the Holy See may not be in the public domain in all contexts.

  • Some jurisdictions recognize "perpetual moral rights" that survive
    the expiration of economic copyright.

  • Digitized reproductions of public domain works may carry new rights
    in some jurisdictions (e.g., the "sweat of the brow" doctrine),
    though this is increasingly disfavored (see Bridgeman v. Corel).

Users are responsible for verifying the public domain status of
individual texts under the laws of their own jurisdiction.
