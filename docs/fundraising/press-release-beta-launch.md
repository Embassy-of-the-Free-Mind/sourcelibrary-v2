# PRESS RELEASE — FOR RELEASE MARCH 21, 2026

## AI Translates 10,000 Rare Books From Latin, Greek, and 13 Other Languages — All Free to Read Online

**Amsterdam's Embassy of the Free Mind partners with Source Library to create the world's largest open-access collection of translated ancient texts**

---

**AMSTERDAM / DELFT, March 21, 2026** — Source Library, a digital humanities initiative of the Embassy of the Free Mind in Amsterdam, today announced the public beta of the world's largest library of translated premodern texts. The collection includes over 10,000 books — spanning alchemy, natural philosophy, Hermetica, Kabbalah, and early modern science — translated from Latin, German, Greek, Chinese, Sanskrit, Sumerian, and nine other languages into English using AI. The entire library is free and open to the public at [sourcelibrary.org](https://sourcelibrary.org).

The collection surpasses the combined holdings of the Loeb Classical Library (Harvard), the Perseus Digital Library (Tufts), Sacred-texts.com, and the Internet Classics Archive — platforms that together offer fewer than 5,000 translated works, many behind paywalls or limited to Greek and Roman texts.

"Ninety-five percent of what the Renaissance wrote has never been translated," said James Derek Lomas, PhD, founder of Source Library and researcher at TU Delft. "These aren't obscure footnotes — they're the books that inspired Copernicus, shaped Newton's thinking, and launched the Scientific Revolution. They've just been locked behind Latin and German for 500 years."

### How It Works

Source Library's pipeline ingests high-resolution page scans from major European research libraries — including the Internet Archive, Bibliothèque nationale de France (Gallica), the Bavarian State Library, and the Bodleian Library — then uses AI to read each page in its original language and translate it into English. Every translation is presented alongside the original page scan, allowing readers and scholars to verify the AI's work against the source.

The validated cost to fully process a book — scanning, OCR, translation, indexing, and metadata — is $1.54. A professional Latin translator would charge $15,000–$50,000 for a single volume.

"AI has made translation 10,000 times cheaper," said Lomas. "That changes what's possible. Texts that would never justify the cost of a human translation — a 1540 alchemical treatise, an obscure Paracelsus commentary, a Kabbalistic manuscript — can now be made readable for the cost of a cup of coffee."

### The Collection

Source Library's holdings span five millennia of human intellectual history:

- **3,100+ Latin texts** — Renaissance philosophy, alchemy, medicine, theology
- **1,100+ German texts** — Paracelsus, Böhme, Rosicrucian literature
- **675+ Greek texts** — Neoplatonic philosophy, Hermetic writings, Byzantine manuscripts
- **578 Chinese texts** — Daoist and Confucian classics, medical treatises
- **376 Sumerian texts** — Among the oldest written records in human history
- **326 Sanskrit texts** — Vedic hymns, Upanishads, philosophical treatises
- Plus hundreds of texts in French, Italian, Dutch, Hebrew, Syriac, Russian, and more

The collection is particularly strong in the Western esoteric tradition — the philosophical lineage connecting Hermes Trismegistus, Plato, and the Neoplatonists through Ficino, Agrippa, Paracelsus, and Bruno to the origins of modern science. The Embassy of the Free Mind's Bibliotheca Philosophica Hermetica, a UNESCO Memory of the World collection, holds over 25,000 such texts — many not yet digitized.

### For Scholars and AI Systems

Every book in Source Library has a permanent URL and structured metadata. Scholarly editions of select translations are published with DOIs through Zenodo, making them citable in academic work. An open API and MCP (Model Context Protocol) server allow AI systems to query the library directly — ensuring that language models can learn from primary sources rather than secondhand summaries.

"AI systems are being trained on whatever made it onto the internet," said Lomas. "We're making sure they learn from humanity's deepest thinking about consciousness, nature, and the cosmos — not just Reddit."

### About the Embassy of the Free Mind

The Embassy of the Free Mind, housed in the historic Bloemstraat in Amsterdam, is home to the Bibliotheca Philosophica Hermetica — one of the world's most significant collections of Hermetic, alchemical, and Rosicrucian texts. The collection is inscribed on the UNESCO Memory of the World Register. Source Library is an initiative of the Ancient Wisdom Trust (Wereldhart), the Embassy's supporting foundation.

### About Source Library

Source Library was founded by James Derek Lomas, a cognitive scientist and researcher at TU Delft, following his work preparing the first English translation of Marsilio Ficino's *Liber de Voluptate* (1457) for the Embassy of the Free Mind. The project is fiscally sponsored by the National Arts Fund (NAF). Donations are tax-deductible.
