# Banned Books — Acquisition Shortlist

_Generated 2026-05-16. Sources: Roman *Index Librorum Prohibitorum* (1559–1948), cross-referenced against Source Library catalog (Atlas)._

After matching the catalog against ~85 curated Index entries, 442 books in our existing collection are now tagged `banned-books` and surfaced at `/collections/banned-books`. The gaps below are the books we should acquire to make this collection scholarly-complete.

---

## Tier 1 — Author already in library, banned title still missing

These are the lowest-effort highest-payoff acquisitions: we already host the author's other works, so adding their suppressed title slots cleanly into existing author pages.

### Johann Reuchlin — *Augenspiegel* (1511)
- **Why:** The book that triggered the Reuchlin–Pfefferkorn affair (1511–20) and the Letters of Obscure Men. Pope Leo X condemned it 1520. Reuchlin's defense of the Talmud is the founding moment of Christian Hebraism — and we have 21 of his other works, including *De arte cabalistica*, but not this one.
- **Source:** [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/AugenspiegelReuchlin) — 1511 Tübingen first edition is scanned by Bayerische Staatsbibliothek (BSB München).
- **MDZ:** https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/ (search: "Augenspiegel Reuchlin").

### Sebastian Castellio — *De haereticis an sint persequendi* (1554)
- **Why:** Foundational tolerationist response to Servetus's execution by Calvin. On the Roman Index 1559.
- **Source:** Hathi/Münchener DigitalisierungsZentrum has the 1554 Magdeburg edition. Also reprinted by van der Aa, Hagae 1670 (more legible).
- **We have:** *De arte dubitandi* and three other Castellio works.

### Lorenzo Valla — *De falso credita et ementita Constantini donatione* (1517/1520)
- **Why:** Valla's philological demolition of the Donation of Constantine — the most consequential humanist exposé. Placed on Index 1559. Hutten edited the 1517 print run that detonated in Protestant hands.
- **Source:** IA scan of Hutten's 1517 Mainz edition (under the title *De donatione Constantini*). Gallica also has the 1520 Strasbourg.
- **We have:** 8 other Valla works.

### John Locke — *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1690)
- **Why:** On Roman Index 1734. Foundational text of empiricism; we have Locke but not the *Essay*.
- **Source:** Internet Archive — first edition Thomas Bassett, London 1690 (multiple scans). Gallica has the 5th edition 1706 in French.

### Arnaldus de Villa Nova — *Tractatus de antichristo* / *De adventu antichristi*
- **Why:** Condemned posthumously 1316. The theological-apocalyptic side of Arnaldus (we have his alchemical *Opera Chymica* and ~10 other works). Specifically condemned propositions.
- **Source:** Manuscript tradition — Vat. lat. 3824 is the canonical witness. Bonner edition (1899) and Perarnau's modern critical edition.

## Tier 2 — Important author NOT in catalog at all

### Bernardino Telesio — *De rerum natura iuxta propria principia* (1565/1570/1586)
- **Why:** Teacher of Campanella; founder of naturalist Renaissance philosophy. Banned 1596 *donec corrigatur*. We have Campanella but not his master.
- **Source:** IA + MDZ scan the 1586 Naples expanded edition (definitive). Gallica has 1570 Naples.
- **Priority:** HIGH — fills a structural gap (Telesio → Campanella → Bruno).

### Cecco d'Ascoli — *Acerba* (c. 1325, first print 1476)
- **Why:** Astrologer-poet executed by the Inquisition in Florence 1327 — one of the earliest book-and-author burnings of the print era. *L'Acerba* is the surviving popular astrological-cosmological poem in Italian.
- **Source:** IA has the 1546 Venice and 1581 editions. ICCU has multiple incunable scans. *L'Acerba etas* is the modern critical edition (Crespi 1927).
- **Note:** His commentary on Sacrobosco was also suppressed — that's harder to find.

### Cornelius Loos — *De vera et falsa magia* (1592, suppressed before publication)
- **Why:** Earliest systematic refutation of witch trials by a Catholic theologian. Print sheets seized at Cologne 1592 before publication; Loos imprisoned. Fragments survive in Delrio's *Disquisitiones magicae* (which quotes Loos to refute him).
- **Source:** What survives is in Delrio's anti-witchcraft compendium. Burr's 1886 article reconstructs from sources. A scholarly edition by Behringer would be the modern source.
- **Acquisition path:** Likely needs to be a digital edition of Delrio's *Disquisitiones* with Loos passages flagged. We may already have Delrio — check.

### Babylonian Talmud — pre-1564 editions
- **Why:** Burned wholesale at Paris (1242) and Cremona (1559). Sixtus V permitted expurgated editions 1590. We have only a 1902 Rodkinson edition and modern commentary.
- **Source:** The 1520–23 Bomberg Venice edition (the first complete printed Talmud, edited by Daniel Bomberg) is the basis for all subsequent Hebrew Talmuds and is widely scanned (NLI, Hebrew University, IA). Several volumes available on archive.org/details/babylonian-talmud.
- **Priority:** Significant gap — having the Bomberg Talmud would anchor a Hebrew studies axis.

### Pseudo-Paracelsus — *Archidoxis magica* and *Liber Azoth*
- **Why:** Of the "magical Paracelsus" corpus the Index suppressed most aggressively — talismanic/sigillary magic attributed to Paracelsus. Often included in Huser's 1589 edition of Paracelsus's *Bücher und Schrifften*, sometimes excised.
- **Source:** MDZ has the Huser edition (10 vols., Basel 1589–91). The Geneva Paracelsi Opera Omnia (1658, Glaser/Bitiskius) bundles many of these.
- **Note:** We have 66 Paracelsus works — likely include some of this corpus already, but verify the *Archidoxis magica* specifically.

## Other indices worth pursuing as collections

If we want to extend beyond the Roman Index:

1. **Spanish Inquisition indices** — Quiroga 1583, Sotomayor 1640. These suppress books the Roman Index permitted (especially Erasmus, who was *donec corrigatur* in Rome but Class I in Spain). Bujanda's *Index des livres interdits* has the complete data.
2. **Nazi *Liste 1 des schädlichen und unerwünschten Schrifttums*** (1935, expanded 1936/38/39) — digitized by the Berlin Staatsbibliothek. Catches Freud, Einstein, Mann, Brecht, Marx, Stefan Zweig, etc. Largely out-of-scope for Source Library but a powerful 20c. exhibition.
3. **Soviet Glavlit lists** — fragmentary; Memorial archived much before its forced dissolution.
4. **Holy Office *Errores variorum recentiorum Doctorum*** — internal lists circulated by Roman censors that didn't make it into the printed Index.
