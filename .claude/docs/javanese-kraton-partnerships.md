# Javanese Kraton collection — partnership targets for the gated holdings

Context: the openly-licensed Javanese manuscript corpus on Wikimedia Commons
(BL Yogyakarta CC0 + WiLMa, ~298 manuscripts / 52k pages) is being archived
automatically (see GitHub issue #2144). But that open corpus is the material
that *left* the kratons — chiefly the manuscripts the British carried off from
the Yogyakarta palace in **1812**. The manuscripts that **stayed in Indonesia**
— the living royal libraries — are digitized-but-gated and need a relationship,
not a crawler. This note tees up that relationship work.

## Why these are "locked"
A mix of: institutions asserting copyright on the *digital reproductions* (even
though the centuries-old texts are PD by age); deliberate anti-theft /
provenance-control policies; active colonial-restitution sensitivities; and much
simply digitized only to internal systems.

## Targets (highest leverage first)

1. **B-NICE (Stichting Behoud Nederlands Ind(ones)isch Cultureel Erfgoed)** —
   our existing source for the Dutch scholarship. As a Netherlands-based Javanese
   diaspora heritage foundation, B-NICE is the natural **bridge/introducer** to
   the kratons and to Perpusnas, and fits the "for the king" framing. *Action:
   open the conversation here first — it can warm every door below.*

2. **Perpustakaan Nasional RI (Perpusnas) / Khastara** — ~837 ancient-manuscript
   titles, free but login-walled with asserted © on reproductions; no API/IIIF.
   *Action: formal MoU for a data-sharing / co-publication arrangement.*

3. **Karaton Ngayogyakarta (Widya Budaya / Krida Mardawa) & Pura Pakualaman** —
   the Yogyakarta palace's own departments; manuscripts digitized via the
   Leipzig / UIN Sunan Kalijaga program, access-restricted. *Action: MoU with the
   Kraton; the strongest "reuniting the king's library" story — pair the in-situ
   manuscripts with our CC0 1812-dispersed set.*

4. **Kasunanan Surakarta (Sasana Pustaka) & Pura Mangkunegaran (Reksa Pustaka)** —
   Solo courts. Reksa Pustaka has ~3,132 collections / ~400k pages scanned
   (internal only); Sasana Pustaka ~700 digitized (reading-room only). *Action:
   MoU per court; possibly via UNS / IAIN partners already working with them.*

5. **Sonobudoyo Museum (Yogyakarta)** — ~1,400 mss digitized but *deliberately*
   on-site-only by anti-theft policy. Hardest yes; approach via the Yogyakarta
   cultural-heritage board.

6. **Cornell University (Kroch/SEAP)** — the 1980s Surakarta Manuscript Project
   (Nancy Florida): ~5,000 titles / ~600k pages on microfilm — the definitive
   surrogate for the Solo court libraries. *Action: SEAP conversation re:
   digitization-from-microfilm + access.* Florida's three published catalogs are
   the public finding aids in the meantime (Vol. 3 is on Internet Archive).

## The pitch (one line)
"Source Library has digitally reassembled the Yogyakarta kraton library that was
dispersed in 1812 (CC0). We'd like to partner to reunite it — with full
attribution and on your terms — with the manuscripts that remained in the
palaces, and make the whole corpus readable and citable."

## Status

- Partnerships: not started. Owner/next step: open with B-NICE.
