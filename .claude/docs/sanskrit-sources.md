## Museums of India / JATAN (museumsofindia.gov.in) — an ARTWORK source, not a books source

Assessed 2026-08-30 against a live record (`alh_ald-AM-MIN-361-ix-1453`,
*Madhumalti*, Allahabad Museum). Recorded here because the name suggests Indian
manuscripts and the answer is "yes, but not as books".

- **Metadata is excellent** — the best-structured of any source in this doc: 25+
  museum fields including accession number, gallery, object type, main material,
  manufacturing technique, artist + nationality + life dates, country,
  provenance, origin place, find place, scribe, style, school, patron/dynasty,
  period, inscription, tribe, costume, culture, dimensions, and brief + detailed
  descriptions.
- **Images are capped at 1240x1800** (~2.2 MP, ~500 KB). Exactly two variants
  exist: `_h` and a 300x400 `_l` thumbnail; `_o`/`_f`/`_orig`/`_full`/bare all
  404. There is no master, no original, no IIIF. At the record's stated
  20.32 x 11.43 cm that is roughly 225-275 DPI — usable, but there is nothing
  better to ask for, so this cannot satisfy the native-resolution norm (#3186).
- **The unit is a PAINTING.** `Object Type: Miniature Paintings`,
  `Gallery Name: Miniature Painting`. The record is two images of leaves from a
  manuscript, catalogued as an artwork accession, with no related-record links
  and no manuscript grouping. Imported, these are `/artwork/` records, not
  paginated reading editions. NOT a route to Sanskrit texts.
- **Not harvestable as it stands, on two counts.** `robots.txt` is a blanket
  `User-agent: * / Disallow: /`, and discovery is a JS SPA whose data endpoint
  (`/repository/search/basic/fetch`) 404s to non-browser callers — the sitemap
  is 80 pages of chrome with no record URLs. Record pages ARE server-rendered
  and readable *if you already know the id*, which is the part we cannot get.
- **Verdict:** worth an institutional approach if we ever want Indian miniature
  painting for the gallery — the provenance metadata would be a genuine
  upgrade on our artwork corpus. It is not an acquisition channel for texts,
  and it should not be scripted without permission.

## E-text repositories (pairing material, NOT scan imports)

Like Kanripo/CBETA for Chinese: these give transcribed text to pair with scanned editions (OCR ground truth, work-identity anchoring, first-translation research). They do not enter the image pipeline.

- **GRETIL** (Göttingen Register of Electronic Texts in Indian Languages, gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de) — the standard scholarly e-text hub; Sanskrit, Pali, Prakrit and more; TEI XML + HTML + plain text; **cumulative bulk downloads per language group** (VERIFIED page). License: per-text, contributed — check before republication.
- **SARIT** (sarit.indology.info; github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus — VERIFIED live repo, ~95 TEI files) — smaller but high-quality TEI with page breaks of the printed editions (good for scan↔text alignment); śāstra-heavy (nyāya, alaṃkāra, Buddhist pramāṇa).
- **Muktabodha e-text library** (see table row) — the KSTS (Kashmir Series of Texts and Studies) transcriptions and other Śaiva/tantra editions; 570+ searchable e-texts, free, no login. Directly on-mission for tantra.
- **sanskritdocuments.org** — community-maintained, thousands of texts in ITX/TeX/HTML, git-backed; devotional/stotra-heavy but broad. Good for popular canon, weak on śāstra editions.
- **DCS** (Digital Corpus of Sanskrit; github.com/OliverHellwig/sanskrit — VERIFIED live repo, ~1.9 GB) — lemmatized/analyzed corpus (CoNLL-U); linguistics-grade, useful for OCR/lemma tooling rather than reading editions.

## Guidance

For **mission subjects** (rasaśāstra, tantra, yoga, āyurveda, jyotiṣa, Vedānta), the two channels that combine yield and scriptability are **eGangotri** (unique manuscripts, at-source digitization, subject-tagged filenames, IA import path we already have) and **Wellcome** (āyurveda/iatro-chemistry, best-in-class API, confirmed big manifests, permissive licenses). **DLI/Public Library of India** is the volume channel for printed Sanskrit editions (54K `language:san` texts) but needs aggressive metadata triage — treat it as a mine, not a feed. **Cambridge, Bodleian, SBB, Gallica** are quality European MS channels with verified IIIF but small digitized Sanskrit subsets (tens to low hundreds each) — good for curated, named-work acquisitions rather than sweeps. **BL is down, NAMAMI/BORI/NDLI are not import channels** — their value is catalogue lookup and future partnerships.

### Metadata hazards (read before writing any importer)
- **DLI/JaiGyan `language:san` lies in both directions**: photo albums and audio tagged `san`+`texts` (the 2,204-item Lakshman Joo photo cluster is INSIDE the san+texts slice), and Sanskrit books hide under `hin`/`eng` or junk tags. `language:(sanskrit)` spelled out matches only 133 items — the corpus convention is `san`. Titles are often garbled all-caps romanization ("GAGYANARAYANABHUSVALLITHI"). Never trust DLI title/author fields for display — re-extract from the title page during QA (we own the scans post-import; OCR the title page).
- **eGangotri items have NO structured metadata** — the filename IS the record, but it is systematically structured: usually `{Title} {Author?} {MS number} {Alm N Shlf N} {Script} {Subject}` (e.g. "Muhurta Manjari 5441 Gha Alm 25 Shlf 1 Devanagari Jyotish"). An importer should parse trailing script+subject tokens (Devanagari/Sharada; Jyotish/Ayurveda/Tantra/Alankar Shastra/…) into subject metadata and strip shelf-locator noise from the title.
- **"rasa" is ambiguous** — VERIFIED: most `creator:"eGangotri" AND rasa` hits are *alaṃkāra-śāstra* (rasa = aesthetic sentiment: Rasa Mañjarī, Rasa Gaṅgādhara), not alchemy. Filter rasaśāstra sweeps on `rasashastra OR rasayana OR rasa-vidya OR "Rasa Ratna"` plus the filename subject token `Ayurveda`, and hand-check the candidate list (it is small: IA-wide `mediatype:texts AND (rasashastra OR rasasastra OR "rasa shastra")` => 96).
- **`books.language` is the EDITION's language** (see `.claude/docs/invariants/language-fields.md`). A Gita Press Gītā with Hindi commentary is a Hindi(-Sanskrit) edition; a Wellcome English Mahānirvāṇa Tantra translation is an English edition. Tag `language` from what the edition prints, `original_language` = `san` (or `pra`/`pi`) for the underlying work. Many DLI "san" items will correctly become `hin` or bilingual editions — expect this and don't "fix" it backwards.
- **Manuscripts vs prints**: eGangotri, Wellcome MSS, Cambridge, Bodleian, SBB, Gallica are largely manuscripts — often unpunctuated, Sharada or old Devanagari hands, worm-eaten leaves. OCR quality will be far below print; route through the OCR-difficulty QA loop before bulk-translating, and expect palm-leaf/pothi aspect ratios (very wide pages) to stress the reader UI.
- **Gallica `cc…` arks are finding aids** with no manifest; only `btv1b…` arks resolve to images. Pelliot sanscrit items are often 2–10 canvas fragments — fine for preservation, weak as reading editions.

## Importer notes
- IA channels use the normal IA identifier import; enumeration via advancedsearch (`collection:`/`creator:`/`identifier:` queries above are copy-paste ready). **Collection IDs are case-sensitive** (`ServantsOfKnowledge`, not `servantsofknowledge`). Imports land hidden pending QA per the standard loop (`.claude/docs/import-workflow.md`).
- IIIF channels: all five verified manifests (Wellcome, CUDL, Bodleian, SBB, Gallica) are **Presentation v2** — no v3 surprises found in this set, but `/api/import/iiif` handles both.
- Wellcome is the only channel where enumeration → manifest is one clean API loop (works query → `items[].locations[].url`). Budget throttling for Gallica (429s), and note CUDL blocks its search JSON (scrape collection HTML for IDs; manifests themselves are open).
- IA advancedsearch drops connections on long OR-chains (ECONNRESET observed) — retry with backoff, keep queries short.
- e-Gangotri/DLI title cleanup: run the filename parser at import, then title-page OCR at QA — same two-step we use for other junk-metadata mirrors.

## Recommended first waves

1. **eGangotri tantra + jyotiṣa manuscript sweep** (mission core: tantra, jyotiṣa; unique MSS not held anywhere else digitally)
   - Enumerate: `creator:"eGangotri" AND mediatype:texts AND (tantra OR agama)` (=> 1,706) and `… AND (jyotish OR jyotisha)` (=> 219); optionally start with the two coherent sub-collections `collection:dharmarthtrustjkcollectionbyegangotri` (3,572) + `collection:kashijangamwadimath` (5,641) filtered on subject tokens.
   - Expected yield after dedupe/filename-QA: ~1,500–2,000 manuscript items. Import hidden, parse filenames per the hazard note, hand-check the "rasa" ambiguity cases.
2. **Wellcome āyurveda/rasaśāstra IIIF wave** (mission core: āyurveda + Indian iatrochemistry; best licenses, biggest clean manifests)
   - Enumerate: `GET api.wellcomecollection.org/catalogue/v2/works?languages=san&items.locations.locationType=iiif-presentation&include=items&pageSize=100` (=> 147 works), plus the same with `query=ayurveda` minus the san set for English/vernacular āyurvedica if wanted.
   - Expected yield: ~150 digitized works (some multi-volume, canvases into the thousands). One clean scripted loop; licenses explicit per manifest.
3. **DLI printed-śāstra probe wave** (volume channel, bounded): pick one subject slice, e.g. `collection:digitallibraryindia AND mediatype:texts AND language:san AND (rasashastra OR rasayana OR "rasa ratna" OR rasarnava)` (=> ~30) plus `(ayurveda OR vaidyaka)` and `(tantra OR agama)` slices, capped at ~500 items sorted by downloads desc.
   - Expected yield: 300–500 printed editions after junk triage. Use this wave to calibrate the DLI metadata-repair pipeline before any larger DLI sweep.
