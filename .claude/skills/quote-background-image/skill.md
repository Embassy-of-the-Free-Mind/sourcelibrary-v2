# Quote Background Image Selection

Picking the image that sits behind an overlaid quotation on a collection page. The image must be striking on its own AND let the text read cleanly on top. Legibility wins ties: a stunning image the quote can't be read over is the wrong choice.

## The job in one line

Choose the most beautiful image from the collection that has a calm area for text, no competing text of its own, even mid-tone lighting, and fits the collection's subject.

## Evaluate every candidate against these

### Legibility (non-negotiable — fails here = rejected)
- **A calm zone for the text.** Part of the image must be even-toned and low-detail where the quote will sit. Busy detail belongs at the edges, not where words land.
- **Even, mid-tone lighting.** Not too light, not too dark. Avoid high-contrast images (a bright window beside deep shadow) where text would be readable over one half and lost over the other.
- **No text on the image itself.** No title pages, captions, printed words, or labels. Overlaid type fighting printed type is a mess.
- **No strong subject dead-centre.** A face, a single object, or the focal point in the middle collides with the quote. Want the focus offset, or a texture/pattern with no single focus.

### Composition
- **Survives a wide crop.** Quote banners are wide. Portrait images with the action at the very top or bottom get cut off. Favour images that still read when cropped to a wide strip.
- **No faces looking straight at the reader.** Distracting behind text, and slightly unsettling at banner scale.

### Beauty & fit
- **Genuinely striking.** This is a feature slot; pick the best visual in the collection, not the first usable one.
- **Atmospheric over literal.** A texture, pattern, ornamental border, or a detail crop reads better as a background than a busy full-scene illustration.
- **On-theme.** Match the collection's subject and mood: an alchemical emblem for alchemy, a celestial diagram for astrology, a botanical plate for a plant collection. Not a random pretty image.

### Content safety
- **Nothing disturbing.** These collections include demonology, witchcraft, anatomy, and torture imagery. Avoid gore, distressing scenes, or anything grim behind an inviting quote.
- **No heavy degradation.** Foxing, water stains, mould spots, and tears on old scans look like mess or damage behind text. Avoid badly degraded pages even if the underlying image is good.
- **High enough resolution.** It will be stretched wide; it must not blur or pixelate.

## The override

**If no image is genuinely good, a plain tonal background beats a bad photo.** Don't force a weak, busy, or illegible image into the slot just to have one. A solid or subtly textured colour from the site palette is the correct fallback.

## Selection method

1. Pull the collection's available plates/illustrations.
2. Discard anything that fails a legibility rule (text on image, too dark/light, focus dead-centre, can't survive wide crop) or a safety rule (disturbing, degraded, low-res).
3. From what remains, rank by beauty and thematic fit.
4. Prefer atmospheric details and textures over busy full scenes.
5. If nothing clears the bar, recommend the plain tonal fallback.

## Quick checklist (per candidate)

- [ ] Calm, even-toned zone for the text?
- [ ] Mid-tone, not too dark or too light?
- [ ] No printed text on the image?
- [ ] Focus offset, not dead-centre?
- [ ] Reads well cropped to a wide strip?
- [ ] On-theme for this collection?
- [ ] Nothing disturbing or badly degraded?
- [ ] High enough resolution?

Any "no" on the first half (legibility/safety) = reject. "No" on theme/beauty = deprioritise.

## Examples

**Good:** a softly lit alchemical emblem with ornament around the edges and an even mid-tone centre; a star chart with the dense constellations to one side; a botanical plate cropped to a calm field of leaves.

**Bad:** a title page (printed text); a stark woodcut of a demon centred on the page (disturbing, dead-centre focus); a brilliant-white engraving on blank paper (too light for overlay); a water-stained manuscript leaf (degradation reads as mess).
