The text focuses on the properties of numbers in "continuous proportion"—what we today call a geometric progression (like 2, 4, 8, 16)—and the relationship between these sequences and prime numbers.]]

...the numbers which, by the 23rd [proposition] of the first [book], will measure **a**, **b**, **c**, and **d**: which is the first point intended in the proposition. And through the 17th of the third [book], the found greatest number [the greatest common divisor] is found to measure **a**, **b**, **c**, and **d**; otherwise, by the corollary of the 16th, it would happen that a larger number measures a smaller one. And because **h** denotes **n** in **l**, and **l** in **e**: therefore, by the 23rd of the first [book], the number which is made from **h** times itself, which is **o**, denotes **n** in **e**. And because **h** also denotes **e** in **a**: therefore, by the same 23rd, the number made from **h** times **o**, which is **q**, denotes **n** in **a**. By the same reasoning, because **l** denotes **n** in **m** and **m** in **g**: therefore, by the 23rd of the first, the number made from **l** times itself, which is **p**, denotes **n** in **g**: and also because **l** denotes **g** in **v**: therefore, by the same, the number made from **l** times **p**, which is **r**, denotes **n** in **v**. Therefore, **n** measures the number **a** according to **q** and the number **v** according to **r**. And because **h** and **l** are prime to each other [[notes: "Prime to each other" (coprime) means they share no common factors other than 1]]: therefore, by the 12th of the third, **q** and **r** are prime to each other; wherefore by the 20th of the same, they are the least in their proportion [[notes: Being "least in their proportion" means the ratio is in its simplest form, like 2/3 instead of 4/6]]. Therefore, by the corollary of the 23rd of the same, **q** and **r** will measure **a** and **d** according to the greatest number measuring **a** and **v**. Thus, **n** is the greatest number that measures the extremes **a** and **d**, which is the second point.

¶ The first corollary is known from this. For because the same number **h** [multiplied] into **n**, **l**, and **e** produces **l**, **e**, and **a**, therefore by the 7th of the first, **a**, **e**, **l**, and **n** will be continuously proportional. And by the same, because **l** in **n**, **m**, and **g** produces **m**, **g**, and **d**, therefore **d**, **g**, **m**, and **n** will be continuously proportional; wherefore just as between **a** and **d** there fall two mean proportionals **b** and **c**, so between **a** and **n** there fall two, **e** and **l**. And between **v** and **n** there fall two mean proportionals **g** and **m**, namely just as many mean proportionals between two communicating numbers [[notes: "Communicating" numbers are those that share a common factor]] and just as many between each of them and the greatest number measuring them commonly: which is the first corollary. The second, indeed, becomes known from here. For because by the 23rd of the third and the 19th, **h** into **e**, into **f**, and into **g** produces **a**, **b**, and **c**. Therefore, by the 7th of the second, **a**, **b**, **c**, **d**, **e**, **f**, and **g** are continuously proportional: which is the second corollary and the goal proposed.

[[margin: 3]]
### To take numbers of different proportions whose greatest common divisors are continuously proportional.

¶ Let **a** and **b** be numbers prime to each other, **a** being the smaller and **b** the larger. Let **a** times itself produce **c**, and times **b** produce **d**, and let **b** times itself produce **e**: by the 7th of the second, whatever the proportion of **a** to **b**, such is the proportion of **c** to **d**. And by the 8th of the same, again, whatever the proportion of **a** to **b**, such is the proportion of **d** to **e**. Therefore, **c**, **d**, and **e** are continuously proportional [[notes: This describes a geometric progression, for example, if a=2 and b=3, then c=4, d=6, e=9]]. I lead **a** into **c** to make **f**, and into **d** to make **g**: and by the 7th of the second, **g** to **f** will be as **b** to **a**. Wherefore also as **b** to **a**, and because the proportion of **a** to **b** is as **c** to **d**: therefore by the 25th of the second, that which is made from **a** times **d** is equal to that which is made from **b** times **c**. But that which is made from **a** times **d** is set to be **g**. Therefore, what is made from **b** times **c** is **g**. And because what is made from **c** times **a** is **f**. Therefore, by the corollary of the 23rd of the third, **c** is the greatest number measuring **f** and **g**. Likewise, I lead **d** into **e** and let **h** result: because **d** into **a** produces **g**, and **d** into **e** produces **h**. Therefore, by the 7th of the second, whatever the proportion of **e** to **a**, such is **h** to **g**: and the proportion of **e** to **a** is not as **b** to **a**. For **e** and **b**, namely the whole and the part, would be equal: which is impossible [[notes: This is a "reductio ad absurdum" argument, showing that the proportions must be different]]. Therefore, the proportion of **h** to **g** is not the proportion of **g** to **f**. Likewise, because **a** and **b** are prime to each other and **e** is produced from **b** times itself, therefore **e**, by the 11th of the third, is prime to **a**. Therefore, **e** and **a** are the least in their proportion. But **d** measures **g** according to **a**, and the same **d** measures **h** according to **e**. Therefore, by the corollary of the 23rd, **d** is the greatest number measuring **g** and **h**. I take therefore **k** and **l**, the first and the larger **d** in another certain proportion than **b** to **a** and **e** to **a**: and I lead that **k** into **e** and let **l** result. Because therefore **e** in **d** makes **h** and **e** in **l** makes **l** [[notes: Possible transcription error for another letter, likely m]], by the 7th of the second, whatever the proportion of **l** to **d**, such is **l** to **h**. But the proportion of **l** to **d** is set to be different from the proportion of **b** to **a** and **e** to **a**. Therefore, it is other than **g** to **f** and **h** to **g**. And because **l** and **d** are prime to each other and thus the least in their proportion and they measure **h** and **l** according to **e**. Therefore, by the corollary of the 23rd of the third, **e** is the greatest number measuring **h** and **l**. Thus have been taken the numbers **f**, **g**, **h**, and **l** of different proportions whose greatest common measures **e**, **d**, [and] **b** taken two by two are continuously proportional: which is what was proposed.

[[margin: 4]]
### If the two extremes of numbers in continuous proportion are prime to each other, all of them will be the least in their proportion.

¶ Let **a**, **b**, and **c** be continuously proportional, and let **a** and **c** be prime to each other [[notes: This means they have no common factors, like 4 and 9 in the sequence 4, 6, 9]]: I say that the three numbers **a**, **b**, and **c** are the least in the proportion of **a** to **b**. For if there were three smaller ones, let them be **d**, **e**, and **f**: because by the 21st of the second and equal proportionality, whatever the proportion of **a** to **c**, such is **d** to **f**, and **a** and **c** by hypothesis are prime to each other. Therefore, by the 23rd, they are the least in their proportion. Therefore, the numbers **a** and **c**, by the 19th of the same, measure the numbers **d** and **f**: namely, larger numbers measuring smaller ones, which is impossible.

[[margin: 5]]
### If any numbers are the least in their proportion, their two extremes will be prime to each other.

¶ This is the converse of the preceding. Let therefore **a**, **b**, and **c** be the least in their proportion: I say that **a** and **c** are prime to each other. For if **a** and **c** were communicating [sharing a factor], there would be some same number, by the 2nd of this book, measuring them commonly, let it be **d**. Since **a**, **b**, and **c** are three unequal numbers, it will measure them according to three different numbers. Let it measure **a** according to **e**, **b** according to **f**, and **c** according to **g**: by the 7th of the second, **e**, **f**, and **g** are in the same proportion with **a**, **b**, and **c**. And since they are smaller than **a**, **b**, and **c**, being their parts, then **a**, **b**, and **c** are not the given least: which is against the hypothesis.

[[margin: 6]]
### To find any number of leasts according to a given proportion. One will be manifest from two leasts in some proportion: the first [multiplied] into both and the second into itself makes three; and from the same first into those three and the same second into the third: four leasts of that proportion are continuously born. If you continue to lead the first into all of them and the second into the last in this way, you will produce as many leasts as you wish in some proportion.

¶ Let the leasts of that proportion be found through the 23rd of the third, let them be **a** and **b**: I lead **a** into itself and let **c** result, and **a** into **b** and let **d** result, and **b** into itself and let **e** result. By the 7th and 8th of the second, the three numbers **c**, **d**, and **e** will be continuously proportional and by the 12th of the third, the extremes **c** and **e** are prime to each other, wherefore by the 4th of this book, those three numbers are the least in their proportion. Likewise, I lead **a** into all of those and let **f**, **g**, and **h** result, and **b** into **e** and let **l** result. By the same reasoning, **f**, **g**, **h**, and **l** are continuously proportional and the extremes **f** and **l** are prime to each other, and those four are the least in their proportion. And by this process five, six, seven, and as many as you want may be found. The corollary is known from the manner of the demonstration.
