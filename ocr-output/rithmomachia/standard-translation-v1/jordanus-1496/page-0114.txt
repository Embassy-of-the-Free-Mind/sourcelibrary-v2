¶ There are five **tetrachords** <note>original: "Tetrachorda"; a tetrachord is a scale of four notes spanning a perfect fourth</note>: the **Tetrachord of the Lows**, the **Tetrachord of the Middles**, the **Conjunct Tetrachord**, the **Disjunct Tetrachord**, and the **Highest Tetrachord**.

¶ We define a **Mode** here as the lowering or raising of all the tetrachords step by step within any genus of melody, while maintaining the progression of its own kind.

¶ To demonstrate the division of the **regular monochord** <note>A one-stringed teaching tool used to measure intervals</note> in the **diatonic genus**.

¶ Although many musical instruments may be found—such as **Citharas** <note>Ancient lyres</note>, **Pipes**, **Trumpets**, **Curved horns**, and various multi-holed **Flutes** (right-handed, left-handed, single, and double, which are animated by a single breath)—as **Apuleius** <note>A 2nd-century Roman philosopher and author</note> says: it is said that **Hyagnis** <note>A mythical musician from Phrygia</note>, the king of the Lydians and father of **Marsyas**, was the first to separate the hands while playing. Furthermore, there are instruments like the **Pelta**, the **Choracicite**, the **Sambuca** <note>A triangular harp</note>, **Water organs**, **Nablia** <note>A type of Hebrew harp</note>, **Psalteries**, **Magades**, **Barbitos**, **Naula**, **Petades**, **Horns**, **Heptagons**, **Melodic pipes**, the **Tortoise-shell lyre**, **Barbati**, **Plectrums**, **Monochords**, **Tetrachords**, **Polychords**, and other musical instruments of that kind. Nevertheless, it pleased the Philosophers to demonstrate their principles through Monochords, Tetrachords, and Polychords so that we might understand the logic and composition of all others. Therefore, just as we understand the composition of Monochords and Tetrachords, so must we understand the rest. Now, let us turn our attention to the demonstration of the **regular monochord**.

¶ The monochord is called **regular** because the harmonic rule of musical consonances is investigated upon a single string. Therefore, let **a-b** be the string upon which we wish to regularly place the consonances in the **diatonic genus**.
