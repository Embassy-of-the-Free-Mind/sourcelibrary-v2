¶ As in the preceding [demonstration], let **h** and **k** be the smallest numbers of the minor semitone [[notes: In Pythagorean tuning, the minor semitone is represented by the ratio 256:243]], namely 256 and 243. By the tenth [proposition] of this book, I take the seventeenth part of the number **k** (243), which is 14 and 5/17. I then add these 14 and 5/17 to **k**, and let the resulting number be **l**, which is 257 and 5/17. Therefore, the ratio of **l** to **k** is a *sesquiseptimusdecimus* [[notes: A ratio of 18:17, or "one and one-seventeenth"]]. But **l** (257 and 5/17) exceeds **h**, which is only 256. Thus, by the eleventh [proposition] of this book, the minor semitone consists of a smaller proportion than the 18:17 ratio. Consequently, it follows even more strongly that it consists of a smaller proportion than the *sesquiseptadecima* [[notes: The author is distinguishing between slightly different ways of calculating these "one-and-a-fraction" ratios]]. The corollary of this is well known: by dividing the space of the outer limits of the tone in this manner, it is known from the fifth [proposition] of this book that the 18:17 and 17:16 ratios are established on either side, both of which this present demonstration has shown to be larger than the minor semitone.

## ¶ The sesquioctauadecima proportion: it turns out to be larger than the minor semitone.

| 256 | 1 | 256 | 243 |
|-----|---|-----|-----|
| c | 2 | a | b |

¶ Let **a** and **b** be the smallest numbers of the minor semitone found through the eighteenth [proposition] of this book, namely 256 and 243. By the tenth [proposition] of this book, I take the eighteenth part of the number **b**, which I find to be 13 and a half. When I add this to the number **b**, let the sum be **c**. The ratio of **c** to **b** is then a *sesquioctauadecima* [[notes: A ratio of 19:18]]. However, the number **c** is larger than **a**; for while **a** contains only 256, **c** contains an equal number of parts plus an additional half of one. Therefore, the 19:18 proportion is larger than the minor semitone.

## ¶ The sesquinonadecima: it is smaller than the minor semitone. From this, it follows that the minor semitone is situated between the 19:18 and 20:19 proportions.

| 256 | 255 | 15 | 243 |
|-----|-----|----|-----|
| a | c | 19 | b |

¶ As before, let the smallest terms of the minor semitone be **a** and **b**. I assert that the *sesquinonadecima* proportion [[notes: A ratio of 20:19]] is smaller than the proportion of **a** to **b**. For, by the tenth [proposition] of this book, I take the nineteenth part of number **b**, which I find to be 12 and 15/19. I add this part to number **b** and let it become **c**. The ratio of **c** to **b** is 20:19. But **a** (256) is greater than **c** (255 and 15/19). Therefore, by the eleventh [proposition] of this book, the minor semitone is larger than the 20:19 proportion, and the 20:19 proportion is smaller than the minor semitone. The corollary of this can be known from the fourteenth [proposition] of this book.

## ¶ To establish a major semitone: on a given string.

->[A diagram of a monochord string divided into segments. Points 'a' and 'c' are near the left end; 'd' is to their right; 'b' is at the far right.]<-

¶ Let **a b** and **c b** be the interval of a minor semitone [[notes: This represents the length of the string remaining for the notes]]. I divide **c b** into eight equal parts, and I create a part **c d** equal to one of those. Thus, **d b** contains nine such parts. Therefore, the ratio of **d b** to **c b** will be a tone [[notes: The 9:8 ratio]]. If I then subtract the minor semitone (the distance between **a b** and **c b**) from the whole tone (the distance between **d b** and **c b**), there remains the distance between **d b** and **a b**. This is the remaining part of the tone, which is the amount by which the tone exceeds the minor semitone. Therefore, by definition, the **d b** to **a b** interval is a major semitone [[notes: In this system, a Minor Semitone + a Major Semitone = one Whole Tone]] established on the given string, as was proposed. And just as you have placed the major semitone at the lower part [[notes: The "heavy" or longer end of the string]], it would be very easy to place it at the higher-pitched part as well.
