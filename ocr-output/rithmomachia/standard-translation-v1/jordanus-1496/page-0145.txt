### Bathillus / Alcmeon / Brontinus.

**Alcmeon:** You arrive just in time, Bathillus. **Alcmeon:** What is this, young men? **Bathillus:** Brontinus and I desire to learn a thing or two from you. **Alcmeon:** And what would that be, Bathillus? **Bathillus:** Before we encounter Pythagoras among the hundreds of his followers, we wish to be somewhat prepared in the basics. And since the disciples of Pythagoras are already returning from their nightly meeting, we seek to learn certain lighter matters that he did not intend to be kept secret. We have already thoroughly mastered the abacus and the counting table; [[notes: The *abacus* and *mensula* (counting table) were the standard tools for calculation before the widespread adoption of Arabic numerals and pen-and-paper math.]] now, however, we seek a certain game of "conflict" among you—one that might lighten our cares after serious study, and which you yourselves are accustomed to practice with numbers, as you do with many other things. **Alcmeon:** Perhaps you mean *Rithmomachia*, [[notes: *Rithmomachia* (The Battle of Numbers) was a complex medieval board game that taught Boethian number theory. It was often called "The Philosophers' Game."]] Bathillus. **Bathillus:** That very one, Alcmeon. **Alcmeon:** Come then, tell me, Bathillus, how many simple inequalities of numbers are there? **Bathillus:** Three: the *multiplex*, the *superparticular*, and the *superpartient*. [[notes: These are the three primary types of ratios in ancient mathematics. *Multiplex* are simple multiples (2, 4, 6). *Superparticular* is a ratio of (n+1)/n (e.g., 3:2). *Superpartient* is a ratio of (n+m)/n (e.g., 5:3).]] **Alcmeon:** That is, of the greater relationship to the lesser. And the game you and Brontinus now seek from me consists entirely of these three kinds. What is the *Greatest Harmony*, and what is the *Lesser Harmony*? **Bathillus:** The *Greatest Harmony* is when, among four numbers established in a geometric proportion, the extremes maintain an Arithmetic mean relative to one of the middle terms, and a Musical mean relative to the other. [[notes: This refers to the "Musical Proportion" (6:8:9:12), which contains all the primary mathematical means and the ratios of the basic musical intervals.]] And the *Lesser Harmony* maintains two means within only four terms. We have already learned what the Arithmetic, Geometric, and Musical means are called, and we are quite confident we can hold our own in them. **Alcmeon:** You have many things right, Bathillus. However, in this place, they use the name of the "Greatest Harmony" a bit more broadly: namely, it is spoken of whenever these three means of proportions are found within four terms—the larger following the smaller with difficulty. Soon they arrange the *multiples* in two rows from the first four even numbers and named after them: the doubles, quadruples, sextuples, and octuples, on a smooth, flat surface divided into sixty-four square spaces by intersecting lines. After these, they likewise place the similar *superparticulars* in two rows: the sesquialters, sesquiquartals, sesquisextals, and sesquioctavals. [[notes: These Latin terms describe specific ratios: *Sesquialter* is 3:2; *Sesquiquartal* is 5:4, etc.]] Next, they place the *superpartients* related and akin to them: the superbipartients, superquadripartients, supersextupartients, and superoctupartients. They call those spaces "fields" and the numbers arranged in them the "battle array of the evens." And directly opposite, in as many fields and by a similar process, they arrange the *multiples* derived from the first four odd numbers and named after them: the triples, quintuples, septuples, and nonuples. After these, the similar *superparticulars*. After the *superparticulars*, the *superpartients* of similar affinity. Thus, the battle arrays will be structured opposite each other in two rows: here the array of odds, and there the array of evens, in this manner:

**Bathillus:** I grasp this arrangement of numbers well enough. **Alcmeon:** Everywhere the plan corresponds, so that the fields placed opposite each other hold doubles, then sesquialters, then superbipartients; and again, those opposite each other hold quadruples, then sesquiquartals, then superquadripartients. And it is done in this way for the rest. However, the assigned numbers claim for themselves specific pieces inscribed with those same numbers: the *multiples* are **round**, the *superparticulars* are **triangular**, and the *superpartients* are **square**. Excepted is the number of the nineteenth place of the evens, which is a **Square Pyramid** [[notes: The Pyramid is a "boss" piece in Rithmomachia. It is a stack of squares. The "Even" pyramid is the sum of the first six squares: 1+4+9+16+25+36=91.]] arising from the first squares: and for this reason, it is called a pyramid of six bases and six faces. Therefore, the shape of a pyramid is owed to this number, which should have the first six squares inscribed upon it, with the larger placed under the smaller. This is called a *perfect Pyramid* because the tip and vertex of the pyramid do not fail to reach unity. In a similar way, the shape of an *imperfect square pyramid* is owed to the number in the twentieth place of the odds; three of the first squares are missing to complete this pyramid, and for that reason, it is called "triple-truncated" (*triccurta*). And because it rises from the five following squares, that same pyramid is said to have five bases and faces, and those five squares are inscribed with their sides in their own way.
