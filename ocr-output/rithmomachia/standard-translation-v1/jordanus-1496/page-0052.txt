## 55 ¶ Every number produced from only two prime numbers is deficient.

¶ Let **a** and **b** be two prime numbers which, when multiplied together, produce **c**. I say that **c** is deficient. For its parts [[notes: its proper divisors]] are **a**, **b**, and unity. Therefore, because **c** is produced from **a** multiplied by **b**, and **a** is greater than unity, it follows that **b** must be smaller than the whole number **c**. And because **b** is prime, it has no other divisor except unity; the same is true for **a**. Therefore, the sum of **a**, **b**, and unity cannot reach the total value of **c** [[notes: For example, if we take the primes 3 and 5, their product is 15. The divisors are 1, 3, and 5. Their sum is 9, which is less than 15, making 15 a "deficient" number]].

## 56 ¶ If a number is produced from any number of primes, and the greatest of them is less than all the others taken together with unity, it is necessary that the number is abundant.

¶ If a number is formed by multiplying several primes together—for instance, **a, b,** and **c**—and the largest prime factor is smaller than the sum of the other factors plus one, that number will always be "abundant" [[notes: An "abundant" (*habundans*) number is one where the sum of its proper divisors exceeds the number itself. For example, in the number 30 (2 × 3 × 5), the largest prime 5 is less than 2 + 3 + 1. The divisors of 30 (1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15) sum to 42, which is greater than 30]].
