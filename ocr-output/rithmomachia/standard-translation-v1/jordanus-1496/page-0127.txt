¶ The diatonic Hypolydian exceeds the diatonic Hypophrygian by a semitone; it exceeds the Dorian by three semitones [[notes: *trisemitonio*, a minor third]]; the Phrygian by a fourth; the Lydian by a fourth and a semitone; and the Mixolydian by the consonance of a fourth and a minor third. Furthermore, the Dorian exceeds the Hypolydian by a tone; the Phrygian by two tones [[notes: *ditono*, a major third]]; the Lydian by a fourth; and the Mixolydian by a fourth. The Lydian, however, sounds a tone above the Dorian, while the Lydian is three semitones above [the Phrygian] and the Mixolydian a fourth. The Lydian is a semitone above the Phrygian, and the Mixolydian is a minor third; finally, the Mixolydian is a tone above the Lydian.

¶ I create the chromatic fifteen-string scale [[notes: *pentadecachordum*]] **r** through the tenth, eleventh, and twelfth rules of this book, and similarly six others, namely **s, t, v, x, y, z**. I make the scale **s** higher than **r** by a tone; and scale **t** higher than **s** by a semitone. Then **v** is higher than **t** by the interval of an apotome. **x** is higher than **v** by a minor third [[notes: *trisemitonio*]]. **y** is a semitone higher than **x**, and **z** exceeds **y** by an apotome. Therefore, I say that the seven modes of chromatic modulation are thus ordered. For **r** will be the Hypodorian, **s** by definition the chromatic Hypophrygian, **t** the Hypolydian, **v** the Dorian, **x** the Phrygian, **y** the Lydian, and **z** the Mixolydian.

[[margin: 28]]
### ¶ To show how each individual chromatic mode corresponds to the others.

¶ By the preceding, the chromatic Hypophrygian is distant from the Hypodorian by a tone, and the Hypolydian from the Hypophrygian by a semitone. Therefore, the Hypolydian is higher than the Hypodorian by a minor third. The Dorian is distant from the Hypolydian by an apotome; therefore, the Dorian is removed from the Hypodorian by two tones [[notes: *ditono*]]. The Phrygian is distant from the Dorian by a minor third; therefore, the Phrygian is removed from the Hypodorian by three tones and a semitone—that is, by the interval of a full consonance of a fifth [[notes: *diapente*]]. The Lydian is distant from the Phrygian by a semitone; therefore, the Lydian is a fifth plus a semitone from the Hypodorian. The Mixolydian is distant from the Lydian by an apotome; therefore, the Mixolydian is higher than the Hypodorian by the consonance of a fifth and a tone. In this manner, the remaining relationships are easily shown from the preceding: such as that the Hypolydian is a semitone from the Hypophrygian, the Dorian a tone, the Phrygian a fourth, the Lydian a fourth and a semitone, and the Mixolydian a fifth. The Dorian is distant from the Phrygian by an apotome, the Phrygian by two tones, the Lydian by a fourth, and the Mixolydian by a tritone [[notes: three whole tones]]. The Phrygian is distant from the Dorian by a minor third, the Lydian by a tone and two minor semitones, and the Mixolydian by a fourth. The Lydian is a semitone from the Phrygian and the Mixolydian is a tone. As has already been said, the Mixolydian is distant from the Lydian by a major semitone [[notes: *maiore semitonio*, here referring to the apotome]]. Thus the proposition is constructed.
