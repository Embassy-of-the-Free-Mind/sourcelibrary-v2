# ¶ To establish the Chromatic of the principal tetrachords.
<page-num>10</page-num>

| | | | |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: |
| Proslambanomenos [[notes: The "added note" at the bottom of the Greek scale, corresponding to a low A]] | A | | A |
| Hypate hypaton [[notes: The "lowest of the low" notes]] | b | Tone | b |
| Parhypate hypaton | c | Minor Semitone | c |
| Lichanos hypaton | d | Apotome [[notes: The "major semitone" in Pythagorean tuning]] | d |
| Hypate meson [[notes: The "lowest of the middle" notes]] | e | Trisemitonium [[notes: An interval of three semitones, known today as a minor third]] | e |

¶ Timotheus of Miletus [[notes: A famous Greek musician and poet known for his innovations and for adding strings to the lyre]] once introduced Chromatic [melodies] among the Lacedemonians [[notes: The Spartans]], providing a softer song than the previous Diatonic: in which mode of singing we now seek the *hypaton* tetrachord [[notes: A tetrachord is a block of four notes spanning the interval of a fourth]]. Therefore, let **b c d e** be the tetrachord assigned to the *hypatae* [[notes: The principal or lowest notes]] to be established in chromatic melody. I place the string **a** before it: let this be the **proslambanomenos**: which I make a **sesquioctave** [[notes: A 9:8 ratio]] to **b** (the *hypate hypaton*), the first string of the tetrachord to be established. Then I make **a** a **sesquialter** [[notes: A 3:2 ratio, forming a perfect fifth]] to **e** (the *hypate meson*): and so, as in all cases, **a** to **b** will be a tone, and **a** to **e** a **diapente** [[notes: A perfect fifth]]. Then I make **b** to **c** a minor semitone and **b** to **d** a tone. Therefore, **c** to **d** will be an **apotome**. And because, by the thirteenth [proposition], if a tone (**a b**) is taken away from the consonance of a **diapente** (**a e**), a **diatessaron** [[notes: A perfect fourth]] is left behind. Therefore, **b e** will measure a **diatessaron**. But since, by the sixth [proposition] of the third [book], a **diatessaron** consists of two tones and a minor semitone, and **b c** and **c d** together make a tone: therefore **d e** contains a tone and a minor semitone. Thus, **d e** is a **trisemitonium**. Since, therefore, **b c** (*hypate hypaton* and *parhypate hypaton*) is a minor semitone, and **c d** (*parhypate hypaton* and *lichanos hypaton*) is a major semitone [[notes: Referred to here as an apotome]], and **d e** (*lichanos hypaton* and *hypate meson*) is a **trisemitonium** as has been shown: it is established by definition that the tetrachord **b c d e** is constituted in the chromatic genus. For we call the **trisemitonium** and the **trihemitonium** the same thing.

# ¶ To join the middle Chromatic tetrachord.
<page-num>11</page-num>

| | | | |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: |
| Proslambanomenos | A | | A |
| Hypate hypaton | b | Tone | b |
| Parhypate hypaton | c | Minor Semitone | c |
| Lichanos hypaton | d | Apotome | d |
| Hypate meson | e | Trisemitonium | e |
| Parhypate meson | f | Minor Semitone | f |
| Lichanos meson | g | Apotome | g |
| Mese [[notes: The "middle" note, the central pivot of the Greek system]] | h | Trisemitonium | h |

¶ Let **e f g h** be the middle tetrachord (*meson*) to be established in the chromatic genus: I make **a** to **h** a double and sounding **diapason** [[notes: An octave, having a 2:1 ratio]]. And I make **e** to **f** a minor semitone, and likewise I make **e** to **g** a tone. Thus **f** to **g** will be an **apotome**. But since the consonance of a **diapente** has been shown from **a** to **e**, if that is subtracted from the **diapason** consonance of **a h**, then by the twenty-second [proposition] of the third [book], a **diatessaron** remains. Therefore, **e** to **h** (*hypate meson* to *mese*) is a **diatessaron**. And since a **diatessaron** fills out two tones and a minor semitone, and **e g** is a tone, therefore **g h** contains a tone and a minor semitone. Thus **g h** will be a **trisemitonium**. Therefore **e f g h**, consisting of two semitones (**e f** and **f g**) and the **trisemitonium** (**g h**), will be a tetrachord established in the chromatic genus. This was to be demonstrated.
