# The contents of this work.

**Arithmetic**, demonstrated in ten books
**Music**, demonstrated in four books
An **Epitome** [[notes: A concise summary or abridgment]] of the books on arithmetic by the divine **Severinus Boethius** [[notes: An influential 6th-century philosopher whose works on the "quadrivium" (arithmetic, music, geometry, and astronomy) were the standard textbooks of the Middle Ages.]]
The game of **Rithmimachia**, which is also called the "Battle of Numbers" [[notes: A complex medieval board game designed to teach mathematical proportions and harmony.]]

### G. Gonterius of Labilon: In praise of Arithmetic and Music.

For a long time now, the learned sisters have lain hidden,
Whom golden-haired Minerva [[notes: The Roman goddess of wisdom and the arts.]] keeps as her companions.
Now, after many ages, they peacefully revisit the earth,
And the pleasing land of Gaul [[notes: France]] pleases them above all others.
Once, all the Achaean [[notes: Greek]] land was famous for them,
Back when Pythagoras fled his homeland of Samos.
Now they leave Greece and the learned cities of Pallas,
Seeking the Seine and the homes of Paris.
The one [[notes: Arithmetic]] comes surrounded by a crowd of every kind of number,
And teaches through numbers whatever exists in the world.
The other [[notes: Music]], with sweet-sounding song and tuneful strings,
Is said to have tamed both men and wild beasts;
She alone holds her name among the Pierian Muses,
And nothing is usually more pleasing to the Muses than she.
Yet the "Staple of the Sea" [[notes: A wordplay on the name of the author, Jacques Lefèvre d’Étaples; "stapule marine" refers to the town of Étaples, a trading port]] sent the master
Who, with a joyful brow, might dedicate this work to your studies.
This is his only study and this his care: to be of help,
Lest his labor be in vain or come to nothing.
