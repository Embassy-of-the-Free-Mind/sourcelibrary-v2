¶ Let the numbers **a b c d e f g h k l m n o p q** be the numbers of the **Diatonic pentadecachord** [[notes: A fifteen-string scale spanning two octaves]] established in the first position, found according to the eighth rule of this book. I place **a b e h k n q** again in a second position: in the corresponding places, I change **c** in the second position to **d**, and **f** to **g**; **l** to **m**, and **o** to **p**. With these arranged in the second position: from those in the second position, I take the difference of **b d**, and I add half of it to **b**, and let it become **c**. Similarly, I take the difference of **e g** and add half of the difference to **g**, and let it become **f**. I take the difference of **k m** and with the half of the difference added to **m**, let it become **l**. Finally, I take the difference of **n p** and with the half added to **p**, let it become **o**. 

I declare that the numbers **a b c d e f g h k l m n o p q** placed in the second position are the numbers of the **Enharmonic pentadecachord**. For **a b** will be a tone. **b c** and **c d** are two **dieses** [[notes: In the Enharmonic genus, the semitone is split into two very small intervals called quarter-tones or 'dieses']] constituting the minor semitone **b d**. For that which was **b c** in the Diatonic is made **b d** in the Enharmonic, and **d e** is a **ditone** [[notes: A major third, consisting of two whole tones]]. For what is **c e** in the Diatonic is here **d e** in the Enharmonic. But in the Diatonic modulation, **c e** is a ditone, for it contains the tone **c d** and the tone **d e**. In this same manner, **e f g** will be found to be two dieses, and **g h** a ditone. **h k** is a tone; **k l m** two dieses; **m n** a ditone. **n o p** two dieses; **p q** a ditone. Thus, the numbers of the Enharmonic pentadecachord have been collected just as was proposed. This is what was intended.

<margin>17</margin> ¶ That the Diatonic **parhypate hypaton** passes into the **lichanos** in Enharmonic modulation; and the **parhypate meson** into the **lichanos meson**; and the **trite** into the **paranetes**.

¶ This is immediately easy to see. For in the Diatonic pentadecachord, a tone falls between the **proslambanomenos** [[notes: The lowest "added" note]] and the **hypate hypaton**; and a minor semitone falls between the **hypate** and the **parhypate hypaton**. In the Enharmonic, however, a tone similarly falls between the proslambanomenos and the hypate hypaton; and a minor semitone falls between the hypate and the **lichanos hypaton**. Therefore, the Diatonic *parhypate* is changed in Enharmonic modulation and passes into the *lichanos*. This can be shown for the remaining notes in the same way. This is also easily perceived by the senses: if the *proslambanomeni* are placed in unison in both the Diatonic and Enharmonic genera of modulation, then it will be manifest that the *lichanos* of the latter is equal in sound (unison) to the *parhypate* of the former; and likewise, the *paranetes* of the latter are equal in sound to the *tritis* of the former.

<margin>18</margin> ¶ Certain notes agree across the three genera of modulation: the **proslambanomeni**, the **principal hypatae**, the **middle hypatae**, the **mese**, the **paramese**, the **nete** (both disjunct and conjunct), and the **excellent netes** [[notes: These are the "fixed" notes that provide the skeleton of the scale; they do not change pitch regardless of whether the music is Diatonic, Chromatic, or Enharmonic]].

¶ For in all three genera—through the fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, tenth, eleventh, twelfth, and fifteenth rules—the *proslambanomeni* sound a tone relative to the *hypatae hypaton*. These same notes sound a **diapente** [[notes: A perfect fifth]] relative to the *hypatae meson*, a **diapason** [[notes: An octave]] to the *mesas*, and a **diapason plus a diapente** [[notes: An octave plus a fifth, or a twelfth]] to the *netas disiunctas*. To the *netas coniunctas*, they would sound a **diapason plus a diatessaron** [[notes: An octave plus a fourth, or an eleventh]] if the tone were removed. And to the *netas excellētes*, the *proslambanomeni* harmonize as a **bis diapason** [[notes: A double octave]]. Therefore, the voices that have been brought forward agree across the three genera. This is what was proposed.

¶ We call those strings **stable** and **immobile** which, in every division of the pentadecachords, maintain the same intervals and the same relationship to the *proslambanomenon*. We call those **unstable** and **mobile** which do not maintain this. Thus, we shall find the stable and unstable notes. Since, according to the preceding rule, the *proslambanomeni*, the principal *hypatae*, the middle *hypatae*, the *mese*, the *paramese*, and the *netai* (whether conjunct, disjunct, or excellent) agree in the three genera, they maintain the same intervals, as is now clear from what has been demonstrated. Therefore, the *proslambanomenos*, *hypate hypaton*, *hypate meson*, *mese*, *paramese*, *nete synezeugmenon*, *nete diezeugmenon*, and *nete hyperboleon* will be simply immobile and firm.
