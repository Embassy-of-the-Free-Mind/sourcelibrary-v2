This page features a decorative sheet of **marbled paper**, a technique traditionally used in bookbinding for endpapers—the sheets of paper that are pasted to the inside of the covers. 

The pattern displayed here is known as a **"shell"** or **"stone"** pattern. It consists of large, irregular spots of **dark brown** and **black**, which are intricately outlined by veins of **tan**, **muted yellow**, and **dark red**. This organic, textured appearance was meant to mimic the look of polished stone or minerals.

[[notes: To create this effect, a craftsman would float pigments on a bath of thickened liquid (often made from carrageenan or gum tragacanth) and use tools like combs or brushes to manipulate the colors before laying a sheet of paper on top to "lift" the design. Because the paint moves freely on the liquid surface, no two sheets of marbled paper are ever identical.]]
