¶ Let **k l m n o p q** be used to complete the remaining tetrachords: since the *meson* [[notes: middle]] tetrachord is separated from the *neton diezeugmenon* [[notes: the "disjunct" tetrachord, usually starting an octave above the lowest note]] tetrachord, I therefore make the distance from the *mese* [[notes: the central note, "middle"]] to the *paramese diezeugmenon* a full tone. I also make the *mese* consonant with the *nete diezeugmenon* as a **diapente** [[notes: a perfect fifth]]. And I make it a **diapason** [[notes: an octave]] with the *nete hyperboleon* [[notes: the highest note of the scale]]. I divide the tetrachord **k l m n** just like the *hypaton* tetrachord; however, I divide the tetrachord **n o p q** as in the preceding *meson* tetrachord, and both will be divided according to the Chromatic genus. Since it is known that **a** to **h** is a **diapason**, and **h** to **q** is likewise a **diapason**, it follows that **a** to **q** sounds a **bis diapason** [[notes: a double octave]]. Since it is comprised of fifteen voices and strings, it is clear that what was proposed has been achieved. But if you make **k l m** the *synemmenon* [[notes: the "connected" tetrachord used for modulation]] tetrachord, you should divide it like the Chromatic *meson* tetrachord; you will see the proposal is easily accomplished.

¶ Let **b** and **c** be the smallest numbers of the minor semitone, found through the eighteenth [proposition] of the second [book] of this work. Since **b** lacks a ninth part, I increase **b** and **c** by nine, and let **b** and **c** come into the second place. Therefore, since in the second place **b** has a ninth part, I increase it by eight and let it become **d**; then **b** to **d**, through the seventh [proposition] of the second [book] of the Arithmetic, will be a **sesquioctave** [[notes: a 9:8 ratio, the mathematical definition of a whole tone]]. Therefore, **b** and **d** contain a tone. And **b** to **c**, by the same logic, is a minor semitone. Therefore, **c** to **d** is an **apotome** [[notes: the remainder of a tone after a minor semitone is removed]]. Again, since **b** has an eighth part, I add its own eighth part to it and let it become **e**: **a** to **b** will be a **sesquioctave** and a tone; and since **a** has a third part, I make **a** to **e** a **sesquialter** [[notes: a 3:2 ratio, or a perfect fifth]]. And since **h** has a second part, I likewise make **a** a double to **h**. I find likewise that **h** has a ninth, a third, and a second part. I therefore make **h** a **sesquioctave** to **k**, a **sesquialter** to **n**, and a double to **q**. This done, I multiply **b** of the second place into **a b c d e h k n q** and they come into the third place as **a b c d e h k n q**. Then I multiply **c** and **d** of the second place into **e** of the second place, and **f** and **g** come into **k**; and **l** and **m** come into **n**, and **o** and **p** come into the third place. By the seventh and eighth [propositions] of the second [book] of Arithmetic, the numbers of the Chromatic fifteen-string scale will be in the third [column]: **e** and [others] are the numbers of the **diapente**. And **a** and **b** are the numbers of the tone. With the tone **a b** removed, there remains **b e**, a **diatessaron** [[notes: a perfect fourth]], and **b c** and **c d** together are a tone. Therefore, by the sixth [proposition] of the third [book], **d e** is a **tritemitonium**. And **a h** is a **diapason**, and **a e** is a **diapente**. Therefore, **e h** is a **diatessaron**. And by the eighth [proposition] of the second [book] of Arithmetic, **e f** and **f g** are a minor semitone and an apotome. Therefore, **g h** is a **tritemitonium**, and you will demonstrate in a similar way that **h k** is a tone, **k l** and **l m** are two semitones, and **m n** a **tritemitonium**, and **n o p q** two semitones and **p q** a **tritemitonium**. Thus the proposal becomes clear.

<margin>14</margin> ¶ That the *parhypate* of the Diatonic and Chromatic correspond to the *parhypate*: the *paramese* to the *paramese*: and the *trite* to the *trite*.

¶ For in both genera, the *hypate* to the *parhypate* observes the interval of a minor semitone. And the *mese* to the *paramese* a tone. And the *trite* to the *trite* a minor semitone. It is therefore known that those small voices in each mode of singing correspond to one another. And not only is this true, but the *hypate hypaton* and *mese meson* and *nete neton* also correspond. For in both, the *proslambanomenos* to the *hypate hypaton* sounds a tone; and to the *hypate meson* a **diapente**; to the *mese* a **diapason**; to the *nete* of the disjunct [tetrachord] a **diapason** and **diapente**; and to the *nete hyperboleon* a **bis diapason**. Therefore, what was proposed, and even more, is easily understood.
