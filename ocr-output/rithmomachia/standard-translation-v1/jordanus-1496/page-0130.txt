# ¶ Jacques Lefèvre d'Étaples to the magnificent lord: Gian Stefano Ferrero, Bishop-elect of Vercelli, a most devoted lover of studies.

<margin>b</margin>
In these days, now that the elements of Arithmetic and Music alike have been committed to **metallic types** [[notes: *metallicis formulis*; a reference to the then-revolutionary technology of movable type printing]], I was considering, most worthy prelate, what might prepare the way for an easy understanding of them. It occurred to me that an **Introduction** should be brought forward, drawn from that divine and Pythagorean *Principles of Numbers* by the holy Severinus Boethius [[notes: Boethius (c. 477–524 AD) was a Roman statesman and philosopher whose textbooks on the "Quadrivium" (arithmetic, music, geometry, and astronomy) were the standard authorities in medieval and Renaissance universities]]. This work would serve as a domestic and familiar guide, to be held in hand before the reading of others and before facing such great weights of mathematical demonstrations. For it is so arranged that unless the mind is properly prepared, it can reap no benefit from the disciplines. Just as skilled physicians provide digestive potions before administering strong medicines to ensure a firmer recovery of health, so too in every kind of discipline it is worthwhile to provide introductions, so that we may attain an easier understanding of the subject—as if achieving a certain perfect health of our own mind.

For this reason, I have immediately undertaken an **Epitome** [[notes: A condensed summary or handbook designed for students]] from the books of the holy Severinus, the utility of which will be manifold. Once this is mastered, students will approach the demonstrations of numbers as if they were initiated and prepared by certain Pythagorean rites [[notes: The followers of Pythagoras viewed mathematics not just as a tool, but as a spiritual initiation into the secrets of the universe]]. They will then grasp with great ease the teachings of those ancient books, so full of wisdom, on the *Principles of Arithmetic*. Indeed, from the final comparison of the systems of the holy Severinus and **Jordanus** [[notes: Jordanus de Nemore, a 13th-century mathematician whose work provided the more advanced proofs that Lefèvre printed alongside Boethius's foundational text]], they will most easily follow the entire commentary on the most elegant and never-sufficiently-praised study of numbers. It will provide a very easy path and entrance to whatever things require the labor of numbers.

Therefore, most worthy prelate, I have not hesitated to dedicate this little introduction to your name, even though the matter is small when your dignity is taken into account. For I know how fervently you embrace all studies and how diligently you are occupied in them, being a proponent of what is nearly a **Socratic school**: that there is no good except wisdom, and no good except the study of letters. Thus, my devotion to you in this literary matter cannot fail to be pleasing, for by honoring letters and all learned men, you ensure that all letters must, in turn, honor you. I only wish that I might one day be able to do justice to your virtue, which has been proven by long acquaintance—I, your constant and diligent admirer.

->**Farewell.**<-
