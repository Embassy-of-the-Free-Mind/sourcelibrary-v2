### ¶ To investigate the multiple superparticular which, when doubled, produces a multiple superparticular.

¶ Take any number **a**, which I multiply by itself to produce **b**. To this I add one, and let the whole be **c**. It is manifest that the ratio of **c** to **a** is a *multiple superparticular* [[notes: A ratio where the larger number contains the smaller multiple times, plus one extra part, such as 5:2 (two and a half)]]. If this ratio is multiplied by itself, the result is as much as **a** squared, plus the part twice, and the part multiplied by itself. But **a** squared and the part taken twice produces a whole number, and the part by itself produces a fraction. Therefore, that which is produced is a number and a part. Thus, the compounded ratio will be a multiple superparticular. You could also prove this as in the fifty-ninth [proposition] of this book.

[[margin: 66]]
### ¶ To inquire into the multiple superparticular which, when taken twice, composes a multiple superpartient.

¶ Let **a** be any odd number and **b** be its nearest even number; let its half be **c**. I take a multiple superparticular whose multiple number is **c** and whose part is named from **a**. Let this ratio be **d** to **e**. I say that the multiple superparticular ratio **d** to **e**, when taken twice [[notes: Meaning squared or compounded]], constitutes a *multiple superpartient* [[notes: A ratio where the larger number contains the smaller multiple times, plus several fractional parts]]. For if this is multiplied by itself, there will result the square of **c** and similar parts taken according to **b**, and a part of the square of **a**. Those parts, however, constitute one [whole] and one remains over with it. And because those two parts, by the twelfth [proposition] of this book, do not constitute a single whole, the product will be a number and parts. Therefore, the compounded ratio will be a multiple superpartient. In the proposed case, it would be a "quintuple superquadrupartient ninths" [[notes: This refers to the ratio 49:9, or 5 and 4/9]].

### ¶ To examine the multiple superpartient which, when doubled, composes a multiple superparticular.

¶ I take, through the sixty-third [proposition] of this book, a *superpartient* which, when doubled, composes a multiple superparticular. Let the number naming the parts be **a**, the numerator be **b**, and let **c** be one greater than **a**. Let **c** be multiplied by **a**, and let **b** be added to the product. Let the whole be **d**. It is manifest that the ratio of **d** to **a** is a multiple superpartient, where the number naming the multiple is **c**, the denominator of the parts is **a**, and their number is **b**. I say that this ratio, when doubled, is a multiple superparticular. For its denomination multiplied by itself would produce a number and a part, which is easy to see as in the preceding sections.

### ¶ To reduce a given ratio into ratios of which any number are equal superparticulars.

¶ Let the smallest terms of the given ratio be **a** and **b**, and let **c** be the number according to which equal superparticulars are to be taken. Then let the larger **c** be multiplied by **a** and by **b**, and let them become **d** and **e**, but in such a way that between **d** and **e** there are more numbers than there are units in **c**. Let the first after **d**, which will be the greatest of them, be **f**. It is manifest that the ratio of **f** to **d** is a superparticular ratio, and the smallest of all those that are continuous between the numbers from **d** up to **e**, because it is denominated by the smaller part. Continue the ratio of **f** to **d** taken according to the number **c**. And because **l** [is to] **m**, the ratio of **h** to **m** will be composed of the ratios of **f** to **d** taken according to the number **c**. And because **b**, by the nineteenth [proposition] of the third book, is in **h** a certain number of times, let **e** be in **n** just as many times. It is manifest that **h** is to **n** as **d** is to **e**, and therefore as **a** is to **b**. It is therefore established that the ratio **a** to **b** is divided into ratios of which several taken will be equal superparticulars according to the assigned number, which is what was proposed.
