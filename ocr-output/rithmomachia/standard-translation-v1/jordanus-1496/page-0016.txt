[[margin: 13]]
# If the ratio of the whole to the whole is greater than the ratio of the part removed to the part removed, then the ratio of the remainder to the remainder will be greater than the ratio of the whole to the whole.

[[notes: This proposition explains how inequalities "grow" when you look at what is left over. If you take a larger proportional "slice" out of a second quantity than you do from the first, the leftover piece of the first quantity is proportionally even larger than the original whole was.]]

<image-desc>Diagram for proposition 13: Two sets of semicircles at the top. The first shows the number 8 split into 3 and 5, labeled a and b. The second shows the number 4 split into 2 and 2, labeled c and d. Below these are mathematical representations: 64 (split into 24 and 40) labeled e, f, g; 32 (split into 16 and 16) labeled h; and straight lines for 12 and 20 labeled k and l.</image-desc>

Let **a b** be a whole, from which **a** is removed and **b** is the remainder. Likewise, let **c d** be another whole, from which **c** is removed and **d** is the remainder. Let the proportion of the whole **a b** to the whole **c d** be greater than **a** to **c**. I say that the proportion of the remainder **b** to the remainder **d** is greater than the proportion of the whole **a b** to the whole **c d**. 

For I multiply **a b** by **a** and by **b**, and let **e f** result, of which **e** is the subtracted part and **f** the remainder. And I also multiply **a b** by **c** and by **d**, and let **g h** result, of which **g** is the subtracted part and **h** is the remainder. Next, I shall multiply **a** by **c d** and let **k** result; let **k** be subtracted from the whole **g h** and let **l** be left behind. 

Since by the ninth [proposition] of the first [book], that which is produced by multiplying any number by all the parts of a whole is equal to that which is produced by multiplying that same number by the whole itself: the whole **e f** will be equal to that produced by multiplying **a b** by itself. And since the same **a b** multiplied by itself produced **e** (the part removed from **e f**), then by the seventh [proposition] of this [book], whatever the proportion of **a b** to **a** is, the same will be **e f** to **e**. By the same reasoning, as **a b** is to **b**, so is **e f** to **f**. And as **a b** is to **c d**, so is **e f** to **g h**. And as **c d** is to **c**, so is **g h** to **g**. And **g h** to **h** as **c d** is to **d**. 

And since two numbers—namely the whole **a b** and the part removed **a**—have been multiplied by the same **c d**, and the number **a b** produced the number **g h** and **a** produced the number **k**: by the eighth [proposition], whatever the proportion of **a b** to **a** is, the same is **g h** to **k**. But since **e** is produced from **a** times **a b**, and **k** from **a** times **c d**, by the seventh [proposition], the proportion of **a b** to **c d** will be the same as **e** to **k**. Therefore, as **e f** is to **g h**, so is **e** to **k** (the part removed to the part removed). Therefore, by the fifth [proposition], as **e f** is to **g h**, so is **f** to **l** (the remainder to the remainder). 

And since the proportion of **e** to **g** is the same as **a** to **c**, the proportion of **e** to **g** will be less than **e** to **k**. Wherefore, by the tenth [proposition], **k** is less than **g**. Therefore **l** (which is shown by **h** and the difference between **k** and **g**) will be greater than **h**. Therefore, by the same tenth [proposition], the proportion of **f** to **h** (the smaller number) is greater than **f** to **l**. But the proportion of **f** to **l** was proved to be the same as the proportion of **e f** to **g h**, and therefore the same as the proportion of the whole **a b** to the whole **c d**. Therefore, the proportion of **f** to **h**, which is the same as the proportion of the remainder **b** to the remainder **d**, is greater than the proportion of the whole **a b** to the whole **c d**. Which is what was to be shown.

[[margin: 14]]
# But if the ratio of the part removed to the part removed is greater than the ratio of the whole to the whole, the ratio of the remainder to the remainder will be less than the ratio of the whole to the whole. From these it also becomes clear that if the ratio of the remainder to the remainder is as the part removed to the part removed, the ratio of the part removed to the part removed is as the whole to the whole.

[[notes: This is the converse of Proposition 13. It also includes a "Corollary" (though not explicitly labeled as such in the header) explaining that if the remainders and parts are in the same ratio, then the wholes are also in that same ratio.]]

<image-desc>Diagram for proposition 14: Two semicircles. The first represents the number 10 split into 6 and 4, labeled a, b, c. The second represents the number 5 split into 2 and 3.</image-desc>

Let **a b** be some whole number, with **a** removed and **b** the remainder; and **c d** another whole, with **c** removed and **d** the remainder. Let the proportion of **a** to **c** be greater than **a b** to **c d**. I say that the proportion of **b** to **d** is less than the proportion of **a b** to **c d**. 

Because the proportion of **a** to **c** is greater than **a b** to **c d**, by the eleventh [proposition] of this [book], conversely, the proportion of **c** to **a** will be less than **c d** to **a b**. Thus, since the proportion of the whole **c d** to the whole **a b** is greater than the part removed **c** to the part removed **a**, then by the preceding [proposition], the proportion of the remainder **d** to the remainder **b** is greater than the whole **c d** to the whole **a b**. 

I understand, therefore, **d** as the first number, **b** as the second, **c d** as the third, and **a b** as the fourth. Because the proportion of **d** to **b** is greater than **c d** to **a b**, by the eleventh [proposition], conversely, the proportion of **b** to **d** is less than **a b** to **c d**. Thus, it is known that the proportion of the whole to the whole is greater than that of the remainder to the remainder, which was proposed. 

The Corollary is evident: for if the proportion of the whole to the whole were greater than the part removed to the part removed, then by the preceding [proposition], the proportion of the remainder to the remainder would be greater than the whole to the whole. Thus, by a much stronger reason, the proportion of the remainder to the remainder would be greater than the part removed to the part removed, which is against the hypothesis. If, however, the proportion of the whole to the whole is less than the part removed to the part removed, then by the present [proposition], the proportion of the remainder to the remainder will be less than the whole to the whole. Wherefore, and by a much stronger reason, the proportion of the remainder to the remainder would be less than the part removed to the part removed, which is again against the hypothesis. Since, therefore, the proportion of the whole to the whole can be neither greater nor less than the part removed to the part removed, it remains that it is equal to it.

[[margin: 15]]
# If the ratio of the first to the second is greater than the third to the fourth, then the ratio of the first and second [combined] to the second will be greater than the ratio of the third and fourth to the fourth; but compared to the first, it will be less than the third and fourth to the third.

[[notes: This proposition deals with "composing" ratios—essentially adding the denominator to the numerator. If a/b > c/d, then (a+b)/b > (c+d)/d.]]

<image-desc>Diagram for proposition 15: Two semicircles. The first represents the number 8 split into 6 and 2, labeled a, b, c. The second represents the number 10 split into 5 and 5, labeled d.</image-desc>

If the proportion of **a** to **b** is greater than **c** to **d**: I say first, that the proportion of **a b** to **b** is greater than **c d** to **d**. Second, that **a b** to **a** is less than **c d** to **c**. 

The first is clear: for if **a b** to **b** and **c d** to **d** are assumed to be equal, such that the proportion of **a b** to **b** is the same as **c d** to **d**, then alternately by the third [proposition] of this [book], the proportion of **a b** to **c d** will be as **b** to **d**. Therefore, by the fifth [proposition], the proportion of **a b** to **c d** will also be as **a** to **c** (the remainder to the remainder). Therefore, it would also be as **b** to **d**. And since **a** to **c** and **b** to **d** are similar [proportions], then alternately by the third [proposition], the proportion of **a** to **b** is as **c** to **d**, which is against the hypothesis. 

But if it is assumed that the proportion of **a b** to **b** is less and **c d** to **d** is greater: since **c d** to **d** is a greater proportion than **a b** to **b**, then by the twelfth [proposition] of this [book], **c d** to **a b** is greater than **d** to **b** (the part removed to the part removed). Therefore, by the thirteenth [proposition], **c** to **a** is greater (remainder to remainder) than **c d** to **a b**. Wherefore, it is much more so than **d** to **b**. Since, therefore, **c** to **a** is greater than **d** to **b**, by the eleventh [proposition], **a** to **c** is less than **b** to **d**. 

I understand here four numbers: **b** the first, **d** the second, **a** the third, and **c** the fourth. Because the proportion of **b** to **d** is greater than **a** to **c**, by the twelfth [proposition], the proportion of **b** to **a** is greater than **d** to **c**. Therefore, by the eleventh [proposition], **a** to **b** is less than **c** to **d**, which is against the hypothesis. It remains, therefore, that since the proportion of **a b** to **b** can be neither equal nor less, it must be greater than the proportion of **c d** to **d**. This is the first part.
