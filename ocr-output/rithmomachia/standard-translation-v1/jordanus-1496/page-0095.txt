**Consonance** is a mixture of a low and a high sound, falling sweetly and uniformly on the ears, perfected from a multiple or superparticular ratio. [[notes: A "superparticular" ratio is one where the numerator is exactly one greater than the denominator, such as 3:2 or 4:3.]] ¶ **Dissonance** is a harsh and unpleasant striking reaching the ear of two sounds that do not naturally mix sweetly. ¶ **Tone** is the beginning of consonance, arising from a *sesquioctave* ratio [[notes: 9:8 ratio]] of one sound to another. ¶ **Minor Semitone**, which is also called a *diesis*, is a part of a tone by which a *sesquitertia* ratio [[notes: a perfect Fourth, 4:3]] is greater than two tones. ¶ **Major Semitone**, which they also call an **Apothome**, is the remaining part of a tone, and that which exceeds the minor semitone itself. ¶ **Comma** is that by which a *sesquioctave* ratio is greater than two minor semitones; it is also the same amount by which an *apothome* conquers and exceeds a minor semitone. ¶ **Schisma** is half of a comma. ¶ **Diaschisma** is half of a minor semitone. ¶ **Hemispherium:** a musical instrument [[notes: likely a movable bridge for a monochord]] through which, when placed properly under a nerve or string, we investigate by our senses the semitones, tones, consonances, and the small parts of consonances. ¶ **Emmelic sound** is that which we use fittingly in a melody. ¶ **Ecmelic sound** is said to be that which a melody or harmony does not admit. ¶ **Equal and similar sounds** are said to be those which are born from the same ratio of interval. ¶ That part of **Numbers** and intervals is greater which is named by a smaller number, and smaller which is named by a larger number. [[notes: This refers to fractions; 1/2 is larger than 1/3.]] ¶ A **Multiple ratio** is greater when named by a larger number, and smaller when named by a smaller number. ¶ A **Superparticular ratio** is greater when named by a larger part, and smaller when named by a smaller part.

### ¶ To place a tone upon a given string.

[[notes: The image shows a string $a$—$b$ with a division point $c$ to illustrate the 9:8 ratio.]]

¶ Let **a b** be any string upon which we are ordered to place a tone: I divide the string **a b** into nine equal portions by the third petition, so that **c b** holds eight of those nine and **a c** holds one. I say that **a b** and **c b** contain a tone, and thus a tone is established upon the given string. For the whole space of the string **a b** contains the space **c b** and moreover an eighth part of it, because **a c** is equal to one of those eight parts. Therefore, by definition, the space **a b** is *epogdous*—that is, *sesquioctave*—to the space **c b**. Wherefore, by the first petition, that will be the ratio of the sound of the whole string **a b** to the sound of **c b**. Therefore, a tone is placed in the string **a b**, which consists in the *epogdoa* or *sesquioctave* ratio.

### ¶ To join a tone to a tone, and as many as you please, on a given string.

[[notes: This image illustrates a string divided multiple times to create a sequence of three whole tones.]]

¶ Let there be a given string **a b** in which it is proposed to join three consecutive tones. I divide the space of the whole string **a b** into nine equal portions through the third petition, as was done in the preceding. And at the mark of the eighth portion I place **c**, so that **b c** holds eight of those nine parts. It is manifest through the preceding that **a b** and **b c** constitute a tone. And by the same petition, I divide the space **c b** into nine equal portions. And at the third [[notes: This likely refers to the point marking the eighth segment of the new division]] of the eight small parts I place **d**, so that **d b** contains eight of those parts of which **c b** contains nine. By the preceding, **c b** to **d b** sounds a tone; and now one tone is joined to another tone. Again, I divide the space **d b** in a similar way into nine equal portions, and I designate the mark of the eighth section with the letter **e**, so that **e b** contains eight of those parts of which **d b** contains nine. By the preceding, **d b** to **e b** sounds a tone. Therefore, in the given string **a b**, there are three continuously joined tones: namely, **a b** / **c b**; **c b** / **d b**; **d b** / **e b**, which was proposed. And in this manner, it is very easy to join as many as you like. And if you wish to experience and grasp this by the senses, after striking the whole string **a b**, place the **hemispherium** of the string **a b** at the sign **c**, so that only the portion **c b** sounds and rings out; and by the judgment of the senses, you will perceive the sound of the whole **a b** to the sound **c b** to be the interval of a tone. Then, if you transfer the *hemispherium* to the mark **d**, from the striking of **c b** and **d b** you may again perceive a tone. But from the sound of the whole **a b** to the sound of the portion **d b**, the hearing will weigh two tones and the interval of two tones. And in this way, you may commit as many tones as you wish to be perceived by the judgments of the senses, and discern their mixtures—sometimes sweet, sometimes unfitting [[notes: *inconcinnas*, meaning discordant]] (which the hearing, as if offended, dreads and flees from).
