---

The composition of this is very easy. For, as in the others preceding, I make the interval from **a** to **b** the distance of a tone, and from **a** to **e** a **diapente** [[notes: a perfect fifth]]. And I make the distance from **b** to **d** a minor semitone. I then divide the string **c** in the middle of the difference between **b** and **d**; therefore, there will be a **diesis** of a quarter-measure and four-part division equally from **b** to **c**, and likewise from **c** to **d** a **diesis**. 

However, according to the fourth [chapter] of the second [book] of this work, this [diesis] is wider, while the other is more contracted; nor, by the thirty-second [chapter] of the same, can a minor semitone be divided into two equal parts by a certain and established number [[notes: A reference to the Pythagorean mathematical proof that a tone or semitone cannot be divided into two perfectly equal ratios using whole numbers]]. And from **a** to **e** is a **diapente**; therefore, when the tone of **a** to **b** is subtracted, what remains is the **diatessaron** [[notes: a perfect fourth]] from **b** to **e**. And since a **diatessaron** encompasses a minor semitone and two tones, and since **b** to **d** is a minor semitone, it follows that what remains from **d** to **e** is a **ditonus**. 

Therefore, **b c d e** will be the *hypates hypaton*, *parhypates hypaton*, *lichanos hypaton*, and *hypates meson* tetrachord: proceeding through **b** to **c**, then to **d**, and then the **d** to **e** ditone; this is divided by definition into the Enharmonic genus. Similarly, you may establish the **e f g h** *meson* tetrachord by making **a** to **h** a **diapason** [[notes: an octave]], and **e** to **g** a minor semitone, and partitioning the middle difference through **f** so that **e** to **f** and **f** to **g** are two dieses. 

For when the **diapente** consonance of **a** to **e** is subtracted from the **diapason** consonance of **a** to **h**, the remaining distance **e** to **h** is a **diatessaron**. And since **e** to **g** is a minor semitone, it follows that **g** to **h** will be a **ditonus**. And since **e** to **f** and **f** to **g** are two dieses and **g** to **h** a **ditonus**, this partition of the tetrachord is seen to be made in the Enharmonic genus. 

And having made the **h** (*mese*) a **diapente** to the *nete diezeugmenon* and a **diapason** to the *nete hyperboleon*, and the string *mese* being separated by a tone from the *paramese* of the disjunct tetrachord—just as the *proslambanomenos* is from the *hypate hypaton*—you will partition the disjunct tetrachord **lz l m n** just like the *hypaton* tetrachord **b c d e**, and the **n o p q** *hyperboleon* (the highest) tetrachord like the *meson* tetrachord **e f g h**. Furthermore, you will partition the **h lz l m** conjunct tetrachord [[notes: the *synemmenon*]] like the *meson* tetrachord, and that too is easily done.
