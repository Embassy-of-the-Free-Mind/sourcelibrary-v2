...we say are the proportions. Therefore, let **a** and **b** first be the smallest numbers of the minor semitone, found through the eighteenth [proposition] of this book. For it was also demonstrated there that **b** arises from the number nine multiplied by itself [[notes: 9 × 9 = 81]] and the number three multiplied into the product of nine by itself [[notes: 3 × 81 = 243]]. But the number which arose from the multiplication of nine by itself was a square, and three is not a square. Therefore, by the fourteenth [proposition] of the sixth [book] of *Arithmetics* [[notes: Likely referring to a standard textbook of the era, such as Boethius’s *De Arithmetica*]], the number **b**, the extreme of the minor semitone, resulting from the multiplication of a non-square by a square, is not a square number. 

If it is possible, therefore, for the proportion of a *diaschisma* [[notes: A "diaschisma" is half of a minor semitone]] to be known in numbers, let there be two joined diaschismata, **c, d, e**, in their smallest numbers, according to the sixth [proposition] of the fourth [book] of *Arithmetics*. For it is manifest, since a diaschisma is half of a minor semitone, that **c-d** and **d-e** together are a minor semitone, and the interval from **c** to **e** is that of a minor semitone. But since the proportions **c-d** and **d-e** are continuous in their smallest terms, then by the fifth [proposition] of the fourth [book] of *Arithmetics*, **c** and **e** are in their smallest proportion. Thus, they are the smallest numbers in the proportion of a minor semitone. 

But **a** and **b** were also established as such; therefore, **c** and **e** will be the same numbers as **a** and **b**—namely, **c** is the same as **a**, and **e** is the same as **b**. Furthermore, because the proportion of **c** to **d** is the same as **d** to **e**, then by the first [proposition] of the sixth [book] of *Arithmetics*, the number **c** is a square and the number **e** is a square. Wherefore **b**, being the same number as **e**, would also be a square. But **b** was demonstrated to be non-square. Therefore, the same number would be both square and non-square, which is impossible. Thus, the *diaschisma* cannot be held in a known [rational] proportion. 

The same shall be shown for the proportion of the *schisma* [[notes: A "schisma" is half of a musical comma]]. For let **a** and **b**, designated below, be the smallest numbers of the comma. Since by the twenty-eighth [proposition] of this book, **b**, the smaller term of the comma's proportion, arises from the multiplication of the number eight by the square of the side of two hundred fifty-six [[notes: The author is performing complex calculations to show the comma cannot be split into two equal rational parts]], and eight is not a square; therefore, by the fourteenth [proposition] of the sixth [book] of *Arithmetics*, **b**, the smaller term of the comma, is not a square. Therefore, just as in the demonstration of the first part, the designated numbers **a** and **b** cannot be divided into equal proportions. Thus, the ratio of the *schisma*—namely its half—is unknown and irrational. The corollary is known from the method of demonstration.

[[margin: 33]]
# The Tone: It consists of two minor semitones and a comma.

$\P$ For the *sesquioctave* ratio [[notes: The 9:8 ratio, which defines a whole tone]] consists of two minor semitones and one comma. For it exceeds two *dieses* and two minor semitones by one comma; and the tone exists in that same ratio. Therefore, the tone consists of two minor semitones and a comma.

[[margin: 34]]
# $\P$ The Tone differs from two minor semitones by one comma.

$\P$ For when one comma is subtracted from the *sesquioctave* proportion, two minor semitones are left; therefore, when the same comma is drawn away from the tone, two *dieses* and two minor semitones remain. Thus, the tone differs from two minor semitones by one comma.
