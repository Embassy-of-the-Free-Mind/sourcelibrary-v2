### OF THE PHILOSOPHERS.

If there are therefore 16 **squares** [[notes: *Quarreaux* refers to the individual cells or spaces on the grid]] in the length of your board, begin to arrange the pieces at the sixth square along that length. If there are only ten, begin at the fifth, following the natural order of the even numbers: placing the smallest number of the round pieces, which is 2, at the third square on the right, and in the following three squares, the other three even numbers, which are 4, 6, and 8. And because the said pieces 2, 4, 6, and 8 are of **multiple proportion** [[notes: In Pythagorean arithmetic, "multiple proportion" refers to a relationship where a larger number is a multiple of a smaller one. Here, the author uses the squares of the base numbers to determine which pieces belong in the ranks behind them]], multiply each one by itself, and the result will show you the piece that must be placed behind it: saying 2 times 2 are 4; place 4, therefore, behind 2. After, say 4 times 4 are 16; place 16 behind 4. Say again 6 times 6 are 36, and place 36 behind 6. Consequently, 8 times 8 are 64; place 64 behind 8. Having done this, you will have also completed the multiple proportion of the even numbers, in imitation of which you may easily arrange the multiples of the odd numbers.
