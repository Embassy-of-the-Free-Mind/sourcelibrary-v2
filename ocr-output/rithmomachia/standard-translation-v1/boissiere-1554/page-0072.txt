## On Musical Proportion.

Musical proportion occurs when the differences between the two outer numbers and the middle number share the same ratio as the two outer numbers themselves. For example: 3, 4, 6. It is easy to see that the difference between the first number (3) and the middle number (4) is 1, and the difference between the last number (6) and the middle (4) is 2. Seeing that these differences, 1 and 2, have a double ratio between them just as the two outer numbers 3 and 6 do, we conclude that this is a musical proportion; and so it is for the others.

[[notes: In the Renaissance, this was called "Musical Proportion" because it describes the mathematical relationship between the lengths of strings that produce harmonious intervals. For instance, in the example 3, 4, 6: the ratio of 3:6 (the outer numbers) is 1:2, which in music is an octave. The differences (1 and 2) also form a 1:2 ratio. This proportion was considered the most "perfect" and difficult to achieve in the game.]]
