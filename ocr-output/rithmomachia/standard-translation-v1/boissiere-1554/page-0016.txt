### OF THE PHILOSOPHERS.

[...held] between 1 and 10, and such numbers, taken individually, will be the source and origin of all proportions: observing however that the even numbers should be in one color, and the odd in another, insofar as the pieces [[notes: The original French uses the word "tables," which in the context of early games refers to the playing pieces or counters, much like the "tables" in backgammon.]] of the even numbers must not be mixed with those of the odd numbers; nonetheless, because a number taken individually cannot by itself create any proportion: you must presuppose that each of the four even numbers requires another of its own color to make a proportion according to itself. Each of the odd numbers does the same. Therefore, the said 4 evens and 4 odds shall be called *requiring* or *claiming* numbers. [[notes: *Nombres requerans* or *pretendans* are the foundational numbers from which more complex ratios on the board are derived. They "claim" or "require" another number to form a mathematical relationship, such as 2 requiring 4 to create a "double" proportion.]]
