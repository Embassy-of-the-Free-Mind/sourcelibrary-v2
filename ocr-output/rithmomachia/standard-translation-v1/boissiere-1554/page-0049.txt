In this game, these ratios determine the values of specific playing pieces.]] belonging to the fourth row. For example: I add one unit to the first multiple **requerant** [[notes: The "requerants" (petitioners) are the base numbers from which other, more complex numbers are derived.]] which is 2, and it results in 3; these [3], multiplied by themselves, will give me 9: the first required value for the superparticulars.

### To find the required values of the superpartients that belong to the last order.

Double each of those from the first order who are the multiple **requerants**, and to the product add one unit; then, multiply the whole by itself, and you will have the required **superpartient** [[notes: A superpartient ratio is more complex than a superparticular one, involving a ratio of (n+m):n where m > 1. These represent the most advanced pieces in the game's hierarchy.]] number fitting the last order. For example: I double the first multiple **requerant**, 2, which results in 4; this, with one unit added, produces 5. Therefore, 5 multiplied by itself gives me 25: the first required superpartient value, belonging to the last row across from 2; and so it is for the other required values. As for the **requerants**, it is a general rule that they are always composed by the addition of the two preceding numbers, as we have said above [[notes: The author is describing a recursive sequence, where the base numbers for the game board are generated by adding previous terms together, ensuring a mathematical harmony across the board.]].
