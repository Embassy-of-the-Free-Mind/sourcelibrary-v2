[[notes: This page serves as a bibliography, likely compiled by a scholar or collector. It lists several works on "Rythmomachia," a complex medieval board game based on Pythagorean number theory, often called "The Philosophers' Game."]]

Georgius Draudius [[notes: Georg Draud (1573–1635) was a renowned German bibliographer known for his massive catalogs of books.]] calls this author Claudius Buxerius [[notes: The Latinized name for Claude de Boissière, a 16th-century French mathematician and maker of instruments.]] in the *Bibliotheca classica* on page 1028; it was published in Latin in Paris in octavo [[notes: "In 8" refers to *octavo*, a book size where the printed sheet is folded three times to create eight leaves.]].

There exist two anonymous descriptions of *rythmomachia* or *arithmomachia* [[notes: Alternative names for the "Battle of Numbers" game]]. Erfurt, 1577, in quarto [[notes: "In 4" refers to *quarto*, a book size slightly larger than octavo, where the sheet is folded twice into four leaves.]].

William Fulke's [[notes: "Gui. Falconis" refers to William Fulke (1538–1589), an English theologian and academic.]] *Metromachia*, or the geometric game. London, in quarto.

Hermannus Contractus’s [[notes: Hermann of Reichenau (1013–1054), a Benedictine monk and polymath known as "Hermann the Lame."]] *On the Conflict of Rithmomachia*, one book.

Claude de Boissière also wrote on the property and use of quadrants [[notes: An instrument used to measure angles up to 90°, vital for astronomy and navigation.]], in octavo.

Also, *The Art of Arithmetic*, in octavo.

Also, *The Explanation of the World Map of Gemma Frisius* [[notes: Gemma Frisius (1508–1555) was a Dutch cartographer and mathematician famous for his work on triangulation and globes.]], in octavo.
