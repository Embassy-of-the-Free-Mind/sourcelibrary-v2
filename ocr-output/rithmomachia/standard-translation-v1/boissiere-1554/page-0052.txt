# RYTHMOMACHIA, THE GAME [[notes: The name "Rythmomachia" (or Rithmomachia) is derived from the Greek words for "number" and "battle." It was a highly popular educational board game in the Middle Ages and Renaissance that taught arithmetic and music theory through a tactical simulation.]]

### Concerning the movement of the pieces.

First, all shall move straight forward, or to the right, or to the left, or backward; some wish to allow movement in all directions, which I leave to the discretion of the players.

Secondly, the group of **squares** moves by 4 steps, or 4 **spaces** [[notes: The original term *loges* (lodgings) refers to the individual squares on the grid.]]: the **triangles** move by 3, and the **rounds** [[notes: Circles.]] move into the next adjacent space. When I say the fourth space, I mean counting both the place where the piece is and the place where it goes [[notes: This is "inclusive counting," a standard mathematical practice in the Renaissance where the starting square is counted as "one." Therefore, a square moves three empty squares and lands on the fourth.]].

## The procedure of their combat.

The numbers may take one another by **encounter**, **assault**, **ambush**, and **imprisonment** [[notes: These are the four primary ways to capture an opponent's piece. Unlike Chess, where capture is purely positional, Rythmomachia requires specific mathematical conditions—such as a piece's value equaling its target's, or two pieces' values adding up to the target's—to effect a capture.]]: and for these captures, an example is proposed to you on this **board** [[notes: *Tablier* refers to the physical game board.]] in which you may consider them according to how we shall declare them hereafter...
