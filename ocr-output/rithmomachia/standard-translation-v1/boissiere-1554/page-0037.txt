->## THE PHILOSOPHERS' GAME.<-

...**superpartients** [[notes: This term refers to a ratio where the larger number contains the smaller number plus several fractional parts of it—for example, 5:3, which is 1 plus 2/3. This completes the sentence from the previous page.]], in even numbers, which are: **super-bipartient thirds**, **super-quadripartient fifths**, **super-sextupartient sevenths**, and **super-octopartient ninths** [[notes: These are complex ratios used to determine the values of the square pieces. "Super-bipartient thirds" means a value of $1 + 2/3$. Thus, the ratio of 25 to 15 is super-bipartient because 25 contains 15 once, plus 10 (which is two-thirds of 15).]]. 

The first value, therefore, which is 15, requires another number which, together with it, completes the **super-bipartient thirds** proportion: specifically, a number that contains 15 plus two-thirds of its value. Now, one-third of 15 is 5. Therefore, the number containing 15 and two of its thirds (10) will be 25 [[notes: Calculation: $15 + (5 \times 2) = 25$.]]. You shall place this piece behind the 15; and follow the same logic for the others. 

Note that the **Pyramid** [[notes: The Pyramid is the most powerful and complex piece in Rithmomachia. It is "built" from several other pieces and represents multiple mathematical proportions simultaneously.]] will be counted as one of the **square tables** [[notes: In this context, "tables" refers to the game pieces themselves, derived from the Latin *tabula*. In Rithmomachia, circles represent simple multiples, triangles represent "superparticular" ratios ($1 + 1/n$), and squares represent the complex "superpartient" ratios described here.]]. If these calculations seem difficult, refer to the arrangement in the following diagrams.
