...so abundant toward you, and the affection and goodwill that it has always pleased you to have toward me, unworthy as I am, I have endeavored to prepare something, according to my small capacity, which might be pleasing to you. Now, finding nothing for the present more suitable to refresh your spirit than the aforementioned **game of the philosophers**, called *Rythmomachia*, I have endeavored to arrange it in such a layout and order that what has until now been presented in riddles [[gloss: enigmes]] and obscurity might, under the protection of your excellence, be clarified and offered to all people of virtue. [[notes: *Rythmomachia* (The Battle of Numbers) was a highly complex board game based on Boethian mathematical ratios. It was considered the "philosophers' game" because it required deep knowledge of number theory, unlike the more "common" game of Chess.]] May it please you, then (my lord), to look favorably upon this small gift of mine; should this happen, you will give me the heart to always apply even greater diligence in preparing something of even greater excellence for you in the future. [[notes: It was standard practice in Renaissance prefaces for an author to humble themselves by calling their work a "small gift" or "little thing," while promising a greater work if the patron accepts the current one.]]

[[summary: The author concludes his dedication by presenting the book—a manual on the mathematical game Rythmomachia—to his patron. He claims to have simplified the game's complex and "obscure" rules to make them accessible to virtuous readers.]]
