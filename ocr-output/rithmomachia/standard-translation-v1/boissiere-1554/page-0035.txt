Having thus situated the *multiple* pieces [[notes: The author refers to the game pieces as "tables." The multiples are the circular pieces whose values are multiples of the base numbers 2, 4, 6, and 8]], you will easily find the arrangement of the particular triangular ones by adding them together. First, add the first and smallest proportion of the said multiples, saying 2 and 4 make 6. This is the first superparticular [[notes: In Pythagorean arithmetic, a "superparticular" ratio is one where the larger number contains the smaller number plus one equal part of it (e.g., 3/2, 4/3). These values are assigned to the triangular pieces]] triangle which must be placed behind 4, and so with the other three. Having placed the first four triangular pieces, place behind them the other four according to the natural order of the particulars in even numbers: which are *sesquialter*, *sesquiquartal*, *sesquisextal*, and *sesquioctaval* [[notes: These terms define specific ratios: sesquialter (1 ½), sesquiquartal (1 ¼), sesquisextal (1 1/6), and sesquioctaval (1 1/8)]]. And because the first, which is 6, requires another that has a *sesquialter* proportion to it—that is to say, one which contains it and furthermore its half, which is 3—the required number will be 9, which contains the said 6 and furthermore its half, 3. And so with the other three, as we have shown in the description of the characters [[notes: "Characters" refers to the numbers or symbols inscribed on the pieces]].
