## Another arrangement of the pieces, with an allegory not to be disregarded, and first concerning the multiple numbers.

The arrangement of the **round tables** [[notes: "Tables" refers to the game pieces; here, the round pieces represent the simplest numerical ratios]] containing the **multiple proportion** [[notes: A ratio where a larger number is a perfect multiple of a smaller one, such as 2:1 (double) or 3:1 (triple)]] is in no way different from the one we have shown above. That is to say: one must arrange the first rows according to the natural progression of numbers, such as 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9; choosing for the **field of the even** [[notes: "Champ" or field refers to the side of the board belonging to the player using even numbers]] 2, 4, 6, 8, and for the **field of the odd** 3, 5, 7, 9. Similarly, you will find the number required to complete this proportion by multiplying each of these by itself—for example, 2 times 2 makes 4—for 4, when compared with 2, creates a type of multiple proportion, namely **double**. And so it is with the others, as you may better observe in this table.
