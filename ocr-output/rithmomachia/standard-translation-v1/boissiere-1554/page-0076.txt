## On the excellent victory.

*The excellent victory* [[notes: Known in Latin as the **Victoria Praestantissima**, this was the most difficult and prestigious way to win the game.]] *is the situation and arrangement of 4 pieces* [[notes: The original French uses **tables**, which refers to the playing pieces themselves, similar to how we use "men" in checkers.]] *in the enemy's camp, equipped and armed with such numbers that, in their magnitude and harmony, they contain 3 proportions: namely Arithmetic, Geometry, and Music* [[notes: "Music" here refers to a Harmonic proportion. The Renaissance player sought to mirror the "Music of the Spheres" or the mathematical order of the universe on the game board.]]: *which are of such great harmony and agreement that there is no* [[notes: The text ends mid-sentence on this page, likely continuing with a description of how these mathematical ratios represent a perfect, unassailable order.]]
