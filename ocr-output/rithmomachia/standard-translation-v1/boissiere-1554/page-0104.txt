# OF THE PHILOSOPHERS.

*...the rounds in the second place, the triangles in the third, and the squares in the fourth: counting the place where they are situated.*

2 *The pieces [[notes: The original uses the term "tables," which in the context of board games like backgammon or Rythmomachia refers to the playing pieces or counters.]] can take one another in their vicinity, moving no differently than the **Rook** [[notes: Referred to as "le Roch" in the original, this is the chess piece we now call the Rook or Castle.]] in chess.*

3 *A number finding another equal to itself captures it.*

4 *If two numbers are equal to another and can be positioned around it: they shall capture it.*

5 *If you multiply a smaller number by the empty spaces [[notes: The "loges vuides" are the empty squares between two pieces.]] that lie between it and a larger number, and if the product of such multiplication is equal to the larger number, that number shall capture it.*

6 *A number which cannot move straight like a Rook, being besieged by the enemy, shall be taken.*

7 *If any piece equal to the largest base of the **Pyramid** [[notes: The Pyramid is the most complex piece in the game, made up of several stacked values. Capturing it required specific mathematical totals.]] encounters that Pyramid, it shall capture it—or be captured itself, if it is the one encountered.*

8 *If any number multiplied by the intervening spaces becomes equal to the great base of the Pyramid, it shall capture it.*

9 *If it happens that the other bases of your Pyramid are encountered by any pieces equal to them, said base shall be taken, if it is there.*
