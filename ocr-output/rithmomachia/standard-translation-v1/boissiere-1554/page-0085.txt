...ancients, such as **Gilbert the Pope**, [[notes: Gerbert of Aurillac (c. 946–1003), who became Pope Sylvester II; he was a prolific mathematician credited with introducing Arabic numerals to Europe.]] **Hermann the Cripple**, [[notes: Hermann of Reichenau (1013–1054), an 11th-century scholar and monk who wrote one of the earliest treatises on this game.]] **Castrense**, and **Nicole Oresme**, [[notes: A significant 14th-century philosopher and mathematician.]] who have diligently treated said game; contenting themselves with contemplation, through too great a severity they despised and omitted that which was delightful and pleasant in it. And in our time, **Oronce Finé of Dauphiné**, [[notes: A famous 16th-century mathematician and cartographer.]] **Jacques Lefèvre d’Étaples**, [[notes: A leading French humanist and theologian who published a famous work on Rythmomachia in 1496.]] and we ourselves most recently, imitating them, have written of it. However, seeing that contemplation without recreation can sometimes be tedious, I am very joyful to have recovered what might previously have been truncated, and which is now greatly required for the completion and perfection of our subject. Nevertheless, so as not to detract from the authority of such authors, I have placed this pleasant and complete way of playing as an appendix or addition, leaving the previous form pursued by them in its entirety and according to their opinion, hoping this will be agreeable to good minds, who are often refreshed and relieved from their labors by variety and rare things.
