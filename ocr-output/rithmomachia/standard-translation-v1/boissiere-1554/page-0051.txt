nevertheless, we shall grant that they possess some form of a **battalion** [[notes: The author is continuing the military metaphor from the previous page, treating the mathematical game as a tactical simulation.]], wherefore we shall speak first of the arrangement and the movement.

### Concerning the movement of the pieces.

First, all shall move straight forward, or to the right, or to the left, or backward; some wish to allow movement in all directions, which I leave to the discretion of the players.
Secondly, the group of **squares** moves by 4 steps, or 4 **spaces** [[notes: The original term *loges* (lodgings) refers to the individual squares on the grid.]]: the **triangles** move by 3, and the **rounds** move into the next adjacent space. When I say the fourth space, I mean counting both the place where the piece is and the place where it goes [[notes: This is "inclusive counting," a common method in the Renaissance where the starting square is counted as "one." Therefore, a square moves three empty squares and lands on the fourth.]]

## The procedure of their combat.

The numbers may take one another by **encounter**, **assault**, **ambush**, and **imprisonment** [[notes: These are the four primary ways to capture an opponent's piece. Unlike Chess, where capture is purely positional, Rythmomachia requires specific mathematical conditions—such as a piece's value equaling its target's, or two pieces' values adding up to the target's—to effect a capture.]]: and for these captures, an example is proposed to you on this **board** [[notes: *Tablier* refers to the physical game board.]] in which you may consider them according to how we shall declare them hereafter, with their rules, examples, and numbers proper to each capture.
