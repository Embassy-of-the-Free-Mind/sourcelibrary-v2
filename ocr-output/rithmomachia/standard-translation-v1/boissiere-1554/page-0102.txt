<header>OF THE PHILOSOPHES</header>

...willingly we would desire some game which you others (who practice numbers) use greatly to ease your minds after too much study. **Alcm.** Perhaps (Bathillus my friend) you mean *Rythmomachia* [[notes: Rythmomachia, or "The Battle of Numbers," was a complex board game popular in the Middle Ages and Renaissance that required players to capture pieces using mathematical ratios.]]. **Bathillus**: It is the very same, Alcmeon my friend. **Alcm.** Now tell me then, my son Bathillus, how many simple inequalities of numbers are there? **Bathillus.** Three: multiple, superparticular, and superpartient [[notes: These are categories of ratios. A "multiple" is an integer multiple (2, 3, 4); "superparticular" is a ratio where the larger number contains the smaller plus one part (like 3:2); "superpartient" is where the larger contains the smaller plus several parts (like 5:3).]]. **Alcm.** It is true, and this is by the relation and ratio of the greater number to the lesser: and in this consists the game that you and Brontinus have asked of me. But what is great harmony and lesser? **Bat.** It is when, of four numerical terms arranged according to Geometric proportion, the two extremes make proportions with the two in the middle: one by the means of Music, and the other of Arithmetic [[notes: This refers to the "Great Triumph," a winning condition in the game where a player arranges four pieces to satisfy arithmetic, geometric, and harmonic means simultaneously.]]. And the minor harmony is when, among the said four numerical terms, only two means are found. Now as for the means of Arithmetic, Geometry, and Music, there is no need to trouble you: for we believe we understand these things well. **Alc.** You understand many good things, Bathillus: It remains that in such great harmonies the four terms must be arranged in a row. And as for the arrangement of the game, it is thus: first one arranges...
