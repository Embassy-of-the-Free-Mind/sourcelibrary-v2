It may also be taken in one stroke by imprisonment [[notes: Also called "siege," as explained on the previous page.]]: for all its power is then lost in a single blow, and the same shall happen if one can capture the number which contains all the bases [[notes: In Rythmomachia, the Pyramid's total value is the sum of the numbers on its layers. This total "base" number is the target for mathematical capture.]]: which some mark at the peak [[notes: "Coupeau."]] of the pyramid, and others at the bottom.

## General Rules.

1 No piece [[notes: The original French uses "table," referring to the game counters.]] can move out of its path to capture, or cause another piece to be captured, by an irregular movement, except in siege: for one may imprison irregularly [[notes: This means that while normal captures require pieces to move in their set patterns (like chess), a "siege" capture allows you to use any available piece to surround the enemy, regardless of whether that piece's specific mathematical value usually allows that movement.]].
2 In all captures, only one move is made.
3 Forasmuch as multiplication and division are reciprocal operations, if the adversary fails to capture one of your exposed pieces by multiplication, you may afterward capture theirs by division; or if they have failed to capture by division, you may capture it by multiplication, then...
