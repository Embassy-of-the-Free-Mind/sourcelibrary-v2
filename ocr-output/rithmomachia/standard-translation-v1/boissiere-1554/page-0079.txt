RYTHMOMACHIA, GAME
DC B

you will see these to be in sesquialter proportion [[notes: A sesquialter ratio is 3:2. In music, this ratio produces the interval of a Perfect Fifth.]], which proportion, as appears by our music, relates to the consonance of *diapente*, which is worth a fifth. Again, if you take the two middle numbers 3 and 4, you will see between them a sesquitertial proportion [[notes: A sesquitertial ratio is 4:3, which produces a Perfect Fourth.]], which is nothing other than the consonance of *diatessaron*, which is worth a fourth. And if you take the first, 2, in respect to the third, 4—or indeed the second, 3, in regard to the last, 6—you will have a double proportion [[notes: A 2:1 ratio.]], which proportion resounds as a *diapason*, which is an octave. Furthermore, you will find the first, 2, in respect to the last, 6, to have a triple proportion [[notes: A 3:1 ratio.]], which represents *diapason* and *diapente* together, which is a twelfth. Consequently, the difference between three and four (which is one unit) has a quadruple proportion in respect to the third number, 4, which is the consonance of [dis-]diapason [[notes: The text says "diapason," but a 4:1 ratio is actually a "dis-diapason" or double octave.]], which is a fifteenth. And note that what we have said of the first Victory (2, 3, 4, 6) shall also be understood of the following one (4, 6, 8, 12). However, to better specify these things for you, I will propose here all those victories through mathematical figures and demonstrations.
