...taken according to weight, as with metals [[notes: This refers to the legendary discovery of musical ratios by Pythagoras, who supposedly heard the harmonious intervals produced by blacksmiths' hammers of different weights]], or according to measure, as with strings [[notes: Referring to the monochord, a tool used to demonstrate how shortening a string by specific ratios creates musical notes]]: they will always yield to you true musical harmony.

## On the Great Victory.

The Great Victory—termed "minor" by the ancients compared to the "excellent" victory that follows [[notes: In this game, there are multiple tiers of victory. The "Great Victory" is the second-highest, while the "Excellent Victory" is the most difficult and prestigious]]—consists of the arrangement of 4 pieces [[notes: The original French uses *tables*, the standard term for the circular, square, and triangular pieces used in Rythmomachia]] in the enemy’s camp. These, in their placement and order, must represent two proportions together: whether Arithmetic and Geometric, Arithmetic and Musical, or Geometric and Musical. 

To explain this more clearly, I shall provide the following table. In the first column on the left [[notes: *Seneſtre* is the archaic French word for left]], I will place the Great Victory consisting of two proportions contained within four numbers. In the two columns on the right [[notes: *Dextre* is the archaic French word for right]], I will separate these two proportions to show each one individually as they are found within the aforementioned pieces.
