### Response to objections.

Now, someone might ask me: why have you not set out the victories of even numbers and those of odd numbers, each one specifically, as Faber Stapulensis [[notes: Jacques Lefèvre d’Étaples (c. 1455–1536), a leading French humanist and mathematician whose 1496 work on Rythmomachia was the standard academic text of the era.]] did? I answer you first that the game itself would be nearly infinite due to its great length, and would cause frustration to the players; I believe this is the reason why the said game had previously fallen into disuse. Therefore, by making the victories common [[notes: Meaning the win conditions can be met by using a combination of even and odd pieces, rather than strictly one or the other.]], I have made the game more open so that victory may be achieved more easily. Secondly, you know well that although the said Faber divided the victories—specifically establishing those for even and those for odd—he nevertheless says that the player with the even numbers may achieve or complete his victory using the pieces [[notes: *Tables* in the original French.]] he has acquired from the odd numbers.
