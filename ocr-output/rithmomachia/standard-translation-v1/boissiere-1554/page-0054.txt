# OF THE PHILOSOPHERS.

...rightfully, it may carry it off by a **capture of assault**, as soon as it has moved, without moving the piece again into its place [[notes: In an "Assault" capture, the capturing piece does not move to the opponent's square. The phrase *la table* refers to the game piece itself, as "tables" was a common term for game pieces in the 16th century, similar to "tablemen" in backgammon.]].

### Example

If between the 5 of the odd numbers and the 45 of the even numbers there are nine spaces [[notes: Called *loges* or "lodgings" in the original French, these are the squares on the board.]], as you see in the second column of the previous board [[notes: The *tablier* refers to the gridded game board, which in Rythmomachia was typically an 8x16 rectangle.]], since the said 5 of the field of the odds, being moved and multiplied by the nine interposed spaces, makes itself equal to the said 45 of the field of the evens: It can take that 45 as soon as it plays that 5 into the place where it stands. And here you may consider the other numbers suited for this.

[[notes: The "Assault" is one of the most famous rules of Rythmomachia. It demonstrates the game's mathematical nature: a piece captures an enemy at a distance if its own value, multiplied by the number of empty squares between them, equals the enemy's value. Here, 5 (the piece) × 9 (the distance) = 45 (the target).]]
