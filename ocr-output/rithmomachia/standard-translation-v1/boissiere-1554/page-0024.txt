# OF THE PHILOSOPHERS.

Here you may consider this most noble alliance: how the parts of even numbers always proceed by progression from the odd number; conversely, the parts of the odd number have their progression according to the even number. [[notes: The author is highlighting the reciprocal relationship in the game's mathematical sequences, where even-positioned pieces are derived from odd-numbered logic and vice versa.]]
Now that you have all the specific numbers corresponding to their proportion to mark the 16 triangular tables [[notes: "Tables" refers to the game pieces.]], it remains for us to come to the third proportion, called the superpartient.

## Of the superpartients.

Superpartient proportion occurs when one number contains another plus more than one of its parts—just as a *blanc* contains a *liard* and two of its third parts, which are two *tournois*. [[notes: To explain this complex ratio to a 16th-century reader, the author uses currency. At the time, a *liard* was worth 3 *deniers tournois*. Therefore, two-thirds of a *liard* is 2 *tournois*. A *liard* (3) plus two *tournois* (2) equals 5. The ratio described is 5:3, a "superpartient" ratio because 5 contains 3 once, plus two-thirds of 3.]] And because this superpartient proportion contains more than one extra part beyond a whole, the shape of the **square** will be very suitable for it; for it has more sides than the **triangle**, which has already been assigned to the "particulars" [[notes: The "superparticular" ratios mentioned on the previous page.]]. After you have separated the squares of one color from the others, you will find the eight first required numbers needed for them (according to their order and color) by the addition of the two numbers contained in each of the preceding "particular" proportions of the triangular tables. You shall do this just as you previously made the first eight *regrantes* [[notes: Likely "regirans," referring to the governing or base numbers used to calculate the values.]] of the particular even triangular tables: through the addition of the multiplied *rands* [[notes: The "Rounds," which are the base-level pieces.]]. You must always observe and apply the tables to those of the same color and "livery" [[notes: "Livery" refers to the specific set or color of the pieces, similar to "suits" in cards.]].
