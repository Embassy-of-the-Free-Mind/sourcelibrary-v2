# OF PHILOSOPHERS.

...and its other part: namely its half. Then you will find that the number which contains 6 and its half is 9: for 9 contains 6 and the half of 6, which is 3. You will say, therefore, that 6 is the **required number** matching with the **seeking number** 9, in the particular **sesquialter** proportion [[notes: A "sesquialter" proportion refers to a ratio of 3:2. In this game, the players calculate the value of pieces by adding a fraction of a number to itself. Here, 6 + (6/2) = 9]]. 

Afterward, we come to the second number, which is 12, an odd number [[notes: Though 12 is mathematically even, the author classifies it here as "odd" (non-pair) because it belongs to the sequence of pieces derived from the odd-numbered row (3 + 9 = 12) mentioned on the previous page]], and we will consider the name of the second particular species, which is **sesquitertian** [[notes: A ratio of 4:3]]. And thus, saying "tertian" [tierce], you will look for what the third part is, and you will find that it is 4. Thus, find a number that contains 12 and its third part 4, and you will see that it will be 16, which you will say is the particular **required number** paired with 12. 

Consequently, for the third particular number, which is 20 (an even number), consider the name of the 3rd species which is **sesquiquartan** [[notes: A ratio of 5:4]]. Having seen and placed above that it is its fourth part, find a number that contains it and its fourth part, which is five, and you will have the **required number** 25. And so it goes for the others.

But I will repeat here the aforementioned numbers found: and so that you may work to find their companions in particular proportion, above each of them you shall place the number that makes up their part [[notes: That is, the increment or the fraction being added]]. This part must be contained along with them in the **required number**, matching each of the aforementioned **seeking numbers** in particular proportion, following the number designating the preceding parts according to the natural order of numbers starting at 3.
