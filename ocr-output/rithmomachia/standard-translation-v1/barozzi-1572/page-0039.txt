The **fifth rule** is that all captured pieces and pyramid levels must be turned upside down outside the board on the winner's side. This is so that, as they are now of the color of the winner's faction, the winner may make use of them at the appropriate time and place, putting some or all of them back onto the board at their pleasure, according to what is most convenient for them. [[notes: Unlike modern Chess, Rithmomachia allows players to "recruit" captured pieces and return them to the board under their own control, similar to the modern game of Shogi.]]

The **sixth rule** is that when the two "square" levels of the pyramid are removed from the game, that pyramid can no longer move using the path of the Square [[notes: Each component of the Pyramid grants it the movement abilities of that shape: Circles move 1 space, Triangles 2, and Squares 3. If you lose the squares, you lose the long-range move.]]; the same shall be understood for the triangular and circular levels.

The **seventh rule** is that when the pyramid is "besieged," it can free itself by making the "knight's leap" from the game of chess [[notes: The Knight's move is an L-shape. This is the only circumstance in Rithmomachia where a piece is permitted to move in this specific way.]], or otherwise by being helped by another piece of its own faction taking one of the pieces that are besieging it. But if by chance it were so strongly besieged that it could not be helped in any way, then it is totally removed from the game, and the opponent can use it as their own by turning all its levels upside down so they show the color of their faction. [[notes: "Besieged" refers to a capture method where a piece is surrounded on all four sides so it has no legal moves.]]
