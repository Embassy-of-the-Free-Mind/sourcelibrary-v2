Therefore, it is necessary to explain in how many ways the opponent's pieces can be made prisoners [[notes: "Prigioni" (prisoners) is the period-appropriate term for captured pieces in this mathematical "battle."]]. In seven ways [[notes: Although the author states "seven," the following list only names six methods: counting, summing, subtracting, multiplying, dividing, and siege. This may be an erratum in the original manuscript or a reference to a seventh rule of "progressions" explained later.]], then, one piece can be made a prisoner by another: by **counting**, by **summing**, by **subtracting**, by **multiplying**, by **dividing**, and by **siege**.

By **counting** [[notes: Also known as the rule of Equality.]], one piece takes another in this manner: if, after a piece is played, it finds another piece of the same value belonging to the opposing side occupying a square that the first piece could reach by its own proper path, the opponent’s piece is removed from the game without the first piece moving into the square from which the other was taken. As in the example below: if the black **circle 25** is found in square **A**, and the white **triangle 25** is played into square **B**, as soon as the said triangle is played, the said circle shall be removed, while the triangle remains in square **B**. In this way, one piece takes another by counting the value of both to see if they are equal in number. And the numbers that can be won in this game by this method are these: **9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81**, and no others [[notes: These are the perfect squares used for the circular and triangular pieces in the game's standard setup.]].

By **summing**, pieces are taken in this manner: two smaller numbers can overcome one that is greater than either of them when (being such that when joined, that is, summed together, they make a number equal to the said greater one) they are found in such squares that they could both, by their own proper paths, go where that greater piece is located. As soon as the two smaller numbers are found in such a position relative to the greater one, the greater piece is removed from the game, while the two lesser pieces [[notes: The text here says "dui maggiori" (two greater) but the logic and the margin note "minori" (lesser) indicate he means the two smaller pieces that performed the capture remain where they are.]] remain where they are.
