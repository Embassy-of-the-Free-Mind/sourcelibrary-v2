tric, and Musical [[notes: In the Renaissance, this referred to the "Harmonic proportion," which relates to the mathematical intervals found in music.]]. The Arithmetic proportion is when,
given three numbers, they exceed one another by one and the
same difference; such as the proportion that exists between these
three numbers: 2, 4, 6; because the difference from 2 to 4
is the same as that from 4 to 6. Geometric proportion is when,
given three numbers, the smaller is contained within the middle one as
many times as the middle is contained in the larger. As in the num-
bers 2, 4, 8: the 2 is contained twice in the 4, and similarly the
4 is contained twice in the 8. Musical proportion is when, given
three numbers, the difference between the two smaller ones is contained by
the difference of the two larger ones as many times as the
smaller number is contained by the larger; as seen in the num-
bers 2, 3, 6. In these, the difference from 2 to 3 is one, and the
difference from 3 to 6 is 3. And just as one is contained three
times within 3, so too is 2 contained three times within 6. These
are the three proportions—Arithmetic, Geometric, and Mu-
sical—necessary for the victories of our game. Now we shall de-
clare the victories, which are three: one is called the Great,
the other the Greater, and the other the Greatest. The Great victory
is nothing other than to place three pieces in the opponent's field,
the numbers of which have among themselves one of the three afore-
said proportions: either the Arithmetic, the Geometric, or
the Musical. Thus, this victory can be achieved in three
ways according to these three proportions. But as for which are the
