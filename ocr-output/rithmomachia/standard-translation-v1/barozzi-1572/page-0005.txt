# THE MOST NOBLE AND ANCIENT PYTHAGOREAN GAME
## NAMED
# Rythmomachia
# THAT IS, THE BATTLE
## OF THE CONSONANCE OF NUMBERS,

### *Rediscovered for the utility and delight of Scholars.*

### And now composed in the vernacular in the form of a paraphrase by Francesco Barozzi, Venetian Gentleman.

[[notes: **Rythmomachia** (literally "The Battle of Numbers") was a highly complex board game popular from the 11th through the 16th centuries. Unlike Chess, which is about military strategy, Rythmomachia was a pedagogical tool used to teach Boethian arithmetic, geometry, and the mathematical basis of music. The "consonance" mentioned here refers to the harmonious relationships between numbers that players had to master to capture their opponent's pieces.]]

[[notes: **Francesco Barozzi** (1537–1604) was a distinguished Venetian mathematician and humanist. By writing in the **vernacular** (*lingua volgare*—common Italian) rather than Latin, Barozzi was participating in a Renaissance movement to make specialized scientific and mathematical knowledge accessible to the educated public outside of strictly academic circles.]]
