# TO THE VERY MAGNIFICENT
## AND ILLUSTRIOUS LORD
### THE LORD CAMILLO PALEOTTO.

->*One of the Lords of the Forty of the Magnificent City of Bologna.*<-

[[notes: The **Lords of the Forty** (*I Quaranta*) was the prestigious governing senate of Bologna during this period, composed of the city's most influential noble families. **Camillo Paleotto** (1511–1589) was a prominent legal scholar and member of this elite council.]]

---

<image-desc>A large decorative initial 'Q' featuring an engraving of a rider on a horse, set within a landscape with foliage.</image-desc>

That most beautiful and most delightful Game, discovered by the most wise Pythagoras, which already six years ago I explained orally to Your Illustrious Lordship from that little book composed in the Latin language by Claudio Buxero; and I had barely begun to show it to you upon the board with the pieces, when immediately you, with your most fine spirit and very sharp intellect, learned it in such a manner that you became its master; and often, playing it with Signor Francesco Caporacia, our fellow academic, and with me, you took pleasure in it as a pastime: now, it having been composed by me in the Italian language, and reduced to that greatest ease, clarity, and brevity that I could: and ow-

[[notes: **Claudio Buxero** is the Latinized name of Claude de Boissière, a French mathematician who published a well-known manual on Rythmomachia in 1554. Barozzi is refining and simplifying Boissière’s complex Latin rules for an Italian-speaking audience.]]

[[notes: The "academic" mentioned, **Francesco Caporacia**, likely refers to a member of one of the private literary or scientific societies (*accademie*) that were the center of intellectual life in Renaissance Italy.]]

[[notes: The word **"ow-"** at the end of the text is the start of the word "owing" (*dovendo*), which is completed by the catchword on the following page. This was a common printing practice to ensure the pages were bound in the correct order.]]
