The first rule is this: one must first achieve the **Great Victory**, then the **Greater**, and finally the **Greatest**; for one cannot achieve the Greatest unless the Greater has first been made, and similarly, one cannot achieve the Greater if the Great has not first been made. The second rule is that one must achieve these victories while playing and moving the pieces according to their own proper paths. [[notes: Each shape in Rithmomachia—circles, squares, and triangles—moves a specific number of spaces.]]

The third rule is that one should try as much as possible to achieve these victories using the opponent’s pieces that are currently in play; that is, arrange your own pieces near those of your opponent so that together they form the victories. You should also compel your opponent through play to move his pieces into places where they form victories, either on their own or together with yours. Where the opponent's pieces and your own pieces in play are not sufficient, you may also make use of those pieces you have taken prisoner.
