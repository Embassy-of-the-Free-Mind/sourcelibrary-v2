## TABLE OF CONSONANCES

->First Example.<-

| D. | C. | B. | A. |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 4. | 6. | 8. | 12. |

The **Diapente** [[notes: The Greek term for a "Fifth," an interval produced by the ratio 3:2.]] consonance is shown to us by the numbers A [and] B, or the number A compared to the difference of the numbers A [and] D. Similarly the numbers C [and] D, or the number C compared to the difference B [and] D, and the difference A [and] C to the difference B [and] D, or even to the number D. [[notes: Using the table: A(12):B(8) is 3:2. A(12) to the difference of A-D(12-4=8) is 12:8 or 3:2. C(6):D(4) is 3:2. C(6) to the difference of B-D(8-4=4) is 6:4 or 3:2. Difference of A-C(12-6=6) to difference B-D(8-4=4) is 6:4 or 3:2.]]

The **Diatessaron** [[notes: The Greek term for a "Fourth," an interval produced by the ratio 4:3.]], the numbers B [and] C, and the difference A from the difference A [and] C, or to the C, and the B to the difference A [and] C. [[notes: B(8):C(6) is 4:3. B(8) to the difference of A-C(12-6=6) is 8:6 or 4:3.]]

The **Diapasson** [[notes: The Greek term for an "Octave," an interval produced by the ratio 2:1.]], the A [and] C, and the B [and] D, or indeed the B to each of the differences B [and] D, and A [and] D: and the difference A [and] D to the D, or to the differences A [and] B & B [and] D: and the A to the difference A [and] C. [[notes: A(12):C(6) is 2:1. B(8):D(4) is 2:1. Difference A-D(12-4=8) to D(4) is 2:1. A(12) to difference A-C(12-6=6) is 2:1.]]

The **Diapasson with the Diapente** together [[notes: An Octave plus a Fifth, known as a "Twelfth," produced by the ratio 3:1.]], the A [and] D, and the A to the differences B [and] A & B [and] D, and the C to the differences C [and] B & C [and] D, and the difference A [and] C to the differences C [and] B & C [and] D. [[notes: A(12):D(4) is 3:1. C(6) to the difference C-B(8-6=2) is 3:1.]]

The **Disdiapasson** [[notes: The Greek term for a "Double Octave" or "Fifteenth," produced by the ratio 4:1.]], to the differences B [and] C & C [and] D, and the difference A [and] D to the said differences B [and] C & C [and] D. [[notes: Difference A-D(12-4=8) to the differences B-C(2) or C-D(2). 8:2 is 4:1.]]

->Second Example.<-

| D. | C. | B. | A. |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 3. | 5. | 15. | 25. |

The **Diapente**, the B to the differences B [and] A & B [and] C, and the D to the difference D [and] C. [[notes: B(15) to difference A-B(25-15=10) is 15:10 or 3:2. D(3) to difference C-D(5-3=2) is 3:2.]]
The **Diatessaron**, the difference C [and] A to the number B. [[notes: Difference A-C(25-5=20) to B(15) is 20:15 or 4:3.]]
The **Diapasson**, the difference A [and] C to the differences A [and] B & B [and] C. [[notes: Difference A-C(20) to difference A-B(10) is 20:10 or 2:1.]]
The **Diapasson with the Diapente** together, the B to the C. [[notes: B(15):C(5) is 3:1.]]
The **Disdiapasson**, the difference B [and] D to the D. [[notes: Difference B-D(15-3=12) to D(3) is 12:3 or 4:1.]]
