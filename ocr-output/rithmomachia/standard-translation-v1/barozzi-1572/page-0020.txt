In the same way, by **joining** the four first white triangles with the four second white triangles, the four first white squares will be produced, which will be these: **15, 45, 91, 153**. This is because from the 6 joined with the 9, 15 is born; from the 20 with the 25, 45; from the 42 with the 49, 91; and from the 72 with the 81, 153. As the following figure shows you.

[[notes: In Rythmomachia, "Square" pieces are often calculated by summing the values of "Triangle" pieces. The author uses the word "accozzato," meaning joined or coupled, to describe this addition.]]

->
| ▲ 6 | ▲ 20 | ▲ 42 | ▲ 72 |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| ▲ 9 | ▲ 25 | ▲ 49 | ▲ 81 |
| **■ 15** | **■ 45** | **■ 91** | **■ 153** |

Having already found the numbers of the first 4 white squares, and desiring to find those of the other 4 as well, you will proceed in this way: Begin from the number **5** and take the 4 following odd numbers, always skipping one odd number between them [[notes: The author is describing a sequence of odd numbers where you skip every other one: 5, (7), 9, (11), 13, (15), 17.]], which will be these: **5, 9, 13, 17**. Then, multiply these numbers by themselves, and the numbers you desire will be born, which will be: **25, 81, 169, 289**. This is because from 5 multiplied by itself, 25 is born; from 9, 81; from 13, 169; and from 17, 289, as is seen in the following figure.

[[notes: The author is now using "squaring" (multiplying a number by itself) to generate the values for the remaining square pieces. This marks a shift from simple addition to more complex arithmetic operations.]]

->
| 5 | 9 | 13 | 17 |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **x 5** | **x 9** | **x 13** | **x 17** |
| **■ 25** | **■ 81** | **■ 169** | **■ 289** |
<-
