[[notes: This pattern is a classic example of "stone" or "agate" marbling. Marbling is a traditional bookbinding technique where pigments are floated on a bath of thickened liquid (usually carrageenan, a type of seaweed extract) and manipulated into patterns before being transferred onto a sheet of paper. This specific style, with its large "cells" of dark brown and black outlined by veins of reddish-brown, blue, and cream, was frequently used in the 18th and 19th centuries to decorate the inside covers—known as endpapers—of valuable books.]]

[[notes: Beyond its beauty, marbled paper served a functional purpose in bookbinding: the busy, intricate patterns helped to hide the uneven textures of the leather hinges and the glue used to attach the paper to the cover board.]]
