->**Third part, in which the placement of the pieces**<-
->**upon the Board is taught.**<-

Having finished the second part of the treatise, it follows that we should teach how to place the pieces upon the board [[notes: The *Tauoliero* or board for Rithmomachia is typically a rectangular grid of 8 by 16 squares, double the size of a standard chessboard.]]. Let us, therefore, set the board before us in such a way that its length extends from our left side to our right side for two reasons. First, because by standing in the middle, we will be masters with our eyes over the entire board, and we will have both sides of the field closer to our eyes than we would if the board were placed in any other way. Second, so that we may play with more agility, always moving our pieces with the right hand toward the left [[notes: The author suggests a "landscape" orientation for the board to provide a better ergonomic reach and visual command of the game state.]]. 

The board being arranged in this manner, so that one can easily place the pieces upon it, it is first necessary to make a **preliminary arrangement** [[notes: *disposition finta*, literally a "feigned" or "mock" layout.]], from which the **perfect arrangement** [[notes: *perfetta dispositione*, the final starting position for the game.]] may then immediately and easily arise, which would be a difficult thing to achieve in a single step. The first preliminary arrangement shall be made in this way. Begin with the white pieces, and first with the **rounds** [[notes: *tondi*, the circular pieces, which are the simplest units in the game.]]; and counting the squares from that corner of the board which is nearest to our right hand across the width of the said board, as one reaches the third square, let one count along the length of the board until the sixth square: and in the said square, place...
