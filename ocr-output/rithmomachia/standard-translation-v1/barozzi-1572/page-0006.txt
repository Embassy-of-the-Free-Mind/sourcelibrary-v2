# THE MOST NOBLE
# AND MOST ANCIENT
## PYTHAGOREAN GAME
## NAMED
# RYTHMOMACHIA
## THAT IS, THE BATTLE
### OF HARMONIES
### OF NUMBERS

[[notes: **Rythmomachia** (from the Greek for "The Battle of Numbers") was a highly sophisticated board game played by monks, scholars, and noblemen from the 11th through the 17th centuries. Unlike Chess, which simulates physical combat, Rythmomachia is a "clash" of mathematical ratios. The **Pythagorean** label refers to the ancient belief that numbers are the fundamental building blocks of the universe and music.]]

->Together with another newly invented one.<-

[[notes: Barozzi refers here to a supplemental game or a variation of the rules he developed himself, which he includes later in this treatise to expand upon the traditional medieval rules.]]

->Translated by Francesco Barozzi, Venetian Gentleman,<-
->into the vernacular so that<-
->everyone may understand it.<-

[[notes: **Francesco Barozzi** (1537–1604) was a brilliant Venetian mathematician and humanist. His decision to write in the **vernacular** (the "volgare" or common Italian language) instead of Latin was a deliberate choice to democratize knowledge, allowing people outside of university circles to master complex mathematical and musical theories.]]
