6, which are the first four in the said table, and create the **Greatest Victory**: we will find in them all the proportions from which all **Musical consonances** are born. Because if we compare the last two among them—the 4 and the 6—or the first two—the 2 and the 3—we will find them joined in *sesquialter* proportion [[notes: A ratio of 3:2, where the larger number contains the smaller plus half of its value.]]; that is, the greater contains the lesser once and a half. This proportion gives rise to the consonance called *Diapente* by the Greeks, and the *Fifth* by common musicians.

Then, if we compare the two in the middle, namely the 3 and the 4, the *sesquitertian* proportion [[notes: A ratio of 4:3, where the larger number contains the smaller plus one-third of its value.]] will appear; that is, the greater contains the lesser and its third part, from which is born the consonance called *Diatessaron* by the Greeks, and the *Fourth* by common musicians.

Afterwards, if we compare the first, which is 2, to the third, which is 4—or the second, which is 3, to the last, which is 6—we will find the *double* proportion; that is, the greater contains the lesser twice, which proportion generates the consonance called *Diapasson* by the Greeks, and the *Octave* by common musicians.

Furthermore, if we make a comparison of the first, which is 2, to the last, which is 6, it will yield the *triple* proportion; that is, the greater contains the lesser three times, which produces the consonance called *Diapasson and Diapente* together by the Greeks [[notes: An Octave plus a Fifth, known as a Twelfth.]], and the *Twelfth* by modern common musicians.

Finally, if we compare the difference between the 3 and the 4—which is unity [[notes: The number 1.]]—with the third number, which is 4, we will find the *quadruple* proportion; that is, the greater contains the lesser four times, from which is born the musical consonance called *Disdiapasson* by the Greeks [[notes: A double octave.]], and the *Fifteenth* by common musicians.
