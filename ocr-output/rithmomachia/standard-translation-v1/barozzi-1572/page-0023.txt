...the said square, and in place of it, we use this pyramid [[notes: The "Pyramid" is a special piece in Rithmomachia that acts as a "super-piece," representing a stack of squares. Because its value is equal to one of the existing square pieces, that square is removed from the set to avoid duplication.]]. Similarly, starting from the first level of the black pyramid and moving from its base to the summit, we will mark on the said first level 64; on the second, 49; on the third, 36; on the fourth, 25; and on the fifth, and last, 16. Under the base, we will note the number that arises from the sum of all these five numbers added together, which will be 190 [[notes: The black pyramid is composed of five layers: $8^2, 7^2, 6^2, 5^2, \text{and } 4^2$. Their sum is indeed 190.]]. And because this same number is found written on one of the black squares, by setting aside the said square, we will use this pyramid instead, as will be explained further below in its proper place.

Thus, we have now described the numbers that were to be painted upon the levels of the pyramids and under their bases. These numbers—both those of the pyramids and those of the individual pieces—were devised by the authors of this game with great **artifice** [[notes: *Artificio* here refers to a skillful, complex system or scientific design.]], which depends upon the proportions taught by the science of **Arithmetic**. I have deliberately chosen to leave aside this formal system and instead teach how to find the aforementioned numbers through practice rather than through theory [[notes: *Scientia*. The author is distinguishing between "practice" (rote calculation) and "science" (the underlying Boethian mathematical theory of proportions).]], so as not to obfuscate and entangle the minds of those who desire to learn this game with ease. I intend to simplify it in such a manner that it can be learned even by those who are not well-versed in the study of proportions. 

Now, we shall only note a few things necessary for the labeling of the aforementioned numbers, and then we will conclude this second part.

First, then, it must be noted that since all the pieces and the levels of the pyramids have two faces—one white and the other black—it is necessary to paint the same numbers we have calculated on both sides: using black ink on the white face, and white color on the black face. This is done because it is very convenient for the gameplay, as you will understand in the proper place [[notes: This suggests pieces might be flipped or captured and used by the opponent, requiring the value to be visible regardless of which side is up.]].

Beyond this, you will take care to write the numbers of the pyramid levels on multiple sides, so that they may be more easily seen by all those who are playing.
