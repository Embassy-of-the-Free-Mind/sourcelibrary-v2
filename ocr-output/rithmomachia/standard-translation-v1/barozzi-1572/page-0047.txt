[[margin: Note that all the 4 aforementioned numbers have within them the 3 proportions together, but in the 3 marked with a cross [[notes: The dagger symbol † in the table.]] the Musical consonances are not found. And therefore **Buxero** [[notes: Claude de Boissière, a 16th-century French mathematician who wrote a famous manual on Rithmomachia.]] did not include them, even though **Fabro** [[notes: Jacques Lefèvre d'Étaples, also known as Faber Stapulensis, whose 1496 work on the game was a primary source for later authors.]] does include them.]]

This is therefore the greatest, most excellent, and final [[markup: vittoria]] (victory) that ends the [[markup: giuoco]] (game). For when one has, with great effort, arranged 4 pieces in the enemy's field whose numbers form these three proportions together, those who are playing come to feel a wonderful delight. They contemplate how, in these 4 numbers so situated, are found all those proportions from which all musical [[markup: conſonantie]] (consonances) are born. If they then wish to experiment with these same proportions on strings of various kinds, or in wind instruments using the weights and measures of these numbers, they will hear the sweetness of all those harmonies that Music can provide.
