[[notes: This woodcut illustration depicts the rectangular grid used for Rythmomachia. While the preceding text described an 8x16 board (the equivalent of two chessboards), this specific diagram shows a grid of 8x12 squares. Such discrepancies were not uncommon in early printed manuals, reflecting variations in play or errors in woodblock carving.]]

[[notes: Modern readers will notice faint lettering appearing in the upper portion of the grid. These Latin words—including fragments like *solis* (of the sun), *non* (not), and *quid* (what)—are not part of the game board itself. They are "show-through" or "ghosting," where the heavy ink from the text on the reverse side of the page has bled through the paper over time.]]

[[notes: The small gap at the bottom center of the border line likely served as a guide for the printer or a symbolic entry point for the "armies" of numbers that would occupy the white and black squares.]]
