Finally, wishing to find the numbers of the other 4 black squares, we will operate in the same manner as we did when finding the numbers of the last 4 white squares, except that for these we will not begin from 5 to take the four following odd numbers, skipping one as we did then: but we will begin from 7. These odd numbers [[notes: *Numeri impari*. The game relies heavily on Pythagorean number theory, where squares are generated by the summation of odd numbers.]] will be 7, 11, 15, and 19. When multiplied by themselves, they will produce the desired numbers, which will be 49, 121, 225, and 361. This is because from 7 multiplied by itself is born 49; from 11, 121; from 15, 225; and from 19, 361. As the following figure demonstrates.

Thus far, we have found all those numbers that were to be painted upon the 48 pieces of our game [[notes: *Giuoco* refers to Rithmomachia, also known as "The Philosophers' Game," a complex board game that taught arithmetic and proportions.]]—that is, upon the 16 circles, 16 triangles, and 16 squares, both white and black. Now, before we conclude the second part of our treatise, there only remains for us to find the numbers that must be written upon the levels of the two pyramids, which we have said are counted in this game as two squares [[notes: The Pyramid is a "super-piece." It is physically composed of several smaller shapes stacked together, and its total value is the sum of those layers.]]. 

We shall begin, then, with the white pyramid, which has six levels [[notes: The author uses *gradi*, meaning steps or grades.]]. Upon the sixth level, which is the first foundation at the bottom, or its base, we will note the number 36; and upon the second level moving from the base toward the summit, we will write 25; and upon the third, 16; and upon the fourth, 9; and upon the fifth, 4; and upon the last, unity [[notes: "Unity" refers to the number 1.]]. Beneath the base, we will note the sum of the six aforementioned numbers, which, joined together, make 91. And because this number 91 is already found in one of the white squares, it is therefore cast away [[notes: The text ends abruptly here; it likely refers to a rule where duplicate numbers are handled specifically or where the pyramid's base value must be unique.]]
