...to such a sum of the value of the numbers; let that person have won the game, having considered together both the pieces, the digits [[notes: *Cifre*—referring to the physical number of digits written on the captured pieces, regardless of their value.]], and the value of the numbers.

And note that these seven victories are derived from the three principal victories of this game. For just as in one of those—that is, in the "Great Victory"—there were three simple ones, namely **Arithmetic**, **Geometric**, and **Musical** [[notes: In the Renaissance, "Music" referred to Harmonic proportions and ratios, a branch of mathematics alongside arithmetic and geometry.]]; from which were composed three others contained in the "Greater Victory"—namely Arithmetic and Geometric together, Arithmetic and Musical together, and Geometric and Musical together; from which finally was composed the "Greatest Victory," which contained all three together: Arithmetic, Geometric, and Musical. 

In the same way, in these last seven **rough** and **plain** victories [[notes: *Roze & schiette*—simple, unrefined versions of the game played for points rather than complex mathematical proofs.]], there are the first three simple ones: that of the pieces, that of the value of the numbers, and that of the digits. From these three simple ones, three other composite ones are born: one of pieces and value of numbers together; another of pieces and quantity of digits together; the third of value of numbers and quantity of digits together. From these also arises the seventh and final victory, composed of all the first three simples: that is, of pieces, value of numbers, and quantity of digits all together. 

I will not fail also to remind you that with the pieces of this game, one could play the game of **chess**, using only half of the board [[notes: Rythmomachia was typically played on a double-sized 8x16 board, so half would be the standard 8x8 chess board.]], and using the **Pyramid** for the King, and three squares stacked one on top of the other for the Queen, and one square for each Rook, and a triangle atop a square for each of the Knights, and two triangles one atop the other for each Bishop [[notes: *Alfiero*—the standard Italian term for the Chess Bishop.]]; and the eight circles for the eight Pawns. Each should be moved according to the movements used in the game of chess, adding to it whatever else may seem fit from our own game to enrich it—especially since the pieces have different numbers on them, which can "fight" in ways beyond the standard movement of the pieces. I truly believe that the game of chess was derived from our game of **Rythmomachia**, the former being more modern than the latter. Wherefore, our game being such...
