## DEDICATORY.

Whereby without a further proof <note>original: "profe"</note>,
all men may be quite sure:
That this game to seriousness <note>original: "gravity"</note>,
and wisdom does allure.

Otherwise <note>original: "Els"</note> would not that philosopher,
Pythagoras <note>original: "Pithagoras"</note> so wise:
Have labored with diligence,
this pastime to devise.

Otherwise <note>original: "Els"</note> would not so well-learned men,
have amplified the same:
From time to time with great labor <note>original: "trauell"</note>,
to bring it into fame.

But let us now proceed closer <note>original: "nerer"</note>,
and let us come to the effect <note>original: "theffect"</note>:
And then we shall assuredly,
this pastime not neglect.

For it with pleasure does soothe <note>original: "assuage"</note>,
the heavy troubled heart:
And with such comforts drives away,
all kinds of surging <note>original: "sourging"</note> pain <note>original: "smart"</note>.

<summary>In this concluding portion of the verse dedication, the author argues for the legitimacy and intellectual value of the game (Rythmomachia). He asserts that its association with Pythagoras and its refinement by learned scholars over time prove its worth. Finally, the author claims the game serves a therapeutic purpose, providing pleasure that can soothe a troubled mind and relieve emotional distress.</summary>
