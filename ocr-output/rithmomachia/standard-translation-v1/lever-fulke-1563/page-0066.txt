# The Philosophers' game.

The pieces <note>original: "men"</note> are 48 in number as before—24 on a side—with two opposing kings of even and odd numbers. They must be of different colors, as has been said. The bottom of every piece must have its enemy's color and its own number mark. This differs in one point from the previous version of play: captured enemy pieces may only serve to celebrate a victory <note>original: "triumphe"</note>, but they may not fight on the side of the player who captures them.

## ¶ Of the marking of the pieces <note>original: "men"</note>.

They are marked with the same numbers that have been shown before, and therefore are to be identified as was previously taught. However, besides their numbers, they are marked with algebraic <note>original: "cossicall"</note> signs. These are signs used in the rule called the rule of the thing <note>original: "regula cossa"</note>, or algebra, representing roots, squares <note>original: "quadrats"</note>, cubes, fourth powers <note>original: "foursquared quadrats"</note>, sursolids <note>original: "sursolides"</note>, and sixth powers <note>original: "quadrats of cubes"</note>. All these six signs must be included in this game.
