# The book's ver-
# dict.

Missing <note>original: "Wanting"</note> I have been truly long,
In the English language <note>original: "tongue"</note> for many a day;
I do confess it very truly,
Yet although I came late on my way,
And played my part in Latin verse,
Making men merry with my recitation <note>original: "rehearse"</note>,
I do not care now, I profess,
Unto the world in many a guise <note>original: "guyse"</note>,
I show myself in English dress,
To whoever desires to know my name <note>original: "Who list my name to advertise"</note>;
And I am sure it is no sin,
To speak in English to my kin.

---

->CATO<-

> *Intermingle your cares with joy from time to time,* <note>original: "Interpone tuis interdum gaudia curis"</note>
> *So that you may be able to endure any labor with spirit.* <note>original: "Ut possis animo quemvis sufferre laborem"</note>

<summary>In this introductory poem, the book personifies itself to address the reader. It acknowledges that it has been unavailable in the English language for a long time, having previously only existed in Latin. The book expresses a sense of pride in finally appearing in "English dress" (a vernacular translation) so that it can be understood by its "kin" (the English people). It concludes with a classical maxim from Cato, advising the reader to balance hard work with moments of joy to better sustain their labors.</summary>
