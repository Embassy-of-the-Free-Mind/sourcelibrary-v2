# THE EPISTLE

It makes the mind circumspect,
and heedful for to be:
The time that is bestowed upon it,
is truly not in vain.

It stirs and moves the body,
to lightheartedness <note>original: "lightsomnes"</note> and joy:
It does not annoy the senses
or the powers in any way.

It practices arithmetic <note>original: "Arithmeticke"</note>,
and shows the use of numbers:
As he who is skilled <note>original: "conning"</note> in it,
assuredly well knows.

It truly wades in geometry <note>original: "Geometrie"</note>,
and has business to do there:
It is doubtless a learned game <note>original: "play"</note>,
and no one can deny it <note>original: "say nay thereto"</note>.

It joins musical proportion <note>original: "Proportion musicall"</note>
with the other two:
So that within it, three noble arts <note>original: "artes"</note>
are certainly exercised.

<summary>In this verse preface, the author outlines the mental and physical benefits of playing the game (Rythmomachia). He argues that it sharpens the mind, promotes a joyful spirit, and requires the practical application of three "noble arts" from the Quadrivium: arithmetic, geometry, and musical proportion. The author claims that the time spent on the game is a productive intellectual exercise rather than a frivolous waste.</summary>
