### Victory of Standards.

They have invented another victory, which is that of standards, by mimicking <note>original: "counterfeyting"</note> two armies: one of the Christians and another of the Turks. The white pieces <note>original: "men"</note>, which are the even army <note>original: "hoste"</note>, contain 1,312 footmen (not counting the square roots <note>original: "rootes of squares"</note> represented on the Kings). Let the first and last be captains, and let them divide the whole army into 10 standards; thus, every standard shall have 130 men, besides the two captains and the ten standard-bearers.

<summary>
This section introduces the "Victory of Standards," a variation of the game where pieces are treated as soldiers in two opposing armies (themed as Christians vs. Turks, or Even vs. Odd). It details the mathematical division of the total numerical values of the pieces into units called "standards," led by captains and standard-bearers. The winner is the player who captures the most standards.
