# To the Reader.

...playing, abhorred by all good men and condemned by all good laws: do I not have some fair justification <note>original: "colour of defence"</note> to teach the game which so many wise men have invented, so many learned men have practiced <note>original: "frequented"</note>, and no good man has ever condemned? 

Its invention is ascribed to Pythagoras; it bears the name of philosophers; prudent men practice it, and godly men praise it. But because many people in this regard (as in a play) have claimed much authority, they have filled this game with much variation <note>original: "diuersitie"</note>. 

In this, as I could perceive the greatest differences of playing to consist of three kinds, I have plainly and briefly set them forth in English—not as though more variations <note>original: "diuersities"</note> might not be discovered <note>original: "espied"</note>, but
