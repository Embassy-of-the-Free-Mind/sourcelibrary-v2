### Of the placing, encamping, or setting in array <note>original: "letting in araie"</note>.

To return again to the plain and
easy playing of this game: following
the description of the army and their armor, next follows
either the order of their battle or encamping. 
Because it is more plain
and easily seen with the eye than learned
by the ear, I refer you to the
table where the battle is arranged in
such order as this kind of play
requires.

This text completes the description of the "King of Odd" piece in Rythmomachia, noting that its pyramidal value (190) is formed by the sum of squares from 8x8 down to 4x4. Because it lacks the final squares (3x3 down to 1x1), the physical piece is flat-topped rather than pointed. The author then transitions to the setup of the game board, directing the reader to a visual diagram of the "battle" array, asserting that the starting positions are easier to understand visually than through written instruction.
