# Lord Robert Dudley

<image-desc>A woodcut portrait of Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester, depicted from the waist up. He is shown in three-quarter profile, wearing a flat hat, a starched ruff collar, and highly detailed plate armor decorated with patterns. He wears a heavy decorative chain with a prominent medallion. The portrait is set within an architectural oval frame adorned with scrollwork, acanthus leaves, and two winged cherubs at the upper corners. A blank, curled scroll lies across the bottom of the oval frame.</image-desc>

Virtue flourishes from a wound. <note>original: "Vulnere virescit virtus"</note>

The physiognomy <note>original: "Physnogmie"</note> depicted <note>original: "figured"</note> here appears by the painter's art,
But valiant are the virtues that possess the inner <note>original: "inward"</note> part.
Which in no way <note>original: "in no wise"</note> can be painted, yet plainly do appear,
And shine abroad in every place with beams most bright and clear.

<summary>This page serves as a formal dedication or frontispiece featuring Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester, a prominent figure in the court of Elizabeth I. It includes a Latin motto and a short poem (quatrain) that contrasts the subject's physical appearance, captured by the artist, with his inner "valiant virtues," which the poet claims are too great to be painted but are evident through his actions.</summary>
