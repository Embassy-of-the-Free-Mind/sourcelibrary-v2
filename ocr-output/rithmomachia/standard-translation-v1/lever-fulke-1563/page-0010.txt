### THE EPISTLE
<sig>A.v.</sig>

So shall we live truly like Christians <note>original: "christianlyke"</note>,
and do our duties well;
So shall we please both God and prince,
no one shall need to compel us.

And then the Lord, in His mercy,
will always prosper us;
And grant us here on earth to have
many godly days.

Yes, then the Lord, in His goodness
and heavenly <note>original: "celestiall"</note> grace,
Will guide and govern our affairs,
and bless all our deeds <note>original: "doings"</note>.

May that Lord grant to your Honor
good and long days to have,
With a great increase of health and prosperity <note>original: "vvelth"</note>
and save you from all harm.

->Your Honor’s most humble,<-
->James Rowbothum <note>original: "Roubothum"</note>.<-

<summary>This concluding section of the verse dedication emphasizes the spiritual and social rewards of a virtuous life. The author, James Rowbothum, suggests that by fulfilling their duties and living in a Christian manner, people will please both God and the state without the need for coercion. He concludes with a formal blessing for his patron, praying for their longevity, health, and prosperity under divine protection.</summary>
