Every number may be taken for a root <note>original: "rote"</note>, such as 2. This number multiplied by itself is a square <note>original: "quadrate"</note>, such as 4. The square multiplied by the root gives a cube or solid square; for example, 4 multiplied by 2 gives 8, which is a cube. 

Multiply the cube by the root, and so you have a fourth power <note>original: "squared quadrat"</note>; for example, 8 multiplied by 2 gives 16, which is a square of a square. Multiply the square of the square (the fourth power) by the root, and the product is the fifth power <note>original: "sursolyde"</note>; for example, 2 times 16 is 32, which is a fifth power. 

Multiply the fifth power by the root, and the product is the sixth power <note>original: "quadrate of a cube"</note>; for example, 2 times 32 is 64, which is a square of a cube. Thus you have the root, square, cube, fourth power, fifth power, and sixth power: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64.
