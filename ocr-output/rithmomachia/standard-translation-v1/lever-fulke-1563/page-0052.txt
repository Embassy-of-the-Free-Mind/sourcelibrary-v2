## Regarding Arithmetical Proportion <note>original: "Of Arithmeticall proportion"</note>.

Arithmetical proportion is when the middle <note>original: "mydle"</note> number differs as much from the first as it does from the third. That is to say, it is when the third number has as much more than the second as the second has than the first—for example, 2, 4, 6. Here, two is the distance <note>original: "distans"</note>, for 4 exceeds 2 by two, and 6 is more than four by two.

### A rule to find out arithmetical proportion between the first and the last.

When you have the first and the last numbers, if you would find out the middle value in the proportion: add the first and the last together and divide the total by 2; the half is the middle value in the proportion.

### Regarding the Various Kinds of Triumphs <note>original: "Of dyuers kyndes of triumphes"</note>.

There are <note>original: "be"</note> three kinds <note>original: "kyndes"</note> of triumphs: a Great Triumph, a Greater Triumph, and the Greatest and most noble of all.

### Regarding the Great Triumph <note>original: "Of the great triumph"</note>.

The Great Triumph consists <note>original: "standeth"</note> in proportion, either **arithmetical**, **geometrical**, or **musical** only.
