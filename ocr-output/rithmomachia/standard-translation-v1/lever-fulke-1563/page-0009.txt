<header>DEDICATORY.</header>

What game, therefore, like unto this,
can be obtained or had?
There is not one that I know;
the rest are all too bad.

This causes no contention,
nor any debate at all;
By this, no hatred, wrath, nor guile <note>original: "guyle"</note>
arises in any way <note>original: "in any vvyse doth fall"</note>.

It does not stir such troubles that
our friend becomes our foe;
This does not move one to mischief <note>original: "mischiefe"</note>
as many others do.

Let us avoid the worst, therefore,
and let us cleave to the best.
So shall we shun all wickedness,
and obtain <note>original: "purchase"</note> quiet rest.

So shall we serve the living Lord,
and walk according to His will;
So shall we do the thing that is good,
and flee <note>original: "flye"</note> that which is evil <note>original: "yll"</note>.

<sig>a.v.</sig>

***

<summary>In this concluding portion of the verse dedication, the author argues for the moral superiority of the game (Rythmomachia) over other pastimes. He claims that unlike other games which lead to anger, deceit, and broken friendships, this game fosters peace and quietude. Ultimately, the author suggests that engaging in such an intellectually and morally sound activity aligns with a Christian life, helping the player avoid vice and follow God's will.</summary>
