# The Philosopher's Game.

By this table, any person <note>original: "man"</note>, though he has <note>original: "haue"</note> little <note>original: "small"</note> or no skill in arithmetic, may learn to play this game and, while playing, learn some part of arithmetic.

## Regarding capturing by proportion <note>original: "Of takynge"</note>.

If the players <note>original: "Gamesters"</note> are so inclined <note>original: "be disposed"</note>, they may also capture pieces <note>original: "take men"</note> by proportion: arithmetic, geometric, or musical. But because it is not necessarily required that they should do so, I will first proceed with the method of victory <note>original: "prosecute the maner of triumph"</note>, in which they may also learn to capture by proportion, as shall be seen afterward. For when they can join two or three of their pieces <note>original: "men"</note> to one of their opponent's <note>original: "aduersaries"</note> pieces in such order as the victory <note>original: "triumph"</note> is set—so that those three or four numbers have any of these three proportions—they may capture their opponent's piece.
