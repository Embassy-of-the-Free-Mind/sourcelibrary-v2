# ON THE ART OF CHESS.

[[notes: The original text is laid out as a dichotomous branching diagram, a popular 17th-century method for organizing knowledge. The following structure mirrors those logical connections.]]

**The Chess Art** is defined by its:
- The Goal being presupposed; its Principles; Properties; Uses; Related things; and Contraries; Book 1, ch. 1. [[notes: "Contraries" likely refers to games that are the opposite of chess, perhaps games of pure chance like dice.]]
- [[unclear: Origin]] and Authors; Book 1, ch. 2.
- [[unclear: Name]], by which the ART is known:
    - by its genus [[notes: its general category as a game]]; Book 1, ch. 4.
    - by its species [[notes: its specific type]], concerning:
        - **The Pieces** [[gloss: *Latrunculis*]]; Book 1, ch. 5 to 11. [[notes: *Latrunculi* was an ancient Roman board game, but Renaissance scholars used the term to refer to chess pieces, literally "little soldiers."]]
        - **The Board** [[gloss: *Tabulâ*]]; Book 1, ch. 11.

**The Plan** [[gloss: *Idea*]] of the game, through:
- The fitness and requirements [[unclear: of the players]] for beginning the Game; Book 2, ch. 2.
- [[unclear: Their]] Duty regarding the preparations for the Game; Book 2, ch. 3.
- The **Form**, through **Canons** or **Rules**, for using the means correctly; Book 2, ch. 4 & 5.
- The [[unclear: Plan]], through:
    - Preparation regarding the following modes of play; Book 2, ch. 6.
    - The division of the modes; Book 2, ch. 7. Where they are either:
        - **Common & usual**, which are contained in 3 classes, of which:
            - **The First** includes:
                - 8 principal modes, of which:
                    - Seven are shown in Book 2, ch. 8 to the end of the book.
                    - The eighth is shown in Book 3, ch. 1 to 10.
                - A less principal mode, which is contained in Book 3, ch. 10.
            - **The Second** is described in Book 3, ch. 11 & 12.
            - **The Third**, which is **less legitimate** [[notes: *minùs legitima* likely refers to irregular openings or "street" play rather than "illegal" moves]], is contained in Book 3, ch. 13.
        - **Singular & Agreed-upon** [[gloss: *Pactitij*]], of which several examples are handed down in Book 4, ch. 1 to 9. [[notes: *Pactitij* refers to "odds" games or variants where players agree to special conditions (like playing without a Queen) before starting.]]
