### The Second Book. Chapter 9:

...and forty and moves to the W. Queen: **The White player** (to rescue his King’s Rook) takes the Black King’s Knight with his King’s **Bishop** [[notes: "Schütze" literally translates to Archer, the 17th-century German term for the Bishop]]: **The Black player** [takes] the White Queen with his King’s Bishop: **The White player** [takes] the Black King’s Knight’s **Pawn** [[notes: "Soldat" or Soldier is the period term for a Pawn]] with the King’s Rook, gives **Check**, and exposes the King’s Bishop against the Black Queen: **The Black player** takes the White Rook with the King: **The White player** [takes] the Black Queen with the King’s Bishop: **The Black player** (in order to prevent the thirty-sixth square [[notes: The author uses a numbered coordinate system for the board]], so that from there the White King’s Bishop may not give **Check** nor trap his King’s Rook) moves his King’s Knight to fifty-three: **The White player** takes the Black King’s Rook with his King’s Knight: **The Black player** [takes] the same with the King’s Rook: **The White player** moves the King’s Rook’s Pawn to twenty-four: the Rook to the fifth [square]: fortifies his King in the Queen’s quarter [[notes: "Quartier" refers to the side of the board, here meaning the Queenside]], and must subsequently—because **the Black player** has a Knight as an advantage (though this is a broken and messy game)—play his moves thoughtfully.

[[margin: 10.]]

Thus **the White player** can easily win the game and bring it to an end with this very good arrangement of his pieces.
