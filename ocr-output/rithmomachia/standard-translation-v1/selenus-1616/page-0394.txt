# The Tenth Chapter.

## How the game is to be conducted when the White [player] moves the King’s Soldier, but the Black [player] in response moves the Queen’s [Soldier].

Before we conclude the manner in which both King’s **Soldiers** [[notes: "Soldaten" or Soldiers is the period term for Pawns]] should be led onto the **field** [[notes: "plan" refers to the chessboard or the field of play]] at the start of the game, we wish briefly to consider how one must arrange the **moves** when Black, right in the first move at the **boundary** [[notes: the center of the board where the ranks meet]], makes an attack on White’s King’s Soldier with his own King’s [[notes: The text here says "K. Soldaten" (King's) but the context of the chapter title and the move to square 37 implies the Queen's Soldier/Pawn. This is likely a historical typo or refers to the "King's side" of the board generally]] Soldier.

If White moves the **King’s Soldier** to **twenty-eight** [[notes: In the 1–64 numbering system used here, 28 corresponds to the square e4 in modern notation]]: then Black does not move his King’s Soldier (as was indicated many times in the previous examples) to **thirty-six** [[notes: e5]], but rather the **Queen’s Soldier** to **thirty-seven** [[notes: d5, known today as the Scandinavian Defense]]. White moves the King’s Soldier (which is indeed not an uneven move) to **thirty-six** [[notes: f4, attempting a King's Gambit structure]]: yet it is better that he take away the **Black King’s Soldier** [[notes: Again, referring to the pawn on d5]] (because he thereby gains a move against the enemy). For when Black takes the **White King’s Soldier** with the **Queen**: he [White] then moves the **King’s Knight** [[notes: "Reuter" literally translates to Rider or Horseman, the period term for the Knight]] against her to **twenty-two** [[notes: Nc3]]: Black moves the same [Queen] back to **sixty-one** [[notes: d8]]: he [White] moves the **King’s Soldier** [[notes: likely the d-pawn here, as the e-pawn was traded]] to **twenty-nine** [[notes: d4]]: and subsequently brings the **six main pieces** [[notes: "Haupt-Steine" refers to the "officer" pieces—the two Rooks, two Knights, and two Bishops]] forward into the field with great caution in order to attack the enemy and damage his position as circumstances allow.
