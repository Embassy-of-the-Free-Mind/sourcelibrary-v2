### The Second Book / Chapter 14.

thirty: White [moves] the Knight [[notes: *Reuter*, literally "Rider," refers to the Knight]] to thirty-one and gives Check. Black moves the King to thirty. White [moves] the Knight (out of danger) to forty-eight. Black [moves] the King to thirty-seven. White [moves] his King to forty-seven. Black must move the King to thirty (otherwise the Pawn [[notes: *Soldat*, literally "Soldier," refers to the Pawn]] is lost, or White, with the Knight on fifty-four, would give Check and likewise capture the Pawn). White [moves] the King to forty. Therefore Black loses the Pawn (whether he lets it stand or moves it to thirty-one), and this game becomes equal [[notes: *gleich wird* implies the material is balanced, leading to a draw]].

16. If, however, Black moves not the Pawn to 39 (as in no. 13) but the Bishop [[notes: *Schütze*, literally "Shooter," refers to the Bishop]] to twenty-eight: then White moves the King to thirty-four. Black [moves] the Bishop toward the White Knight at fifty-five. White takes, with that same [Knight], the Black King's Knight's Pawn [[notes: The original text *Reuteror Soldaten* appears to be a typesetting error for *Reuter-Soldaten* (Knight's Pawn)]]. Black [takes] with the King the White King's Pawn. White moves the King to twenty-seven. Black [moves] the Bishop to forty-six. White [moves] the King to twenty. Black [moves] his King toward the White Knight at fifty-four. White (to give the game a quick conclusion) takes the Black King's Pawn with the Knight. Black [takes] the same with the Bishop. White (to come to the aid of his Pawn) [moves] the King to twenty-nine. Black [moves] the King to forty-seven. White [moves] the King to twenty-two. Black [moves] the King to thirty-nine. For these reasons White cannot make the Pawn into a Queen; rather, the game is at once drawn [[notes: *aufgehoben* here means the game is neutralized or drawn]].
