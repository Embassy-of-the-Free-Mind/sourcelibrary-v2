### The Fourth Book / Chapter 5:

(Black King’s Pawn / taken / and Check given / as completed in no. 11) should not take the Black Queen / (which moved in front of her King / to square 52) but rather take the Black King’s Rook / with his Queen / and move onto his Knight: [[notes: The author is using an older nomenclature for chess pieces: *Soldaten* (Soldiers) are Pawns, *Reuter* (Riders/Horsemen) are Knights, *Schützen* (Archers) are Bishops, and *Rochen* (Rocks/Castles) are Rooks.]] * The Black player / moves the same / (in order not to lose it / and to forbid the White Queen / her exit) to forty-three: [[notes: The numbers refer to the author's specific 1-64 coordinate system for the board, common in 17th-century treatises before algebraic notation was standardized.]] The White player / (in order / to help his Queen) moves his Bishop / against the Black King’s / Bishop / to forty-one: Therefore the Bishops / and the Queens / necessarily / are exchanged / and the White player / gains / besides the Knight / also the Rook / as an advantage: In the occurrence / of the exchange / the Black player / has nonetheless / lost his entire game: For / if He / with the King’s Bishop’s Pawn / takes the White King’s Pawn / and offers a discovered check [[notes: *Ab-Schach-beut* refers to a discovered check, where moving one piece reveals an attack from another standing behind it]] / from his Queen: The White player / can cover / the King / (insofar as He / does not wish to move him) with his Bishop / on square twelve / and will be safe in that manner: if he [Black] now / takes the same [the Bishop] / with the King’s Bishop’s Pawn: then White / lets this Black Pawn / stand before the King / either / as a breastwork [[notes: *Brust-wehre*—a military term for a defensive parapet; here, the author suggests using the opponent's pawn as a shield for one's own King]] / or removes it / with his Knight.

[[margin: 14.]] But if the Black player / before He moves the Knight / to 43 (as in no. 13) / takes the White King’s Pawn / with the King’s Bishop’s Pawn / and offers a discovered check / from his Queen: then White / nonetheless / should cover / the King / with his Bishop / (insofar as He / wishes to have / his game / in the state / as described in no. 13) / on square twelve.

[[margin: 15.]] But if White / does not wish / to lose the Bishop / but rather moves the King / (when Black / has given discovered check / from his Queen in no. 14) / to square five: then Black takes / with his King’s Bishop’s Pawn / the White King’s Bishop’s Pawn / and offers check once again: White / (so that He / does not lose the King’s Knight) / takes the same / with the King: Black / offers / with the Queen / from square twenty-eight / check: White / moves / the King / either to square five / or covers the same / with his Bishop / on twenty-one / and moves it / against the Black Queen: Black / takes / with the same [Queen] / (in this latter case) / the White King’s Knight’s Pawn / offers check once again / and moves her / onto the White King’s Rook: White / covers the King / with his Knight / on twelve: Black / takes the White King’s Rook / with the Queen:
