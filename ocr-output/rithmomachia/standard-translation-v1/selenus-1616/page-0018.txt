**AM I MISTAKEN?** This battle of the pawns [[notes: The Latin *Latronum* refers to *Ludus latrunculorum*, an ancient Roman game often cited by Renaissance scholars as a precursor to chess; here the author uses it to mean the chess pieces themselves.]] shadows forth the human 
Vicissitudes, troubles, and the chances
Of life; which may be tempered by care: And
It is right for Mortals to go forth to meet a harsh Fate.
->G. REMUS, J.C. P.A. [[notes: Georg Remus (1561–1625) was a celebrated jurist (*Juris Consultus*) and Patrician of Nuremberg (*Patricius Altdorfinus*).]] Nuremberg.<-

### In praise of the most illustrious Lord GUSTAVUS SELENUS,
### regarding the Work on the Game of Chess, a Madrigal
### by FILIPPO HAINHOFER, Patrician [[notes: Philipp Hainhofer (1578–1647) was a prominent merchant, diplomat, and art collector in Augsburg, famous for creating "cabinets of curiosities" for European royalty.]]
### of the City of Augsburg.

To enticing battles,
To merciful revenges,
To feigned assaults, to simulated gains,
In a small field, upon these pages,
O **GUSTAVUS SELENUS**, you train the art.
But with charming shadows
To warlike duties
To glorious ardors,
You opened both wit and art;
For it often happens that noble hearts
(A flattering deception)
The Game entices toward arms—the feigned leading to the real.

### ANOTHER.

To command in the city, to rule warrior
Armed squads, and to make them in an instant
Swift, to change their ground, and now from the front
To wheel, now all around, bold and fierce,
