# On the Battle of Numbers

[[image-desc: A mathematical table showing how two sets of triangles are added to create the first four black square pieces.]]

| | | | | |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Row 1: Triangles** | 12 | 30 | 56 | 90 |
| **Row 2: Triangles** | 16 | 36 | 64 | 100 |
| **Row 3: Sum (Squares)** | 28 | 66 | 120 | 190 |

The numbers of the first four **black square stones** [[notes: "Stones" refers to the game pieces used in Rythmomachia]] are found from the sums of the added numbers which are marked upon the eight black **triangular stones**: For 12 and 16 make 28; 30 and 36 make 66; 56 and 64 make 120; 90 and 100 make 190.

---

[[image-desc: A diagram showing four squares containing the numbers 49, 121, 225, and 361. Above each square is its square root: 7, 11, 15, and 19 respectively.]]

The numbers for the remaining four **black square stones** are sought in the same manner as the last four white square stones, except that one begins not from 5, but from 7 (hence the numbers omitted in the list of the aforementioned last four white square stones are marked here) in the following way: 7, 11, 15, 19. Furthermore, 9, 13, and 17 (which were used for the aforementioned three last white square stones) are omitted. These numbers now, multiplied by themselves [[notes: This refers to squaring the numbers, e.g., $7 \times 7 = 49$]], give the sought-after numbers: namely, 49, 121, 225, 361.
