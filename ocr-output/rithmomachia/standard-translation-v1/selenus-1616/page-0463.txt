# On the Game of Chess.

[[margin: Chapter 9]]

[[image-desc: A woodcut illustration of a chess piece named "The Sneaker" (Der Schleich). The piece is depicted as a bust of a jester wearing a hood with bells and holding a fool's scepter (marotte), placed upon a decorative, chalice-like pedestal.]]

->The Sneaker.<-

The **Whimsical Advisors** or ***Moriones*** [[notes: *Moriones* is Latin for fools or jesters]], (whom they call the **Sneakers** because of their creeping gait, as it were), are moved by them like the **Rooks**, yet only one square—forward, backward, or to the right or left side.

The **Soldiers**, ***Fenden***, or ***Finen*** [[notes: These are archaic terms for pawns, derived from the Latin *pedinus* or the French *pion*]] move and capture other pieces just as was mentioned above in Chapter 10 of the First Book. However, the soldiers of both the **Queens** and the **Rooks** on both sides are moved forward two squares for their first move. Furthermore, neither they nor the **Queen** can capture other pieces until they have completed these aforementioned leaps; however, the soldiers of the **King** and the other major pieces are moved only one square forward at the start.

Whoever has the first move must always, at the beginning, leap to the third square with the **Queen's pawn** and both **Rooks' pawns**; and the other player, move for move, must hop toward him with the same pawns. After these three moves, each tries his best with these and the other pieces. [[notes: This describes a "mandatory opening" where the game only truly begins after both players have moved their two Rook-pawns and their Queen-pawn two squares forward.]]

Since the **Secret Counselor** or **Man** is deemed the best piece for the protection of the **King** at the end, he must be well-regarded and protected by both sides.

Let this now be a brief reminder of the **Courier Game**, recorded *in transitu* [[notes: Latin for "in passing" or "along the way"]] as much as could be learned from the oral reports of those in **Ströbeck** [[notes: *Ströpeke* is the local dialect name for Ströbeck, a German village famous for its chess tradition]]; for they had no written records to show, but rather claimed that they inherited it from their ancestors from time immemorial and have practiced it accordingly.
