<margin>5. cap:</margin>
<margin>Of the First Way:</margin>
**The** opinion of Damiano—regarding how one can protect the Black King’s Soldier [[note: The King’s Pawn, specifically the one on the e5 square in modern notation]] in three ways (namely, with one major piece and two soldiers)—has now been sufficiently recounted and improved upon from the Second Chapter until here. Now, we also wish to set down our own opinion, establishing a certain order among the protections (organized into different chapters). Within these, we shall indicate and reveal the harm and the benefit which players will find therein according to the circumstances; also, which among them all is the best and safest, and which one should be most observed and used. This is for the benefit and good not only of those who are already well-experienced in the game, but also, and primarily, for those who do not yet know how to manage it so well.

And it is to be known that one can protect and defend the Black King’s Soldier not only in three, but in five ways (namely, with three major pieces and two soldiers), as follows:

1. Firstly: with the Queen’s Rider [[note: The Queen's Knight]], (which protection Damiano, above in the 2nd Chapter of this book, considers to be the best. He does not, however, consider the inconveniences which arise from it—provided the opponent is well-acquainted with the game—which shall be indicated in the following part of this chapter) from [square] forty-six.
