# The First Book / Chapter 10:

> » things [[notes: the sentence continues from the previous page's "defend"]], for one who is in need, for their conduct and life, and for all their 
> » **sustenance and standing** [[notes: *parat und stampony* refers to one's equipment, provisions, and social status]]. And therefore the **Master** [[notes: This refers to the legendary inventor of chess, often identified in medieval texts as the philosopher Xerxes or Philometor]], who 
> » invented this **Game**, placed the **common people** in front of the **Nobles**, 
> » so that they [[the nobles]] should not despise or oppress them. Secondly, 
> » one places the commoners before the nobles so that they have **empty fields** 
> » before them; this signifies that they should attend to their **crafts** 
> » and work, and they should leave the 
> » **Counsel** to the nobles: for they know what the laws of the land are, 
> » with which the **common man** may not protect or concern himself, 
> » and he is not capable of it; therefore they should entrust the **high counsels** 
> » to the Nobles, and [the commoners] should not speak forth, except for what 
> » belongs to each man's own work and craft: and not use force 
> » wickedly, for through such things **wisdom** is often suppressed and ruined, 
> » and it happens that **castles**, **cities**, and **lands** are lost. 
> » Thirdly, when the **pieces** [[notes: *gestein*, literally "stones"]] are set 
> » upon the **board**, one finds just as many empty fields as 
> » full and occupied ones: this signifies that a **Lord** who has a land 
> » should consider that for as many people as he has, he should have just as many **cities** 
> » and **castles** in which they may dwell; also, before these same 
> » cities, castles, and villages, he should leave as much field as 
> » may be farmed, so that those who are within may **sustain themselves**: 
> » and that he may protect them. For he who has a Lordly 
> » name without lordship, that is a nobility without use: also 
> » **poverty** is much more rejected and harder for [noble] people to bear: for 
> » no one scolds a **common poor man**, but 
> » a **noble poor man** is scolded: unless 
> » his poverty is covered by **discipline and virtue**. etc.
