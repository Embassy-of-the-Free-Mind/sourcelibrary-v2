-> ## The Second Book / 11. Chapter: <-

with the same [[notes: refers to the White King's Rook's Pawn from the previous page]], the **White player** gives **check** [[notes: *Schach*]] with the **Queen** from square thirty-three: the **Black player** moves the **King** to fifty-nine: the **White player** takes the **Black King's Knight's Pawn** [[notes: *S. K. Reuters-Soldaten*]] with the **King's Bishop** [[notes: *K. Schützen*]]: the **Black player** [takes] the same with his **King's Bishop's Pawn**—and not with his **Rook's Pawn**: (otherwise his **King's Rook** [[notes: *K. Roche*]] would be taken by the **White Queen**) the **White player** offers **check** with the **King's Rook** from square three: and **Check-Mate** [[notes: *Schach-Mat*]] with the **Queen** from fifty-one. If, however, the **Black player** moves the **King's Knight** [[notes: *K. Reuter*]] (mentioned at the start of this section) to other squares: or moves another piece in its place: then the **White player** must pay close attention and strive to lead and execute his game correctly, as he can well win the same following the opportunity of the above-mentioned moves.

[[margin: 22.]] Furthermore, if the **Black player** does not move the **Pawn** to 43 (no. 19) but instead moves the **King's Knight** to forty-four: then the **White player** takes the **Black King's Knight's Pawn** with the **King's Rook's Pawn**: the **Black player** moves against him (because he covers the **White Queen**) with his **King's Rook** to fifty-eight. The **White player** [moves] the **Queen** to the **Black King's Rook's Pawn** at nine: [[margin: 11.]]

[[margin: 23.]] * The **Black player** takes, with the same [[notes: the Rook]], the mentioned **White King's Knight's Pawn**: The **White player** plays the **King's Bishop** to twenty-six: The **Black player** moves the **Queen** to fifty-two: The **White player** moves his **Queen** against the **Black King's Rook** at forty-nine: The **Black player** [moves] the mentioned **Rook** against her at fifty: the **White player** moves her [[notes: the Queen]] to forty-one:

[[margin: 24.]] * The **Black player** [moves] the mentioned **Rook** against her again at forty-two: the **White player** offers **check** with the same [[notes: the Queen]] from fifty-seven: the **Black player** covers the **King** with the **Queen** (which is then the best move he can make) at fifty-nine: the **White player** takes the **Black Queen** with his **Queen** and offers **check**: the **Black player** takes the same with the **King's Knight**: the **White player** moves the **King's Bishop** against the **Black Rook** at thirty-five; in this way, he obtains the **Black King's Knight's Pawn** by force, and his game stands in good order.

[[margin: 25.]] If, however, the **Black player** does not move the **Rook** to 42 (no. 24) but instead moves the **King's Bishop's Pawn** (so that the mentioned **King's Rook** might also be protected by his **Queen**) to forty-three: it certainly appears to be a good move: but it is a great mistake: [[margin: 12.]]
