# The Fourth Book / Chapter 2:

Soldier with the King’s Bishop [[notes: *Schütze*, literally "Archer" or "Marksman," was the standard German term for the Bishop at this time]], and declares **Check**: **The Black** [moves] toward him (in order to cover the King) the King’s Bishop to fifty-three [[notes: In the 1–64 numbering system used by Selenus, 53 corresponds to the square e7]]: **The White** (in order to relieve the same) takes the **Black** King’s Soldier [[notes: *Soldat* is the author's term for a Pawn]] with the **Queen**, and through this brings his game into a good order; he can then safely and without danger harm the **White** [[notes: This appears to be a slip in the original text; context suggests he means the Black player is now the one who must defend]], who must take more care to defend and entrench himself than to injure or attack him, and thus, without any special further thought, win the game.
