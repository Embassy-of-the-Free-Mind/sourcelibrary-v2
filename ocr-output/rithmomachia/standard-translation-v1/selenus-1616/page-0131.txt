# Of the Game of Chess.

[[margin: 6th Chapter:]]
The **Knight's pawn** shall be in this form: It shall be a man who has in his right hand a **key**, and in the left a **rod**, like an **ell** [[notes: An *elen* or ell was a traditional unit of measurement, often used for cloth, representing the length of a man's arm from the elbow to the fingertip.]]; this signifies **officials** and guardians of the cities who look after measures and weights. And on his belt, he has a large **purse** with coins, which signifies **tax collectors** and **merchants**. He stands rightly before the Knight, for Knights should organize and direct the people, guarding and caring for them as has been commanded to them. These same people [[notes: the merchants and officials represented by this pawn]] should have a heartfelt love for what belongs to the **commonwealth**, and should be observant and diligent so that they behold and consider all the **dangers** [[notes: *freiß*, a Middle High German term for peril, danger, or injury]] that may happen to a city. They should also be pure in their conscience, honoring God with fear, so that they do not impose anything on anyone without cause—whether out of envy, hatred, or enmity—but only what is just. They should ensure that the common good is seen to increase in their hands, so that they are less likely to be removed from their offices. Among all evil things, it is the worst when a man wishes to increase and spread his own wealth and name through other people's harm or ill repute. The officials of the community should also be just and modest, and not greedy, nor show **partiality** [[notes: *sundercheit*, meaning favoritism or "particularity" toward specific persons]] toward individuals, so that they take no more from those who buy and sell than they rightly should; rather, they should show themselves to be mediators and promoters. For in **under-buying** [[notes: *underkauffen*, the practice of forestalling or cornering the market by buying goods before they reach the public to drive up prices]] many strange things occur; they should diligently and patiently guard against this at all times. For a true mediator who loves justice and honor should disdain to answer shameful talk at every hour; for whoever seeks injustice for another, the shame belongs to him who is unjust. Also, the **toll-takers** and tax collectors should guard themselves from inventing **new laws** at the custom houses with which people are unjustly burdened, nor should they have any tolls except those which are granted by the **Empire** [[notes: Referring to the Holy Roman Empire]] and established for the Empire's sake. Nor should they diminish anything that is rightly established. And when they collect their due, they should demand and receive it gently and virtuously, so that they do no wrong to anyone, and demand nothing from the community to the damage of their conscience; for he who robs shall be robbed.
