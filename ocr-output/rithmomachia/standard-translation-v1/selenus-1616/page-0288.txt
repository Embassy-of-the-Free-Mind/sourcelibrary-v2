->Xerxes Philomater.<- [[notes: According to medieval and Renaissance chess lore, Xerxes (also known as Philometor or "Friend of the Mother") was the legendary philosopher-courtier credited with inventing chess. He supposedly created the game to reform the character of the Babylonian king, Evil-merodach, teaching him that a monarch’s power depends entirely on the well-being and cooperation of his subjects.]]

->Amilinus Evilmerodach.<- [[notes: Amil-Marduk, known biblically as Evil-merodach, was a historical King of Babylon (reigned c. 562–560 BCE). In early chess literature, he is often cast as the student—or sometimes the tyrant—who is humbled and educated by the strategic logic of the chessboard.]]
