## The Ninth Chapter.

### Of the King’s and Queen’s Riders:
-> which fields they take and possess <-
-> at the start of the game, and how they move. <-

[[gloss: A framed woodcut illustration of a chess piece. It depicts a knight in a full suit of armor, holding a lance, mounted on a horse. The horse stands upon a decorative turned pedestal. At the bottom of the illustration, within the border, is the caption "A Rider."]]

Next to the **Archers** [[notes: *Schützen*—the author's name for the Bishops]], and considered equal to or in certain cases higher than them, are the pieces which are called **Equites Auratos** [[notes: Latin for "Golden Knights," a title of nobility]], Riders, Knights, or Leapers: by which, without doubt, the cavalry is signified.

Of these, there are two for each army [[notes: *hauffen*—literally "heaps," referring to the two opposing sets of pieces]], in the same manner: they have their places on both wings, between the Elephants [[notes: *Elephanten*—the Rooks/Castles]] and the Archers: and the White King’s Rider stands on a Black field—the Black King's on a White field—the White Queen’s on a White field—and the Black Queen's on a Black field.

Their move is from their own field into a third [[notes: In early chess manuals, the destination square was counted as the third, with the starting square being the first]], neither straight nor toward the corners [[notes: *nach den ecken*—meaning diagonally]], but rather from a white field into a black—and from that again into a white field. Yet they always move into the second or third, rearward or forward transverse line [[notes: *Zwerch-linie*—referring to the horizontal ranks or vertical files]], or those which are at the sides.
