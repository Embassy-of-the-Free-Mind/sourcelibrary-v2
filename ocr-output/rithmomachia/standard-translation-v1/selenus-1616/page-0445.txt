## The Sixth Chapter.

### How Black—when he has the advantage and plays against White without his Queen’s Rider [[notes: the Knight]]—should behave, and what White should also look out for.

**I**f White takes the King's Rider [[notes: the Knight]] as the advantage [[notes: meaning White gives the odds of a piece by starting without it]], and not the Archer’s Soldier [[notes: likely referring to the Bishop's pawn; the OCR "Schätzens" is likely a misspelling of "Schützen"]] with the move (as described in the previous 5th Chapter), but rather grants Black only the simple advantage of the first move: then from this arises the fifth type [of game], which Damianus [[notes: Pedro Damiano, a Portuguese apothecary and chess author whose 1512 book was the first to analyze the modern game]] recorded in the 8th and final chapter of his much-discussed booklet, and which we, based on him, shall recount in this and the following 7th Chapter, having thoroughly improved upon it.

[[margin: 1.]] Therefore, first (according to Damiano’s indication), Black moves his King’s Soldier [[notes: Pawn]] to thirty-six:
White moves his King’s Soldier to twenty-eight:
Black moves his King’s Rider [[notes: Knight]] to forty-three:
White (to defend it) moves his King’s Rider to twenty-two:
Black moves his King’s Archer [[notes: Bishop]] to thirty-eight:
White moves his King’s Archer to thirty:
Black moves his King’s Archer’s Soldier to forty-six:
[[margin: 2.]] White moves his King’s Archer to twenty-three:
Black (in order to break White’s game) moves the King’s Soldier to White’s King’s Soldier at thirty-seven:
White (to protect it further) moves the King’s Soldier to twenty-one:
[[margin: 3.]] Black takes White’s King’s Soldier with the King’s Soldier:
White takes the same with his King’s Soldier.
[[margin: 4.]] But if Black does not take White's Soldier (No. 3), but instead moves the King’s Rook’s Soldier [[notes: "Rochens-Soldaten" refers to the Rook's pawn; "Roch" being the old name for the Rook]] to forty-one (so that White cannot capture his King’s Rider with the King’s Archer from thirty-four):
then White advances the King’s Rider to Black’s King’s Soldier at nineteen:
Black moves the King’s Archer (in order to capture him and thus his Soldier...)
