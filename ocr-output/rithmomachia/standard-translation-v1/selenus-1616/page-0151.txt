This happens not openly, but only through the uncovering of a stone; namely, when that same stone—which stands between a King and another stone that could otherwise give Check if the line to the King were free and open—is moved forward, and thus uncovers the other stone so that it gives Check to the King, it is called a **Discovered Check.** [[notes: The author uses the term "Ab-Schach" (literally "off-check") for what modern players call a discovered check.]] If, however, the stone moved away reveals two stones against the King in such a manner (as an example can be found in the third book, chapter 4, number 16), it is called a double **Discovered Check.** Now, through this **Discovered Check**, one can often trap a good stone by moving the displaced stone onto it. For after the opponent’s King is forced to move out of the Check, one can, in the following move, obtain that good stone—which was trapped in the preceding move—without any resistance. If, however, Check is given simultaneously by the stone which was moved and the **Discovered Check** brought about by the other stone, it is called a **Double Check** or **Check and Discovered Check.**

A **Discovered Check** is given by all stones, even by the King himself (with the exception of the Queen), but not by all of them [[notes: This distinction refers to which piece is *moved* to reveal the check. The Queen is rarely the piece that moves "out of the way" to reveal another, as she is usually the most powerful piece being revealed.]]. Regarding the Queen, it happens through a soldier [[notes: "Soldaten" or soldiers refers to the pawns]] when that same soldier, on the nearest line, captures another soldier or stone ahead of him to the right or left hand, and thus clears the line for her against the opponent's King. Likewise, an Archer [[notes: "Schütze," the historical name for the Bishop]], a Rider [[notes: "Reuter," the Knight]], a Rook, or even her own King can make a free path for her to the opposing King. In the same manner, one also uncovers a Rook and an Archer to give Check from them to the King of the other side. From the Rider, however, because he can neither be obstructed nor covered, such a **Discovered Check** is not given [[notes: Since the Knight leaps over other pieces, no piece can stand on a line "behind" it to be revealed when the Knight moves]].
