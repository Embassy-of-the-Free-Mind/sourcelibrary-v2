### The Fourth Book / Chapter 8.

...but [moves] his Queen (in order to still make some use of her) to forty-six [[notes: The author uses a numbering system from 1 to 64 to identify the squares of the board, starting from the white side.]]: then White takes the Black King’s Archer [[notes: *Schütze* or Archer is the period term for the Bishop.]] with his King’s Rider’s [[notes: *Reuter* or Rider is the period term for the Knight.]] Soldier [[notes: *Soldat* or Soldier is the period term for the Pawn.]] and moves him to the Black King’s Rider’s Soldier’s [square]: Black offers check with the Queen from twenty-eight and moves her to the White King’s Archer and the White King’s Archer’s Soldier: White covers the King with the same at twelve: Black (in order to take the White King’s Archer) robs [captures] his Soldier: White (if he does not wish to rescue the same with the King’s Rider from twenty-two—in which case he would trap the Black Queen—but rather with his Queen) moves her Soldier to twenty-one: Black offers check with the King’s Archer from thirty-one: White covers the King with the King’s Rider at thirteen, thus organizing his game well, and finally Black (who lacks an Archer and a Rider) must yield and clear the field [[notes: A military metaphor for resigning or losing the position]].
