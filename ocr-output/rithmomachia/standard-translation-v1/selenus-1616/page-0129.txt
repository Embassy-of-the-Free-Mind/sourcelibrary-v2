### 10. Chapter: On the Game of Chess. 99

...then provide him with warm **medicine**; if it comes from heat / then provide him with cool **medicine** [[notes: This refers to the theory of the Four Humors, where health was maintained by balancing hot, cold, moist, and dry qualities.]]: or whether it comes / from sorrow / from fright / or from joy. It often happens / that a man / who sickens from sadness / and then recovers / is better off than one / who falls ill from sudden joy: for they are often found / to be suddenly dead / or else their limbs become **paralyzed** [[notes: *vergichtig*, meaning stricken with palsy or gout-like stiffness]] and useless. Now it is to be known / and also noted / that **joy** / is a wide pouring out of the spirit / into a state that is fitting / and pleasant: a wise man / is never joyful in a way / that exceeds the strength / of the conscience / and is born from practice / and is neither suppressed nor destroyed / only when strength fails / for what fortune has given / does not concern him: for **Love** and **joy** / may do the same / as a strike / of heaven’s lightning / or thunder. 

Secondly / they [[notes: the physicians]] should be chaste of body / and grave in their manners / coming with honored and orderly words / and they shall comfort the sick / that they may soon become healthy. Therefore they are placed / on the **chessboard** [[notes: *Schachzabel*]] / before the **Queen** / because women have many **sicknesses** [[notes: *Sichtumbs*]] and special **ailments** [[notes: *gebresten*]] / for which they [[the physicians]] should appear / with manners / with discipline / and with honor / before all people. 

Thirdly / that which the **Apothecaries** / among the prominent people / should have / is this / that they prepare with great diligence / whatever belongs to the **medicine** / according to the **Doctor’s** word and art / so that they do not / put one herb in place of another / and that they do not falsify it / and that they do not put forward / old and rotten roots / for new, fresh ones / and that they do not / give poisonous things / to unlearned doctors / and simple people / and that they do not thereby give / or bring anyone / a cause / for death. 

Fourthly / the **Surgeons** [[notes: *WundArtzt*, literally "Wound-Doctor"]] / should be compassionate / toward the aforementioned people / and should not be inclined or too hasty / to cut / or to burn [[notes: Cauterizing wounds with heat was a common but feared practice.]] / wounds or ulcers / lest they lose their **reputation** [[notes: *laumden*]] / and be called butchers / rather than healers of wounds: All **Doctors** should also / strive / to do what they do with good manners / and that they / first / make the sick healthy in spirit / and help the suffering of the soul / before the suffering of the body / so that they do not have / disordered words / promises / sorrow / or joy.
