### The First Book / Chapter 2:

written alongside his two predecessors: So, that **Xerxes** (others say *Xerxes Philometor* [[notes: In the medieval tradition of Jacobus de Cessolis, "Philometor" is the name of the philosopher who supposedly invented chess to teach a king morality]]) lived around the year 3635 after the creation of the world and is said to have moved King **Evil-Merodach** [[notes: Also known as Amel-Marduk, a historical King of Babylon (r. 562–560 BCE)]]—a hideous tyrant—to goodness with this game, among other circumstances. **Lucas Wielius** [[notes: An early 17th-century writer on games]] further confirms this in *Tract: de Ludo Scacch: cap: 2.* with the word **Schach** (Chess), which is said to be **Chaldean** [[notes: Referring to the Semitic language used in ancient Babylonia]]. Likewise, he claims that the chess table was made square, modeled after the **City of Babylon**. 

But one might like to be informed: who actually knew this *Xerxem Philomatorem* in all of **Antiquity**, that he should be sought out again after the passing of a thousand or more years? One might also still doubt whether **Evil-Merodach** was as wicked a tyrant as the Jews depict him. **St. Jerome** [[notes: Hieronymus, the 4th-century scholar who translated the Bible into Latin]], in the 4th Chapter of the 14th Book on the Prophet **Isaiah**, considers it a fable and a Talmudic tale. Similarly, **Philipp Melanchthon** [[notes: A key figure in the Protestant Reformation and a noted scholar]], in *lib: 1. Chronic:*, recognizes him as a god-fearing and pious ruler, proving this from the final chapter of the **Second Book of Kings** [[notes: Referring to the biblical account where Evil-Merodach releases the Judean king Jehoiachin from prison]]. 

Furthermore, if the word **Schach** is **Chaldean**, one asks how it came to be—since they themselves admit this game was brought from the **Chaldeans** to the Greeks, from them to the Romans, and so on to us—that the little word **Schach** remained silent and withheld among the aforementioned peoples for such a long time? The latter does not rhyme well with our game: that one would want to stage public field battles in cities. Therefore, let everyone think of this what they will.

Much less, however, is **Vida** [[notes: Marco Girolamo Vida (1485–1566), an Italian bishop and poet famous for his poem *Scacchia Ludus*, which reimagined the game's origin as a contest between gods]] to be believed when he says an Italian woman, **Scacheides**, supposedly invented it. As he opines in his *Scacchia* with the following verses—perhaps out of affection for his fatherland, or else poetic license:

> » *Vos primæ studia hæc, Italis monstrastis in oris*
> » *Scacchides egregiæ, monimentum insigne sororis.*
> » ->That is:<-
> » You (gods) first made this pastime known
> » Within the Italian land alone,
> » Which received its very name
> » From **Scacheide**, your sister of pure fame.

Finally, **Vincentius Castellanus** in *lib: de reb: à Palam: invent:* and **Thomas Actius** in *tractat: de Lud: Scach:* as well as **Jacob Micyllus** [[notes: A German Renaissance humanist and poet]] hold...
