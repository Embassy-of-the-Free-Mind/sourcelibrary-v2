# The First Book. Chapter 10.

[[margin: 10. cap:]]

> ,, ...one should do good for good / and give gift for gift / no more should one pay / than what one is due. Thirdly / the workers should be faithful and trustworthy.
> 
> The **fifth pawn** / on the **chessboard** [[notes: "SchachZabel" is an archaic German term for a chessboard, from the Latin *tabula*]] / shall stand / before the **Queen** / in such a form and shape / that he sits / on a **master’s stool** / and has in his right hand / a **book** / in the left hand a **box** [[notes: *püchsen*, likely a pyxis or apothecary jar]] / with **electuaries** [[notes: A medicinal paste or "lickable" medicine made by mixing powders with honey or syrup.]] / and there shall be tucked / under his belt a **surgical probe** [[notes: *wundeisen*, literally "wound-iron," used by surgeons]]. By the book / are signified / all **learned physicians** / and all teachers of the **Liberal Arts** [[notes: The traditional medieval curriculum consisting of the Trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and the Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy).]]. A true physician / must be able / to speak right and good Latin / that is / **Grammar** : and what **Logic** concludes : what **Rhetoric** has in pretty / ornate / and earnest words : the points and figures of **Geometry** [[notes: The text uses *geomantrey*, but in the context of the Liberal Arts, it refers to the study of shapes and measurements.]] : the numbers of **Arithmetic** : the harmony of **Music** : and **Astronomy**, that is / the course of the **stars and seven planets** : all this and more / he needs to know well / when one should give or take a potion / or perform **bloodletting** [[notes: *lassen zu der Aderen*, a common medieval medical practice of withdrawing blood to balance the "humors."]] : By the box / are meant and signified / **Apothecaries** / ointment-makers / and what belongs thereto : By the surgical probe / are signified / **surgeons** / and "wound-cutters" and such people / who all together should be able / to make all the sick / healthy / according to the teaching / of the **heathen masters** / **Galen**, **Origen** [[notes: Likely referring to Oribasius, a Greek medical compiler, or perhaps Rhazes, as Origen was a theologian.]], **Hippocrates**, and **Avicenna** : and when the life of the sick / stands and is in the physician's hand / and he does not know the art / fully and completely / then he is more a **murderer** / than a physician / and therefore / when many physicians come / to a sick person / they should not be **disputers** / and finders of strange, contradictory matters : So that they seek / to appear more skillful through outward words / than they care / how they might make the sick person healthy / which is necessary above all things : for if they wish / over the sick person / to fight and argue / then before a question is resolved / the patient is dead. The **Jurists** [[notes: Lawyers or legal scholars.]] should do the opposite / for they do not consider / what is inside the man / but what is fitting according to outward customs / that is what they consider. And for that reason they should not be quick / with their judgment. The physicians should seek with certainty / what is good for the sick : if the wound is a puncture / and small [[notes: *scheubleit*]] / then he should bind it accordingly : if it is a blow and long / then he should bind it accordingly. He should also know / from whence / the sickness came. Is it from cold /
