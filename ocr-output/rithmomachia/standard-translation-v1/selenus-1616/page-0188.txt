### The Second Book / Chapter 5:

...[the] **Rook's Soldier** [[notes: *Rochens-Soldaten*; the Rook's Pawn]], who stands on **twenty-five**, has been captured) still has remaining: he has committed himself to **checkmate** [[notes: *ermatten*; literally "to exhaust," the standard term for checkmating in this period]] and defeat the **Black King** with his **Queen's Knight's Soldier** [[notes: *Königin-Reuters-Soldaten*; the Queen's Knight's Pawn]], [moving] from **fifty-five** to **sixty-four** or **sixty-two**: [he must] place and record it.

White must therefore (in order to put this into effect) first drive and compel the **Black King** so that he must retreat back to the same line on which he stood at the beginning of the game, next to his principal pieces: subsequently, on the other **horizontal** [[notes: *Zwerch-linie*; referring to the rank or horizontal row]] or **Soldiers' line**, he must block the passage with one of his **Rooks** so that he [the King] may not come back out into the open field; and—since it cannot be otherwise if the **Black King** is to be driven more narrowly at the start—move the same to **forty-nine**. 

Now, if **Black** moves his **King** to **fifty-nine**: then **White** (so that the **Black King** cannot come to the **White Rook** on **fifty-eight**, and specifically for the shortening of the game, being driven further forward) moves the **Knight** [[notes: *Reuter*; literally "Rider," the standard term for the Knight]] to **fifty-two**: but not to **forty-three** (because otherwise the **King** would be boxed in) nor to **forty-one** (because he would then have to make two moves—namely this one to 41, and the other to fifty-eight—in excess).
