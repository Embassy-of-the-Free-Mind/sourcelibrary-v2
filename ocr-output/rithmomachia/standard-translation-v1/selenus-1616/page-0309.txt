# On the Game of Chess.

[[margin: Chapter 3:]] [the **White** player moves] the King to **thirteen** [[notes: Square 13 is d2]] (in order to defend him). The **Black** player (to take the same by force) moves the King’s Bishop [[notes: "Schütze" literally means Archer, the standard term for the Bishop in this period]] to **thirty-five** [[notes: Square 35 is f5]]. The **White** player (to prevent this attack) moves the King’s Rook to **six** [[notes: Square 6 is c1]]. The **Black** player (to capture the White Queen if the King’s Rook were to move against her to **fifty-seven** [[notes: Square 57 is h8]]) moves the King’s Knight to **fifty-three** [[notes: Square 53 is d7]]. The **White** player moves the King’s Bishop’s Soldier [[notes: "Schützen-Soldat" refers to the pawn in front of the Bishop, here the f-pawn]] against the Black Queen to **nineteen** [[notes: Square 19 is d3]]. The **Black** player moves her to **fifty-two** [[notes: Square 52 is e7]]. The **White** player moves the King’s Bishop to **forty-one** [[notes: Square 41 is h6]]. The **Black** player moves the King’s Rook to **fifty-eight** [[notes: Square 58 is g8]]. The **White** player takes the Black King’s Bishop with his King’s Bishop. The **Black** player (if he does not wish to give check with the Queen from **thirty-four** [[notes: Square 34 is g5]]; in which case the **White** player would place the King on **twelve** [[notes: Square 12 is e2]], or cover him with his Bishop's Soldier on **twenty-seven** [[notes: Square 27 is f4]], and then move him onto it) takes him with the Rook. The **White** player: moves the Queen to **forty-one** [[notes: Square 41 is h6]] and thus saves her quite easily.

[[margin: 10.]] If, however, the **Black** player does not cover the King with the Soldier on **37** [[notes: Square 37 is d5]] (as mentioned in no. 9), but instead moves him to **fifty-two** [[notes: Square 52 is e7]] (to further protect his Knight): then the **White** player gives check with the King’s Knight from **thirty-seven** [[notes: Square 37 is d5]]. The **Black** player takes him with his King’s Knight. The **White** player offers check with the Queen from **thirty-six** [[notes: Square 36 is e5]], obtains the aforementioned Black Knight with the King’s Bishop, moves him against the Black Queen, and at the same time defends the King’s Rook (if the Black King has moved to **sixty-one** [[notes: Square 61 is d8]]). But if the King has moved to **fifty-one** [[notes: Square 51 is f7]], he wins the Black Queen with this Bishop; for the **Black** player must take him with her (to prevent **Checkmate**).

[[margin: 11.]] If, however, the **Black** player does not take the White Soldier with the Queen (no. 8), but instead moves her to **forty-five** [[notes: Square 45 is d6]]: then the **White** player offers check with the King’s Bishop from **thirty** [[notes: Square 30 is c4]]. Now, if the **Black** player covers the King with his Knight on **thirty-seven** [[notes: Square 37 is d5]]: then the White Queen is freed. But if he moves the King out of check: then the **White** player moves the King’s Bishop against the Black King’s Knight at **thirty-four** [[notes: Square 34 is g5]] and in this way saves his Queen.
