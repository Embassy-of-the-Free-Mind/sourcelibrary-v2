# Of the Game of Chess.

[[margin: Chapter 11:]] ...the host's [[notes: *Hauffen*, literally a "heap" or "host," refers here to the collective set of pieces]] order (as mentioned) having been well-formed in one’s mind, the position of the other host can also be known without effort. For if one understands the first line [[notes: *Linie*, here referring to a "rank" or row of squares]] and the square of the piece—whether it be black or white—he can also know that the third square has the same color as the first: and the fourth like the second. For instance, if the first square of the **Rook** [[notes: *Rochen*, from the Persian *Rukh*]] is white, then the third must also be white; if the second is black, then the fourth is also black. Thus, he has a reliable guide and calculation: that those squares which are white for the White player are black for the Black; while those that are black for the White are white for the Black.

A chess player [[notes: *Schach-Zieher*, literally "chess-mover"]] must now know these aforementioned paths and moves very well—especially one who wishes to play from memory while traveling, riding, or walking—and must take care to keep in his memory [[notes: *gedechtnus*]] which side or which square he hits, and which piece he can take or has taken (which is, however, rather difficult to put into practice).

The second way to imagine the chessboard is this: that one marks each square with a number, counting forward until all squares from the first to the last on the table are recorded, concluding with the sixty-fourth number; and (so that one may grasp it more easily) one does not distinguish the squares by the white and black colors. Therefore, the **White King’s Rook’s** square is marked one; his **Knight’s** [[notes: *Reuter*, literally "Rider"]] square, two; his **Bishop’s** [[notes: *Schütze*, literally "Archer" or "Shooter"]], three; his own [the **King’s**], four; the **Queen’s**, five; her **Bishop’s**, six; her **Knight’s**, seven; her **Rook’s**, eight. Returning upon the following horizontal line [[notes: *Zwerch-linie*, what we now call a "rank"]] upwards, the first square [above the last] is marked nine; the second, ten; and in this manner, the remaining squares and lines are recorded until the end of the last line. 

If a table has been described in this way with its lines and numbers, then one does not need to keep in memory the names of the pieces or soldiers [[notes: *Soldaten*, referring to the Pawns]] after which the squares must be named, nor the number of those squares and their color, but only pay attention to the number marked in each square. For example: White moves his **King’s Soldier** [[notes: Pawn]] to the fourth horizontal line where twenty-eight is marked. Then Black plays his **King’s Soldier** to the fifth horizontal line into the square where thirty-six is found, and so forth.
