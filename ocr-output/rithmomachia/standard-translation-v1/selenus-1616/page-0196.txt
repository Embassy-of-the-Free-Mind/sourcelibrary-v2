# The Second Book / 5th Chapter:

...Checkmate can be given. Now, if it is the turn of the player who wishes to promote the **Pawn** [[notes: *Soldat*; literally "Soldier"]] into a **Queen**, then the other player must observe this rule: that if his opponent (in order to protect this **Pawn**) moves around with his **King** and thus steps out somewhat from behind the **Pawn**, he should place himself directly opposite him on the same line, with one square between them [[notes: This describes the fundamental chess concept of "Opposition," where Kings face each other to block progress]]. For then the opponent can neither make a **Queen** out of the **Pawn** nor give **Checkmate**; especially if he does not place his **King** behind the **Pawn**, he will lose it. If, however, he only traps the other’s **King** [[notes: Stalemate]], he wins half the stake according to the **Spanish rule** [[notes: *Spanier ordnung*; in certain historical regional rules, a stalemate was not a draw but a "half-win" or resulted in a specific penalty for the player who could not move]].

->Provided that the **White** player has only the **King** (it being understood that his<-
remaining pieces can no longer be moved) and his **Bishop** [[notes: *Segesner*; an archaic term for the Bishop, likely referring to a "scythe-bearer," though later the text uses *Schütze* or "Archer"]], the **Queen’s Rook’s Pawn**, and its protector (who cannot reach the square of the **Black Rook** into which his companion, the **Pawn**, is finally to be moved and thus take on the properties of a **Queen**) [[notes: This describes the "wrong-colored Bishop" endgame, where a Bishop cannot control the promotion square of its Rook's Pawn]]: then such a game is regarded as a **robbed and closed** one [[notes: *geraubtes und geschloßnes*; a stalemate or draw]], in which nothing is won, but rather all is cancelled out. Therefore, everyone must strive (in order to lie in wait for the advantage and win it from the **White Pawn** with his own **King**) to reach the place from which he could defend himself, namely, the **Rook’s**...

...into which the aforementioned **Bishop** [[notes: *Schütze*; literally "Archer"]] cannot reach, nor drive him out, as well as the **Rook’s** or the **Knight’s Pawn’s** [[notes: *Reuters-Soldat*; the Pawn on the Knight's file]] square.
