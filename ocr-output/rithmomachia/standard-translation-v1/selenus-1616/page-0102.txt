2. An **Archer** [[notes: *Schütze*, the modern Bishop]] strikes his enemy / from afar / much better / and more subtly / than a **Rider** [[notes: *Reuter*, the modern Knight]].

3. He protects / his **King** / from all sorts of **Checks** [[notes: *Schachen*]] / which may be offered to him / much more than a **Rider**.

4. When he covers his **King** in such a manner / and places himself between the same / and the opponent's **Piece** [[notes: *Stein*, literally "stone"]] / which offers him check / he has a more favorable opportunity / than the **Rider** / even if the **Rider** likewise covers the **King** / to take away the **Piece** / which wishes to finish off his **King**. [[notes: This refers to the Bishop's ability to "interpose" or block a check while simultaneously threatening the attacking piece along the same diagonal.]]

5. He can / in turn cover / and hold captive / all / both the **Principal** [[notes: *Heupt-Steine*, the major pieces like the Queen or Rook]] as well as the **Common Pieces** / of his enemy / which is impossible for the **Rider**.

6. He protects / his **Soldiers** [[notes: *Soldaten*, the modern Pawns]] / much better / and is also defended by them: an example of which / is in Chapter 14 / of the Second Book.
