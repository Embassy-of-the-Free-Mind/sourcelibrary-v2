### # # Vom Schach-Spiel.
### # # On the Game of Chess.

[[margin: 4. cap:]] [The Black moves] his **King’s Bishop** [[notes: *K. Schützen* literally translates to "King’s Archer," the standard 17th-century German term for the Bishop.]] and moves it onto the **White King’s Rook’s** [square]:
The White moves the same [the King] to **two**: (for, if he were to set him on a **white field**, then the Black would offer **discovered check** [[notes: *Ab-Schach* refers to a discovered check, where moving one piece reveals an attack from another.]] from his **Queen** by way of her **Bishop**, and move the same onto him). The Black offers **discovered check** from the **Queen** with her **Bishop** at **twenty-one**: The White sets the **King** on **eleven**: The Black offers **check** with the **Queen** from **twelve**: The White must place the **King** on **eighteen**: The Black (so that the **King’s Bishop** may subsequently be moved against the **White Queen** at **forty-five**)
[[margin: 12.]] moves the **Queen** to **forty-four**: * The White takes the **Black King’s Bishop** with his **King’s Bishop’s Pawn** [[notes: *K. Schützens-Soldat* or "King’s Archer’s Soldier."]]: The Black moves the **King’s Bishop** against the **White Queen** at **forty-five** and captures the same by force.

[[margin: 13.]] If, however, the White (for the reasons mentioned above) does not take the **Black Bishop** (*no. 12*) but instead moves the **Queen** to **twenty-six** (in order to exchange her for the **Black Queen**) or wherever he pleases: he is nonetheless either **Mated** or has lost the **Queen**: For, if the Black (provided the Queen was moved to 26) offers **check** with the
[[margin: 14.]] **King’s Bishop** from **forty-five**: * He [White] moves the **King** to **seventeen**: The Black moves the **King’s Bishop** against his **Queen** at **thirty-five** and thereby brings the same into his power.

[[margin: 15.]] But if the White plays the **King** not to **17** (*no. 14*) but to **twenty-five**: the Black nonetheless moves the **King’s Bishop** against the **White Queen** at **thirty-five**.

->This preceding material is treated by *Damianus* [[notes: Pedro Damiano (1480–1544) was a Portuguese chess player who wrote one of the earliest manuals on the game. The author here is criticizing Damiano’s lack of clarity.]] without any<-
->order, and so obscurely that even those who understand this game<-
->sufficiently can hardly (to say nothing of beginners)<-
->grasp or comprehend his meaning.<-

[[margin: 16.]] If then, he [Damiano] writes further, * the White is compelled by the Black (*no. 15 inf.*) to move the **Queen** to **nineteen**: then the Black plays the **King’s Rook** [[notes: *K. Rochen* refers to the Rook or Castle.]] against her at
[[margin: 17.]] **fifty-nine**: * The White moves the same [the Queen] to another place (so as not to lose her):
[[margin: 18.]] * The Black gives **check** with the **Queen** at **thirty-six**, and soon thereafter **Check-Mate**.
