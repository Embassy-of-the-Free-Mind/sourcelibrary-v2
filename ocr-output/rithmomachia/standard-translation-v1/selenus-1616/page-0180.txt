Now, even though / these two **soldiers** [[notes: *Soldaten*; the standard 17th-century term for pawns]] / who stand in one line / toward the enemy’s troops / can at times / set upon them like two stones / nevertheless they usually do / no more damage / than just a single one / and often hinder / their own companions / and in this disorder / cannot inflict such damage upon the opponent / (unless it happens perhaps without danger / and through his negligence) as if they / found themselves in their proper order / and right place / but rather / are regarded and held / no differently than if they / were removed from their place / and the **chessboard** [[notes: *Schach-Tabel*]] / and the **battle order** [[notes: *Schlacht-Ordnung*]] / were changed and broken.

Furthermore, the **main pieces** [[notes: *Haupt-Steine*; refers to the "officer" pieces like Rooks, Bishops, and Knights as opposed to pawns]], which have been granted freedom in the game / and have **safe conduct** [[notes: *geleyde*; a historical rule in some variations where certain pieces were protected from capture under specific conditions]] / so that they / may be moved here and there without fear / as it is useful to the players / and one need not fear / that they will be hindered or stolen / can in no way / be taken by the opponent. With the **soldiers** however / who receive **safe conduct** / and cannot be taken / it is a different matter / than with the **main pieces**. For when these **soldiers** / are made into a **queen** [[notes: *Königin*; the process of pawn promotion]] / they can (unless / specifically excepted and stipulated) / quite easily be taken. Considering / that as soon as they / become a **queen**, they are no longer **soldiers** / and one cannot say / that a **soldier** / but rather a **queen** / has been taken away. Therefore, one must / in this case / also explain oneself correctly / so that no one is disadvantaged [[notes: The author is warning that if you have a "house rule" where pawns cannot be taken, that protection usually ends the moment the pawn promotes to a Queen]].

Finally / the players are sometimes accustomed to agree / that none among them / shall take more than three pieces / out of the **sixteen** [[notes: *Sechszzehen*; the total number of pieces each side starts with]] / throughout the entire game.
