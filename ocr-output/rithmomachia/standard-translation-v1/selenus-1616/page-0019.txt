Where there is need to encounter the enemy ranks
With an intrepid and constant heart,
Plotting a thousand deceits, and performing just as many
To the shame of the hostile banners;
In how many ways and how valorously
One can win with reason and art,
Knights and Pawns in a feigned war [[notes: "Finta guerra" (feigned war) was a standard Renaissance metaphor for chess, emphasizing its role as a bloodless substitute for military strategy.]]—
Here it is taught to everyone; now pay attention,
And you will say that it is the work of him, within these pages,
Whose famous name fills the earth.

### OR RATHER.

Chess [[notes: The Italian *Scacchi* sounds like *scacci* (you drive away), allowing for the pun in the first line.]], as if you drive away every care:
A game of wit alone, not of luck,
A singular and proportionate image
Of the human condition and the worldly Empire.
Man, who is born proud among the animals,
Is sometimes sent to his grave by a worm;
Often a bold Pawn, or a courageous Bishop [[notes: The *Alfiero* (standardbearer) is the Italian name for the Bishop piece.]],
Manages to take the scepter from the King.
Voracious time and malignant novelty
Had already prescribed for you a narrow end,
And had brought cards and dice in your place.
Today, merciful Heaven and a benign Moon [[notes: A pun on the author's pseudonym, *Selenus*, derived from the Greek *Selene* (the Moon).]],
Restore to its former glory, in a beautiful town [[notes: Likely referring to Wolfenbüttel, the Duke's residence and the site of his famous library.]],
The work and labor of a New Augustus [[notes: A direct reference to the author, Duke Augustus the Younger.]].

### MACARONIC VERSE. [[notes: Macaronic verse is a form of humorous poetry that mixes vernacular words (Italian) with Latin grammar and endings.]]
