Yet the Jurists [[notes: Legal scholars and experts in Roman and Canon law.]] grant / that one may well stake / one gold guilder [[notes: The *Goldgulden* was a standard gold coin used throughout the Holy Roman Empire; here it represents a modest, acceptable sum for a gentleman's wager.]] / but nothing beyond that / (as may be seen from *Guimier*, in that place, and *Claro*, §. Alearum*, is set aside) / and according to the same / yet with good modesty / behave oneself.

Let this now / be enough reported of the qualities [of the game].
The manifold benefits / which likewise / spring from this / may be noted as follows: namely:

That in this game / (1.) the intellect is exercised / and to other higher / and weightier business / to proceed wisely therein / is immediately directed: Not / that this exercise of the mind / is the Chess-player's / chief purpose or goal / (as *Steph: Costa*, in the said treatise: *de Ludo*, indeed opines) but / that this / as well as the following / (which indeed / must be taken into account) / are merely the uses and fruits / which the Game of Chess / alongside the delight of the mind / as the chief purpose / brings with it / and which an experienced Chess-player / with special pleasure / can harvest and enjoy.

That also (2) the memory / be served: insofar as it / strengthens the same / and *Jacob: Publicius Florentinus*, in his treatise: *de Arte Memoriæ & Imaginibus*, book 2. at the end / testifies / that the Chess-board / with its squares and images / provides and presents / very excellent signs and figures / for the Art of Memory. [[notes: Jacobus Publicius was an influential 15th-century author on mnemonics; he suggested using the grid of the chessboard as a mental "storage system" for memorizing information.]]
