But if he were to pick up his opponent’s piece, and afterwards realize that he could arrange his moves in another way, more effectively and usefully, and therefore set that piece back down again: that would not be **proper play** [[notes: *Spiel-mässig*; meaning "according to the rules of the game"]] at all. And in truth, whoever employs such play at any time shall be liable to lose at least half of his wagered money.

Furthermore, one should pay very close attention to the opponent when he removes or captures a piece: for it happens at times that he—perhaps out of error, or else in a deceitful manner when he finds his opponent somewhat careless and inattentive—removes his own piece which is hindering him, in order to help his game and gain an advantageous move. In such a case, that move is held to be illegal, and the other player, if he has noticed it and does not wish to accept it, is empowered to compel him to change the move and set his piece back in the place from which he took it. It happens, nevertheless, very rarely that one takes away one’s own pieces. But if it has happened, he should rightly excuse himself for the error and argue that it was not undertaken out of fraud or malice; and he should set this piece of his, which he took by oversight, back in its previous place (as it does not run contrary to the manner and nature of this game) before the other player moves a piece, and then arrange his moves in another fashion. Otherwise, if he meanwhile made another move and let this error pass in silence, it would be held very much against him, indeed as a **dishonorable act** [[notes: *unerbar stücke*; a breach of the "gentlemanly" conduct expected in high-stakes chess]], and be reproached.

Likewise, if one removes his opponent's piece with a piece by means of which he does not have the power to do so [[notes: This refers to making an illegal move, such as capturing a piece with a Bishop that is not on the same diagonal]]: one should then observe whether it be his own or the enemy's piece. If it is his own [the piece he moved illegally], he must play and move that same piece, provided he cannot take the illegally captured piece with another of his pieces. But if it is the other's [the piece he moved was the opponent's], he is excused and, in that case, bound to make another, legal move.
