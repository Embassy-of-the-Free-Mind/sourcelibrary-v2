### The First Book. Chapter 10.

(a further report follows) One places that **Soldier** [[notes: The Pawn.]] / by which / the other / in this case / is taken / into the square / from which the latter / in the move immediately preceding / had advanced / and in which the one / who should have taken him / and now moved alongside him / had stood / and thus removes / this Soldier / from the **Table** [[notes: *Tabel*, the chessboard.]]. Which is now to be noted / whenever one / contrary to the custom of war / (from which this Game / takes its first origin) moves / through a **Pass** / that is occupied and held by the Enemy: for such / must happen / either through his consent / (because he has the power / to forbid this freedom / as well as to grant and allow it) or else with danger / and by good fortune / (because one is not certain / whether one will be struck down / or left there). But where this custom is not held / nor otherwise / agreed to by the Opponent / one is free / to pass by / or to take [[notes: This describes the "en passant" rule, where a pawn moving two squares can be captured by an adjacent enemy pawn as if it had only moved one square. The author justifies this through the military logic of passing through an enemy-controlled "pass."]].

Furthermore / these Soldiers / cannot turn back / until they / come into their Enemy's / furthest squares [[notes: The eighth rank.]], and there / take upon themselves / the **Queen's** virtues and movement. For those / who face the Enemy / must imagine / that flight / is a great vice / and very harmful / to those / who hold the line / behind them: For when the front ranks give way and flee / those following / are also easily led astray: (In this regard, see Chapter 20 of Deuteronomy, verse 8, at the end [[notes: Deuteronomy 20:8 describes how the fearful and faint-hearted are sent home from the army so they do not discourage their fellows.]]) Thus one can also / from the fact / that the Soldiers / move two steps at the beginning / but thereafter / only one step / and cannot go backward: observe / the nature and quality of the **faint-hearted** / who at the beginning / when danger is least perceived / show themselves heated: but afterward are afraid / and hold back: whereas on the contrary / **brave people** / when it comes to the "hand-work" [[notes: *Hand-Arbeit*, here meaning close-quarters combat.]], are indeed heated and joyful enough / but beforehand / are still / and quiet: or else / they are moved in this way / so that right at the beginning / they show their brave and eager spirit for the Battle / as if they could hardly / wait for the time / when the alarm shall begin.
