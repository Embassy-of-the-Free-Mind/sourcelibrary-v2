# Of the Game of Chess.

[[margin: 8th cap:]]

### The Eighth Chapter.
### In what manner White should observe his moves when he grants Black the advantage and the latter plays without the King’s Rider [[notes: "Reuter" (Rider) is the early modern German term for the Knight.]].

But if White does not take the Black Queen (as described in the previous three chapters) but instead takes the Black King’s Rider: and gives Black, in return, the advantage [[notes: In handicap chess, "giving the advantage" usually refers to allowing the opponent to move first or removing one's own piece to balance skill levels; here, it refers to the specific setup of the "Sixth Kind" of game.]]: then the Sixth Kind shall arise in this manner.

[[margin: 1. 2. sig: [[notes: "sig" likely refers to "significatio" or "signum," marking specific move sequences or variations.]]]]
Black moves the King’s Soldier [[notes: "Soldat" (Soldier) is the period term for a Pawn.]] to thirty-six: White moves his King’s Soldier to twenty-eight: * Black (in order to block White’s intention) moves the King’s Archer’s [[notes: "Schütze" (Archer) refers to the Bishop.]] Soldier to forty-six: White (to also break Black’s plans) moves the King’s Soldier to the Black King’s Soldier at twenty-nine: Black takes it with the same: White takes the same (since it can be done without fear of any danger) with the Queen: Black moves against her the King’s Archer’s Soldier (so that their Rider [[notes: Knight]] may come out) to thirty-eight: White offers check with the Queen from thirty-six: Black covers the King either with the Queen (in which case the Queens are captured on both sides) or else with his Archer at fifty-two: White takes (in this latter case) the Black King’s Rider’s Soldier with the Queen, moves the same to the Black King’s Rook, thereby obtains that Soldier, and disrupts Black’s formation.
