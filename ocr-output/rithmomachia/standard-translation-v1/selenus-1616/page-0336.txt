# The Third Book / 5. cap:

...his Rook [[note: "Rochen" refers to the Rook]]: the **White** [player] moves the same to three [[note: Square c1]]: the **Black** [player] moves the King’s Archer [[note: The "Schütze" or Archer is the Bishop]] to the **White** Queen, to twenty-six [[note: Square b4]]: the **White** [player] moves the King’s Rook to the **Black** Queen, to eleven [[note: Square c2]], and protects his Soldiers [[note: "Soldaten" refers to Pawns]]: the **Black** [player] offers **Check** with the Queen from two [[note: Square b1]]: the **White** [player] must cover the King with his Rook at three [[note: Square c1]]: the **Black** [player] takes the same with the Queen and offers **Check**: the **White** [player] takes the same with the King: the **Black** [player] takes the **White** Queen with his King’s Archer: He has thus a Rook and Soldiers as an advantage, and can—when he brings both his Rooks and the King’s Rider [[note: The "Reuter" or Rider is the Knight]] into the field—easily maintain the victory against the **White** player.
