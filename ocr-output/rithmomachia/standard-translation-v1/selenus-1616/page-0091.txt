### 6. cap: Vom Schach-Spiel.
### [[gloss: Chapter 6: On the Game of Chess.]]

his wedded consort / who, on account of her Lord's rays / shines as if reflecting them / and in important matters / often gives / with deliberation / her advisable consideration.

Their positions / they have / at the King’s sides: the white [Queen] / on the left: in the white [square]: the Black [Queen] / on the right side / in the black field: and that / for the following reasons: for 1. the Chessboard is so placed / so that the outermost white field / is on the right hand / of both players: 2. so that the Queens / like the Kings / and other pieces / stand opposite one another.

However, it is no hindrance / that the Black Queen / has taken a higher position / than her King: For 1. although the right hand / is commonly held to be higher / such can only / take place among persons of equal / but not unequal rank: 2. The Ancients had the custom / to mark that which was good and laudable / with chalk / or white lines: in contrast / that which was shameful and ill / with coals / or black strokes: So that they now / might give it to be understood / that it would be very shameful / to honor women higher / than men / they wished to indicate such / with the black sign: [[notes: This passage reflects 17th-century social hierarchies, using the "black" square as a symbolic warning against placing a wife’s status above her husband’s.]] 3. Among the Ancients / a different order / than is held in our times / was used and customary / in sitting. For / (as *Actius* [[notes: Likely referring to a classical source or a contemporary commentator cited in early chess literature like that of Augustus the Younger (Gustavus Selenus).]] asserts) the one / to whom one wished to show honor / was placed on the left / and not / on the right hand. Just as King **Cyrus**, (according to the indication of **Xenophon, lib: 8. Cyropædia**) used to have his dearest and most pleasant guests / sit on his left side / at the Royal table: and thereby / wished to give it to be understood / that this side / because it was closest / to the heart / and therefore subject to many dangers / could in such a way / be kept so much more safely. It is therefore / in the demonstrations of honor / to take heed throughout of the land's custom: which then / the inventor of this game / perhaps wished to indicate / and give to be understood herewith. Further information / whether the right / or left position / was held in higher honor / among the Ancients / is to be found / in **Lipsius, Elect: lib: 2. c. 2. pag: 452. ad 457.** [[notes: Justus Lipsius (1547–1606) was a famous Flemish philologist and humanist; his *Electa* (Choices/Select Works) dealt with ancient customs.]]

Finally / the aforementioned *Actius* sets; the following reason / in the **Index**, of his treatise on the **Chess-Game / lit: R. Undè factum fit, ut uxor Regis nigri, stet ad dextram; uxor albi, ad sinistram, posset etiam considerari hæc ratio, penitùs non ingrata. Nam dextra pars, cùm sit sinistrâ calidior, (Macrob: lib; 7. Saturn: au-** [[notes: The Latin translates to: "Whence it happened that the wife of the black King stands at the right; the wife of the white, at the left, this reason might also be considered, which is not at all unpleasing. For the right part, since it is warmer than the left (Macrobius, Book 7 of the Saturnalia)..." This refers to ancient medical theories that the right side of the body possessed more vital heat.]]
