# -> Of the Game of Chess. <-

[[margin: Chapter 8]]

...[as] can be found in No. 21, figure f.: where / the **Black King’s Archer’s Soldier** [[notes: This refers to a Pawn on the f-file]] / in the **White King’s Archer’s** / third square [[notes: Square f3 from White's perspective or f6 for Black]] / is protected by the **Black Queen’s Archer** / in the **White King’s** / second square.

He [the Archer] helps / (7.) better / than the **Rider** / his **Soldier** [[notes: *Soldat*, the common term for a Pawn]] / forward / to harm the enemy / or to / make him into a **Queen** [[notes: Referring to the Archer's long-range ability to "babysit" a pawn during its promotion march]].

He is able / (8.) with a **Rook** [[notes: *Rochen*, the traditional name for the Rook]] / to win / the game / against the enemy’s single **Rook** / which / is left alone with his **King** / (as can be seen from the sketch) / which the **Rider** surely fails to do. The enemy **King** / (9.) cannot / capture him [the Archer] alone / as he can the / **Rider** : For / when the **Black King** / encounters the **White Rider** / in his **Rook’s** square / and the same **King** / is moved into his **Archer’s** / third square / then it is impossible for the **Rider** / (as the sketch shows) / to escape. For when the **King** / is moved into his **Rider’s** / second square / he slays [[notes: *erleget*, meaning to capture or take]] him / or in the **Rook’s** / (provided he has not moved out of it) / or in the **Archer’s** / second / or the **Rider’s** / third square.
