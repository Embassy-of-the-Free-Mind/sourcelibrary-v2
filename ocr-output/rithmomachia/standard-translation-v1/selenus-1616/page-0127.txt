# On the Game of Chess.
## N ij

[[meta: catchword: what]]

[[margin: 10. cap:]]
...what one then does, one enjoys in friendly constancy. But what one does in [good] fortune, that may be attributed to flattering **hypocrisy**. The other friendship is based on a good that is **honorable**, and this friendship belongs to a friend who is **virtuous**. The second thing that the workers should have is **chastity**, so that the men who work for them should not desire or ask anything of them that is against their honor; [and] that no woman who comes to him is asked or desired for anything that is against her honor. The third is that they be **truthful**, so that their heart and purpose follow their words and deeds; for truth is a virtue, and whoever has it may speak without fear, in every place, with a free spirit.

The **fourth pawn** on the chessboard shall be placed before the **King**; he shall be shaped thus: He shall have in his right hand a **scale** and a **weight**, representing **money-changers** and **lenders**; and in the left, an **ell** [[notes: An "ell" was a traditional unit of measurement, often used for cloth, roughly the length of a man’s arm.]], representing **tailors** or **cloth-makers** and such people. And on his belt, a large purse or a bag with gold, representing the **Lords' tax collectors** and **receivers**, who should altogether flee **avarice**. Avarice is an immeasurable love for having, taking, or keeping property beyond what is necessary. This vice is harmful to all people, and most of all to those who are supposed to lead the community, such as Lords and their officials. It reigns and rules most of all among people, though it is foolish that a person takes so much provision for a short journey [[notes: A metaphor for life being a "short journey" toward death, making the hoarding of wealth unnecessary.]] when they must live but a short time, yet strive and set so much store by property. A greedy man is bad to himself, but the poor man is worst of all, for he always has some reason to refuse. Avarice is often a cause for falling into sin; avarice also destroys loyalty, piety, and other virtues. Secondly, these same people should guard themselves against many debts. **Poverty** is not a vice or an evil; but to owe and not to pay is a shame. If you are poor or rich, do not ask for a loan; for if you are rich, you do not need it, and if you are poor, consider that you can hardly repay it.
