### ->The First Book / Chapter 2:<-

» [considered] higher and more noble / esteemed and used. They are
» indeed / not very unlike / our own Chess-pieces / but yet in
» the following [ways] / distinct. For the King / does not move / beyond the /
» four places / or little houses / nearest to his station: as likewise /
» the two Elders [[notes: The *Shi* or Advisors, often called Mandarins, who are confined to the "Palace" area of the board]] : whom / **Riccius** [[notes: Matteo Ricci]], calls the King’s learned Assessors. They do not / employ a Queen / but
» rather two other pieces / which they / call **Powder-** / or **Fire-**
» **Mortars** [[notes: The *Pao* or Cannons, a unique piece in Chinese Chess]] / and which have / ingenious properties / about them.
» These precede two Knights [[notes: Referred to as "Riders" in the text]] / and follow two Infantrymen / (who / in these two spaces / or lines / precede them / in the next space)
» closely at their heels. The movement / of this piece / is fashioned /
» almost like the movement of our Rook or Elephant [[notes: The Rook was historically often depicted as a war-elephant in certain traditions]]. Yet it does not strike / the leader of the opposing army /
» the King : unless it be / that between it / and the King / whom one wishes to attack / there stands a piece / either from its own /
» or the opponent’s troop [[notes: This describes the "cannon" mechanic where the piece must hop over exactly one "screen" piece to capture]]. From this it then follows / that the
» King / can prevent and ward off / this assault in three ways:
» First / when he / in his own person / (so to speak) retreats /
» and without shame or reproach / saves himself / with flight / to the next space: Secondly / when he / opposes it / with yet another piece [[notes: By placing a second piece between the Cannon and the King, the "screen" is doubled and the attack is blocked]]:
» Lastly / when he / entirely clears the side / where he has been /
» attacked / and lets the piece / which covered him / withdraw /
» and places it / upon another space [[notes: By removing the single "screen" piece, the Cannon no longer has a hurdle to jump and thus cannot attack the King]].

» They have furthermore / yet a more excellent game / among
» them / of the following sort:
» Many of them play / with two hundred pieces / of which
» some are white / some black / upon a board / divided into three
» hundred spaces [[notes: This is a description of *Weiqi*, known in the West as Go. While a standard board has 361 intersections, early European accounts often approximated the number]]. With these pieces / each one / attempts / to hunt / and to enclose / the other’s pieces / in the middle of the board / (so that he / can all the better / master the remaining spaces). Whoever now / has occupied the most spaces / on the board / to him / at the last / the victory / is ascribed. This game / is vehemently favored / and used / by those / who sit in the Government [[notes: The "Literati" or Mandarin class of scholar-officials]] : they also often / spend the entire / day / therewith.
