# The First Chapter / 1.cap:

The **Board** [[notes: *Tafel*, literally "table" or "tablet," here referring to the game board]], which is used for this game, matches a **Chess-Board** in its white and black squares, into which it is equally divided.

Regarding its form or shape: it is twice as long as it is wide. Its length is divided into sixteen squares—and its width into eight squares (just as if two Chess-Boards were joined together).

<image-desc>A background of a checkered board (8 by 16 grid). Three geometric playing pieces are shown resting on the board: a low cylinder (marked with 'a'), a low triangular prism (marked 'e'), and a low rectangular prism (marked 'i').</image-desc>

Regarding the **Pieces** [[notes: *Steine*, literally "stones"]]: there are forty-eight of them: namely sixteen **Round**, as many **Triangular**, and then **Square** ones. They are made one finger high and painted with white color on one side—and black on the other.

[[notes: Claude de Boissière (Latinized as Claudius Buxerius) was a 16th-century French mathematician. His 1554 work *Nobilissimus et antiquissimus ludus Pythagoreus qui Rythmomachia nominatur* is a primary source for this game's rules. The practice of flipping a captured piece to show the captor's color is similar to how pieces are promoted in Shogi or captured in Reversi.]]
