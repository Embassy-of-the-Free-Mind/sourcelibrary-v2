### The Sixth Chapter /

->(Buxer : fol : 39. b.)<-
> We have explained before that Victories are obtained within the enemy's camp. However, it will often happen that not all the Counters intended and placed for achieving Victory are located within the Enemy’s line, but rather some remain in Our own camp, which might seem to hinder the attainment of Victory. Nevertheless, let this not delay us; let us consider it sufficient for our purpose if the Half or the Greater part is established in the Enemy’s field. You will also notice that those Counters which you have won from the Enemy are suitable for the perfection of Victory, if you do not find it undesirable to join them with those in the opposing camp, or if you can fashion a Harmony representing Victory from the Counters you have attacked, which you may then arrange as if it were already achieved. Nor is it our intention to diminish the freedom of the Players; thus, it is not strictly required that they fix and unfix the laws of the Game according to a single opinion. For since Games serve the will of the Players, they must also depend upon their judgment. [[notes: This Latin passage from Buxer emphasizes that while victory usually happens in the opponent's territory, players have the flexibility to use captured pieces to complete their mathematical "harmonies."]]

Before we now conclude this description, we shall first
indicate several **Main Rules** which one must generally note and
observe regarding these three different victories, and
add them to the previous ones.
1.) One must first obtain the **Great**; followed by the **Greater**;
and finally the **Greatest** victory. Therefore, the Greatest cannot
happen unless the Greater has preceded it; nor can the Greater be
put into effect unless the Great is first attained. [[notes: In Rithmomachia, victories are tiered based on how many mathematical progressions (Arithmetic, Geometric, and Harmonic) a player can arrange in the opponent's end of the board.]]
