Now that the six methods of combat [[notes: These "Kampf-Stücke" or "battle-pieces" are the various ways to capture: Meeting, Assault, Ambuscade, Proportion, and now Besieging.]] have been described, before we proceed to the preservation of victory and how the game must be brought to an end: we will first set forth Ten Rules which are further useful for the aforementioned combat.

1.) One cannot take another’s stone with his own stone, so long as it is not moved according to its proper gait: [[notes: "Gait" refers to the specific number of spaces a piece is allowed to move.]] however, four cases are excepted:

*Stones cannot be moved from their place to capture others at the same time and in the same throw, unless their movement is legitimate and straight, except in a Siege.*

->*In all captures, only one step must be made.)*<-

2.) The first excepted case is this: When one moves a stone—which stands between two other stones that could take one another, and which covers them from each other—to another space: then he may indeed remove from the board that stone (out of these two now-exposed stones) which he can take according to the rules of the game, but he may not move another stone at the same time; instead, because he has moved one stone and taken another as mentioned, it is counted as one move (which is not unlike a **Discovered Check** [[notes: The original German uses "Ab-Schach," a term from Chess where moving one piece reveals an attack from a piece standing behind it]]). But if he leaves the stone standing which he could and should have taken, and the opponent becomes aware that he overlooked it, then the opponent may not only duly take his stone, but also move one of his own stones regardless.
