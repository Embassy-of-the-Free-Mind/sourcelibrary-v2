# About the Chess-Game.

[[margin: 1. cap: [[notes: Chapter 1]]]]
...stood. The **Warden** [[notes: *Wacht-Meister*, referring to Sadoc]], meanwhile, besieged **Philocopus’s** game-king with many **Chess-Pieces** [[notes: *Schach-Steinen* — literally "chess stones"]], and had left him only a single square remaining (namely, if he were to leap toward his **Rook** [[notes: *Rochen* — an archaic term for the Rook or Castle, derived from the Persian *rukh*]]) to save himself. But **Philocopus**, whose turn it was, instead of using his other **Knight** [[notes: *Reuter* — literally "Rider," the standard term for the Knight]] with which he could have given **Checkmate**—even though he saw it clearly—moved his **Rook** onto that very square which had been the only one left free for his King’s escape. The **Warden** began to laugh for joy, seeing that **Philocopus**, who could have mated him, was himself now in **Checkmate**. He moved a **Soldier** or **Pawn** [[notes: *Bawren* — literally "Peasant"]] forward, gave him **Checkmate** with it, and drew the money toward himself quite neatly with a laughing mouth, saying: "Young man, you do not understand the game," despite the fact that he had noticed quite well how **Philocopus** had arranged his moves. However, because of his great greed for the staked money, he could not reflect on it so quickly, finally pretending as if he had not noticed the intent. To this, **Philocopus** then replied: "My lord, thus must fools be led to school and pay their **tuition** [[notes: *lern-geld* — literally "learning money," a common German idiom for the cost of a mistake]]."

Now the second game was arranged in turn, and the previous stake on both sides was doubled. The **Warden** played very cautiously and sharply; **Philocopus** no less so. Nor did the **Warden** make any move that was good without saying at the same time: "Good fellow, it would have been better for you to have simply let your **Falcon** [[notes: The "Falcon" is a recurring metaphor in the story of *Filocopo*; Sadoc uses it here to taunt Philocopo about his quest or perhaps a specific high-value piece]] fly away, rather than to pursue him this far." **Philocopus** remained silent, acting as if the loss of the money caused him great pain, even though he could have easily given the **Warden** **Checkmate** after bringing the game nearly to its end. However, although he signaled with a gesture of his hand that he had seen clearly how he could have easily brought the **Checkmate** to fruition, he nevertheless managed the outcome of the game in such a way that neither won, but rather the **Chess-Pieces** were removed at the same time [[notes: This describes a draw or stalemate, where the board is cleared without a victor]]. The **Warden** noted very well the politeness...
