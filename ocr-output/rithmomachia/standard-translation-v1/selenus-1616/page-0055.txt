# Concerning the Game of Chess.

[[margin: Chapter 2:]]
that the **Lydians**, who were so named after **Lydus** [[notes: In Greek mythology, Lydus was the ancestral king of the Lydians in western Anatolia]], invented nearly all games—except for the one the Latins called *Ludum Talarium* [[notes: A game played with knucklebones or anklebones, similar to jacks or dice]]—though nothing certain is confirmed by this.

Therefore, others—perhaps with better evidence—attribute such an invention to a wise and clever man named **Palamedes**. It was he who added four more letters to the Greek alphabet, namely **θ ξ χ φ** [[notes: Theta, Xi, Chi, and Phi]], and during the long-lasting siege of the **City of Troy**, around the year 2780 after the creation of the world, he invented two games to still the unrest and mutiny of the moderate soldiery. These were **κυβειαν** (*kybeia*), *Cuborum* or *Tesserarum ludum*, the **Game of Dice** (which **Plinius Secundus** [[notes: Pliny the Elder, author of the *Natural History*]], Book 7, Chapter 56, also attributes to him); and then **πεττειαν** (*petteia*), *Calculorum*, *Pessorum*, and *Latrunculorum ludum*, the **Game of Stones or Chess**. This is attested by **Eustathius** [[notes: A 12th-century archbishop and scholar]], a distinguished commentator and interpreter of the very famous poet **Homer**, in the *Iliad*, folio 490; **Cael. Rhod.** [[notes: Ludovico Ricchieri]], Book 20, Chapter 27; **Ant. Lect.**; **Alexand. ab Alexandro**, Book 3, Chapter 21; **Paris de Puteo** in his treatise *Concerning Games*; **Benin. Casius**; **Hadrian. Junius**; and **Franc. Philelphus** in the first book of his *Convivia*. **Homer** himself, in the *Odyssey*, Book I, makes good mention of this: that the *Proci*, or the suitors of **Penelope**, amused themselves with the Game of Stones, of which we shall speak further through **Athenaeus**. **Cassiodorus** [[notes: A Roman statesman and historian]], in Book 8, Chapter 31, calls the pieces *Calculos Palamediacos*, as they originated from **Palamedes**.

There are still others who attribute it to the Persian Monarch **Xerxes**. Indeed, the **Spaniards** in particular have previously asserted this in their treatises on the Game of Chess, and for that reason, they call it *Axedres* [[notes: *Ajedrez*, the Spanish word for chess, which actually derives from the Arabic *al-shatranj*]]. But because this has no foundation and therefore finds little belief, the **Italians**, in order to give the matter an appearance of truth, turned a King into a Philosopher. They called him **Xerxes Philometor** and attributed the invention to him. So that it might not lack authority, **Friar Jacobus de Casallis** (others say **Cessolis** or **Thessolis**), of the Preaching Order [[notes: The Dominicans]], who lived around the year 1300 after the birth of Christ, states that he took this opinion from a Greek philosopher whom he calls **Diamedes**. Following him (according to **Conrad von Ammenhausen**) were **Dr. Jacob Mennel**, **Polydorus Vergilius**, and others who have published works on the Game of Chess in our own times.
