## Francesco Barozzi to the Reader.
[[notes: Francesco Barozzi (1537–1604) was a Venetian nobleman and mathematician. This text is likely a translation of his 1572 Latin work *Rythmomachia*, which revived interest in this complex medieval "Battle of Numbers."]]

The ancient philosophers [[notes: The original German uses the term *Weltweisen*, literally "World-wise ones," a standard early modern term for the great thinkers of antiquity.]]—
in order to delight and refresh the spirits of men
when they were somewhat weary from studying or
mental labor—
invented many and various amusing games
in times past. However,
due to the long passage of time, through war,
or also through a lack of those who
would have taken pains—as the ancients did—to
bring these games back into existence and standing,
almost all of them have fallen into decline.

Consequently, in our times, we have
almost no knowledge of such games,
except for this single one, which the very
wise man Pythagoras invented, and in his
mother tongue called [[markup: Rythmomachian]] [[notes: Derived from the Greek *rhythmos* (proportion/harmony) and *makhē* (battle). It was a mathematical board game played on a double-sized chessboard.]], (that is, a
battle and harmony of numbers).

This game is generally regarded and held
by all knowledgeable people to be ingenious, useful, and
pleasing—indeed, in truth, a game worthy
of such a distinguished and famous inventor;
considering that it is founded and directed
upon numbers and the harmony
of the **Art of Singing** [[notes: *Singe-Kunst* refers to the science of Music. In the Pythagorean tradition, music was considered a branch of mathematics because musical intervals are determined by numerical ratios.]] (which has
its origin in those same numbers).

[[summary: Francesco Barozzi introduces Rythmomachia as a "battle of numbers" designed by Pythagoras to provide intellectual recreation for weary scholars. He emphasizes that the game is a lost ancient treasure that teaches the mathematical foundations of musical harmony.]]
