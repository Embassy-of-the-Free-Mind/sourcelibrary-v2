[[margin: 10.ca:]]
## The Tenth Chapter.

### Concerning the pieces / which are placed before the Major Pieces [[notes: *Haupt-Steine*, referring to the back-rank pieces like the King, Queen, Rook, Knight, and Bishop.]] / and in this description / are called Soldiers : their positioning and movement : their virtues and utility : also the precedence / which one has over the other.

After the eight most noble / there are still / on each side / eight common pieces / or little men of war : which in several other languages / [are called] *Pedinæ* or *Pedonæ* [[notes: Latin terms for pawns, derived from *pes* (foot), signifying foot soldiers or infantry.]] : in our mother tongue / by those / who draw [compare] this game / to a whole Regiment [[notes: *Regiment* here refers to a system of government, a social order, or a "rule" of the various estates of society.]] / its estates and offices / (of which mention has already been made / in the five previous chapters / and further information shall be provided / at the end of this chapter ) [called] Peasants : (For in times past / there were only two estates / namely the Rulers / to which the eight noble pieces are compared : and then / those who till the soil / for which reason they are called *Bawere* [[notes: An archaic spelling related to "building" or "cultivating."]] among us / and by the unlearned / are called *Bauren* [[notes: *Bauern*, the standard German word for peasants or farmers.]] : to these, however, their various offices are assigned / and / as will follow hereafter / special names / have been given) By others / [called] *Linden* / *Lenden* / as if to say *Wenden* or *Wenden* [[notes: A complex etymological play. The author links the pieces to the "Wends" (Sorbs), a Slavic minority in Germany. In this period, the term was often used to denote commoners, servants, or "foreign" subjects.]] / that is / humble folk / (as the small remnant of the Wends / is still present in Germany : Whom one otherwise / after they were long ago / overcome and expelled by the Saxons : Of which, among other historians, D.
