## The Twelfth Chapter.

### Concerning the Seventh Method; how the same, in turn, can be played in five ways: and then how the first of these is to be arranged.

The seventh method is: **When the White**, after the Soldiers [[notes: "Soldaten" or Soldiers refers to the Pawns.]] of both Kings have been led into the field in the first moves, immediately advances with the King’s Archer-Soldier [[notes: The "King's Archer-Soldier" refers to the King’s Bishop’s Pawn. In this period, the Bishop was often called the Archer (Schütze).]] to twenty-seven, right to the **Black’s** border, and offers his head to their troop.

This method is primarily directed toward the destruction of the opponent's order: and, due to the **Black's** counter-defense in the second move, is again divided into two distinct ways of playing: For the same either takes the aforementioned **W.** Soldier, or moves another piece [[notes: "Stein," literally "stone," was the common term for a chess piece.]]. Of the former way, it is treated in this and the following thirteenth chapter; of the latter, in the fourteenth and fifteenth chapters. In the first way, when one looks at the **White’s** intention in the third move, three further ways arise: For the same moves either (1.) the King’s Rider [[notes: "Reuter" or Rider refers to the Knight.]] to nineteen: or (2.) the King’s Soldier to twenty-nine: (of which we treat in this chapter) or (3.) the King’s Archer: (of which we treat in the following chapter) to thirty.
