-> ### Of the Game of Chess. <-

[[margin: 1. cap.]]
That it (1) be moderate / and then / when necessity requires it / be put into practice.

That it (2) be not improper. It is improper / when the last things are put first / and the game is esteemed higher / than other important / and official duties: how absurd this is / *Seneca* [[notes: Lucius Annaeus Seneca (c. 4 BC – 65 AD), a Roman Stoic philosopher and advisor to Nero.]] *Epist: 117. near the end* gives us to understand with a fine example / in a clever way:

> *Nemo, (he writes) qui ad incendium domus suae currit, cc*
> *Laterunculariam perspicit, ut sciat, quomodo alligatus exeat cc*
> *Calculus.* [[notes: The Latin *Laterunculariam* refers to *Ludus latrunculorum*, an ancient Roman strategy game often translated as "chess" by early modern scholars.]] That is: cc
> No one / who must run to his house / cc
> to rescue the same / from the danger of fire / will cc
> hold the Game of Chess / more dear / and consider first cc
> how he might / rescue his piece / which has fallen into danger / cc
> and dwell long upon it.

So now shall every man also / attend to what is necessary / and pertains to serious matters / (since Nature has indeed directed us / toward those / and not primarily to games and pastimes) / and make use of gaming / only in its proper time.

Of this / we wish to present / *Scævola* [[notes: Refers to Publius Mucius Scaevola or his son Quintus Mucius Scaevola, both renowned Roman jurists and "Pontifex Maximus" (high priests).]], an excellent Roman lawmaker / and jurist / who is said not to have been named *Quintus* / (as *Pomponius* [[notes: Sextus Pomponius, a 2nd-century Roman jurist.]] *l. 2. §. 41. ff. de Orig: jur:* calls him) / but rather *Publius Mutius* / (according to the opinion of *Bernhard: Rutilij*: in the description of his life / pag: 197. 198. and 134.): as an example / (as *Valer: Maximus, lib: 8. c. 8.* [[notes: Valerius Maximus was a 1st-century Roman author of a collection of historical anecdotes.]] writes of him):

> *Scævola, Alea & Calculis vacasse, interdum dicitur, cum cc*
> *benè ac diù, jura Civium & caeremonias Deorum, ordinasset. Ut cc*
> *enim, in rebus serijs Scævolam, ita & in scurrilibus lusibus, Homi- cc*
> *nem agebat, quem rerum natura, continui laboris, patientem esse, cc*
> *non finit, & ad hoc diverticulum, animum suum, forensibus mini- cc*
> *sterijs fatigatum, transferebat:* That is: cc

> The histories report / that *Scævola*, when He / had for a considerable time / well attended to and arranged / secular and spiritual matters / occasionally amused and delighted himself / in games of dice and chess. For cc
> just as he acted in important matters / according to his station / and office / so in the merry, entertaining games / he acted like any cc
> other man / who cannot endure constant labor / cc
