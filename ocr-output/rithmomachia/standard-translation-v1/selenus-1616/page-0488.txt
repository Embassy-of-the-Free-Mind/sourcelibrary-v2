### The Second Chapter

The aforementioned arrangement, which is observed with the eight white round stones, is also to be applied to the **Black Round Stones**: however, in place of the even ones, the first four **Odd Numbers** [[notes: The original German uses *Ungleichen Zahle* (unequal numbers), a common period term for odd numbers because they cannot be divided into two equal whole parts]] (namely 3, 5, 7, 9) are used. Subsequently, on the four lower black round stones, the sums that arise from multiplying the said numbers by themselves [[notes: Squaring the numbers]] (namely 9, 25, 49, 81) shall be written.

( Buxer : fol : 7. a. ) [[notes: Claude de Boissière, known as Buxerius, was a 16th-century mathematician. His work "The Noble Game of Arithmetic" is a primary source for these rules.]]

> » These numbers (3, 5, 7, 9) are also contained within the scope of the monad and the decade [[notes: The "monad" is 1 and the "decade" is 10; Buxerius is noting that these are the single-digit odd numbers]]; though they, like the preceding Even Numbers, are singular, nevertheless every ratio of numbers flows from them as if from a fountain and origin. Furthermore, since in Multiple Proportion [[notes: A "multiple" ratio is one where a larger number contains a smaller number exactly two, three, or more times, such as 2:1 or 3:1]], one number entirely encompasses the other, they are most suitable for describing the circles.
