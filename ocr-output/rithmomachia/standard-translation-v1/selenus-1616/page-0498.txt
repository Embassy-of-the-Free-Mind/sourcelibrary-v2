[[image-desc: A full-page engraving of a Rithmomachia game board, showing a portion of a checkered grid. Twenty-four game pieces are arranged in the central area of the visible board, spanning four rows and six columns. The pieces are rendered with three-dimensional shading.]]

[[notes: **Rithmomachia**, or "The Battle of Numbers," was a highly sophisticated board game popular in Europe from the 11th through the 17th centuries. Unlike chess, which is a game of military strategy, Rithmomachia was a game of mathematical harmony, used to teach Boethian arithmetic and Pythagorean number theory. Players "captured" pieces not just by landing on them, but by using mathematical equalities or progressions.]]

[[notes: The numbers on these pieces follow specific mathematical relationships. The **Circles** represent simple "even" numbers. The **Triangles** represent "Superparticular" ratios (like 12 and 16, where 16 is 12 plus one-third of 12). The **Squares** represent even more complex "Superpartient" ratios.]]

[[notes: The **Pyramid 91** is a special composite piece. As noted in the illustration, its value is derived from the sum of the first six squares: 1² (1) + 2² (4) + 3² (9) + 4² (16) + 5² (25) + 6² (36) = 91. In gameplay, the Pyramid is the most powerful piece, acting somewhat like a Queen in chess but with significantly more complex rules for its capture.]]

[[gloss: **Rithmomachia**: From the Greek *arithmos* (number) and *mache* (battle).]]
[[gloss: **Pyramid**: A "perfect" piece made of stacked layers, each representing a square number.]]
