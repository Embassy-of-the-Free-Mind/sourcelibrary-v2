The Fourth Book / Chapter 2:

...nevertheless [takes] his Soldier [[notes: "Soldier" is Selenus’s term for a Pawn]] with the Queen, and thereby causes him no small disorder: For, when he moves the King’s Knight [[notes: "Reuter," literally "Rider," refers to the Knight]] (to protect the King’s Soldiers) to nineteen [[notes: Selenus uses a 1–64 numbering system for the board. 1 is a1, 8 is h1, 9 is a2, and so on. Nineteen corresponds to g3.]]: then the Black player moves the King’s Bishop [[notes: "Schütze," literally "Archer" or "Marksman," refers to the Bishop]] to thirty-eight: He moves the King’s Bishop’s Soldier (to give no cause for an exchange of Queens from four or twelve: and also to guard against Checkmate from twenty) to twenty-two: The Black player offers Check with the Queen from twenty: He must move the King to fourteen and exposes his King’s Bishop to the Black Queen: The Black player offers Check with the same from eleven: He covers the King with the King’s Bishop at thirteen: The Black player moves the King’s Soldier to his King’s Soldier at forty-five: He moves the King’s Knight’s Soldier to twenty-three: The Black player offers Check with the King’s Bishop from thirty-five: He moves the King to fifteen: (For if he had covered him with his Bishop at twenty-one: the Black player would take his Knight’s Soldier with the Queen, gaining three Soldiers for one, and thus bring the Queen back into the field): The Black player (to clear his Queen, provided he does not wish to move the King’s Bishop to forty-seven) takes his King’s Soldier with the King’s Soldier: He takes the same with the King’s Knight: The Black player [moves] the King’s Bishop against the same at forty-five: He moves him (out of danger) to thirty: nineteen: or twenty-six: The Black player moves the Queen back into the open field at thirty-eight, and has thrown him quite off his stride [[notes: The phrase "auß den sprüngen gebracht" literally means "brought out of his leaps," a metaphor for disrupting an opponent's planned maneuvers or rhythm]].

[[margin: 4.]]
To now prevent this disorder, which arose from the previous moves: *the White player* should not move both center Soldiers (n.1), but rather move the King’s Soldier to twenty-eight and his Knight to nineteen (so that his King is not rushed by the Black Queen from twenty-five): The Black player moves the King’s Soldier to thirty-six: The White player does not take it with his King’s Knight (on account of the aforementioned Check from the Black Queen) but moves his Bishop to thirty: The Black player moves his King’s Bishop to thirty-eight: The White player moves the King’s Bishop’s Soldier to twenty-two: The Black player moves the King’s Knight (to protect the King’s Soldier) to forty-six:

[[notes: In this section, Selenus is demonstrating how a seemingly strong opening with two moves can quickly collapse into "disorder" if White is too aggressive or fails to anticipate the Black Queen's mobility. He then provides a corrected line of play beginning at margin note 4.]]
