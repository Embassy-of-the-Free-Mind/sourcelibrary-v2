### Chapter 16: On the Game of Chess.

Concerning finally the **examples**, they are arranged as follows:

[[margin: cc cc cc cc cc cc cc cc cc cc]]
1.) When the **White** has placed his **King** on four; his **Queen** on five; the **Rooks** [[notes: *Rochen*, from the Old Persian *rukh*, refers to the Rook or Castle]] on sixty and on sixty-three; three **Soldiers** [[notes: *Soldaten* refers to the Pawns]] on thirteen, on fourteen, and on twenty-three: then the **Black**—when he has moved his **King** to thirty-seven; the **Queen** to forty-five; both **Rooks** to sixteen and fifty-one; both **Riders** [[notes: *Reuter*, the Knights]] to twenty-five and thirty-three; and a **Soldier** to fifty-six—can give him **Checkmate** with this Soldier in the seventh move.

For, when the Black moves a **Rider** (from 25) to ten and offers check [[notes: *Schach-beut*, literally "offers check"]]: The White moves the **King** to twelve;

The Black offers check with the other **Rider** from eighteen [[notes: The text says 18, though the setup mentioned 33; this may refer to a previous move in the sequence]]: The White moves the **King** to twenty-one;

The Black moves a **Rook** (from 51) to nineteen and offers check: The White (since the **Queen**, which can only be moved one field [[notes: In older versions of chess, the Queen—or *Fers*—was the weakest piece, moving only one square diagonally. Here the author notes she cannot move far enough to capture the Rook]], must not take it) places one of his **Rooks** (from 60) in between on twenty;

The Black offers check with the first **Rider** from four: The White (since he can neither strike the same with the **Queen**, considering that she cannot strike sideways next to herself [[notes: Another reference to the Queen's restricted diagonal movement]], nor take it with the **Rook**, which the Black **Rook** holds captive) moves the **King** to twenty-two;

The Black also offers check with the other **Rider** from twenty-eight: The White moves the **King** to thirty-one;

The Black moves the **Queen** (since she does not strike from afar) to thirty-eight and offers check: The White moves the **King** to thirty-nine;

The Black moves the King's Rook's-Soldier against him to forty-eight and gives **Checkmate**.
