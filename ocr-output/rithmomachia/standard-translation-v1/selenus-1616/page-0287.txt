### The
# Third Book
## of the Description / of the Chess Game.

->Xerxes Philomater.<- [[notes: According to medieval chess lore, Xerxes (also known as Philometor or Philometer) was the legendary philosopher-courtier who invented the game of chess. He supposedly did so to teach the Babylonian king, Evilmerodach, the virtues of patience and the importance of a king’s dependence on his subjects.]]

->Amilinus Evilmerodach.<- [[notes: Amil-Marduk (known biblically as Evil-merodach) was a historical King of Babylon. In the context of early chess literature, he is often depicted as the student or the tyrant who is reformed by learning the strategy and logic of the game.]]

->Leipzig, at the house of Henning<- [[notes: "Lipsiæ" is the Latin name for Leipzig, a major center for printing and the book trade in the 17th century.]]

->Gross the Younger.<- [[notes: Henning Gross the Younger (1582–1649) was a prominent German bookseller and publisher who continued his father’s influential publishing house in Leipzig.]]
