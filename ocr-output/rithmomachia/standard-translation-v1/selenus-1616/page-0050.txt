### The First Book / Chapter.

,, of ***Philocopus***, that he applied himself more to
,, losing than to winning, saying to himself: "In truth,
,, no more noble or courteous youth has yet come before me."
,, Finally, they set the pieces once again for the sake of the third
,, game, and also increased the sum of the stake [[notes: *Aufsatzes* — the money placed on the table as a bet]] by
,, a considerable amount. But before this same game
,, was started again, the **Warden** [[notes: *Wacht-Meister*, the official in charge of the tower]] exhorted ***Philocopus*** very highly,
,, saying to him: "My youth, I pray and charge you
,, by the power of all the gods, that you play as best
,, you can, and do not spare me as you have done in the
,, previous games." ***Philocopus*** answered: "My lord, a pupil
,, can play poorly with his **Master** [[notes: *Meister*]], so that he should not be defeated [[notes: *erleget* — literally 'slain' or 'brought down,' here meaning checkmated]];
,, but since it is so pleasing to you, I will arrange my moves
,, in such a manner, so far as I understand **Chess** [[notes: *Schach*]]."
,, They then began the third game, and after they
,, had moved for a considerable time, ***Philocopus’s*** game
,, became the better one. When the **Warden** now
,, noticed this, he began to grow angry and turn red
,, in the face, and also sought out all his tricks [[notes: *rencke* — deceptive maneuvers or ruses]]
,, to see if he could bring his ruined game back
,, into good standing. But the more he attempted
,, this, the worse his game became. ***Philocopus*** now took a
,, **Knight** [[notes: *Reuter* — literally a 'Rider']] from him with one of his **Bishops** [[notes: *Schützen* — literally 'Archers,' the early modern German term for the Bishop piece]], and gave **Check** [[notes: *Schach*]] to his **King** [[notes: *Könige*]].
,, The **Warden** became enraged beyond measure
,, by this move—more because of the loss of the staked
,, **gold coins** [[notes: *gülden*]] than for the game itself.
,, He knocked the **Chess-pieces** [[notes: *Schach-Steine*]] into
,, a heap so forcefully that the board, together with the pieces,
,, fell to the floor. When ***Philocopus*** saw this,
,, he said: "My lord, since wise and sensible people
,, are well accustomed to getting angry at this game,
,, I hold you no less sensible even if you have
,, become somewhat incensed over the **Chess-pieces**.
,, However, if you had paid close attention to the game
,, before you disrupted and confused it, you would
,, certainly have found that I could have been defeated by you
,, in two moves. I also believe that you were well
,, aware of it, but that you wished to use courtesy toward me
,, and lose the game intentionally by pretending to be
,, angry. But it shall not be so; rather, this money..."
