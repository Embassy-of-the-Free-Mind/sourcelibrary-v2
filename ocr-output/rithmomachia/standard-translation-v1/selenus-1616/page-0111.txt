### Chapter 9: On the Game of Chess. 81

...his treasures / are not struck: of which / an example / is to be found / in Chapter 14, no. 12 / of the Second Book.

2. When one player / has only his **Archers** [[notes: *Schützen* (Archers) is the term used here for Bishops.]] / and the other / has only his **Riders** [[notes: *Reuter* (Riders) is the term used here for Knights.]] remaining: then the latter / with the Riders / can easily take away the Archers / in check / because the same / cannot relieve or watch over [[notes: *entsetzen und verwachen* refers to the way chess pieces protect one another.]] one another / as the Riders can.

3. The Archers / are often enclosed and hemmed in / by their own stones—or those of the enemy / if they would have to remove them / to their own detriment: which / cannot happen to the Riders / because they have the power / to slip in / as well as to slip out / to leap over the others / and to save themselves / by breaking away in such a fashion.

4. The Archers can only / be moved and strike on one color: the Riders / can hit and be moved / on both colors.

In conclusion, it is to be remembered / that through their leaping over / one perhaps wished to suggest / that the Riders / should always be placed and ordered / in such places / where the **Infantry** [[notes: *Fuß-Volck* refers to the Pawns.]] / cannot be a hindrance to them: or else / that the mighty / are accustomed to suppress the weak: which is, however, unjust / and can be perceived / from their uneven [[notes: *ungerad*—referring to the L-shaped, non-linear move of the Knight as a symbol of devious or "crooked" behavior.]] path. That they / from their field / can only reach a few squares / gives it to be understood: that / while they stay near their own troop and acquaintances / they should always be humble and friendly—but when they / come to the enemy / on the field / they should be on their guard at all times / and behave bravely against them.

Finally, they / were placed at the wings of the Infantry / next to the **Rooks** [[notes: *Rochen* is the old term for the Rooks/Castles.]] / (in order to protect the same).

That which has now been recorded / concerning these principal pieces / from the translated book of **Fr. Jacobi de Casallis** [[notes: Jacobus de Cessolis (c. 1250 – c. 1322) was a Dominican friar who wrote the famous *Libellus de moribus hominum et officiis nobilium ac popularium super ludo scachorum*, a treatise on chess as a metaphor for society.]], is herewith / according to the promise / also added to the preceding:

> [[margin: ,, ]] The Knight on the **Chess-Table** [[notes: *Schach-Zabel*, from the Latin *tabula*, an old term for the chessboard.]] / shall sit / upon
> [[margin: ,, ]] a horse / in full heraldry / and the horse / shall be
> [[margin: ,, ]] covered. The Knight / when he is blessed / shall first / be
> [[margin: ,, ]] dedicated or bathed / so that he may carry and lead / a new life /
> [[margin: ,, ]] henceforth: He shall also appeal to God with devotion /
> [[margin: ,, ]] that God may give him by grace / that / which he may not have /
> [[margin: ,, ]] by nature. The Prince shall also strike him / upon
> [[margin: ,, ]] the neck / and dub him knight / so that he receives knightly dignity from him...
