# On the Game of Chess

[[margin: 10. cap:]]
The **Husbandman** [[notes: *BawMan*, referring to a farmer or peasant. In the allegorical tradition of Jacobus de Cessolis, each pawn represents a different profession; the King's Rook's Pawn is usually the Husbandman.]] / must first / recognize God / so that he may serve Him who gives to him and to us temporal things / such as are a necessity for our lives / and thank Him for His grace / with the tithe [[notes: *zehend*, the traditional tenth of agricultural produce given to the church]] / which is only right: Because He protects for us / the other nine parts / from storms / and from enemies. 2. The **Husbandman** / should be obedient / so that he guards his Lord's property / diligently / and faithfully / and tends to it well and beautifully; there are, alas, many / who take the name of servant / and wear a servant’s clothes / and eat his food / but they have not the manners of a servant / nor do they flee from unfaithful habits / for they do not conduct themselves / according to wisdom. 3. He should love life / and yet not fear death: For it is a mockery / for one who is so weary of life / that he foolishly rushes toward death [[notes: *zu dem tod gauchen*, literally "to gawk or act like a fool toward death"]]: A wise man / should not / rush foolishly toward death / but rather walk toward it calmly. 4. He should apply himself to labor / and yet / ensure that he observes the holidays / which Christendom has established / unless there is a legal necessity [[notes: *eehaft not*, a term for a legitimate excuse or dire emergency that excuses one from religious or legal obligations]] / which he must by no means overstep. To the **Husbandman** belong by right / three things: First / that he should work and turn over the earth / and for this he should have / in his right hand / a hoe or mattock [[notes: *wagen fuen oder hawe*]] / with which he / splits the earth. He should also raise livestock / and for that he should have in the left / a goad or rod / with which one / drives the livestock out to the field. He should also be a guardian / not only / to lead and drive the livestock / but that he practices virtue and loyalty therein / and gives the best to God. He should also prune and clean trees / of excess and barren shoots / and tend the vineyards: For this reason he has / under his belt / a pruning knife [[notes: *besneid-Messer*]].

The **Second Pawn** [[notes: *Der Ander Wend*, here referring to the King's Knight's Pawn]] / on the **Chessboard** / signifies those / who forge with hammers / with axes / and with carpenter’s hatchets [[notes: *Zimer parten*]]: and this one is placed before the **Knight** / on the right hand / of the **King**: For the **Knight** has need of the smith for knives / and for swords / for spurs / for saddles / and also all other things. His form should be / like a smith / who has in his right hand / a hammer / and in his left hand an axe / and under his belt an apron [[notes: *fell*, literally "skin" or "hide," referring to a leather smith's apron]].
