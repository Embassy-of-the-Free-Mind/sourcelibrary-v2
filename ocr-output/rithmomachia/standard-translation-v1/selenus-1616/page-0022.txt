[[meta: This page continues the Italian poem "Chess Duel" by Bartolomeo Burchelato, where the speaker is boasting about the strength of his white chess pieces against the black army of his opponent, Bombene. The tone is mock-heroic, comparing the board game to great classical battles.]]

And though they were from Jove, arrayed for war.
If you want Baldi [[notes: Likely a reference to Bernardino Baldi (1553–1617), a contemporary polymath and poet who wrote a famous poem on chess, "De ludo scaccorum."]] on your side, go on, there is no escape;
Do you want a Judge as well? Take him, for I
Now wish to enter the field with my people.
Certain and sure am I, that this strong
Knight of mine will assault your men,
Will strike them down, and make a river of blood.
Do not believe it until you see it later,
How much my terrible spear is worth,
And how it returns to its own, laden with prey.
For often, in the midst of the battle,
It captured the Captain of the enemies;
And caused such slaughter as was never seen at Pharsalia [[notes: The Battle of Pharsalia (48 BC) was the decisive engagement of Caesar's Civil War, famous in literature for its scale and bloodshed.]].
Now, so that I do not seem to you a vain Thrason [[notes: Thrason is the archetypal "Braggart Soldier" from the Roman comedy *The Eunuch* by Terence. The speaker is saying he isn't just making empty boasts.]],
And that I do not give myself such a boast without reason,
Arrange your men here, on this varied plain [[notes: The "varied plain" is the checkered surface of the chessboard.]];
For I will show you if I am worthy, and how much.

### On the WORK itself.

A skillful genius and a noble will to act
Have offered these things; Momus [[notes: Momus was the Greek god of satire and mockery. The author is challenging critics to try and produce something better than what he has written.]], give us something better.

->Joannes Schwartz / J. C. [[notes: *Juris Consultus*, meaning a legal scholar or lawyer.]] to the Author<-
->from his inner circle.<-

### Another, by the same [author].
### On the EMBLEM engraved in copper,
### And prefixed to the Work.

The mind of man is varied, and does not seize the right path of its own accord,
It rushes more into the forbidden, and deeply despises the good,
