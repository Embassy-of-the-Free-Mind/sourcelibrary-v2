### The Second Book, 13. Chapter:

...[the **King’s Bishop**] to twenty-three: **Black** gives check with the **King’s Knight** [[notes: "K. Reuter" (King's Rider) refers to the Knight.]] from forty-eight: and can subsequently easily obtain the **White King’s Rook**, along with the whole game) [White] must take him with the **Queen**: **Black** takes the **White King’s Bishop** with the **Queen** (so far as he does not wish to exchange her), offers check, and moves her onto [the square of] the **White King’s Rook**: **White** covers the **King** with the **King’s Knight** on twenty-two, and in this way comes to the aid of the **King’s Rook**: **Black** will easily win against **White** (who has lost four **Pawns** [[notes: "Soldaten" (Soldiers) refers to Pawns.]] and one **Bishop** more) when he brings the **King’s Knight** to forty-eight and the remaining **Major Pieces** [[notes: "Haupt-Steine" literally translates to "Head-Stones," used here to denote the non-pawn pieces.]] into play.

[[margin: 31.]] But if **White** does not move the **King** to 3 (as in note 26) but rather to twelve: then **Black** does best to nevertheless take away the **White King’s Knight** with the **Pawn**: **White**, however, is free to take the new **Black Queen** [[notes: This describes a Pawn reaching the final rank and promoting to a Queen.]] with the **King’s Rook** or the **Queen**: or else to take the first **Black Queen** with the **Rook**: In this latter case, **Black** may exchange the **Queen** or continue to use the new one. 

If, however, **Black** does not take the **White Knight** (as mentioned at the beginning of this section) but instead offers check with the **Queen** from twenty-six or thirty-three: then **White** covers the **King** with his **Knight** on nineteen: **Black** takes the **White King’s Pawn** with the **King’s Bishop’s Pawn** and moves it onto the aforementioned **White Knight**: **White** takes the **Black King’s Pawn** with the **King’s Rook**: (or, if **Black** had given check to the **King** with the **Queen** from 33: [White takes] the aforementioned **Black Bishop’s Pawn** with the **King’s Bishop** and thus saves the **King’s Knight** and this **Bishop**) **Black** [takes] the **White King’s Knight** with the **King’s Bishop’s Pawn** and offers check: **White** takes him with the **King’s Bishop** and moves the same onto the **Black Queen**: **Black** gives check with her from forty-four: **White** moves the **King** to three: **Black** (so that **White** does not trap his **Queen** with the **King’s Rook** from twelve) must be careful and further arrange his affairs for victory with deliberation against **White** (whose **King’s** quarters and breastworks [[notes: "brustwehren" is a military term for defensive parapets, used here to describe the protective pawn structure around the King.]] he has entirely destroyed, in addition to obtaining two **Pawns**).
