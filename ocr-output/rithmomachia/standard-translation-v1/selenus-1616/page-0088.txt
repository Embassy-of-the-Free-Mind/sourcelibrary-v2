# The First Book / Chapter 5:

"…whoever holds the Kingly name and has no subjects who
"serve him. Also, one may say to the King, 'Check' [[notes: *Schach*. In this context, the author uses the chess term to symbolize a legal challenge.]], which
"signifies a demand for justice, just as if one were to say: 'O
"King, do me justice!' This must then come to pass, unless
"he defends himself with wisdom or with force. For
"it often happens that when a knight or servant, noble or
"common, suffers a great injustice or excessive
"oppression, and they can do no other, they then flee
"from him when they come into the field [[notes: *ze veld kumment* refers to the battlefield.]] with him, and leave him
"alone among the enemies; for they can have no other
"revenge against him, and thus they 'check' him because
"he has ruled with a harsh hand. And therefore,
"when 'Check' is said to the King, it means
"that he stands exposed, stripped of nobles
"and commoners, so that he cannot
->"shield himself behind anyone;<-
->"and that same 'Check' puts a<-
->"King in 'Mate' [[notes: *Mat*. From the Persian *shāh māt*, meaning 'the King is dead' or 'the King is defeated.']], and thereby he falls<-
->"into the hands of all his enemies,<-
->"wherever he turns and moves, etc.<-
