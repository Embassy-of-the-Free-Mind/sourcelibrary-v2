The **foundation stone** of both is square and equal in size to the sixteen squares mentioned before; indeed (as will follow later in the report), it is one taken from among them. Upon this is placed another **square stone** of the same height as the lowest one, yet somewhat narrower. Over these two squares, a **triangular** one is placed, yet narrower than the square immediately below it. Upon this, an even narrower triangular stone; finally, upon these four stones, an even narrower **round stone** is laid, and the **Tower** [[notes: *Turn*, modern German *Turm*. In Rithmomachia, these complex pieces are usually called "Pyramids"]] is thus tapered to a point.

In this way, one assembles (two Towers of five levels—namely, of two squares, two triangles, and one round stone). However, so that they may be better distinguished from one another, one lays two round stones upon one of them, ensuring the uppermost is slightly smaller, thus making it of **six steps**. 

These aforementioned stones, laid one above the other, are joined together—perhaps with a **screw** through their center—so that the players may, at their pleasure, remove one of them if necessary and then reattach the others and make them firm. They are also, like the other
