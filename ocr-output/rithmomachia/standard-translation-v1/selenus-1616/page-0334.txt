### The Third Book / 5. cap:

<margin>10.</margin>But if the White [player] does not move the King to 13 [[note: square e2]], but rather covers with the King's Archer [[note: The "Archer" (Schütze) is the Bishop]] on twenty [[note: square d3]], and helps to protect those Soldiers [[note: "Soldiers" (Soldaten) are Pawns]]: then the Black [player], with the King's Archer's Soldier, offers check on thirty-one [[note: square g4]], and rescues him [[note: his piece]] from the White King’s Soldiers. He must also endeavor to move his Queen away from twenty-eight [[note: square d4]] as follows: for, if he leaves her standing there (after the White King has been freed from check, perhaps on three [[note: square c1]] or twelve [[note: square d2]]) and moves another piece: White takes his [[note: Black's]] King’s Archer’s Soldier with the King’s Archer’s Soldier (provided the same has not previously moved to forty-three [[note: square c6]] to block the path of his King’s Rider [[note: The "Rider" (Reuter) is the Knight]]). If he [Black] now captures the same with the King: then White offers check with the King’s Rider on thirty-four [[note: square b5]], and thereby wins his Queen without any remedy.

<margin>11.</margin>But if White does not move the King’s Soldier to 29 [[note: square e4, see note 8]], but rather moves to twenty-one [[note: square e3]] (in order to relieve the King’s Soldier in the future): then the Black [player] plays that same Soldier to forty-five [[note: square e6]] (to further protect the King’s Soldier and to open the path for the King’s Archer). White [moves] the King’s Rider onto the Black King’s Archer’s Soldier at thirty-four [[note: square b5]]. Black (to protect him) [moves] the King’s Rider to sixty-one [[note: square e8]], and thus has a well-ordered game.
