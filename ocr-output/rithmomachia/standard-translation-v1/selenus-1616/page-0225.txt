### The Eleventh Chapter.

## How one must play in the fifth, but especially in the sixth manner.

The fifth manner is: when the Black player, on the second move, in the recorded example of the Eighth Chapter, varies the play; and instead of the King’s Bishop [[notes: *Schützen*, literally "archers" or "shooters," was a common early German term for the Bishop]], moves the King’s Bishop’s Pawn [[notes: *Soldaten*, or "soldiers," refers to the Pawns]] to forty-six: The White player, however, to thwart the opponent’s plans, moves the King’s Knight [[notes: *Reuter*, or "rider," refers to the Knight]] to nineteen: or his Bishop’s Pawn to twenty-seven.

The sixth manner is: When the Black player, in the second move, moves neither the Bishop to 38: (as mentioned in the three previous Chapters) nor the Bishop’s Pawn to 46: (as set down shortly above at the beginning of this Chapter) but rather, with the King’s Knight, attacks the White King’s Pawn from [square] forty-three, in the following manner:

[[margin: 1.]] The White player moves the King’s Pawn to 28: The Black player
[[margin: 2.]] moves his King’s Pawn to 36. * The White player moves the King’s Bishop to 30: The Black player performs the attack, as stated, with the Knight upon the White King’s Pawn from [square] forty-three: The White player (to defend the Pawn) moves the King’s [side] Pawn [[notes: likely the Queen's Pawn or the King's Bishop's Pawn depending on the coordinate orientation]] to twenty-one: The Black player moves the King’s Bishop to thirty-eight: The White player moves the King’s Knight to the Black King’s Pawn at nineteen: the Black player, to protect the same and to [attack] the White King’s Bishop’s Pawn, moves his King’s Knight to twenty-six. The White player moves the Queen (so that this Black Knight does not capture her, or the King’s Rook [[notes: *Rochen* is the traditional term for the Rook, derived from the Persian 'rukh']) to twelve: the Black player captures the White King’s Bishop’s Pawn with the King’s Bishop and offers Check: The White player moves his King to five. The Black player moves the King’s Bishop to forty-seven (so that he does not lose the same, or the Knight, if perhaps the White player moves his King’s Rook’s Pawn to seventeen): the White player [moves] his King’s Rook (in order not to [lose] the same in Check from the Black King’s Knight at eleven...
