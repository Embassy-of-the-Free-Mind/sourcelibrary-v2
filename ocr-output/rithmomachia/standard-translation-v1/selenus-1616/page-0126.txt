# The First Book /
## Se

[[meta: catchword: what]]

...[to] pervert [[notes: The text here completes a thought from the previous page, likely warning that scribes must not pervert the truth or the law.]], and no matter that is harmful to one’s neighbor should ever be heard from them. They should, every day, read and know the city’s laws and rights, and keep in memory what the city decrees and makes law; and as much as is in their power, they should not allow anything to be decreed in the cities that is against God or against justice. They should also guide the **Judges** and give **Counsel** regarding everything in which they are in the wrong; for no matter or law is right if it is made against God or good morals. 

But one finds many now in the cities who do not heed the **common good** [[notes: *Gemein guß* (likely *nutz*) refers to the "commonweal" or public welfare, as opposed to private interests.]]; they pursue only what is good for them, joining with the masses and binding themselves with oaths. Thus they create factions among themselves, and thereby the cities are destroyed, oppressed, and ultimately laid waste. 

And so that a unity might exist and remain in the cities, they should advise and move all people toward it. There should be, between the **Citizens** who wish a city well, a whole and steady **friendship**: which is a shared will for good things toward a friend, for the sake of those things that one should prioritize above all else. There is nothing so good, nor so pious, and so fitting to Nature in both good fortune and adversity, as friendship; its power is so great that it binds the society of the entire **human race**, whether between two people or among the few in whom it is found. 

In the world, there is nothing more pleasant than having a friend with whom a man may speak as if with himself. Indeed, he is not called a joyful man who does not invite and cherish a friend within his secrets. It is to be noted that some friendship is built upon **utility** [[notes: *Nuß gut* or *nutz* refers to friendship based on profit or what one can gain from the other.]], and when the utility departs and the goods are gone, so too does the friendship depart. Likewise, when one has good fortune, one has many friends; but when one does not have it, one remains alone. Therefore, a friend who seeks his own profit and not the welfare of his friend is more a **merchant** than a friend. Such friendship extends no further than the profit does. 

In this way, people prefer fields, horses, houses, children, and other possessions; but the love of men is often common **purity** [[notes: *Lauterkeit* here suggests sincerity or the "naked truth" of a relationship.]] or vanity. And this sincerity is recognized when people are faring poorly; if the friend then remains steady, that is a sign of whole and sincere friendship.
