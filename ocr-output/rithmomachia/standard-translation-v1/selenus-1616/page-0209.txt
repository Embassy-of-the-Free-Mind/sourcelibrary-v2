Schützens-Soldaten*; the Pawn located in front of the Bishop]] on forty-six [[notes: Square f6]]: then **the White player** likewise makes an attack with the **King’s Bishop’s Soldier** on twenty-seven [[notes: Square c4]]: **The Black player** takes him with his **King’s Soldier** [[notes: *K. Soldaten*; the King's Pawn]]: **The White player** plays the **King’s Soldier** onto [the square of] the **Opponent's King’s Bishop** [[notes: *G. K. Schützen*; likely meaning the square e4, attacking the diagonal]] on twenty-nine [[notes: Square e4]]: **The Black player** offers check with the **Queen** on twenty-five [[notes: Square a4]]: **The White player** moves the **King** to five [[notes: Square e1]]:

[[margin: 17.]] * **The Black player** [moves] the **King’s Bishop** against the **White Queen** on twenty-six [[notes: Square b4]]: **The White player** [moves] the **King’s Knight** to nineteen [[notes: Square c3]], covers his **Queen** with the same, and pursues the **Black Queen**: **The Black player** (so that he does not lose the **King’s Bishop**) takes the **White King’s Knight** with his **King’s Bishop**: **the White player** [takes] the same with the **Queen**, and thus wins, with the help of her **Bishop**, the **Opponent's King’s Soldier** [[notes: The Black King's Pawn]].

[[margin: 18.]] But when **the Black player** does not move the **Queen** to 26. (no. 17.) but instead [moves] the **King’s Bishop** to forty-seven [[notes: Square g6]]: then **the White player** plays against the **Black Queen** (and to protect the **King’s Rook’s Soldier** [[notes: *K. Rochens-Soldaten*; the King's Rook's Pawn]]) his **Knight** to nineteen [[notes: Square c3]]: **the Black player** [moves] the same [the Queen] (in order to further protect her **King’s Soldier**) to twenty-six [[notes: Square b4]]: **the White player** [moves] the **King’s Rook’s Soldier** against the same to seventeen [[notes: Square a3]]: **the Black player** [moves] the same (to protect the **King’s Soldier** once more) to eighteen [[notes: Square b3]]: **the White player** [moves] the **King’s Knight** to thirteen [[notes: Square e2]]: **the Black player** /

[[margin: 19.]] [moves] the **King’s Knight** to forty-three [[notes: Square c6]]: * **the White player** [moves] the **King’s Knight** against the **Black Queen** on three [[notes: Square c1]]: **the Black player** [moves] the same [the Queen] (because otherwise she can flee nowhere else) to forty-two [[notes: Square b6]]: **the White player** takes the **Black King’s Soldier** with his **King’s Bishop**, or advances the **King’s Bishop** to twenty-one [[notes: Square e3]].

[[margin: 20.]] But when **the White player** does not move the **Knight** to 3. (no. 19.) but instead moves the **King’s Rook** [[notes: *K. Rochen*; an archaic term for the Rook or Castle]] to four [[notes: Square d1]]: then **the Black player** advances (for the future protection of his **King’s Soldier**) his **Knight** to thirty-three [[notes: Square a5]]: **the White player** [moves] the **King’s Soldier** against the **Black King’s Soldier** to thirty-six [[notes: Square d5]] and will, with this well-ordered game, so long as he is careful, easily win.
