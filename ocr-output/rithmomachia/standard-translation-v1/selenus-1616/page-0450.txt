### The Fourth Book / Chapter 7:

**White** should leave the **King’s Archer’s Soldier** [[notes: The Bishop's Pawn]] (no. 5) standing, and (to free the **King’s Rider** [[notes: The Knight]]) move their **Archer** [[notes: The Bishop]] to thirteen [[notes: The author uses a numbered system for the 64 squares of the board; square 13 is likely d3 in modern notation]]: The **Black** player moves the **King’s Archer** [[notes: The Bishop]] (to defend the mentioned **Rider’s Soldier** [[notes: The Knight's Pawn]]) against the **White Queen** at forty-six: **White** moves her (to protect the **King’s Rider’s Soldier** [[notes: The Knight's Pawn]]) to twenty-six: **Black** takes the **White King’s Soldier** [[notes: The King's Pawn]] with the **Queen**: **White** moves the **King’s Rider** [[notes: The Knight]] to nineteen, and thus arranges his game not badly: he can also remain quite a match for the **Black** player (if he [Black] finally moves the **King** to two or seven, behind the **Soldiers** [[notes: The Pawns]]).

<margin>10.</margin> takes the **White King’s Soldier** [[notes: The King's Pawn]] with the **King’s Archer** [[notes: The Bishop]]: * **White** moves the **King’s Rider** [[notes: The Knight]] onto the **Black King’s Soldier** [[notes: The King's Pawn]] at nineteen and wins the same by force.

<margin>11.</margin> However, if **White** does not move to 19 with the **Rider** [[notes: The Knight]] (no. 10), but rather (to prevent **Check** from the **Black King’s Rock** [[notes: The Rook]] from sixty: and to obtain the **Black King’s Soldier** [[notes: The King's Pawn]] by force) leaps with the **King** (since he has never yet moved from his field, though Check was offered him in no. 3) to twenty-one [[notes: This refers to an early rule regarding the King's "leap," a precursor to modern castling]]: then **Black** moves the **King’s Rock** [[notes: The Rook]] (so that he may move the same to four following this, to confuse **White’s** game) to sixty: **White** moves the **King’s Rider** [[notes: The Knight]] (to protect the fourth field with the help of his **Rock** [[notes: The Rook]]) to nineteen: next takes the **Black King’s Soldier** [[notes: The King's Pawn]] with the **King’s Rider** [[notes: The Knight]]: moves their **Archer** [[notes: The Bishop]] to twenty: and his **Soldier** [[notes: The Pawn]] to twenty-two: whereby he will finally win the game from **Black**, even if the latter might delay him somewhat with his moves.
