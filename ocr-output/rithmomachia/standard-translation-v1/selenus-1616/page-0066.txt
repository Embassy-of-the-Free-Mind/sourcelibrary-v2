### ->The First Book / Chapter 2:<-

,, One finds among them, at times, ivory chess-pieces which are formed and carved like whole persons. But they use, in my opinion, another piece in place of the stone which we call the **Queen**.

If we look further, we find the **Persians**; next in **Africa**, the **Mauritanians** [[notes: A historical term for the Berber inhabitants of the Maghreb in North Africa]] and **Moors**, who likewise seek their pleasure and pastime in this: Of whom **Garcia de Orta** [[notes: A Portuguese Renaissance physician and pioneer of tropical medicine who lived in India]], as summarized by **Carolus Clusius** [[notes: Charles de l'Écluse, one of the most influential botanists of the 16th century, who translated and abridged Garcia de Orta's work from Portuguese into Latin]], writes in the following manner in *Book 2, Chapter 28*:

,, The word **Xa** [[notes: Shah, the Persian word for King]] invites me to add something here about the **Game of Soldiers** [[notes: *Ludus Latrunculorum*, a Roman strategy game often compared to chess by Renaissance scholars]], which is very familiar to the Persians and Mauritanians, although they have a different way of playing. They call the King **Xa**: but whenever they attack him, they by no means say *Xaque* [[notes: Check]], but simply **xa**, as if to say, 'I warn you, King, to move from your place.' They call the Queen **Goazir** [[notes: Vizier or Wazir, the King’s advisor or minister]], that is, the Prefect of the Kingdom, or what they call the Constable. The Bishop [[notes: Literally "Dolphin or Archer" in the Latin text]] they call **Fil**, that is, an Elephant. The Knight they call **Guora**, that is, a Horse. The Tower—or what we call the Elephant [[notes: The Rook, which in many medieval sets was depicted as an elephant carrying a tower]]—they call **Rochba**, that is, a Tiger. The Pawn they call **Piada**, that is, a man who fights on foot. **That is, in brief:**
,, Among the Persians and Mauritanians, the game of Chess is quite common and customary. It is played by them, however, in a different way than by us.

Likewise, **Peter Martyr d'Anghiera** [[notes: An Italian-born historian of Spain and its discoveries in the Americas]], the Milanese, writes in his booklet *On the Recently Discovered Islands and the Customs of Their Inhabitants*, page 360, also concerning the Americans [[notes: Referring to the indigenous peoples of the Americas during the early Spanish exploration]].

,, Various cotton blankets, woven with white, black, and yellow colors, two rich with gold and gems, and three others woven with feathers and cotton in the manner of a chessboard: which is evidence that they also have the use of chessboards. **That is:**
,, These peoples brought and showed us various blankets made from a special kind of cotton, woven with white, black, and yellow colors: two adorned with gold and precious stones: but three others on which the chess-fields [[notes: The squares of a chessboard]] were distinguished with feathers and similar cotton. Which then gives an indication that they also make use of the chess-table.
