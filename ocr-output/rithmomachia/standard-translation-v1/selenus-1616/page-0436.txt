## The Fifth Chapter.

### In what manner the White player (according to both Damiano's [[notes: Pedro Damiano was a Portuguese chess master whose 1512 manual was a primary source for this book]] and the Author's opinion), when he has set aside his King’s Archer’s Soldier [[notes: The King's Bishop's Pawn]] from his other chess pieces, and has likewise set aside the Black player’s Queen’s Rider [[notes: The Queen's Knight]], and gives the Black player the advantage [[notes: The "advantage" or "Vorzug" here refers to the first move or "odds"]], can begin and complete the game in three ways.

But if the White player does not take the advantage in moves, but rather a major piece—namely the Queen’s or the King’s Rider—as an advantage, and in return grants another advantage, the three final types of play emerge from this. For when the White player takes the Black King’s Rider and gives the Black player the advantage and the King’s Archer’s Soldier [[notes: King's Bishop's Pawn]] at the start: one arrives at the Fourth Type, which Damiano explains in three ways in the 6th and 7th Chapters of his little chess-book, and which is set down and improved in this chapter in the following manner.
