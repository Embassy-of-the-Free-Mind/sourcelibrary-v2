# The First Book / Chapter 1.

> „ [That] he [shall] / capture many enemies: Otherwise the opposite
> „ happens: However / this conflict / will not affect / the main
> „ matter.
> „ If a King / or a powerful man / or a military commander / 
> „ dreams / how He has lost his Chess-Game / or how it was 
> „ broken / then He will / lose his army / and fall into 
> „ great misfortune.

Even though Artemidorus Daldianus [[notes: A professional diviner and author of the *Oneirocritica* (The Interpretation of Dreams) from the 2nd century AD]], *Graecus Philosophus, lib: 3. c. 56. de Somnior: Interpret:*, departs very far from this interpretation—perhaps because He focused too much on the unlawful gambling of some—we nonetheless wish to set down his dream-visions here (as they were translated into Latin by Janus Cornarius [[notes: A German humanist and physician (1500–1558) known for his Latin translations of Greek medical and philosophical texts]], Physician):

> „ *Calculis sivè Latrunculis ludere sibi videri eum, qui non scit,*
> „ *multam utilitatem significat, ex deceptione & mendacio, propter-*
> „ *eà, quod multos calculos furetur, eosq; aliàs aliter ostendat, non*
> „ *juxtà aliquem simplicem modum, sed malitiosè. Si verò quis, ali-*
> „ *um, Calculis ludentem videat, eum deceptum ac defraudatum de-*
> „ *trimentum, ac damnum passurum esse, significat:* [[notes: This Latin passage describes how dreaming of playing *Latrunculi*—an ancient Roman board game often compared to chess—signifies different outcomes depending on whether the dreamer is the player or the observer.]] **That is:**
> „ **When someone / who does not understand the Chess-Game /**
> „ **dreams / how He moves the Chess-pieces / that signifies to him /**
> „ **a great benefit / and advantage / gained through deceit and lies: because**
> „ **He / steals many pieces / and shows them / in all sorts of ways /**
> „ **not in a simple or honest manner / but quite maliciously.**
> „ **But when someone / sees another / playing with the Chess-pieces /**
> „ **then such indicates / that He will be deceived / and**
> „ **bypassed / and must also suffer / harm and disadvantage.**

Because this game is an important, high, heroic, and knightly game, and contains nothing common or vulgar within it: therefore (4.) this art properly belongs not to common people, but to those who sit in government, to those experienced in war, to the learned, and to the studying youth; but especially to those who have now outgrown their *disciplinis* [[notes: Formal education or basic schooling]] and are otherwise unburdened by the weight of common utility [[notes: Manual labor or trade business]], being provided with temporal goods for their honest pastime and refreshment. Yet, by this, no one who carries a desire and love for this game shall be forbidden anything.
