-># On the Game of Chess.<-

[[margin: Chapter 5:]] ...and thirty-one: and twenty-seven: and gives **Checkmate** from square ten.

If **Black** wishes to obtain the **White Queen** and her **Rook** with his **Knights** [[notes: *Reuter*; literally "Riders," the standard term for Knights in this period]] in the seventh move: he plays the **Queen’s Knight** to forty-eight: then to thirty-one: the **King’s Knight** to forty-one: thirty-five: and twenty-nine: he then takes the **White Queen’s Bishop’s Soldier** [[notes: *Schutzens-Soldat*; the Bishop was often called the Archer (*Schütze*), so this is the Bishop's Pawn]] from the fourteenth square with one **Knight** and declares **Check**: **White** takes him with the **Queen**: he takes the same with the other **Knight**, giving **Check** once more, and also wins the **Rook**. [[notes: This describes a complex series of Knight forks common in early modern chess theory, where squares are numbered 1 through 64 rather than using modern coordinate notation like "a1" or "h8."]]

(5.) That (to avoid all sorts of discord which might arise among players for that reason) if one commits himself to give **Checkmate** to another on a specific square, that square from which the **Checkmate** is to be delivered to the **King**, as well as the square in which the **King** shall then stand, should be marked. For, unless these two squares are clearly named and noted, a disagreement and misunderstanding (as if one had understood different squares and not the same ones) could easily arise and grow between them. And therefore, he who has committed to give **Checkmate** is bound to perform the same, according to the custom of the game, from and in the marked squares. For otherwise, if he announces **Checkmate** from another square—even if the **King** is standing in the marked one—or if he announces it from the agreed-upon square but the **King** is not in the designated spot, he does not win the game. [[notes: In 17th-century chess, it was common for masters to set "problems" or wagers where they promised to mate the opponent on a specific square or with a specific pawn.]]

(6.) That he who commits himself to give **Checkmate** with a **Soldier** [[notes: *Soldat*; a Pawn]] or another piece is obliged to perform it with that same piece, and may not, for example, give **Check** with the **Soldier** and **Checkmate** with another piece. However, if he still has two **Soldiers**, he may give **Check** with one and **Checkmate** with the other on the following move; for otherwise, the commitment would not be fulfilled.
