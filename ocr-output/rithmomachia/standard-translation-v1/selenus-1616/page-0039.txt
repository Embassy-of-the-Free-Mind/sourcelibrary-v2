# Of the Game of Chess.

[[margin: Chapter 1]]
(Around 1150 A.D. [[notes: The author is likely referring to a medieval source of the legend of Sissa, the mythical inventor of chess.]]) of a man / who, taking occasion from the numbers of the squares / of the Chess-Board / to mightily **outwit** / a King and soon enrich himself: we wish to mention in passing: His words / found in a place / where He treats / *de multiplicatâ analogiâ, quæ citò crescit* [[notes: Latin: "of multiplied proportion, which grows quickly"]]: are to be found / thus:

> "Just as it is said, of a certain man, who asked, from a certain "
> "King, that he should provide for him, as his reward, grains of wheat, in "
> "a number multiplied by the number of the squares of the Chess Game. "
> "And the King refused this, after it became clear that from this "
> "there resulted a reward too great."

That (4.) by this game / the state of common Civic / and Courtly Life / is quite cleverly / depicted. For / if one wished to consider the friendship / which is commonly / practiced in the same / only for the sake of profit / then one has / in the Chess-pieces / a fine example: as <term>Petronius Arbiter</term> [[notes: A Roman courtier during the time of Nero, traditionally identified as the author of the *Satyricon*]], page 70, gives to be understood / in the following verses:

> "The name of friendship remains as long as it is expedient, "
> "The counter, on the board, leads the noble (alt: mobile) work."

That is / as <term>Turnebus</term> [[notes: Adrien Turnèbe (1512–1565), a renowned French classical scholar and humanist]], *Adversaria lib: 1. cap: 15.* explains it:

> "So far as we wish to highly esteem / and hold friendship / "
> "only for the sake of utility / and not rather for the sake of "
> "Virtue and Honor / then / Human Life / is not unevenly "
> "compared / to the Chess Game. For / in the same / "
> "the pieces are divided into two heaps / in such a way / "
> "that some among them are held / as friends / some as enemies / "
