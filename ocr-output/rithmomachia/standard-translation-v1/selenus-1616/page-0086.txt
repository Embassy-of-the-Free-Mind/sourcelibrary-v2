### The First Book / Chapter 5.

>> we see this / among the animals / and birds / that the fathers [[notes: The original text uses "Vetter" (kinsmen/cousins), but the context of parentage and the following lines suggest "fathers" or "male parents."]] / with the mothers / raise the children: these same children / stay with one another / so that they / do not bond with any other: / as one sees in the turtledoves [[notes: Turtledoves were a common medieval symbol of lifelong fidelity and monogamy.]]: but those who do not raise their children together / they associate / with many / and one sees that / in the heath-cocks [[notes: "haynischen Hanen" refers to the black grouse or similar wild fowl known for lekking—a mating behavior where males compete for multiple females, contrasting with the monogamous turtledove.]]. And therefore / since among all animals / for humans most of all / Father and Mother / raise, lead, and care for [them] together / it is therefore / against propriety / for the King to associate / with other women.

->**The Black King / on the Chessboard /**<-

>> stands upon the fourth field / and has / at his right hand / on a white field / a Knight / and on a black field / an Elder [[notes: "Alten" (Old Ones) refers to the pieces we now call Bishops; in the medieval period, these were often depicted as judges, sages, or counselors.]] / and a Rook: At the left side / are these same stones / on contrary fields [[notes: This describes the mirrored setup of the pieces on the board.]]. And that is because the Knights are / a King's honor / and his crown / and he sits with them / at the left hand / on a square identical to the Queen's / the same is done by the Elder / at the left side / and also other stones / on contrary fields / with the Queen: so that he / and the Queen / are protected on both sides. For when Judges and Counselors / are faithfully by the King / that is what his right hand means / and these same [people] protect the Queen / in honor and in wisdom / at the left / so will the whole Realm / be all the better governed / and when it happens / that each person considers / his own particular profit and benefit / and does not heed / the common good / and the honor of the Realm / then the Realm loses / its ornament [[notes: "Zier" here implies the beauty, dignity, and proper order of a state.]]. Since the King is before them / in lordship / and in principality / he should therefore not wander too far / out of his Realm / which signifies / that the King / does not move further / than to the third field / when he leaves his place [[notes: In medieval chess, the King was allowed a one-time "leap" of two or three squares on his first move, a rule that eventually evolved into modern castling.]]: But when he / has moved from his place / then he moves only / to the next field / like a Pawn [[notes: The text uses "Bend," an archaic term for "Bauer" or pawn.]].
