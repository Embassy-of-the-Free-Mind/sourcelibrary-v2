# Of the Number-Battle.

6.) When a stone—whether from one’s own or the opponent’s side—is present between a piece and the space into which it could otherwise be moved according to the rules, then it [[notes: This refers to the "blocking" rule. Unlike Chess knights, stones in Rhythmomachia generally cannot jump over occupied spaces.]] cannot be moved there.

7.) The **Towers** [[notes: These are the "Pyramids," the most powerful pieces in the game. They are composite stacks of several stones of different shapes.]] are moved in as many ways, and likewise capture other stones or are captured by them, as there are **Square**, **Triangular**, and **Round** stones present within them. Furthermore, they are endowed with this freedom: that they may exchange and recover stones which are meant to be taken from them, using other stones from their own side or those they have won from the enemy, provided those stones bear the same numbers as are marked on the stone they were about to lose. [[notes: This "exchange" rule allows a player to sacrifice a captured stone from their reserve to keep their Pyramid whole.]] If, however, no stone with such a number is to be found among them, it remains at the victor's discretion to take away whichever stone he wishes. But when the bottom-most layer, or the **base-stone**—and thus the sum of all sums of the layers, namely **91** and **190**—is to be taken, one cannot exchange it, nor can it be placed back upon the board a second time; rather, it must be removed from the game entirely. [[notes: In the game, the White Pyramid totals 91 (the sum of squares from 1 to 6) and the Black Pyramid totals 190 (the sum of squares 4 through 8, plus some additional values). Losing the base means the Pyramid is "slain."]] This is also how other layers are removed when one cannot provide another stone in their place.
