# The Second Book / Chapter 13:

[[margin: 16.]] But if **the Black** / does not [move] the Soldier [[notes: "Soldier" (Soldat) refers to a Pawn.]] / to 34. (n. 15.) but / [moves] the King’s Rider [[notes: "Rider" (Reuter) refers to the Knight.]] / against the White King’s Soldier / to forty-three: then **the White** plays / the King’s Rider / against the Black Queen / to nineteen: **the Black** / [moves] the same / to thirty-three: **the White** / the Queen / (to protect his King’s Soldier) to twelve: **the Black** / the King’s Rider-Soldier [[notes: The King's Knight's Pawn.]] / to thirty-four: [[margin: 17.]] * **the White** / must not move the King’s Rider / (even though it seems a good move) to thirty-six: For when that happens / at the prompting of the Black / the Queens / will be exchanged. If **the White** then / takes the Black Queen / with his King: **the Black** plays / the King’s Soldier / against the White King’s Rider / to forty-five: **the White** / robs with the same / the Black King’s Archer-Soldier [[notes: "Archer" (Schütze) refers to the Bishop; thus, the King's Bishop's Pawn.]]: **the Black** / moves the aforementioned / King’s Soldier / against the White King’s Archer / to thirty-seven / and wins a piece / to his advantage: For / if **the White** / slays the Black King’s Rook [[notes: "Rook" (Rochen) refers to the Castle.]] / with his King’s Rider: He / [meaning Black] takes the mentioned White Archer / with the King’s Soldier / and obtains the White Rider / also without hindrance.

[[margin: 18.]] It is therefore better / that **the White** / does not move his Rider / (in the manner set above / n. 17.) to 36. but instead in its place / moves the King’s Soldier / to twenty-nine: **the Black** / advances / the King’s Soldier / to forty-five: **the White** / [moves] the King’s Soldier / against the Black King’s Rider / to thirty-six: **the Black** / takes the same / with his King’s Soldier: **the White** / [takes] the same / with his Queen / and offers **Check**: **the Black** / (so as / not to lose the King’s Rider) covers the King / with his Archer / to fifty-two: **the White** / takes / the Black King’s Archer-Soldier / with his Queen / moves the same / to their Archer and Rider-Soldiers / and has also arranged / his game in such a way / that He will ultimately / well maintain the victory / and carry it away.

[[margin: 19.]] If however **the Black** / according to the third manner / moves neither the Rider / to 43. (n. 1.) nor the Queen / to 25. (n. 8.) but rather the King’s Archer-Soldier / to forty-six: then **the White** advances / (so that / the King’s Archer / can take the Black King’s Soldier) the King’s Soldier / to twenty-nine: **the Black** / [moves] his King’s Soldier / against the White King’s Archer / to thirty-seven: **the White** / takes it / with the King’s Soldier: **the Black** / [takes] the same / with the King’s Archer-Soldier / and aims again /
