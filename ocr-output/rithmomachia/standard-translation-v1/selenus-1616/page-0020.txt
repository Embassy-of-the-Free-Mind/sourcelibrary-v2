He notes these things, and immediately runs shouting through the world,
That in the Game of Chess, you have no equal,
And that in this game, you surpass the great Lysander [[notes: Lysander (d. 395 BC) was a famous Spartan general and admiral; the comparison suggests the author’s tactical mind is superior to that of ancient military heroes.]],
Who, conquering everyone, is hailed as victor everywhere.

## CHESS DUEL.

->Taken from an Ode by *TUCCIO*, by the Excellent Sig:<-
->**Bartolomeo Burchelato.**<- [[notes: Burchelato (1548–1632) was a prominent Italian physician and scholar from Treviso, known for his historical and poetic works.]]

->To the Illustrious Sig: **VANNI Bombene.**<-

<image-desc>Decorative woodcut initial letter 'B' entwined with foliage and featuring a small human figure.</image-desc>
Illustrious Bombene, I have a great desire that we
Enter the field for a daring battle
With Pawns, Knights, Princes, and Heroes.
Here is a people made of boxwood [[notes: Boxwood (*bosso*) was the preferred material for carving high-quality chess pieces due to its density and fine grain.]], heavy and swift,
Which courageously awaits the moves, ready
To go forth once the trumpet is heard.
And although you seek to do me insult and shame
With the black Knights and the dark Pawns,
Take whichever you wish, it matters not to me.
I, who am "The Severe One" among the **CONSPIRATORS** [[notes: *Il Severo* was likely Burchelato’s academic name within a literary society, the *Accademia dei Cospiranti* (Academy of the Conspirators).]],
If I give you, as I wish, a fierce assault,
I want to bring with me both prey, and prize, and pride.
And who will have the daring to make either a step or a jump
Against my firm and well-furnished Towers [[notes: The "Tower" is the traditional name for the Rook.]],
That they do not fall like enamel from gold [[notes: A metaphor for devastating loss; just as enamel chips away from a precious gold object, the opponent's pieces will be stripped away.]].
My King will come to end such strife,
With his intrepid and faithful Consort [[notes: In this period, the Queen was often referred to as the King's "Consort" (*Consorte*) or "Lady," reflecting the royal court metaphor.]],
