# Cartel
## Of the Game of Chess
### at the
## Imperial Court.

->1.<-
Every *Pedina* [[notes: From the Italian *pedina*, meaning pawn. In this period, German chess terminology was heavily influenced by Italian masters like Greco and Salvio.]] or **Pawn** may, in its first move, "
leap forward unto the third field. "

->2.<-
However, should a *Pedina* from the opposing side stand "
immediately next to the same third field, this first pawn "
must make a stop at the first [[notes: This describes the *en passant* rule. If a pawn jumps two squares past an enemy pawn, the opponent can capture it as if it had only moved one square.]], and may not move before him except for one field. "
Yet if he wishes to move forward, the opponent has the right "
to cast him out and place his own *Pedina* in that spot, "
just as if the first had only moved forward one field, and the "
opponent had taken him there. "

->3.<-
As long as the King has received no Check, he may "
exchange with the **Rook** [[notes: *Rochen* is the archaic term for the Rook, derived from the Persian *rukh*.]], as far as he wishes with both pieces, "
provided the fields between them are empty. "

->4.<-
Once the King has received a Check, he "
may never exchange [[notes: "Exchange" refers here to the move of Castling. In some historical variations, the ability to castle was lost permanently once the King was put in check.]] again. "

->5.<-
As soon as a *Pedina* reaches the last field of his opponent, "
he becomes a full **Queen**, and can, in that same move, "
immediately offer Check with it. "

->6.<-
As many *Pedinae* as one brings to the end, so many "
Queens does he have; and it is thus permitted to make "
three or four Queens together. "

->7.<-
If the King is robbed of all his pieces [[notes: This refers to the "Bare King" rule. In some historical rulesets, taking all of an opponent's pieces except the King resulted in a different grade of victory or a specific type of draw.]], "
it is not a Checkmate, but rather a ***Robada***. "
