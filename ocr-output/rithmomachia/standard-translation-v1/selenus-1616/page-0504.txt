# The Third Chapter

[[notes: This section provides the layout for Rythmomachia, also known as the "Battle of Numbers." Played on a double-sized chessboard (8x12 squares), it was a staple of medieval and Renaissance education used to teach Boethian arithmetic, which categorized numbers by their mathematical proportions.]]

->**White**<-

Four White Round Stones: (namely: those numbered 2, paired with 4; those with 4, with 16; those with 6, with 36; those with 8, with 64) [[notes: These pieces represent the "Multiplex" class, specifically the squares of even numbers ($n$ and $n^2$).]] Position and set these eight triangular and eight square stones in order, just as they were defined in the preceding second chapter and as clearly illustrated in the diagram.

In the same manner, one must proceed with the placement of the Black Stones, with the only difference being that the squares are counted from the top downward and toward the right hand. The stones, however, are to be situated on the left side, which constitutes the right side from the perspective of the opponent. [[notes: This describes the rotational symmetry of the board setup, ensuring both players see a mirrored version of their own ranks, much like the setup in modern Chess.]]

The Towers [[notes: Also known as "Pyramids," these are the most complex pieces in the game. They are "composite" pieces whose value is the sum of several layers of geometric shapes. For example, the pyramid numbered 91 mentioned below is a "perfect" piece because it is the sum of the first six squares: $1+4+9+16+25+36=91$.]]

### Diagram of the Board Layout (The "Even" Side)

[[notes: The following table recreates the arrangement of the pieces as shown in the engraving. The pieces are arranged in four columns on the player's side of the 8x12 grid.]]

| Column Position | Pieces and Numerical Values |
| :--- | :--- |
| **First Column (Outer)** | Four square blocks: 25, 81, 169, and 289. |
| **Second Column** | Two squares (15, 45), four triangles (25, 20, 42, 49), one tiered pyramid (91), and one square (153). |
| **Third Column** | Two triangles (9, 6), four circles (4, 16, 36, 64), and two triangles (72, 81). |
| **Fourth Column (Inner)** | Four circles positioned in the central rows: 2, 4, 6, and 8. |

[[notes: The engraving uses detailed hatching to show the three-dimensional nature of the pieces—circles are cylinders, triangles are prisms, and squares are cubes. This visual distinction helped players track the different mathematical "classes" of numbers during play.]]
