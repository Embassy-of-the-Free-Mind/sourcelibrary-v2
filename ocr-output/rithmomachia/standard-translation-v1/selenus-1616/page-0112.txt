### The First Book / 9th Chapter:

» [to] receive / and that he may have help from him / for he should / be his guar-
» dian / and of whom he has need: Whoever wishes to be a Knight /
» must necessarily / possess many qualities. First, he should 
» have wisdom: for / before the Knight / girds the knightly belt /
» around himself / he should have been / a squire-at-arms [[notes: *Knap von wapen* (Squire-at-arms) refers to the traditional period of apprenticeship before a man was knighted.]] /
» so that he has practiced and worked in arms: for 
» in battle, experienced wisdom and skill / are more useful than 
» strength / and an unwise man's boldness: for it often happens / that 
» one is defeated in battle / when one attacks unwisely / out of 
» foolish boldness. And therefore a prince should not have / 
» a Master of Knights / who is young / and inexperienced. 
» The Knight should also be steadfast and faithful: for steadfastness 
» illuminates knightly customs / like a translucent pearl / 
» among precious stones. And therefore / a Knight / who is unfaithful 
» to his Lord / loses / by right / the name of Knight. 
» The Knight should also / not only be faithful to his Lord / 
» he should also be faithful / to his knightly companions: 
» For nothing terrifies enemies so greatly / as unanimity / 
» or unity / among a company / and that it remains / 
» unbroken and steady among them: that creates victory / and 
» knightly praise. But where the spirit of companions is divided / 
» they become victory-less / and are defeated / in all good causes. 
» Every man should / love his companion / so much / that it 
» seems to him / that whatever honor happens to the other / 
» that is his own honor too / and that he gladly suffers / 
» all peril [[notes: *Freyß* is an archaic term for mortal danger or peril.]] with him. Furthermore, the Knight 
» should be liberal [[notes: *mild* here means "generous" or "liberal," a core aristocratic virtue.]] / so that he spares nothing in terms of arms 
» for his knightly companions / or associates: thus whatever he 
» wins / that should be held in common. For it happens often and 
» frequently / when a knight greedily [[notes: *geitiglich* comes from *Geiz* (greed/avarice).]] keeps his gain / 
» and sees his companions in need / he turns his back / and lets him be 
» struck / thus his greed strikes him down. It often happens / what 
» nature denies / that goodness gives: And therefore a 
» Lord should take care / when he wishes to wage war / that he 
» does not / at that same time / diminish or take away from his people 
» their property and goods: for the share / of the plunder should be common / 
» just as the victory is: (see 1 Samuel 30:21 to the end; Joshua 22:8) [[notes: These biblical passages refer to the equitable distribution of spoils of war among those who fought and those who stayed behind to guard the baggage.]] 
» A Knight should also be strong / not only in body / but—
