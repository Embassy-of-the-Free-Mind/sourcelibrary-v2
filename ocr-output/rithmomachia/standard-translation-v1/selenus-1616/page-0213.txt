## The Ninth Chapter.

### Concerning the Second and Third Way to proceed further with a game.

[[notes: The decorative drop-cap 'D' begins the instructional text.]]

The **Second Way** is: when the **Black** player, according to the previous arrangement made in the Eighth Chapter, varies his moves in the third turn; and instead of moving the **Queen** to 45, he moves her **Archer’s Pawn** [[notes: "Schützens-Soldat" translates to Archer's Soldier. In early German chess, the Bishop was often called the "Schütze" (Archer), and the "Soldat" is the Pawn.]] to forty-six: if the **White** player now moves his **King’s Archer’s Pawn** to twenty-two in the fourth turn, then the **Black** player makes an incursion with the **King’s Pawn** from thirty-seven. To prevent this, the **White** player—if he wishes to take some care of his game in the beginning—should move not the **King’s Archer's**, but specifically the **King’s Archer’s Pawn** to twenty-seven and attack the **Black** player’s quarter. From this arises the **Third Way** to carry out the game further, specifically as follows:

[[margin: 1.]] After the **White** player has moved the **King’s Pawn** to 28; the **Black** player, the **King’s Pawn** to 36; the **White** player, the **King’s Archer** [[notes: The Bishop]] to 30; the **Black** player, the **King’s Archer** to 38; the **White** player, the **Queen** to 12; and the **Black** player moves not the **King’s Pawn** to 45 (as was set down in the third turn of the 8th Chapter) but rather their **Archer’s Pawn** to forty-six: the **White** player also did not move the **King’s Archer’s Pawn** to 22 (as mentioned at the start of this Chapter regarding the **Second Way**), but instead made the attack with the **King’s Archer’s Pawn** from twenty-seven:

[[margin: 2. *]] then the **Black** player takes the same with his **King’s Pawn**. The **White** player moves the **King’s Knight** [[notes: "Reuter" or "Reiter" (Rider) refers to the Knight.]] to nineteen (in order to keep an eye on the twenty-fifth square). The **Black** player moves the **King’s Archer** to forty-seven (as a precaution). The **White** player moves the **King’s Pawn** (so that their **Archer** may subsequently take the **Black King’s Pawn**) to twenty-nine. The **Black** player (to defend him) moves the **King’s Knight’s Pawn** to thirty-four. The **White** player (to disrupt the opponent's plots) moves the **King’s Rook’s Pawn**...
