## The First Book /
# Of the Description / of the
# Game of Chess.
### The First Chapter.
#### Of the Nature, Kind, and Properties /
##### of the Chess-Art.

[[margin: 1. cap.]]
There is nothing in this world that is to be undertaken with counsel and understanding which must not necessarily be classified as an **Art**. [[notes: In the 17th century, "Art" (Kunst) referred to a systematic branch of knowledge or a technical skill governed by rules, similar to how we might describe a "science" or "discipline" today.]] For our weakened nature [[notes: A common philosophical reference to the "post-fall" state of humanity, suggesting that humans are no longer naturally perfect and require structured systems to achieve excellence.]] is unable to conduct itself as the necessity of a matter requires without certain order, laws, rules, measure, or methods. Therefore, over time, the arts were invented by the wisest and most excellent men, and were further brought to perfection by their descendants: a fact which all **Philosophy** and the various arts flowing from it sufficiently prove.

Accordingly, we shall make a good beginning to our undertaking if we likewise posit that the **Game of Chess** is an art: since the proof will, it is hoped, be provided by the work itself. For this will be revealed and handed to us, first by the nature and property of this game, and subsequently by the measure and manner in which one may properly grasp, teach, and learn it.

Regarding the first: **Aristotle** says, in the introduction to his *Metaphysics*, that all arts are medicines for human needs and frailties.
