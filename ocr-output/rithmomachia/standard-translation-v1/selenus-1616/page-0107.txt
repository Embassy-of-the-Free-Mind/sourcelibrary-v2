## 2nd Chapter: Of the Game of Chess.

[[meta: catchword: The]]

[...neither] undertake nor do [anything]: unless the **Judge’s** counsel be present; and for this reason, it is necessary that they be perfect in their art [[notes: *Kunst*—referring here to the professional skill, wisdom, and legal expertise required of a judge]] and in their morals: and this is demonstrated by the gait and the movement they have when they go from the **Knights** [[notes: *Rittern*—the Knight pieces]] onto the third field [[notes: In medieval chess, the Bishop (or *Alfin*) moved by leaping exactly two squares diagonally. If one counts the starting square as the first, the landing square is the "third field."]]; for since six is the first perfect number [[notes: In medieval numerology, a "perfect number" is a positive integer that is equal to the sum of its proper divisors. As 1 + 2 + 3 = 6, it was considered a symbol of harmony and completeness.]], they complete their gait and movement perfectly: 
-> it is because <-
-> the end joins itself to the <-
-> beginning. &c. <-
