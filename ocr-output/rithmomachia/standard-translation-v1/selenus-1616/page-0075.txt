## The Third Chapter.
### On the Division of this Art.

What is necessary to know in general regarding this Art of Chess—its nature and essence, its origin and name—has been sufficiently detailed in the previous chapters.

However, as to how one may master it, and in what way and manner it may be orderly presented and learned—though it may seem difficult—the Art itself will guide us, provided we simply remember from the first chapter that it belongs to the class of those things called *Artes Operatrices* [[notes: Latin for "practical" or "operative" arts—disciplines focused on action and the creation of a finished work rather than pure theory]].

For regarding these, **Averroës** [[notes: Ibn Rushd (1126–1198), a medieval Andalusian polymath; his *Colliget* was a major medical and philosophical encyclopedia influential in Europe]] writes as follows in Book 1, Chapter 1 of the *Colliget*:

> *Artes Practicæ, in quantùm sunt Artes, continent tria: cc*
> *Primum est, scire Loca suorum Subjectorum; Secundum est, scire cc*
> *Finem quæsitum, ad adducendum ipsum, in Locum subjecti; Ter- cc*
> *tium est, scire Instrumenta, cum quibus valeamus ducere, illum cc*
> *Finem, in Locum subjecti.* That is: cc
> The arts which are directed toward a trade or a work, cc
> insofar as they are actually arts, comprise three things cc
> which should be well-noted: First, that one cc
> knows and learns those things [[notes: The "subjects" or materials, such as the board and pieces]] which are subject to an art, cc
> as much as may be necessary for its purpose. Sec- cc
> ondly, that one recognizes the **Goal** [[notes: The "Finis" or end objective, in this case, checkmate]] which this art has set for it- cc
> self, and through which the use of those things cc
> is modeled. Finally, that one learns to understand cc
> the **Means** and ways [[notes: The "Instrumenta," such as the moves and rules of the game]] with and through which one can cc
> apply the indicated Goal to those same things, cc
> and thus achieve what one has set out to do. ec

The first two belong, so to speak, to *knowledge*; the last—when, alongside the Means, the *Usage* and the *Practical Maneuvers* are also demonstrated—pertains especially to the *work* itself.
