And this should not seem so very strange to anyone. For, we shall omit mentioning how the *Spaniards* (descended from the *Goths*) and the *Gauls* or *French* (descended from the German *Franks*) took their customs and language in part from them and mixed them with their own (on which *Nicod: Frischlini, Julius Redivivus, page 308* [[notes: Nicodemus Frischlin (1547–1590) was a famous German philologist and playwright; *Julius Redivivus* is his comedy comparing ancient Romans and modern Germans]], along with the *Historians*, may be consulted). [Instead, consider] how many German words the *Italians* or *Welsch* [[notes: "Welsch" was an archaic German term used to describe Italians or French speakers]] learned from the Germans, *Goths*, and *Lombards*, by whom *Italy* was long possessed (as the aforementioned *Frischl: in the cited place, page 331*, along with others, mentions) and incorporated into their language? Do not their *Jurists* [[notes: lawyers or legal scholars]] use the words *Feudi* [[notes: Fiefs]], *Fodri* [[notes: A medieval tax for the maintenance of the sovereign’s court]], and countless others, which the learned know to be German? How they *Germanize* [[notes: adopt German-style grammar]] so much in these and similar phrases: *Io ho havato* [I have had], *Io sono stato* [I have been]? Is not their *lasciare* [to let/leave] our German *lassen*? Should they not, in the same manner, have taken their *scacciare*—that is, to hunt up or drive away—from our Old German word *Schacken* [[notes: The author is attempting to link the Italian word for "to drive away" (*scacciare*) to an old German root to prove that the name of the game (*Schach*) is German]] or "Weg-jagen" [to hunt away]?
