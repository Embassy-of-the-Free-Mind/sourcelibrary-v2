## The Fourth Chapter.
### A thorough improvement of the game which was set forth in the previous third chapter, according to the report of Damiano.

After the fourth move of the Black side was carried out in the preceding chapter (wherein he first blocked the check from the White Queen with the **Pawn** on 42, as noted in Chapter 3, No. 2; and subsequently, in its place, moved the **King** out of check to 52, as noted in Chapter 3, No. 18) according to the instructions of Damiano—and the game was played forward, though not without the necessary reminders—this chapter shall now take up the other variation. This occurs when the Black **King**, in the fourth move, is moved out of check to 52 (as in No. 18). This is done so that the two errors which Damiano committed during Black’s sixth move (to be found below at No. 2 and 7) may appear all the more clearly, and so that the moves on White’s side may be fundamentally improved.

[[margin: 1.]] When, therefore, the White moves the **King’s Pawn** [[notes: Selenus calls the pawn a *Soldat* or "Soldier"]] to 28; the Black moves his **King’s Pawn** to 36; the White moves the **King’s Knight** [[notes: *Reuter*, literally "Rider"]] to 19 against the same; the Black protects it with the **King’s Bishop’s Pawn** [[notes: *Schützen Soldat*, or "Archer's Soldier"]] from 43; the White takes the Black **King’s Pawn** with the **King’s Knight**; the Black takes the same with the **King’s Bishop’s Pawn**; and the White gives check with the **Queen** from 33: then the Black moves the **King** (No. 18) to 52. The White takes the Black **King’s Bishop’s Pawn** with the **Queen** and offers check once more. The Black must move the **King** to 51. The White offers check with the **King’s Bishop** [[notes: *Schütze*, or "Archer"]] from 30.

[[margin: 2. *]] The best move which the Black can use against this is to move the **King’s Pawn** to 37 and cover the **King** with it.

Damiano now posits the above in the previous chapter (No. 18), and he is indeed correct thus far. However, it can be seen from his report that he did not understand the reason why the game is better played in this manner. For, if one were to play it forward as he indicates, it would be the worst and most dangerous game for the Black side that could be devised on the chessboard (as can be seen from what follows).
