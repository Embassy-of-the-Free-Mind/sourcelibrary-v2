## Das Ander Buch / 1. Cap:

But regarding Kings, the word **Schach** [[notes: Check]] must be used—not, as many mistakenly believe, merely to warn them to look after their **Schantze** [[notes: This word can mean 'fortification' or 'chance/position']]—but rather much more to indicate what they must now do because of the power of the oncoming Enemy. And this is also done for this reason: so that the King might immediately be moved to another square. For otherwise, if it were not announced to him, the opponent (considering that the King, unlike other pieces, cannot be taken here) would not do so for the sake of his advantage; instead, he would act as if he had not noticed or perceived it, and would therefore move another piece to his benefit. Now, as it befits a diligent player in this case to forbid the opponent—according to the rules of the game—from moving any other piece before he has first moved the King out of danger: So, the subtle inventors of this game have arranged it so that he who attacks the King with one of his pieces shall use the word **Schach** at the same time as that move. This is to make it understood that he not only knows quite well how to attack the other pieces, lying in wait for them to seize them, but also understands how the King can be reached—since he is driven and not seized [[notes: Meaning 'slain' or 'removed from the board' in this context]], but now taken prisoner (to indicate that one prefers to take high-ranking heads prisoner for the sake of a large *rantzioms geldes* [[notes: Ransom money; in medieval and early modern warfare, capturing a noble for ransom was a standard and lucrative practice]] rather than slaying them). Therefore, he publicly announces to the opponent that he should move the King from the square where he no longer wishes him to be, straight to another, and to do so immediately—unless he can still move a piece in front of him, or can take the one offering him this threat—and put this into effect: for he intends to press upon that same square with all his might until he surrenders.

Others believe that by offering this **Schach**, it is signified that no lawful war is being waged unless a renunciation or declaration of the same has gone before: otherwise, in such a case, it should be regarded as a murderous undertaking [[notes: This reflects the "Just War" theory of the era, where a formal declaration differentiated legitimate combat from mere banditry or assassination]].

In like manner, these words, **Schach, Mat**, are to be spoken at the end of the game, for which they were undoubtedly ordained: So that (1.) the Victor indicates that he holds it for certain that he has achieved the Victory; otherwise, he might make his move in such a way that he had indeed won, but was not aware of it himself:
