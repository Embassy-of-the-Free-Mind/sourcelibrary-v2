# The Second Book / 5. Chapter:

...is bound in that case [[notes: *fahl*; case or instance]] to deliver **checkmate** with that same one, as long as it is and remains a **soldier** [[notes: *Soldat*; the standard term for a pawn in this period]], and remains so; but not with any other of the seven remaining [pawns]. But if he makes the same one into a **Queen**, and its standing, power, and nature are thus transformed, then he does not fulfill his promise and obligation. However, if he says, "I wish to give **checkmate** with this **chess-piece**," designates it, and does not mention its nature and properties: then it is equally valid whether he gives **checkmate** with it while it is still a **soldier**, or when it has already become a **Queen**. For in both cases, he satisfies his promise. Otherwise, some wish to maintain this distinction: that he who promised to give **checkmate** with a **soldier** could have delivered **checkmate** with a simple [[notes: *schlechten*; in this context meaning "plain" or "basic" rather than "bad"]] **soldier**, or only with one that has been elevated to a **Queen**. In the first case, he has fulfilled his promise if he perhaps, out of courtesy, made it a **Queen** and gives **checkmate** with it: especially if he said so beforehand, and in an emergency, could still give **checkmate** with the remaining **soldiers**. In the latter case, he has lost and not kept his word, especially if he did not stipulate it beforehand, nor has other **soldiers** in reserve with which he could still do it. Others maintain that when one names this or 그 [[notes: *nahmkündig*; making a specific pawn known by name or designation]], one can indeed give **checkmate** with the same, even if it has become a **Queen**; considering that although it takes on other qualities and properties, none of the other pieces are used, but rather its standing is only elevated and improved, not changed, *l. pen: ff: de Senator:* [[notes: This is a legal citation to the Roman Digest (the *Pandects*), specifically the section regarding the status of Senators, used here to argue that a change in rank does not change the identity of the person (or piece).]]

**Antonius de Prato**, of Trent, (who calls himself, *inter Cæcos Scacchi Lusores, Monoculum*, and *inter Ignorantes, Peritum* [[notes: Latin: "A one-eyed man among blind chess players, and an expert among the ignorant"]]) wishes to reconcile this debatable opinion by *distinguendo* [[notes: "distinguishing" or making a logical distinction]], in the following manner: that if someone gives **checkmate** with a **soldier** which has become a **Queen** in such a way as can be done by a simple **soldier**, but not as a **Queen** (which may well happen): then he has fulfilled his promise, and the piece has not changed its office and effect. But if he gives **checkmate** with the same piece in a way that can [only] be performed by a **Queen**, (which then commonly, and for the most part, occurs): then he has not satisfied his promise, because the **soldier** has entirely changed its move and effect,
