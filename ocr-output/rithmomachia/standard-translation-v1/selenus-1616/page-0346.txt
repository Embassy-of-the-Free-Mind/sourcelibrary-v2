### The Third Book / Chapter 5.

...to be dismayed by the falling of the enemy: So He [[note: the skilled player]] also brings the White [player] / as long as He is knowledgeable of the game / thereby / easily / into a state of disorder. Therefore / this position of the Soldiers [[note: Pawns]] / since by the same / the enemy’s intention / is broken / is not to be considered / as if the Soldiers / stand doubled in vain: As if they stood / perhaps / in other places / or in another type of game / in such a manner: Which one then / in order to remind / the beginning / still inexperienced Chess-players / of this / and to somewhat prevent / the slanders / of those who think themselves clever / wishes to mention and note here. For / what was written regarding this point / above / in the 2nd Chapter / of this Book / after no. 4 and 5 against *Damianum* [[note: Pedro Damiano (c. 1480–1544), a Portuguese chess theorist whose 1512 book was a standard reference]], was not set down without cause and reason / as can be read in that same place.

[[margin: 63.]] But if the White [player] / does not wish to exchange his Archer [[note: Bishop]] / with the King’s Rider [[note: Knight]] / (no. 62.) / but rather / (to / place his King / in a secure location / and to / inflict a greater loss and damage / upon the Black [player] in such a way) / moves the King’s Rook / [[margin: 64.]] onto three [[note: Square c1]] : * then the Black [player] plays / against the White King’s Soldier / the King’s Rider / onto forty-three [[note: Square c6]] : or with more profit / the King’s Archer / (to prevent / that the White [player] / in the event / after the King’s displacement [[note: Castling]] / might win a move to his advantage / which the same / before the said displacement / could not do / without his own harm) / onto forty-seven [[note: Square g6]] : The White [player] / lets the King / jump onto two [[note: Square b1, completing Queenside castling]] : The Black [player] / moves / the King’s Soldier / onto forty-five [[note: Square e6]] : The White [player / moves] / his King’s Soldier / against the Black King’s Soldier / onto twenty-nine [[note: Square e4]] : The Black [player / moves] / the King’s Archer / (to clear his Rider) / onto fifty-three [[note: Square e7]] / and will further be able to arrange his game well.

[[margin: 65.]] Since however it was reported above / (no. 64.) / that it is better / that the Black [player] / move the Archer / (before He / moves the Rider / against the White Soldier) / onto 47 [[note: Square g6]] : It is further to be known / that / although it pleases some better / that the move / of the Rider / because the same / is pulled onto the neck of the enemy / should happen beforehand : it is nevertheless / more beneficial for the Black [player] / that He / protects his King’s Soldier / with the King’s Soldier / before the White [player] / moves his King / immediately after the White King’s Rook’s move / to 3 [[note: Square c1]] (no. 63 at the end) / onto forty-five [[note: Square e6]].
