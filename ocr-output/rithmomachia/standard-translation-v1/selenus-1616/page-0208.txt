# The Second Book. Chapter 8.

...check [[notes: This word completes a sentence from the previous page]]: He must move the King / to fifty-eight [[notes: *acht und funfzig*; Square b8]]: **The White player** / takes the Black King’s Knight [[notes: *K. Reuter*; the Knight]] / with the King’s Bishop [[notes: *K. Schützen*; the Bishop]] / gives a discovered check [[notes: *Ab-Schach*; a move where one piece moves out of the way to reveal a check from another piece]] / from the King’s Rook / and moves against his Queen: He [moves] the King / to fifty-nine [[notes: *neun und funfzig*; Square c8]]: **The White player** / takes his Queen / with the King’s Bishop: He moves the King’s Bishop’s Soldier [[notes: *K. Schützens-Soldaten*; the Pawn in front of the Bishop]] / against the **White Queen** / (so that she / does not immediately / give his King checkmate from fifty-seven [[notes: *sieben und funfzig*; Square a8]]) to forty-three [[notes: *drey und viertzig*; Square c6]]: **The White player** / takes him / with the same [the Queen] / and offers check: He must move the King / to sixty [[notes: *sechzig*; Square d8]]: **The White player** / gives / checkmate with the Queen / from fifty-two [[notes: *zwey und funfzig*; Square d7]].

[[margin: 14.]]
But if **the Black player** / does not / move the King / to 59. (no. 13.) but / to fifty-three [[notes: *drey und funfzig*; Square e7]]: then **the White player** / takes the Black King’s Bishop’s Soldier / with his King’s Bishop / moves against the Black King’s Rook / and can very easily / bring the same / into his power: For **the Black player** / has more to look out for / that the **White Queen** / from forty-four [[notes: *vier und vierzig*; Square d6]] / does not immediately give him checkmate / than that he / should save this King’s Rook / and Knight. As long as **the White player** / understands the game / he will easily prevail over **the Black player** / in this arrangement of the pieces.

[[margin: 15.]]
But when **the Black player** / does not [place] the Rook / on fifty-eight (no. 10.) but / first places his King’s Knight (to protect the King’s Knight [[notes: Likely meaning to protect the Knight's position or the surrounding squares]]) / on fifty-three: then **the White player** / plays the King’s Rook / against the Black King’s Knight / on three [[notes: Square c1]]: **The Black player** / [moves] his King’s Rook / against the White King’s Bishop / on fifty-eight: **The White player** / (in order to / finish the Black player off [[notes: *den garauss zu machen*; a colorful German idiom for completing a destruction or ending a struggle]]) / takes the Black King’s Rook’s Soldier / with his Queen: **The Black player** / [takes] the same / with the King’s Knight / and moves him / against the White King’s Bishop: **The White player** / plunders [[notes: *raubet*; a strong term used here for capturing a piece]], with the King’s Bishop / the Black King’s Bishop’s Soldier / and moves him at the same time / against the Black King’s Rook / and offers check: **The Black player** / places the King / (since he can otherwise / move him / nowhere else) on fifty-nine: **The White player** / takes / the Black Queen / with his King’s Bishop / thus obtaining / the Black King’s Bishop’s and their own Soldiers [[notes: likely referring to the capture of several pawns in the sequence]] / and brings his game / because he / in this way / wins three Soldiers / into a good order / and provided he knows how to play / [brings it] infallibly / to the desired end [[notes: victory]].
