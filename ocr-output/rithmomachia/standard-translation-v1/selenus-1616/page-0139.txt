# Of the Game of Chess.

[[margin: Chapter II:]] ...to be understood by the remaining seven white and seven black **Major Pieces** [[notes: *Haupt-Steine*, literally "Head-Stones," referring to the pieces on the back rank other than the one already mentioned]].

These quadrants, when they are placed back together, are further divided into eight straight, ascending lines [[notes: Known today as "files"]], which are named as follows: the first, the **King’s Rook** line; the second, the **King’s Knight** [[notes: *Reuter*, literally "Rider"]] line; the third, the **King’s Bishop** [[notes: *Schütze*, literally "Archer" or "Shooter"]] line; the fourth, the **King’s** line; the fifth, the **Queen’s** line; the sixth, the **Queen’s Bishop** line; the seventh, the **Queen’s Knight** line; and the eighth, the **Queen’s Rook** line.

Now, although this division of the Chessboard into distinct quadrants, lines, and squares might serve those who wish to play this game **from memory** (that is, in the mind) [[notes: An early reference to blindfold chess]], these divisions alone are not sufficient for them. For they must—in addition to a good memory, long use, and practice—also pay heed to the counting of the squares, either within each individual quadrant or across the entire space.

Regarding the number of the squares in each quadrant, one must begin the calculation from the **King’s Rook** up to the halfway point of the Chessboard (namely, into the fourth square of the **Queen’s Rook**) and keep well in memory which square is white and which is black, in the following manner.

If one uses the white Chess-pieces, then on the line of the **Major Pieces**, counting from the right to the left side (**in the manner of the Hebrews**) [[notes: Referring to the fact that Hebrew is read from right to left]], the **King’s Rook’s** square is the **First**, and white; his **Knight’s** is the **second**, and black; his **Bishop’s** is the **third**, and white; the **King’s** is the **fourth**, and black; the **Queen’s** is the **fifth**, and white; her **Bishop’s** is the **sixth**, and black; her **Knight’s** is the **seventh**, and white; her **Rook’s** is the **eighth**, and black.

In the second horizontal row [[notes: *Zwerch-reige*, meaning a "rank" in modern chess terms]], the **King’s Rook’s second** square is the **ninth**, and black; his **Knight’s second** is the **tenth**, and white; his **Bishop’s second** is the **eleventh**, and black; the **King’s second** is the **twelfth**, and white; the **Queen’s second** is the **thirteenth**, and black; her **Bishop’s second** is the **fourteenth**, and white; her **Knight’s second** is the **fifteenth**, and black; her **Rook’s second** is the **sixteenth**, and white.

In the third horizontal line, the **King’s Rook’s third** square is the **seventeenth**, and white; his **Knight’s third** is the **eighteenth**, and black; his **Bishop’s third** is the **nineteenth**, and white; the **King’s third** is the **twentieth**, and black; the **Queen’s third** is the **twenty-first**, and...
