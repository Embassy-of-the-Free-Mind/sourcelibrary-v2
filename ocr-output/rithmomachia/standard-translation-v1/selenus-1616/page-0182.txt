# The Second Book / 4. Chapter:

1.) When one has no desire / to attack his enemy / with the first moves / but merely wishes to protect himself / then he should / let the other / come forward well / to attack his pieces [[notes: *Steine*; the generic term for chess pieces, literally "stones"]] / but provide sufficient resistance / against the enemy's first charge / and devise and invent / all means / to prevent the other / from retreating.

A **player** (2.) should / not leave a **Soldier** [[notes: *Soldaten*; the common 17th-century term for Pawns]] / without support [[notes: *besatzung*; literally a "garrison," meaning the pawn should be guarded by another piece]] / but rather keep them all / close to one another / so that the hindmost / can protect the foremost / as much as possible / at all times. For the **Soldiers** / who stand alone / can hardly be protected / unless / one must move them so / to gain something special / or to bring the game / into a better state / or for some other significant reason / that might occur in the game. Therefore, unless / extreme necessity / requires otherwise / it is always better / that they are always well-relieved [[notes: *entsetzet*; a military term for reinforcing a besieged position]] / and not placed far / from one another. Considering that / the other **Major Pieces** [[notes: *Haupt-Steine*; refers to the more powerful pieces such as the Rook, Knight, or Bishop]] / because they / have enough to do / with their own protection / cannot always offer them / a helping hand / in times of need / as necessity requires. Likewise, the enemy gains / as soon as the order is broken / and the pieces / are placed here and there / in a disorderly fashion / many better opportunities / to attack them / than when they / stand together / in good order / and hold their ground.

A **player** (3.) / who wishes to defend himself / should diligently strive / so that he may meet / the many encroaching enemies / with only a few [pieces] / and drive back their assault : of which / further instructions / will be shown / and presented / in the following examples.
