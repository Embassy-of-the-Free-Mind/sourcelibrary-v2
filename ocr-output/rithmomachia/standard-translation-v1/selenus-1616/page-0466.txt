# The Fourth Book / Chapter 9.

->” Each of the eight should have<-
->” A little banner [[notes: *Vendelin* (Fähnlein), here referring to the pawns or soldiers who lead the way for the larger pieces]] before him.<-
->” If you count them, there may be sixteen;<-
->” That would make it so that each behind<-
->” Serves those I named before [[notes: referring to the back rank of "officer" pieces]].<-
->” Thus, there would be too many on the board<-
->” As I shall explain to you.<-
->” And I saw written in the Book, [[gloss: meaning the book of Friar Jacobus [[notes: Jacobus de Cessolis (c. 1250 – c. 1322), a Dominican friar whose sermon on chess became the most popular medieval manual on the game]]]]<-
->” That the board should have no more fields<-
->” Than sixty-four, just as it stands<-
->” When it was first established.<-
->” So should the teaching be for as many<-
->” As there are pieces; so have I read<-
->” Where I found it written in the book<-
->” From which I have taken this.<-
->” And therefore, it seems to me<-
->” That this version is certainly more just,<-
->” Which has fewer stones [[notes: *Steine* or "stones" was the common term for chess pieces at the time]];<-
->” For it is also the common way<-
->” Wherever I have traveled in any land.<-
->” Indeed, it is known to me in good measure<-
->” In **Provence** and in **France**<-
->” And in **Kurwalchen** [[notes: Historically referring to Chur-Raetia or the Romansh-speaking parts of present-day Switzerland and Northern Italy]]. Yet I did see<-
->” How a board there had more stones;<-
->” Only in the city of **Constance**<-
->” Did I see one, and never any other<-
->” But that. Whether I rode or walked,<-
->” If anyone else has seen more,<-
->” I grant it to them, if they wish to attest to it.<-
->” Also, I believe if there had been more,<-
->” The Master who composed this little book<-
->” In Latin<-
->” Would have reported more on it,<-
->” Than this smaller version, I believe.<-
->” However, I certainly would not wish<-
->” That there were any more of it,<-
->” For this is already heavy enough for me,<-
->” This task I have taken upon myself.<-
->” I could never bring it to an end<-
->” If there were certainly any more to it;<-
->” Even this smaller version troubles me greatly,<-
->” For my skill is too small;<-
->” Even with the smallest stone<-
->” I would have too much work to do<-
->” To report upon it well.<-

[[summary: The poet Cunrat von Ammenhusen argues in favor of the standard 64-square chessboard, citing the authority of Jacobus de Cessolis and his own travels through France and Italy. He mentions seeing a larger variant only once in Constance and admits that explaining even the standard game is a daunting task for his limited skills.]]
