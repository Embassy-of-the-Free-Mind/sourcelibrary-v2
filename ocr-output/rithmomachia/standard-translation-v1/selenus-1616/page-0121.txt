# On the Game of Chess.

[[margin: 10. cap:]]
Concerning their further properties and virtues: those **Soldiers** [[notes: Pawns]] who stand before the **King** and the **Queen** are considered the best for beginning a **skirmish** with the enemy. They are also arranged as a **breastwork** [[notes: A defensive wall or parapet, emphasizing the pawn's role as a shield]] against the attacks of the opponent, to block his entrance and passage so that he may not wander back and forth across the entire board as might otherwise happen. One can also safely and easily come to their aid; however, if they are captured, one must, as much as possible, appoint others in their place.

Following these, those who stand before the **Archers** [[notes: The Bishops, historically called *Schützen* in this German tradition]] are considered good; for they are able to break the enemy’s intentions, open the way for their own pieces, and cause the enemy some confusion so that he cannot skirmish with such freedom nor come to the aid of his own pieces as would happen in their absence. Furthermore, they are closer to the **King** and **Queen** and are the guardians and watchers of them as well as of both their Soldiers. When they must—indeed, as is almost common—step into this position, they are capable enough to defend and cover the **King**, as well as to block the entrance of the enemy's **Archers**, who presume to hinder the relocation of the **King** to a safe place.

For this reason, they (and especially the **King’s Archer-Soldier** [[notes: The pawn on the f-file, in front of the King's Bishop]], which the ***Itali*** [[notes: Italians]], not without reason, call ***electum pedonam***, the chosen pawn: ***Act: quaest: 6. n. 12.***) are commonly given as an advantage [[notes: This refers to "handicap" games, where a stronger player might start without this specific pawn to balance the match]] upon request. For when this one is gone, the **King** finds himself in great danger of being entirely defeated and cleared away. Likewise, he [[notes: The f-pawn]] must alone hold off the **check** from the opponent's **Queen** and other pieces which might wish to beset the **King** on this side, and he is only relieved [[notes: Supported or replaced in the defensive line]] by the **King’s Knight-Soldier** [[notes: The g-pawn, referred to here as the *Reuters-Soldat*]]. Finally, if the same is lost, it is also impossible to restore a rank of Soldiers to that side of the board for the preservation of the game (which then causes the same a great deal of harm). Although the **King** can be moved to the other side of the board into the **Queen’s** quarter and her **Archer’s** field, where more Soldiers are present to help him, one must nevertheless perform more moves to get him there than if he were moved toward his own quarter, to which he has only one field to go.
