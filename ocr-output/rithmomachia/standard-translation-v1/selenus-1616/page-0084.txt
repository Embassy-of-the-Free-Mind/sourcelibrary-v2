[[notes: The "King's Leap" was not a standard move but a one-time special ability to move the King to safety, similar to how castling functions today, but with more flexible destination squares.]]

These jumps now / occur / in the following way: From his square / he can initially / jump to nine squares: Namely / on the first horizontal line [[notes: A "Zwerch-linie" refers to a rank or horizontal row on the board]] / to his side / in (1.) his **Knight’s** [[notes: *Reuter*, literally "Rider," the Knight]]: on the same line / toward the Queen’s side / in (2.) her **Bishop’s** [[notes: *Schütze*, literally "Archer," the Bishop]]: on the next ascending line [[notes: The second rank]] / in (3.) his other **Knight’s**: in (4.) the other **Queen’s Bishop’s**: on the third / following line / in (5.) his **Knight’s** third: in (6.) the **Queen’s Bishop’s** third: in (7.) his own third: in (8.) the **Queen’s** third: and finally / in (9.) his **Bishop’s** / third square.

If he has then / jumped into the middle of the chess-table / perhaps into his third square: then he can / from the same / be moved to (1.) his: (2.) his Queen’s: or (3.) his Bishop’s fourth: and to (4.) his Bishop’s: or (5.) the Queen’s third: to (6.) his: (7.) his Queen’s: or (8.) his Bishop’s / second / and thus into eight squares. This is also understood / regarding the other briefly mentioned eight jumps: but from his Knight’s = and the Queen’s Bishop’s squares / he can only / just as if he / stood in his own square / reach five squares.

But why he can perform the jump / like the **Knight** / and / like a **Rook**, **Bishop**, and **Pawn** [[notes: *Roche, Schütze, und Soldat*]] / perform his gait / in the manner mentioned / happens because / he is the Head / and a King / of the other pieces / and it so pleased / the inventor / of this laudable game / to arrange / the nature and properties of the pieces / in such a way. Other reasons / cannot be shown. Unless the Ancients / perhaps wished to signify thereby / that a King / as long as he / still remained in his own land / secure / and without all danger / might indeed venture far enough / from the Royal court / into the field: (although one / in recent years / in France / and England / has experienced the opposite) [[notes: This likely refers to the political instability and the execution of monarchs like Charles I of England (1649), suggesting that even in their own lands, Kings were no longer safe.]] When he / must betake himself / outside of his Kingdom / or the enemy has already / fallen into his land / then he should step slowly / and would indeed have need / of good foresight / protection and watch.
