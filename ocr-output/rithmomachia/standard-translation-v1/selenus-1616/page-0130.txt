### 16. Chapter: The First Book. 100

» The sixth **Pawn** [[notes: *Wend*, a term used here for the chess pawn]] shall be a man who has his right hand extended, beckoning people to him, into his house. In his left hand, he shall have a **wheaten roll** [[notes: *semel brot*, a roll of fine white bread]] and upon it a small glass of wine; and under his belt, a key. This signifies an **innkeeper** or **host** [[notes: *leitgeben oder gastgeben*]]. With him, one shall find for sale whatever one needs; furthermore, everything that one brings into his house shall be safe. He shall stand at the left hand and side of the King, before the **Elder** [[notes: *den Alten*. In this specific chess allegory, the "Elder" or "Old Man" represents the Rook, who serves as the King’s Justice or Judge.]]. For the Elder signifies the **Judge**, and since brawls often arise in their houses, which the Judge must settle, he shall therefore stand before him. These same people [[notes: the innkeepers]] should guard themselves against excessive eating and drinking, so that they may be an example of **moderation** [[notes: *messigkeit*]] to other people who come to them. There are many who perish from intemperance; from this, brawls arise, and killings happen, and all manner of evil. A person should eat for the sake of living, and not live for the sake of eating: **Nature** is satisfied with a small amount and very little. But habit and lust add much to it. They should also speak to their guests in a friendly and kind manner, with a handsome bearing and a cheerful face; for these things increase a host’s reputation very far, wide, and broad, and make him esteemed wherever people speak of him in the land. Also, every faithful host shall warn his guests how and of whom they should beware upon the road, and guide and direct them from their lodgings, and faithfully preserve and protect their bodies, honor, and goods. And what is given to them to keep, they shall keep with diligence, as their own property, and return and give it back to him entirely and unchanged. When a stranger goes into an **Inn** [[notes: *Herberge*]], he makes his dwelling there, and therefore his property and honor should be as secure with you as if he were at home. A host should also protect his guests from his own servants, so that they do no damage to the horse’s fodder; for because of this, it happens that the guests cannot reach their proper **day’s journey** [[notes: *tagweid*, the distance a traveler or laborer could cover in a single day]], or that the robbers overtake them and they cannot escape nor get away, and thus are often robbed of life and goods; and the cause of this is an unfaithful, wicked servant.
