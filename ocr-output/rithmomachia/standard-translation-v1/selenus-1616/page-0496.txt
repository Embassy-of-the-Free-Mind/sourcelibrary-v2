2.) That they [the numbers] be marked on the outside, on the edge of the **Towers**, so that they may be better kept in view. [[notes: The "Towers" or Pyramids are composed of multiple stacked layers; marking the outer edges allowed players to see the value of each specific layer even when the piece was fully assembled.]]

3.) That a white or black **point** or **little dot** (to distinguish the bottom side of the number from the top) be placed beneath them:

[[image-desc: Six geometric shapes arranged in two rows. Each shape contains a number with a small dot positioned directly below it to indicate the correct orientation. 
First row: 
- An unshaded circle containing the number 6.
- A shaded circle containing the number 9.
- An unshaded triangle containing the number 81.
Second row: 
- A shaded triangle containing the number 16.
- An unshaded square containing the number 15.
- A shaded square containing the number 66.]]

For in the absence of this (since the **pieces** [[notes: The original German uses the word *Steine* (stones), which was the standard term for board game pieces, much like we use "men" or "pawns" today.]], during play, are often knocked over or moved), one—as the **illustration** indicates—would find it difficult to distinguish 6 from 9, 16 from 91, 18 from 81, 15 from 51, 66 from 99, etc.; rather, one would only be delayed and confused.
