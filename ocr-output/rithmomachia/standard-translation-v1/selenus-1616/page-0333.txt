Fourthly: with the Queen, from [square] fifty-two [[note: square d7]]: Although she could indeed be moved to forty-three [[note: square c6]] to protect this soldier [[note: Pawn]], she is more usefully placed on the former square, because the soldier is equally sufficiently provided for there. Furthermore, she would only block and hinder the path of this soldier [[note: the c7 pawn]], whose assistance one occasionally greatly needs.

<margin>5.</margin>
Fifthly: with the King’s Archer [[note: The German "Schütze" (Archer) was the contemporary name for the Bishop]], from [square] forty-five [[note: square e6]].

<margin>6.</margin>
Regarding the first method [[note: defending with the Knight on c6]], Black plays, etc. (to defend his King’s Soldier) the King’s Rider [[note: Knight]], etc.

<margin>7. (n. 1.)</margin>
To [square] 46 [[note: square f6]]: * White [moves] the King’s Archer to thirty-etc. [[note: square 30, which is f4]].
sig: Black [moves] his King’s Archer to thirty-eight-etc. [[note: square 38, which is f5]].
sig: White [moves] the King’s Archer’s Soldier [[note: the f-pawn]] to twenty-two [[note: square f3]]: Black [moves] to the White King’s Soldier, etc. not the King’s Rider (*as Damiano* believes) to forty-three [[note: square c6]], but rather (to give White no occasion for several moves which are to be found above in the 2nd Chapter of this 3rd Book) the Queen to fifty-two [[note: square d7]].

<margin>8.</margin>
* White [moves] the King’s Soldier [[note: the e-pawn]] to the Black King’s Archer and Soldier on twenty-nine [[note: square e4]]: Black takes him with the Soldier: White [takes] the same with the King’s Archer’s Soldier and moves him again to the Black King’s Archer: Black takes the White King’s Soldier with the Queen and gives check:

<margin>9.</margin>
* White moves the King to thirteen [[note: square e2]]: Black gives check with the King’s Archer from thirty-one [[note: square g4]]: White must cover the King with the King’s Rider on twenty-two [[note: square f3]], and moves him toward the Black Queen: Black gives check with the same from twenty-seven [[note: square c4]]: White moves the King to fourteen [[note: square f2]] and exposes his King’s Archer to the Black Queen: Now, if Black moves the Queen out of danger, White gives check with the King’s Rook [[note: "Rochen"]] from [square] four [[note: square d1]], and thus arranges his game not poorly.
