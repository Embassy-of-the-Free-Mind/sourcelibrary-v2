## The Ninth Chapter.

### How the Black King’s Soldier [[note: Pawn]], in the fifth and final way, can be protected and relieved at the start.

[[margin: 1.]] It follows further, in what manner, in the fifth and final way, the **B. K. Soldier** [[note: Black King’s Pawn]] may be guarded by his **Archer** [[note: Bishop; the German *Schütze* literally means "shooter" or "archer," a common historical name for the Bishop]].

If, accordingly, the **White** and the **Black** [players] play the **Kings’-Soldiers** (as often mentioned) to **twenty-eight** [[note: Square e4]] and **thirty-six** [[note: Square e5]]: The **White** [player] (having the advantage [of the first move]) [moves] the **K. Knight** against the **B. K. Soldier** to **nineteen** [[note: Square f3]]: The **Black** [player] guards it in the fifth way (of which mention was made in Chapter 5, no. 5 of this book) with the **K. Archer** [[note: King’s Bishop]] to **forty-five** [[note: Square f6]].

To say a word also of this method: It is the worst and most dangerous among all others, because the pieces and **Soldiers** [[note: Pawns]] are thereby greatly hemmed in and cannot easily be moved. Although it is not advisable to play such a game as this, one can nonetheless arrange it to some extent; and commonly those who are first learning this game are accustomed to move this **Archer** in such a way—just as it is, indeed, a true **student’s move** [[note: A "Schülers-Zug" or beginner's move. This refers to what is known today as Damiano’s Defense (1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 f6), which is considered a strategic error because it occupies the square needed by the Knight and weakens the King's defense.]]—and to advance it into the field.
