## The Fifteenth Chapter.

### How the game is played when the Black [player]
### does not move the Knight, as the fourteenth
### chapter described, but rather a Soldier
### or an Archer.

[[margin: 1.]]
So that we may now bring the second method (which was mentioned in the 12th Chapter) to an end along with this Second Book: after it was reported in the previous chapter how the Black player should continue his opening moves with the King’s Knight [[notes: *Reuter* (Rider/Knight)]]: there follows the manner in which the moves are to be pursued * when the same player, instead of the Knight (number 1 in f. of the 14th Chapter), moves his King’s Soldier [[notes: *Soldat* (Pawn)]] to forty-five [[notes: The author uses a 1-64 numbering system for the board squares common in early modern German chess manuals]]: then the White player, in response, moves the King’s Archer [[notes: *Schütze* (Archer) refers to the Bishop]] to thirty: the Black player takes the White King’s Archer’s Soldier with his King’s Soldier: the White player moves the King’s Soldier to twenty-nine: the Black player gives check with the Queen from twenty-five: the White player moves the King to three: the Black player [moves] the King’s Archer against the White Queen to twenty-six: the White player (in order to cover his Queen) moves the King’s Knight against the Black Queen to nineteen: the Black player (insofar as he wishes to give no cause for an exchange of Queens) takes him with the King’s Archer: the White player [takes] the same with the Queen and moves her onto the Black King’s Soldier: the Black player (to protect the same) moves the King’s Knight’s Soldier to thirty-four: the White player [moves] the Queen (so that he might, with her, take the Black King’s Knight’s [Pawn] or, with his King’s Archer, take the Black King’s Archer’s Soldier) to twenty-three: the Black player jumps with his King (in order not to let the Queen’s quarter be disrupted, nor to forfeit the right to castle [[notes: *Rochen* here refers to the act of castling]]) to sixty-two: the White player takes the Black King’s Archer’s Soldier with his King’s Archer and moves it to the Black King’s Knight’s [square]: the Black player [moves] the same to the White King’s Soldier at forty-three:

[[margin: 2.]]
* The White player gives check with the Queen from forty-four: the Black player covers the King/
