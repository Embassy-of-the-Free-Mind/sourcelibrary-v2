### The First Book / Chapter II:

...and white: her **Bishop’s** [[notes: *Schütze*, literally "Archer" or "Shooter," the early German term for the Bishop]] third / the twenty-second / and black: her **Knight’s** [[notes: *Reuter*, literally "Rider," the early German term for the Knight]] third / the twenty-third / and white: her **Rook’s** [[notes: *Rochen*, from the Persian *Rukh*]] third / the twenty-fourth / and black: In the fourth and last horizontal line [[notes: *Zwerch-linie*, what we now call a "rank"]], the **King’s Rook’s** fourth square / the twenty-fifth / and black: his **Knight’s** fourth / the twenty-sixth / and white: his **Bishop’s** fourth / the twenty-seventh / and black: the **King’s** fourth / the twenty-eighth / and white: the **Queen’s** fourth / the twenty-ninth / and black: her **Bishop’s** fourth / the thirtieth / and white: her **Knight’s** fourth / the thirty-first / and black: her **Rook’s** fourth / the thirty-second / and white. 

These aforementioned numbers, upon which the **White Army** [[notes: *Hauffen*, literally "heap" or "host," referring here to the collection of white pieces]] is placed, are now (as mentioned) marked and calculated from the right to the left: the others, however, upon which the **Black Pieces** are arranged, are calculated from the left to the right hand, and thus recorded and reckoned as being opposite to one another. 

Furthermore, the square that immediately follows a white square (either on a horizontal or an ascending line [[notes: Rank or file]]) is always black: and the squares nearest to a Black one are always white: Therefore, one can very easily keep the squares in mind with the said distinction from one square to the other, and when one [knows] of the one...

->Hauffens<-
