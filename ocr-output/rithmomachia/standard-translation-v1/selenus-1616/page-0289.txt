## The Third Book/
# Of the Description/ of the Game of Chess.
### The First Chapter.
#### How one/ in the Eighth Manner/ might make an incursion with the King’s Knight/ and move against him.

After/ in the last eight chapters/ of the previous Second Book/ the seven ways/ how the game/ when it/ on both sides/ with the King’s Soldiers [[notes: "Soldaten" or Soldiers refers to Pawns, the foot-soldiers of the chessboard.]]/ is started/ might not unusefully be conducted/ have been described: Thus there remains/ the eighth/ and last manner/ given in the 7th Chapter/ of the aforementioned 2nd Book/ also to be recorded: and this [manner] corresponds/ with the/ seventh way mentioned in the 12th, 13th, 14th, and 15th Chapters/ of the 2nd Book/ in that/ the White [player]/ immediately in the second move/ makes the attack/ on the Black King’s Soldier/ but is distinguished in this/ that in this [manner]/ it is carried out/ not with the Soldier/ from 27 [[notes: Early chess manuals often numbered the 64 squares of the board from 1 to 64. In this specific system, square 27 likely refers to the starting position of a pawn or a specific coordinate in the author's diagram.]], but with the King’s Knight/ from nineteen/ the same is performed.

Now this manner/ with the Knight [[notes: The German text uses "Reuter," literally "Rider," which we know today as the Knight.]]/ was particularly pleasing to Damianus/ of Portugal [[notes: Pedro Damiano (1480–1544) was a Portuguese apothecary and chess player who wrote the first great chess manual of the 16th century, "Questo libro e da imparare giocare a scachi," published in 1512.]]/ in the execution of this game/ even so/ that it almost appears/ as if He/ had disregarded the previous manners/ nor known how to use them. Therefore, [he] has augmented/ and presented this manner/ with all sorts of variations of moves: Which we/ according to our promise/ [translate] here from his Spanish booklet [[notes: Though Damiano was Portuguese, his work was widely translated and associated with the "Iberian" style of play; the author here likely refers to a Spanish edition or the general influence of that region's masters.]]/ yet more clearly/ than was done by Him/ in this and the following chapters/ and shall at the same time point out/ where he has erred/
