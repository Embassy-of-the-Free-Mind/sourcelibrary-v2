# The Fifth Chapter

-> *,, He calls this addition of numbers a fraud and a snare. ,,* <-
[[notes: The Latin quote uses the word "Additionem" (Addition), but the German text below immediately begins explaining "Abziehen" (Subtraction). This may be a general reference to the "fraudulent" complexity of combining multiple numbers to trap a single piece.]]

Through **Subtraction** / the stones / are won / or forfeited / in this manner.

When two stones / of one side / are moved into such spaces / from which they / can strike / a single stone / of the opponent's side: and when the number of one / is subtracted from the number of the other / and produces / the same number / with which / the mentioned opponent's stone / is marked / then that stone is indeed taken: they [the capturing stones] remain / however / on the spaces / where they / were moved for that turn: For example / if the White Square / with 15 is on F: and the White Round stone / with 6 is on G: and the Black Round stone / marked with 9 / has been moved onto H: then for that reason / (since 6 taken from 15 / leaves 9 / and both of these White stones / can reach / the Black one) the Black Round stone / marked with 9 / may easily be taken / from the game. [[notes: In Rythmomachia, "reaching" a stone means the target is within the capture range—or "move distance"—of the pieces involved. Here, the two pieces "team up" mathematically to remove an opponent.]]
