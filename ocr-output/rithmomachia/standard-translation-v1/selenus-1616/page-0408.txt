### The Third Book / 15. Chapter:

...forty: then he should move the **King’s Soldier** [[notes: "Soldat" refers to the Pawn.]] to **thirty-seven** [[notes: In the 1–64 numbering system used by Selenus, square 37 corresponds to e5 in modern notation.]] (so that the **Bishop** [[notes: Selenus uses the term "Schütze" (Archer or Shooter) for the Bishop.]], might, as it were, fire upon the **seventeenth square** [[notes: Square 17 is a3.]]).

Let this now be a sufficient reminder for those who truly desire to become good **chess-movers** [[notes: *Schachzieher*, an archaic term for chess players.]]. However, regarding this matter—as it is not fitting to play in such a manner—we shall break off briefly: and herewith, the manner of how the game is begun without a given or taken **advantage** [[notes: *Vortheil* refers to "odds," a common practice in 17th-century chess where a stronger player would start without a specific piece (like a Pawn or Knight) to balance the game against a weaker opponent.]] is concluded and ended (along with this third Book).
