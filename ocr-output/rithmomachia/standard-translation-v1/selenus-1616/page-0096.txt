### The First Book / Chapter 7:

of the White Queen / in the black [field] : of the Black / in the white field: Accordingly, the battle-order is concluded with them / upon the same line. The reason / was indicated shortly before / by the word **Stat-halter** [[notes: *Stat-halter* (Governor or Steward) was a common name for the Rook in 17th-century German chess, representing a high-ranking official who maintains order in the King's absence]]. Others look to the custom / of the Barbarian peoples / who placed **Elephants** / at the sides of their men-of-war. Some believe / it is hereby given to understand / that it is a useful thing / to have **Fortresses** upon the borders / so that in times of misfortune / and unforeseen attack by the Enemy / the subjects / when they have tried everything / and can no longer hold out / may fly thither for refuge.

> >> *Certain barbarians,* (writes Actius in *Indic: lit: A.*) *place Citadels,*
> >> *where we place Standard-bearers,* (the archers) *and in their place Birds,*
> >> *that walk with the gait of citadels, which among them are called Racchi: and*
> >> *from there perhaps, according to some, they are commonly called Rocchi among us, namely*
> >> *the Italians.* [[notes: This passage reflects the linguistic confusion of the era. The Persian word for a chariot, *Rukh*, sounded like the Italian word *Rocca* (fortress) and the German word for a large bird or crow, *Rache*.]]

Their move / is across the width / and not across the corners [[notes: meaning they move in straight lines along ranks and files, not diagonally]] of the fields / forward / to both sides / and backward. They are now moved / into one / or into more fields / of white or black color / as it may fall most advantageously / for the Chess-player / in his game / and as the way / is opened to them by their companions [[notes: "companions" refers to their own pawns or pieces blocking the way]]. When they also encounter their enemies / upon those lines / upon which they are permitted to go / they may then abolish / and clear them away [[notes: i.e., capture them]]. For they / from their field / in which they were first placed / can be moved along the entire lines / which they have before them / and to the side / as far as the edge / of the **Schach-Tabel** [[notes: the chessboard]]. But if they stand / in the middle of the board / then they have, as it were, four streets / upon which they may step / two on the sides / one before and one behind them. But that they go / the straight and wide way / thereby indicates / that the Captains / and Commanders / of the fortresses / should be upright and faithful.

They are further / because of this free movement / held to be the best pieces next to the Queen / indeed, like a **half-Queen** / because they have half of her moves [[notes: In the chess rules of this period, the Queen's move was often seen as a combination of the Rook's straight move and the Bishop's diagonal move]]. For one of their kind / alone and single-handedly / (as the following sketch / gives to understand) with his King / (which otherwise, besides the Queen / no piece / without other helpers / alongside the King / is able to do /) can give **Schach-Mat** [[notes: Checkmate]].
