**C. Plinius Secundus.** [[notes: Better known as Pliny the Elder, author of the monumental *Natural History*, an encyclopedia of the Roman world.]]
**Julius Pollux.** [[notes: A 2nd-century Greek grammarian and sophist whose *Onomasticon* serves as a valuable dictionary of Greek terms and concepts.]]
**Polybius.** [[notes: A Greek historian of the Hellenistic period, famous for his "Histories" detailing the rise of the Roman Republic.]]
**Polydorus Vergilius.** [[notes: Polydore Vergil, an Italian humanist and priest who spent much of his life in England and wrote the *Anglica Historia*.]]
**Pomponius.** [[notes: Likely Pomponius Mela, the earliest Roman geographer, or Sextus Pomponius, a prominent Roman jurist.]]
**Jacobus Pontanus.** [[notes: Jakob Spanmüller, a Jesuit scholar and teacher known for his works on rhetoric and classical poetry.]]
**Henricus Porsius.** [[notes: Heinrich Porsius, a German neo-Latin poet and humanist.]]
**Præpositus.** [[notes: Generally refers to Nicolaus Praepositus (Nicolas Prévost), a physician from Salerno famous for his medical dispensatory.]]
**Antonius de Prato, of Trento.**
**Procopius.** [[notes: Procopius of Caesarea, the principal Byzantine historian of the 6th century, chronicling the reign of Justinian I.]]
**Jacobus Publicius, of Florence.** [[notes: An Italian rhetorician known for his early printed works on the art of memory (*Ars Memorativa*).]]
**Paris de Puteo.** [[notes: Paride dal Pozzo, an influential Italian jurist specialized in the laws of dueling and chivalry.]]
**Pythagoras.** [[notes: The ancient Greek philosopher and mathematician, founder of the Pythagorean movement.]]

->Q.<-

**Matthis Quad, of Kinckelbach.** [[notes: Matthias Quad, an engraver and cartographer from Cologne known for his maps and historical writings.]]
*Quintilianus.* [[notes: Marcus Fabius Quintilianus, the Roman educator and rhetorician who wrote the *Institutio Oratoria*.]]

->R.<-

*Rafis.* [[notes: Al-Razi, known in the West as Rhazes, a Persian physician, philosopher, and polymath of the Islamic Golden Age.]]
**Johannes Ravisius, Textor.** [[notes: Jean Tixier de Ravisi, a French humanist whose reference works were widely used by students and writers in the Renaissance.]]
**Reinerus Reineccius.** [[notes: Reiner Reineck, a German historian and professor known for his genealogical and historical research.]]
**Cælius Rhodiginus.** [[notes: Lodovico Ricchieri, an Italian humanist whose *Lectiones Antiquae* was a massive compilation of classical knowledge.]]
**Matthæus Ricius.** [[notes: Matteo Ricci, the famous Jesuit missionary to China who introduced Western science and geography to the Ming dynasty.]]
**Rosellius de Rosellis.** [[notes: An Italian legal scholar and jurist.]]
**Bernhardinus Rutilius.** [[notes: Bernardino Rutilio, a 16th-century Italian jurist and humanist scholar.]]

->S.<-

**Arthur Saul, English.** [[notes: An English clergyman known for writing one of the earliest English books on chess.]]
**Josephus Scaliger.** [[notes: Joseph Justus Scaliger, a brilliant French scholar who revolutionized the study of ancient chronology and history.]]
*Seneca.* [[notes: Lucius Annaeus Seneca, the Stoic philosopher and dramatist of the Roman Imperial era.]]
**Emanuel Soarez.** [[notes: Emanuel Soares, a Portuguese jurist who wrote on civil and canon law.]]

**Robertus Stephanus.** [[notes: Robert Estienne, the celebrated French printer and scholar known for his editions of the Bible and his Latin dictionary.]]
**Giouambattista Strozzi.** [[notes: Giambattista Strozzi the Elder, a Florentine poet and member of the influential Strozzi family.]]

->T.<-

**Cornelius Tacitus.** [[notes: The premier historian of the Roman Empire, known for his *Annals* and *Histories*.]]
**Torquato Tasso.** [[notes: The great Italian poet of the late Renaissance, most famous for his epic *Jerusalem Delivered*.]]
**Fr. Jacobus, of Thessaly.** [[notes: Likely referring to a medieval or early modern friar associated with occult or theological manuscripts.]]
**Franciscus Tidicæus.** [[notes: Franz Tidike, a German physician and Neo-Latin poet.]]
**Fra Jacopo, of Todi.** [[notes: Jacopone da Todi, the Franciscan friar and mystical poet who wrote the *Stabat Mater*.]]
**Nicolaus Trigantius.** [[notes: Nicolas Trigault, a Jesuit missionary who edited Matteo Ricci’s journals regarding the mission to China.]]
**Il Tuccio.** [[notes: An Italian moniker, possibly referring to a specific poet or jurist known by this nickname.]]
**Adrianus Turnebus.** [[notes: Adrien Turnèbe, a French classical scholar and director of the King's Printers for Greek.]]

->V.<-

**Marcus Terentius Varro.** [[notes: Ancient Rome's greatest scholar, a prolific writer on agriculture, grammar, and history.]]
**Hieronymus Vida.** [[notes: Marco Girolamo Vida, an Italian humanist poet and bishop famous for his poem on the game of chess and the *Christiad*.]]
**Giouanni Villani.** [[notes: A Florentine chronicler who wrote the *Nuova Cronica*, a vital history of medieval Florence.]]
*Virgilius.* [[notes: Virgil, the Roman national poet and author of the *Aeneid*.]]
**Raphaël Volaterranus.** [[notes: Raffaello Maffei, an Italian humanist whose encyclopedia, *Commentariorum urbanorum*, was influential.]]

->VV.<-

**Johan Jacobi, of Wallhausen.** [[notes: Johann Jacobi von Wallhausen, a military theorist famous for his manuals on infantry and cavalry tactics.]]
**Lucas Wielius.** [[notes: Lucas Wiel, a late 16th-century theologian and writer.]]
**Johannes Wierus.** [[notes: Johann Weyer, a Dutch physician who was one of the first to argue against the persecution of "witches" on medical grounds.]]

->X.<-

*Xenophon.* [[notes: The ancient Greek philosopher, historian, and soldier, a contemporary of Socrates.]]

->Z.<-

**Girolamo Zanucchi, of Conegliano.** [[notes: An Italian legal expert and writer.]]
*Zenodotus.* [[notes: A Greek grammarian and the first librarian of the Great Library of Alexandria.]]
**Theodorus Zwingerus.** [[notes: Theodor Zwinger, a Swiss physician and humanist who compiled the *Theatrum Humanae Vitae*, a massive universal encyclopedia.]]
