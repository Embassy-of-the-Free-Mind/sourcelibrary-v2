### Chapter Thirteen.
## How a game is to be conducted when White moves the King’s Bishop [[notes: *Königes-Schütze*, literally "King's Marksman"]] out, rather than the Knight or the Soldier [[notes: *Soldat*, the common term for a Pawn in this era]], as mentioned in the previous chapter.

NOW, if White (after Black has taken the White King’s Bishop’s Soldier with the King’s Soldier) does not move the Knight to 19, nor the Soldier to 29 (as per numbers 2 and 15 of the previous chapter), but instead moves the King’s Bishop to thirty: then Black can meet him in three ways: namely, with the Knight from forty-three; with the Queen from twenty-five; or with the Soldiers from forty-six and thirty-five.

White (to protect the same) moves

White (to further preserve the same) moves the King’s Knight to twenty-two. Black (to defend his King’s Soldier) moves that Knight’s Soldier to thirty-four. White (to break the enemy’s play and intention) moves the King’s Rook’s Soldier [[notes: *Rochens-Soldat*, the Rook's Pawn]] to twenty-five; he can thus, if he only takes a little care, easily win the game.
