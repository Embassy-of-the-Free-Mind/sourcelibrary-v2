# The First Book. Chapter 8.

,,...called *ferentes alas*, wing-bearers. For certain nations used wings—of an Eagle, for instance—tied to spears as a standard. *Actius in Indic: lit: V.*" [[notes: This Latin passage references the historical practice of using wings as military ensigns, linking the "wings" of the chess board to the wings of an army.]]

**Bodyguards** [[notes: *Trabanten*; these were life-guards or halberdiers who protected high-ranking officials]] / and **Archers** / (which name / suits our warriors / well: just as one understands the **Archers** / in this day and age / to mean the *Doppelsöldner* [[notes: *Doppelsöldner* were "double-pay" soldiers, elite mercenaries chosen for their skill and bravery to stand in the front ranks]] / who carry muskets / and are held to be the **Core** / of the soldiery / because of their great utility) are they called.

There are / in each army [[notes: *hauffen*, literally "heap" or "host," referring to the set of pieces for one side]] / two of them: and they stand / by the King / and the Queen / on both wings / at their side: the **White King’s-Archer** / stands by the same / in the White [field]: the **Black** / in the Black: the **White Queen’s-Archer** / by her / in the Black: the **Black** / in the White field.

Their movement / is toward the corners [[notes: *nach den ecken*, describing diagonal movement]]: for those placed on a White field / always remain on the White: those on a Black field / always in the Black fields / until they are either slain in the battle / or the game is ended: Therefore / the two King’s-Archers—as also / the two Queen’s-Archers—because their fields / are of different colors / cannot / take one another [[notes: because they stay on their own colors, two Bishops of the same side (or the same starting role) never meet]]: however / the **White Queen’s-Archer** / can take the **Black King’s-Archer**: and the **Black Queen’s-Archer** / can take the **White King’s-Archer** / and be taken by him in return. Which those / who wish to practice this game / without Chess-Stones [[notes: *Schach-Steine*, referring to mental chess or playing without a board]] / should take good note of.

Now, if on the line / upon which they are to be moved / no stones / of their own army are present / they may / be shifted through all of those fields / even to the end of the Chess-Table [[notes: *Schach-Tafel*, the board]] / and take away the opponent's stones / if they / encounter one / in the same / and take its place. In former times / they could / in one move / only reach the third field / (as may be seen with more detail in the 9th and 10th Chapters / of the Last Book) playing and striking / forward or backward. This custom, however / has been abolished / by the good players / and / it has been granted and permitted / that one may move them / across all fields / which they can run / without an obstruction.
