# 1. Chapter: Of the Game of Chess.

...if one can offer him a **Check** upon the field [[notes: "felde," meaning a square on the board]] through which he is intended to pass, then he may not step through it, but must instead go around it.

But when necessity draws near, the **King** must also be led forth. And he can [attack] all the **pieces** [[notes: "Steine," literally "stones"]] of the opposing side, with the exception of the **King** and the **Queen**—because the latter employs the moves of both the **Rook** [[notes: "Rochen"]] and the **Bishop** [[notes: "Schützen," literally "archers" or "marksmen"]]—yet he cannot attack them according to their own long-range movements; rather, he may attack them in the adjacent square, and in the following move clear them away and lift them from the board. Furthermore, if they give him **Check** in a square located near the square where he is then found, and they are exposed and without protection [[notes: "entsatzung," referring to a piece being defended or "backed up" by another]], he can immediately take them away—yea, even the **Queen** (provided she was moved so close to him without protection, as an example of this may be found below in Book 4, Chapter 3, Number 23) and in this way remove himself from the danger. He cannot, however, give **Check** to the opposing **King**, for he would otherwise put himself into **Check** and be taken by the other in the following move. This, first of all (as mentioned above), runs contrary to the custom of this game, and furthermore has rational causes. 

For when two **Potentates** [[notes: Rulers or monarchs]] hold their ground against one another in the **Order of Battle**, the start of the battle is not begun, much less carried out, by the high heads who have command over the army—be they **Kings** or other lords—but rather the commanders make arrangements as to which troop shall strike the enemy and which shall relieve them. The **Kings** or **Commanders**, however (who, because much depends upon them, and they are among their soldiers in the field as the soul is in the human body, must reasonably take some precautions for themselves), are not permitted to meet each other directly. Instead, they have their own people, chosen from the elite, around them for protection, and they give orders to the Colonels and Captains as to how the soldiers should be led and managed: *(see 2 Samuel 18:1, 3)* [[notes: This biblical reference describes King David being persuaded not to go into battle himself because his life is worth ten thousand soldiers]]. 

But when it reaches the point that the order is broken and it comes to the main battle, and therefore the **Commanders** must necessarily protect themselves, then necessity knows no law; instead, master and servant must protect their own skins and escape in whatever way they can. Whoever then fights, fights; whoever falls, falls; whoever runs, runs; etc. *(see 1 Samuel 31:2, 3 and 1 Kings 22:32, 33)* [[notes: These verses describe the deaths and narrow escapes of Kings Saul and Ahab in the heat of battle]].
