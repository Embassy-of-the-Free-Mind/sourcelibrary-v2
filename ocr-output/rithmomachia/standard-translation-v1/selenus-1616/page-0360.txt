### The Third Book / Chapter 5.

...having been placed / so he can / against **the White** [player] / for this time / nevertheless obtain nothing [[note: gain no advantage]].

[[margin: 114.]] But if **the White** [player] / moves the King / not to 2 [[note: Square b1]] (no. 66) / but rather to fourteen [[note: Square f2]]: and **the Black** [player] / with the King’s Knight [[note: *Reuter*, literally "Rider"]] / nonetheless takes **the White King’s** Soldier [[note: Pawn]] / and moves the same / toward **the White King’s** Bishop’s-Soldier [[note: the f-pawn]]: then **the White** [player] moves / (so that he / does not lose / the same) the King’s Soldier / toward the Opposing King’s Bishop / and Soldier / to twenty-nine [[note: Square e4]]: **The Black** [player] / takes him / with this Soldier: **The White** [player] / moves / the King’s Rook [[note: *Rochen*]] / (to keep / the Opposing King’s Knight / trapped) to four [[note: Square d1]] / and thus arranges / his game / not badly.

[We] have thus sufficiently / revealed and made understood / to the chess-players / which move / in this place / is to be considered / good or bad: **The Black** [player] will also / because of these / indicated very harmful moves / move the King’s Bishop [[note: *Schütze*, literally "Shooter" or "Archer"]] / (before he / takes **the White King’s** Soldier / with his King’s Knight) with more benefit / to forty-seven [[note: Square g6]] (no. 64. 65.).
