# On the Game of Chess.

[[margin: Chapter 8.]]
...third [square] of the Queen: and the fourth of her **Archer** [[notes: In this context, "Archer's fourth" refers to the square we would now call the Bishop's fourth rank]]: (as far as the border of its **Army** [[notes: *hauffen*, meaning the side or set of pieces]] extends) it is then moved following this into the squares of the opposing side / into the Black **Queen’s-Rider’s** [[notes: *Reuter*, the modern Knight]] fourth / and their **Rook’s** [[notes: *Rochen*, the modern Rook]] / third square. However, if he finds himself / in the middle of the **Field** / perhaps in the fourth square / of his **King**: he can then / strike in four directions / two / toward his opponent / and two / toward his own army. For / toward the opponent / on the right side / ahead of him / he strikes / into the Black **King’s-Archer’s** / fourth: his **Rider’s** / third: and his **Rook’s** / second square: On the left side / ahead of him / into the Black **Queen’s** / fourth: her **Archer’s** / third: her **Rider’s** / second: and finally / into her **Rook’s** square: Toward his own army / on the left side / in his **Queen’s** / third: Her **Archer’s** / second: and her **Rider’s** square: On the right hand / in his third: the **King’s-Rider’s** / second: and in its **Rook’s** square. [[notes: This complex description is an early form of "descriptive notation," tracing the diagonal paths the Bishop can take from the center of the board.]] If he / or the remaining three **Archers** / are now elsewhere: one calculates / by the corners / from one square / to the other / and can / from the above report / easily see where they may strike.

Furthermore, they are considered / nearly equal / to the **Riders** / (of whom / in the following Chapter / a more extensive report / shall follow): Yet / once the game is arranged / sometimes the **Archers** / and sometimes the **Riders** / can better serve and come to the aid of a player: likewise, some play / better / with the **Riders** / than with the **Archers**. Although one should / in the question / of the preference / between **Riders** and **Archers** / actually look to see / which of them / can most help and benefit / the **King** / as the most noble piece / on his side / or on the other side / can inflict / the greatest damage: it nevertheless depends on the player / with which he / can best manage. But the most distinguished / and best **Chess-Movers** / can use the **Archers** / better / than the **Riders** / to their advantage / and that for the following grounds / and reasons:

For / (1.) when it comes to the end / so that in both armies / there are not many pieces / left / one can more easily / give **Checkmate** / with two **Archers** / or with one **Archer** / and one **Rider** / alongside the **King** / than one can accomplish the same / (as can be seen from the attached diagrams) / with two **Riders** / without cornering the **King**: unless there were perhaps / still some **Soldiers** [[notes: *Soldaten*, the modern Pawns]] / present / which in the meantime / (until the **King** / were driven to the right place / where the **Riders** might seize him) / could be moved.
