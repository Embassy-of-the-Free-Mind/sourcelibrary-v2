Black, meanwhile, has only three or four remaining. Although, according to *Damiano’s* style, White (as can be seen shortly before No. 25 of the 3rd Chapter) only wins three **Pawns** and no more. Let every man judge for himself which is better: to win four or five **Pawns** plus a **Rook** for a **Knight**, or to win only three **Pawns** without any other advantage. And furthermore, even if Black does not move the **King’s Pawn** forward (No. 25 of Chap. 3), White still wins the entire game by force, and not just three **Pawns** as *Damianus*—without further reflection—imagined, and indeed in the following manner.

Instead, before he gives check, he should move the **King’s Rook’s Pawn** to twenty-five.
