**The Chess Players**

->[A detailed copperplate engraving of two noblemen at a chess table]<-

[[notes: This engraving by Jacob van der Heyden provides a window into the social world of early 17th-century chess. On the left, an aristocratic man in a stiff ruff collar and decorated doublet—typical high-fashion of the era—is caught mid-move, his hand poised over the board. To the right, his opponent relaxes with a drinking glass. The setting is a private, well-appointed interior featuring a draped bed or alcove, emphasizing that chess was a sophisticated domestic pursuit for the elite. The fine detail of the individual chess pieces reflects the period's interest in the material culture of the game. This plate is famously associated with the work of Augustus the Younger, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg, who wrote under the pseudonym Gustavus Selenus.]]

->I. ab heyden sculpsit.<-
[[notes: "I. ab heyden" refers to Jacob van der Heyden (1573–1645), a prolific Flemish-born engraver and publisher who worked in Strasbourg. The Latin "sculpsit" (he engraved it) was the standard signature for the artist who physically carved the image into the copper plate.]]
