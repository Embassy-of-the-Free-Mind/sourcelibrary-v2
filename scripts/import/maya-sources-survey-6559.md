## Penn Libraries — Berendt-Brinton Linguistic Collection (Ms. Coll. 700)

**Access.** Every digitized item is a directory on OPenn (repository 0002, Penn Manuscripts): an HTML page at `https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0002/html/mscoll700_itemN.html`, a TEI description at `https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0002/mscoll700_itemN/data/mscoll700_itemN_TEI.xml`, and the page images as plain static files: master TIFFs at `.../mscoll700_itemN/data/master/<prefix>_NNNN.tif`, web JPEGs at `.../data/web/<prefix>_NNNN_web.jpg`, and thumbnails at `.../data/thumb/<prefix>_NNNN_thumb.jpg`. `<prefix>` is a numeric id that differs per item (for example 7275 for item 49 and 13774 for item 228); the table gives each item's first web image. No login is needed. Because these are ordinary HTTP directories, you can bulk-download one item's folder with wget/rsync-style mirroring of `Data/0002/mscoll700_itemN/`. The full list of items is in `https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0002_contents.csv`, which has 143 mscoll700 rows. IIIF: OPenn itself does not serve IIIF. Penn's newer Digital Collections site (digitalcollections.library.upenn.edu, e.g. `/items/742be853-63d9-4e40-9df3-e2dc1c05b4fa`, "Sermones en lengua maya") and Colenda probably expose IIIF manifests, but **unverified**: both hosts refused connections from this box. Survey note: all *.library.upenn.edu hosts refused direct connections from this machine and from WebFetch, so every OPenn fact below was read through the r.jina.ai reader proxy, from the item TEI files and HTML pages. An importer run from Hetzner should test direct access first.

## Princeton — Garrett-Gates Mesoamerican Manuscripts

Surveyed 2026-10-15, read-only. The source is the DPUL Spotlight exhibit https://dpul.princeton.edu/mesoamerican: 268 records, and all 268 carry a Figgy IIIF manifest. 210 are catalogued as "Mayan languages" (incl. 2 tagged `quc`), and 150 of those are "No Known Copyright". Categories: Yucatec ~80, K'iche' 113, Caste Wars 26, Wills 51, Chilam Balam 6, Theologia Indorum 8. The material is Garrett-Gates C0744.01 plus Princeton Mesoamerican MSS C0940 and Garrett Mesoamerican MSS C0744.

**How to get the images.** The HTML item pages (`dpul.princeton.edu/mesoamerican/catalog/<ark>`) sit behind a Cloudflare "Verify you are human" wall when fetched by a bot. The JSON endpoints answer plain `curl` without one:
- `https://dpul.princeton.edu/mesoamerican/catalog.json?q=&search_field=all_fields&per_page=100&page=N` lists the records (3 pages).
- `https://dpul.princeton.edu/mesoamerican/catalog/<ark>.json` gives the full Solr document. The manifest is in `content_metadata_iiif_manifest_field_ssi`, rights in `readonly_edm-rights_tesim`, and extent and call number in `readonly_extent_tesim` / `readonly_call-number_tesim`.

Manifests follow `https://figgy.princeton.edu/concern/scanned_resources/<uuid>/manifest` (IIIF Presentation 2.x). Each manifest has a machine-readable `license` (rightsstatements.org URI). Each canvas has a level-2 IIIF Image service at `https://iiif-cloud.princeton.edu/iiif/2/<path>/intermediate_file`, so `/full/full/0/default.jpg` gives the full image. Canvases also expose a `rendering` download under `https://figgy.princeton.edu/downloads/...`. Multi-volume items return a IIIF *collection* of per-volume manifests (e.g. Chilam Balam Texts: "Vol 1", "Vol 2"). Canvas counts include covers and blanks, so they run about 2× the leaf count (Bacabs: 164 leaves → 348 canvases). Each record also links to a Princeton catalog record (catalog.princeton.edu/catalog/99…6421).

**Rights / terms of use (verbatim).**
- Per item, two values are used: "No Known Copyright" = http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NKC/1.0/ (manifest `license`). "Copyright Not Evaluated" = http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/CNE/1.0/ (e.g. Codex Pérez, Great Book of Maya Medicine).
- NKC text (https://rightsstatements.org/page/NKC/1.0/): "The organization that has made the Item available reasonably believes that the Item is not restricted by copyright or related rights, but a conclusive determination could not be made." … "You are free to use this Item in any way that is permitted by the copyright and related rights legislation that applies to your use."
- Princeton Special Collections, "Do I need permission to quote or publish from Special Collections material?" (https://faq.library.princeton.edu/special-collections/faq/56766, updated Aug 19, 2024): "No, you do not need Princeton's permission to publish material from Special Collections, but you may need permission from the copyright holder. While Princeton University Library owns the physical materials in its collections, we generally do not own the copyright to those materials. … The user is solely responsible for determining whether any permissions related to copyright, privacy, publicity, or any other rights are necessary for your intended use of the Library's materials, and for obtaining all required permissions. We do not charge any permission or usage fees for publication."

**Site structure and how images are served.** There are three versions of the site.
- **2008–2013, `bibliotecavirtualdeyucatan.com.mx` (Instituto de Cultura de Yucatán / CAIHY).** A PHP catalogue (`/biblioteca/catalogo_libros`, `catalogo_libros.php?IdTipoDocumento=N`). The categories were Literatura, Historia, Obras en lengua maya, Monografías, Tesis, Publicaciones periódicas, Manuscritos, Facsímiles, Partituras, Fotografías and Grabaciones. Item pages (`detalle_obra.php?idlibro=N`) give a short record and link to **one whole-book PDF**. Seen: `http://bvy.bibliotecavirtualdeyucatan.com.mx/A_D._Juan_Ruiz_de_Alarcon.pdf`, plus `/archivos/libros/<Title>.pdf` and `/archivos/periodicos/<Periodical>/<issue-date>.pdf`.
- **2016–2017, `bibliotecavirtual.yucatan.gob.mx` (`index.php`, `acervo.php`, `busq_rap.php`, `tesoros.php`).** It claimed "más de 450 mil páginas de cerca de 20 mil documentos yucatecos", including "manuscritos de los siglos XVII al XIX ... obras en maya". The user manual (`/pdfs/manual_usuario.pdf`, 2017 capture) says search runs over the physical Biblioteca Yucatanense catalogue. Digitized records open either as PDFs ("los archivos en pdf los puedes descargar y guardar en tu computadora") or in an "acercamiento progresivo (AP)" deep-zoom viewer "desde el repositorio digital". It also warns that "aún no están digitalizados todos los materiales".
- **2024–2026 (Bootstrap site, `/pages/caihly`, `/pages/reservado`, `/pages/biblioteca`, `/pages/hemeroteca`, `/pages/autores?id=N`).** Mostly descriptive pages about the collections plus an anthology of authors. "Búsqueda" now points to the Janium OPAC.
