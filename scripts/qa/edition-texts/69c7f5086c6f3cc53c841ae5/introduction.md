## The author

Johannes Indagine (1467–1537) was a German humanist and priest. His title page describes him as parish priest of Steynheym (Steinheim) and dean of St Leonhard in Frankfurt.

## The book

Indagine's *Introductiones apotelesmaticae*, in Latin, was printed at Strasbourg in 1522 by Johannes Schott, who issued this German version the next year. The title page says Indagine himself compiled and translated it ("züsamen verordnet / vnd verteütscht"), and lists what it contains: chiromancy, "from looking at the hands"; physiognomy, "from the sight of a person"; natural astrology "after the course of the sun"; the complexion of every person; the natural influences of the planets; the faces of the twelve signs; and some rules for recognising human illnesses, "never before described or printed in this way". It was printed under an imperial privilege for six years.

The colophon, read from the image, gives the imprint: *Getruckt zů Straßburg / durch Joannem Schott Bůchtrucker zům Thyergarten. Anno. M. D. xxiij* — printed at Strasbourg by Johannes Schott, printer at the Thiergarten, 1523.

Much of the book is pictures: woodcuts of hands with their lines and mounts, horoscope figures for the astrology, and the planets riding in their chariots.

## This copy

The copy photographed is in the Bibliotheca Philosophica Hermetica, Amsterdam.
