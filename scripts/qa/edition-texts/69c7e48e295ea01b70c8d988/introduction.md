## The author

Charles de Bovelles (c. 1475 – after 1566) was a French philosopher and mathematician, and a canon of Noyon. His *Géométrie en françoys* of 1511 is probably the first geometry printed in French.

## The book

Bovelles's practical geometry exists in several forms: in Latin, as the *Geometricae introductionis libri sex* (Paris, 1503), and in French, as the *Livre singulier et utile touchant l'art et pratique de géométrie* (Paris, 1542). This book is the French work again, five years later. Its title page, read from the image, reads: *Geometrie practique, composee par le noble Philosophe maistre Charles de Bouelles, & nouuellement par luy reueue, augmentee, & grandement enrichie* — composed by Bovelles and newly revised, enlarged and much enriched by him. It was printed at Paris by Regnaud Chaudière and his son Claude in 1547; the printer's device on the title page carries the words *Hanc aciem sola retundit virtus*.

The book teaches the constructions of geometry — lines, angles, figures, measures — with a woodcut diagram for each, and the diagrams are as much the text as the words.
