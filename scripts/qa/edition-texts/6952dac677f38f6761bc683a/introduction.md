## The author

Robert Fludd was born in 1574 at Milgate House, Bearsted, in Kent, and died in London in 1637. He took his BA (1597) and MA (1598) at St John's College, Oxford, spent the years 1598 to 1604 on the Continent, and returned to take his doctorate in medicine at Oxford in 1605. After several refusals he was admitted to the College of Physicians in London in 1609, and practised medicine in London for the rest of his life. In 1616 he published at Leiden an *Apologia compendiaria* in defence of the Rosicrucian brotherhood.

## The book

The engraved title page announces a history "of both worlds, the greater and the lesser" — the cosmos and the human being — "divided into two volumes according to the difference of the world". This is the first volume, on the greater world, the macrocosm. It has two treatises.

The first treatise describes the macrocosm's metaphysical origin and its physical course through generation and corruption: the creation, light and darkness, the heavens and their inhabitants, the elements and the weather.

The second, *De Naturae Simia* (On the Ape of Nature), treats the arts by which human beings imitate nature. The title page lists its eleven parts: arithmetic, music, geometry, perspective, painting, the military art, the science of motion, the science of time, cosmography, astrology and geomancy. Much of it is practical — tables, instruments, fortifications, machines, sundials — and most of it is illustrated.

## The edition reproduced here

The title page gives the imprint: printed at Oppenheim by Hieronymus Galler, at the expense of Johann Theodor de Bry, 1617. The whole work appeared in parts between 1617 and 1624.

The copy photographed for this edition, held by the Getty Research Institute and digitised by the Internet Archive, binds the first treatise of 1617 with the second edition of *De Naturae Simia*, printed at Frankfurt by Caspar Rötel for the heirs of Johann Theodor de Bry in 1624 (its title page, source page 229, reads *Editio secunda … Anno M. DC. XXIV*). That title page is signed by the engraver Matthäus Merian.

## Reception

Fludd's harmonic cosmology drew a direct reply from Johannes Kepler, who criticised it in an appendix to his *Harmonices Mundi* (1619). Fludd answered in *Veritatis proscenium* (Frankfurt, 1621) and again in *Monochordon mundi symphoniacum* (1622). Marin Mersenne attacked him in his *Quaestiones celeberrimae in Genesim* (1623), and Pierre Gassendi published an *Examen philosophiae Fluddanae* in 1630.
