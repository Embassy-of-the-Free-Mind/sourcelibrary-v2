## The author

Michael Maier was born in Rendsburg, Holstein, in 1568, and died in Magdeburg in 1622. A physician, alchemist, poet and amateur composer, he entered the service of the Emperor Rudolf II as physician and imperial counsellor in September 1609, and was made a Count Palatine ten days later — the "Imperial. Consistor. Comit." after his name on the title page.

## The book

In 1617 Johann Theodor de Bry published at Oppenheim Maier's *Atalanta fugiens*, an emblem book of fifty copper engravings, each with an epigram, a musical fugue and a discourse. The engravings are generally attributed to Matthäus Merian.

This book is the same work seventy years later, under a new title and without the music. Its title page (read from the image) calls it a *Scrutinium chymicum* — a chemical investigation of the secrets of more secret nature — made up of "emblems most aptly engraved in copper, accurately fitted to the eyes and the intellect", with epigrams, "brought forth a second time for the good and benefit of the most ample Chemical Republic". It was printed at Frankfurt in 1687 by Johann Philipp Andreae, at the expense of the bookseller Georg Heinrich Oehrling.

Each of the fifty emblems has a motto, a picture, an epigram and a discourse; the last is Emblema L. The pictures carry the argument: a reader who knows the fifty plates knows the shape of the book.
