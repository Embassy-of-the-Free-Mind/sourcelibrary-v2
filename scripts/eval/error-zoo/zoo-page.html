How to read this page

A class is one kind of error

Each has a code. The letter says where the error starts: I the page image O the transcription (OCR) T the English D the display E data derived from the text

Severity is about the reader

serious the reader is misled or text is lost. moderate confusing but recoverable. minor cosmetic. The reviewer sets it per finding.

Findings and pages are different counts

One page can carry several findings. "Pages" counts pages with at least one. Rates on this page are shares of reviewed pages.

Which numbers to trust most

The weekly audit draws one page from each of many random books, so it stands for the whole library. The other review stores were aimed at particular books, so they run hotter.

Map

Where errors cluster, by language and age

Each square is one language in one half-century. The number and the brightness are the share of reviewed pages with a serious error. The colour says what the errors there are like: amber when most are moderate, red when most are serious. Minor findings are left out of the colour, because they turned out to carry no signal. Hover or tab to a square for its numbers.

Hatched squares have fewer than reviewed pages, too few for a rate. Empty squares have none.

Detection

Which errors are easy or hard to catch

A cheap risk score ranks every page from its text and catalogue facts, without reading the scan. The score was never shown the page it ranks: it was trained on other books. Each mark is the share of pages carrying that error whose score lands in the top 30%. Far right means the score finds it; far left means only a reader with the scan will.

How far to trust this. Every label here comes from a single model reviewer. No page has yet been read by two independent reviewers on the same text, so nobody knows how often two careful readers would agree on "serious". Within the random-book audit, the score finds serious transcription errors well (AUC 0.85) and serious errors in general only moderately (0.76).
