Whenever a physician speaks about the sick, is it shameful not to be able to follow what he says, or to contribute anything to it, as if he were just any ordinary craftsman? And whenever a judge, or someone else among those we mentioned a little earlier speaks, is it not absurd in these matters neither to follow what is said, nor to be able to contribute anything? Why would it not be absurd, O Socrates, to have nothing to contribute on such great matters? Shall we say then, I asked, that in these things as well the philosopher must be like a pentathlete, and holding second place is useless so long as one of the specialists is present; or rather, that first of all he ought not to surrender the management of his own household to anyone, nor hold second place in this, but correct everything himself, judging rightly, if indeed he manages his own household properly? He agreed with me. And I added: Then if a friend entrusts him with the conduct of his life, or a city entrusts any matter to be decided or judged, would it not be shameful, my friend, for him to hold second or third place in these matters, instead of standing forth as the leader? So it seems to me, he said. Far be it, then, most excellent man, for philosophizing to be the learning of many things and the dabbling in crafts. When I had said this, the wise rival, blushing at what had been said, fell silent; but the unlearned man affirmed that this was so, and all the others praised what had been said.
