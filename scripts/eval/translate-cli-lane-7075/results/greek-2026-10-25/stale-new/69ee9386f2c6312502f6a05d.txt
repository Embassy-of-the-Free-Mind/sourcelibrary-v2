Natural talent is of the greatest importance in the pursuit of all arts, even the lighter ones. But just as one kind of mind is more suitable for Mathematics, another for Poetry, and another for other arts, so Textual Criticism also demands a natural talent that is proper and peculiar to itself. Its entire power, moreover, arises from two faculties of the mind: from the swift restoration of images previously perceived, and from a prompt and ready subtlety of judgment. Imagine a Critic, such as we are depicting, seeking a remedy for an obstructed and corrupted passage of a writer, and carefully observe by what mental actions he arrives at that which he seeks.
