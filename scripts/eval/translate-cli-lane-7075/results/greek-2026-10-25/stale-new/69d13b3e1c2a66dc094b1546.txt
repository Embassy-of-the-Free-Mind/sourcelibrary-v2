->TENTH SECTION.<-

->CHAPTER I.<-
**T**here *is no way to happiness outside of religion. Only the priests <note>the Egyptians</note> apply themselves to knowing the Gods, to themselves, and to the truth.*

->CHAPTER II.<-
*Priests do not look to empty glory in sacred operations. They assign no place to delusion. Slander ought not to be turned against true priests because some impostors are found.*

->CHAPTER III.<-
*Other arts devised for foreseeing future events are not to be compared with divine <term>mantike</term> <gloss>prophecy</gloss>.*

->CHAPTER IV.<-
*Divine prophecy brings forth many goods for us. The Gods introduce it, or do not introduce it, into our souls according to our need.*

->CHAPTER V.<-
*Man, having fallen from the vision of God into mortal life, will only then be happy if he can be restored to his prior state. This is accomplished through the knowledge of God, such as that knowledge is in contrast to the knowledge belonging to this life.*

->CHAPTER VI.<-
*<term>Theurgy</term> <gloss>divine working</gloss> prepares the soul and accustoms it to divine union: the stages through which it leads to the <term>demiurge</term> <gloss>creator deity</gloss> and the eternal <term>logos</term> <gloss>divine reason</gloss>.*

->CHAPTER VII.<-
*The Egyptians establish one good as divine, namely God himself; another as human, union with God. Bitys. Theurgists are uniquely occupied with these matters. Nor is it credible that they err, or are deceived, in the search for happiness.*
