<margin>17</margin>Following this, the <term>ephors</term> <gloss>senior Spartan magistrates</gloss> called out the military levy for the two remaining <term>morai</term> <gloss>Spartan army divisions</gloss> up to the age of forty years from manhood <note>meaning men up to about fifty-eight years of age</note>. They also sent out troops from the divisions abroad up to the same age. For previously, in the expedition against the Phocians, they had called up men only up to thirty-five years from manhood. They likewise ordered those who had been left behind holding administrative offices at that time to join the army. <margin>18</margin>Now <term>Agesilaus</term> <note>king of Sparta</note> had not yet recovered his strength from illness, so the city instructed his son <term>Archidamus</term> to take command. The men of Tegea joined the campaign eagerly, for the faction of <term>Stasippus</term>, who favored Spartan interests and held great influence in their city, was still alive. The Mantineans also joined the campaign with vigorous spirit from their villages, for they happened to be governed by an aristocracy. The Corinthians, Sicyonians, Phliasians, and Achaeans followed with great enthusiasm as well, and other cities dispatched soldiers. Both the <term>Lacedaemonians</term> <gloss>Spartans</gloss> themselves and the Corinthians manned triremes, and they requested the Sicyonians to assist in manning them, intending to convey the army across the sea upon these vessels. <margin>19</margin>Archidamus, on his part, offered sacrifices for the sea crossing.

Meanwhile, immediately after the battle, the Thebans dispatched a messenger crowned with a wreath to Athens.
