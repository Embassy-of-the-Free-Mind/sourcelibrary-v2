Persuading me with very harsh words, he says that two causes are supposed to govern my nature: good things and bad things. I laugh heartily at humanity. For they bring these upon themselves. Being devoid of upright deeds, they become sluggish under every suggestion, and for no benefit they endure never-ending labors. They dissolve the boundaries of the earth and the boundless recesses of unmeasured desire, melting silver and gold, and never resting from this acquisition, but constantly agitated beforehand so that more becomes less to them. And they are not ashamed of being called fools, because they dig chasms in the earth with bound hands. Some of these men are destroyed when the sheer earth collapses upon them. Others, enduring this necessity for a long time without relief, close their eyes as if in punishment in their own homeland, seeking silver and gold, tracing dust and investigating shavings, gathering one sand from one place and another from elsewhere, cutting out the veins of the earth, creating boundless excess from the mother earth. Yet this very earth, being small and the same, they both marvel at and trample underfoot. For whatever earth is burdensome and hidden, they love it; yet they outrage that which is open to day. Some buy dogs, others horses. Others demarcate vast lands, inscribing them as their own private property; and desiring to surpass many, they cannot even govern themselves. They hasten to marry women whom shortly thereafter they cast out. They love, and then they hate. They long with desire, and then abandon it completely. What is this empty, unreasoning haste, differing in nothing from madness? They wage civil wars; they bid farewell to peace. They strike down kings, commit slaughter, dig into the ground, search for silver. Finding silver, they want to purchase land. Having bought it, they sell the fruits.
