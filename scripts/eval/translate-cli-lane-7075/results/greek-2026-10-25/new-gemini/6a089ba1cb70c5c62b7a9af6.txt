18. Soon after this, Clodius entered upon the <term>aedileship</term> <gloss>magistracy in charge of public works and games</gloss> in the consulship of Philippus and Marcellinus <note>56 BC</note>. He had been elected through a political faction to escape trial. He immediately brought an indictment against Milo for collecting gladiators. Indeed, what Clodius himself was doing and for which he was on trial, he now turned around and charged against Milo. He did this not because he expected to convict Milo, since Milo had many powerful supporters, especially Cicero and Pompey, but so that under this pretext he could attack Milo and insult Pompey and Cicero. 19. Among his other schemes, he devised the following trick: he instructed his partisans that whenever he asked them in the assemblies, "Who was it that did or said such-and-such?" they should shout out together, "Pompey!" He then frequently and unexpectedly raised all sorts of accusations against Pompey regarding his physical traits or anything else, asking about them one by one as if he were not referring to him at all.
