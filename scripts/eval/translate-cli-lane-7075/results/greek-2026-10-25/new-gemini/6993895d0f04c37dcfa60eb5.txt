It is named both <term>sialon</term> <gloss>saliva</gloss> and <term>ptyalon</term> <gloss>spittle</gloss>, of which, I think, a certain difference might be recognized: its faculty must needs differ in animals, whether one's own or belonging to others, and indeed whether we alone have eaten or drunk, or even before this while we are fasting. For just as urine, biles, and sweat, so also must spittle make known something potent or vigorous of the underlying conditions. For perhaps both would tend toward being relieving, though not before eating or drinking. For this reason, with this saliva, people treat the <term>leichenes</term> <gloss>eruptions or scabs</gloss> of children by moistening the scabs with saliva from someone who has not yet eaten. Then they also rub the affected skin, doing this frequently so that it softens and is <unclear>apo</unclear>tendered, in the manner of <gloss>dissolving</gloss>, then testing the power coming from the saliva. But many rustics might not find it, taking it instead of acacia for the eyes, for healings of such ailments are also attributed to it. And indeed it is applied against water snakes, whence those who have eaten do not benefit in like manner, but those who are fasting provide them with strong benefit. And indeed upon soft and thicker bodies, someone now chews wheat bread. Thus chewed by saliva, it stops differences and other pours. And only such bread speaks a small quantity of blood. But if one chews, and thus tastes it, spittle is most hostile to beasts that kill human beings, just as the poet says even of ants. And indeed someone tasting it, just as if killing a scorpion in food, kills it of itself just like fire. Even if in turn one spits saliva continuously upon the same creatures. Then also casting spittle, and when one spits cleanly, it kills like fire. And scorpions die solely from this spittle without enduring eating, being relieved of them. And suffering this very thing quickly from all these swift-passing things, they spit slowly.
