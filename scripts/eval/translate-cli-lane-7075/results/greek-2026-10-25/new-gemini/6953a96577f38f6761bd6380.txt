Having spent much of the day in private discussions over these matters, we parted again with our hopes unfulfilled. Nevertheless, he again claimed and promised that, when we assembled once more, he would grant full liberty of speech, as was fitting, and would no longer advocate equally for Palamas <note>Gregory Palamas, the theologian of Hesychasm</note>, nor lend all his support to that party. 

(3.) Six days elapsed from that point, until their nets against us were adequately prepared and readied. For what they had failed to achieve in those two previous assemblies, they now sought to rectify, as they thought, to their own advantage, and with greater boldness they once more summoned us to the arena of the contest. Once again that assembly and the imperial tribunal held us in attendance.

It seemed better to us, parrying the plots of those lying in wait right from the very start, to read aloud publicly the sacred <term>symbolon</term> <gloss>creed</gloss> of the Catholic faith, in accordance with the common and customary profession of the Orthodox. (2.) When this had been done, the more notorious of Palamas's blasphemous doctrines, selected by us, were read aloud publicly by our party, while his writings were set alongside them, clearly proving the complete absence of any difference. Hence, it was impossible for him to shrink back or evade any of the accusations, although he attempted to do so many times, and many times...
