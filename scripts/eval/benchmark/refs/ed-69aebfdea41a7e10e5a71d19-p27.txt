in the very same solid Vessel, you may
make the very same two contiguous Liquids
to alter their Surfaces; for taking
a small Wine-Glass, or such like Vessel,
and pouring water gently into it, you
shall perceive the surface of the water all
the way concave, till it rise even with
the top, when you shall find it (if you
gently and carefully poure in more) to
grow very protuberant and convex; the
reason of which is plain, for that the
solid sides of the containing body are no
longer extended, to which the water
does more readily adhere, than the air;
but it is henceforth to be included with
air, which would reduce it into a hemisphere,
but by reason of its gravity,
it is flatted into an Oval. Quicksilver
also which to Glass is more incongruous
than Air (and thereby being put into a
Glass-Pipe, will not adhere to it, but
by the more congruous air will be forced
to have a very protuberant surface, and
to rise higher in a greater than a lesser
Pipe) this Quicksilver to clean Metal,
especially to Gold, Silver, Tin, Lead,
&c. Iron excepted, is more congruous
than Air, and will not only stick to it,
