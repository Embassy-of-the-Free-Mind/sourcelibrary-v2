26. §. Some few of the Conjunctions therein set down, may not
be found actually existent in Nature . The abatement of which, will
be much more than compensated two ways. First , by the other Six
Species of Simple Tasts , which are also sometimes compounded. And by
other more complex Conjunctions , as of many Quadruples , and perhaps
some Quintuple or Sextuple ones. Thus far of the Simple Species ,
and Conjunctions of Tasts .


27. §. THE DEGREES of Tasts are also numerous; and
each Species , in every Conjunction , capable of Variation herein. For
the more accurate observing whereof, it will be best, To take those
Bodies , whose Tasts are, as near as may be, the same in Specie : and
that those be first tasted, which are less strong; whereby the true Degree 
will be more precisely taken.


28. §. The Tasts of Bodies will thus appear to be varied, in most
Species unto Five Degrees ; and in some of them, unto Ten . So the
Root of Turmerick , is bitter in the First Degree ; of Gentian , in the
Tenth . The Root of Carduus Benedictus , is Hot in the First Degree ;
the Green Pods or Seed-Cases of Clematis peregrina , in the Tenth . So
that, allowing some to vary under Five ; yet by a moderate estimate,
we may reckon every Species , one with another, to be varied by at
least Five Degrees . Which being added to the several Species of Tasts ,
in all the Treble Conjunctions of the aforesaid Table , come to 1800 sensible
and defineable Variations of Taste . And these are the Diversities 
of Taste , with respect to the Sensation it self.
