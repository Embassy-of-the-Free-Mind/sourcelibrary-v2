 Sell bark to the Tanner, er timber ye fell, 
 cut lowe by the ground, or elſe do ye not well. 
 In breaking, ſaue crooked, or mill & for ſhipps: 
 and euer in heawing, ſaue carpenters chipps. 
 
 
 3 
 Firſt ſee it well fenſed, or heawers begin, 
 then ſe it well ſtaddled, without & within. 
 thus being preſerued, & huſbandly donne: 
 ſhall ſooner raiſe profit, to thee or thy ſonne. 
 
 
 4 
 Leaue growing for ſtaddles, the likeiſt & beſt, 
 though ſeller & buyer, diſpatcheth the reſt. 
 In buſhet, in hedgerowe, in groue & in wood: 
 this leſſon obſerued, is needefull & good. 
 
 
 5 
 Saue elme, aſhe & crabtre, for cart & for plough, 
 ſaue ſtep for a ſtile, of the crotch of the bough. 
 Saue hazell for forkes, ſaue ſallow for rake: 
 ſaue huluer & thorne, there of flayle for to make. 
 
 
 6 
 Make riddance of carryge, er yere go about, 
 for ſpoyling of plant, that is newely come out. 
 To carter (with oxen) this meſſage I bring: 
 leaue oxen abrode, for annoying the ſpring.
