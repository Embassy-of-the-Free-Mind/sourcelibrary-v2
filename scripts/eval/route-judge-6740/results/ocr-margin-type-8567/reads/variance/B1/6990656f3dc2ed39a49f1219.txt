### Overview and Summary
This page comes from Robert Burton's *The Anatomy of Melancholy* (Part 2, Section 3, Member 4, page 410), under the section treating remedies or consolations against servitude and imprisonment. 

* **Theme of Servitude vs. Freedom:** The text reflects on how people often torment themselves over perceived constraints. It argues that many who believe themselves unfree are actually free from hard labor, sickness, and drudgery, yet they naturally yearn for whatever is forbidden.
* **The Milanese Citizen:** Burton cites Gerolamo Cardano regarding an elderly citizen of Milan who had never left the city walls in sixty years, but upon being formally ordered by the Prince never to leave, died of grief because his freedom was taken away.
* **Universal Imprisonment:** The discussion extends from servitude to imprisonment, suggesting that in a broader sense all humans live as prisoners—confined to islands, limited by climate (such as northern peoples confined by winter cold or dwellers in hot climates restricted to the night), or choosing seclusion for study or religious devotion (referencing Demosthenes, monks, and hermits).
