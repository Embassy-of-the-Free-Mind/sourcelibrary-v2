<header>Part.2.Sec.3. *Cure of Melancholy.* Memb 4.</header>

Courtiers to lust and ambition, and all slaves to our affections; who is free? Why then dost thou repine? *Satis est potens, Hierom* saith, *qui servire non cogitur.* Thou carriest no burdens, thou art no prentise, no drudge, and thousands want that liberty, those pleasures which thou hast. Thou art not sicke, & what wouldst thou have? but *nitimur in vetitum*, we must all eat of the forbidden fruit. Were wee enjoyned to goe to such and such places, we would not willingly goe: but being barred of our liberty, this alone torments our wandring soule that we may not goe. A cittezen of ours, saith *k* *Cardan*, was 60 yeares of age, and had never beene forth of <margin>*k Consol. lib. 5.*</margin> the walls of the citty of *Millan*, the Prince hearing of it commanded him not to stirre out, being now forbidden that which all his life he had neglected, he earnestly desired, & being denied, *dolore confectus mortem obijt*, he died for griefe.
