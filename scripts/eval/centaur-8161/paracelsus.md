Yes, in substance, but not in those words. The idea is Paracelsus's. The slogan is a later compression of it.

**The source I found**

Paracelsus, *Complete Medical, Chemical, and Surgical Works* (Genève: De Tournes, 1658), p. 254 [scan 297]. It's the Latin *Opera Omnia*, and the page has this line:

> "Dosis sola facit, vt venenum non sit."

Source Library's English translation reads:

> "All things are poison, and nothing exists without poison. The dose alone makes it so that it is not a poison."

- **Page with scan:** https://sourcelibrary.org/q/u19vXlxpHUynRh5sMgELH6KspN
- **Book:** https://sourcelibrary.org/book/paracelsus-complete-works-latin-paracelsus

**Caveats**
- **Wording:** The text says the dose is what makes a thing *not* a poison. "The dose makes the poison" is a tidy paraphrase of the same point.
- **Edition:** This is a 1658 collected edition, printed about 117 years after Paracelsus died in 1541. I only checked this one page. I can't tell from it whether the wording goes back to his own manuscripts or was shaped by editors.
- **Translation:** The English is Source Library's own 2026 translation, and the metadata doesn't say whether it was machine-made. If an argument turns on a single word, check the Latin on the scan.
- **Where it comes from:** The sentence is widely linked to his *Septem Defensiones* (1538). My search found 28 results for that title, but I haven't opened any of them. This page is therefore not confirmed as that work, and I can't say which of his works it comes from.
- **Marginal note:** The scan has a marginal note, "All things are poisons, even to you." It appears only in that Rome copy.
