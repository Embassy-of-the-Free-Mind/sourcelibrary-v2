**Quote**

> "If the soul were not immortal: no animal would be more wretched than man."

Marsilio Ficino, *Platonic Theology* (*Theologia Platonica de immortalitate animorum*), Paris: Aegidium Gorbinum, 1491. Translated by Source Library (2026), p. 79. The copy is Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Munich, Ph.sp. 277.

**Caveats**
- This is a machine translation of the Latin, not Ficino's own words. If your argument turns on a particular word, check the Latin on the scan.
- The page opens Book One. It is the start of his case, not the whole argument. In outline, he says that without immortality humans would be the most wretched of creatures, because only humans desire the eternal and the divine. I have only the snippet to go on for that, so I haven't checked it against the full text.
- I haven't read his main arguments for immortality, which come later in the work. If you need those, the next step is to search for terms like *immortalitas*, *animus* and *intellectus* in the same book and read the pages that turn up.
- Ficino also takes up the soul's immortality in his letters. One letter in the *Divine Lettere* (page 264) says the soul's natural state is to live eternally in its body. I haven't pulled a quote from it.
- The search was marked partial because the "book" lane was degraded. That is one more reason to treat my list of Ficino sources as incomplete.
