Yes. Serious people in the 1500s did believe in natural magic, and two of them wrote it down.

## Giambattista della Porta, *Natural Magic in Twenty Books* (1589)

Della Porta was a Neapolitan natural philosopher. This is the Latin edition printed at Naples by Horatius Salvianus. In Book I, chapter 2, he distinguishes two kinds of magic:

> "They divide Magic itself into two kinds: the one infamous, unpropitious due to its commerce with unclean spirits"

That is the first half of a sentence. The tool I used capped quotations at about 125 characters, so I don't have the rest word for word. The fetch tool's summary of the surrounding passage is that the other kind is the active part of natural philosophy, that the Egyptians called Nature herself a Magus, and that the Magus is a minister of Nature rather than an artisan. I haven't seen that wording myself, so please check it against the page.

- **Citation:** Giambattista della Porta, *Natural Magic in Twenty Books*, Naples: Horatius Salvianus (USTC 406717), p. 20, trans. Source Library (2026). The copy is from the Wellcome Collection.

## Agrippa, *De occulta philosophia* (1531 and 1533)

Two search snippets give Agrippa's definition. They are search snippets, not full quote retrievals.

- Book II (1531), scan page 658: "Natural Magic, therefore, is nothing other than Physics and Astrology, from the collation of which it can be gathered..." The snippet cuts off there.
- 1533 edition, scan page 367: "Natural magic, therefore, is that which, having contemplated the powers of all natural and celestial things,..." This one also cuts off.

You can read both at https://sourcelibrary.org/book/agrippa-de-occulta-philosophia-book-ii-1531-nettesheim and https://sourcelibrary.org/book/de-occulta-philosophia-libri-tres-agrippa. Page numbers there are scan numbers, not printed page numbers.

## What this shows

Della Porta separates the "infamous" magic that deals with spirits from a magic he treats as part of the study of nature. Agrippa defines natural magic as physics plus astrology. For both, natural magic was a way of studying hidden powers in nature, not a rejection of natural philosophy. That is their claim, and I'm not saying their claims about those powers were true.
