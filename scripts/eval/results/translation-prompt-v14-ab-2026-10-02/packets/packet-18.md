<-*Dell'origine della Virtù.*->

**XL.** La virtù tiene la proprietà d'essere d'origine divina (1); conciossiachè queste tre sue parti tutte insieme costituiscono la vera virtù; e ciascuna di esse si trova sempre colle altre due congiunta. E in effetti nella prudenza avvi ancora la fortezza, in quantochè la mente nel ricercare il vero vi si travaglia e dura fatica, nè si ristà sino a che nol ritrovi; vi è anche la temperanza, essendo che è pur cosa savia l'ignorare talune cose. Nella temperanza poi si ritrova la prudenza, che ci consiglia a non trasandare le cose utili alla vita, per non riportarne detrimento; e perciò la Medicina è di origine divina, perchè dettata da questo vero eterno: vi è anche la fortezza, acciocchè sappia egli tenersi lungi dai piaceri. Infine nella fortezza si trova pure la prudenza per difendere l'onesto; vi è poi la temperanza, acciocchè i vincitori non vogliano distruggere i vinti, che possono senza danno serbare in vita.

<-*On the Origin of Virtue.*->

**XL.** Virtue possesses the property of being of divine origin (1); for these three parts of it, taken together, constitute true virtue, and each of them is always found joined with the other two. Indeed, within prudence there is also fortitude, insofar as the mind, in searching for the truth, labors and exerts itself, and does not rest until it finds it; there is also temperance, as it is a wise thing to ignore certain things. In temperance, one finds prudence, which counsels us not to neglect things useful to life, so as not to suffer detriment; and for this reason, Medicine is of divine origin, because it is dictated by this eternal truth; there is also fortitude, so that one may know how to keep oneself far from pleasures. Finally, in fortitude, one also finds prudence to defend what is honorable; there is also temperance, so that the victors do not wish to destroy the vanquished, whom they can keep alive without harm.

<-*On True Virtue.*->

**XLI.** Therefore, there is no virtue that stands alone, for where you truly find virtue, be certain that all the others are found united with it: since virtues that stand in isolation barely deserve, according to the words of St. Augustine (2), the name of virtue.

<-*The Origin of Divine Virtue.*->

**XL.** Virtue obtains the property of divine origin (1); for these three parts of it are one true virtue; and each of them is always intertwined with the other two. Truly, in prudence there is also fortitude, so that it labors in investigating the truth and does not rest until it is explored; there is also temperance, whereby it is wisdom to be ignorant of certain things. In temperance there is also prudence, so that one may not abstain from things useful to life to the point of one's own destruction: therefore the origin of Medicine is divine; for it is dictated by this eternal truth: there is also fortitude, so that one may sustain oneself by tempering desires. In fortitude there is also prudence, so that one may fight for the honorable; there is also temperance, whereby victors may not wish to destroy those whom they can save as the vanquished.
