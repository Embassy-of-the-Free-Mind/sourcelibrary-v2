AGAINST MEIDIAS.

<meta>Stephanus pagination 516 in margin</meta>

By the gods, they believed that I had suffered terrible things, and at the same time they wanted to exact justice for those things they had seen done to others by this man who is reckless and vile and no longer <margin>3</margin> to be restrained. Since these things are so, all that it was proper for me to preserve has been justly kept for you. I am present, as you see, to bring the accusation now that someone is bringing the case to trial. Many things, O men of Athens, though it was in my power to take money so as not to prosecute, I did not take, and I endured many <margin>4</margin> entreaties and favors and, by Zeus, threats. As for what remains among you after these things, by as much as this man has annoyed and harassed people (for I saw just now before the courts what he was doing), by that much more do I hope to obtain justice. For I would not judge any of you poorly, either that you would neglect those things about which you yourselves have shown concern for me, or that, so that Meidias may commit outrage with impunity in the future, any of you who have sworn an oath will vote for anything <margin>5</margin> other than what you deem just. If, therefore, O men of Athens, I were intending to prosecute him for illegal proposals or false embassy or some <note>other</note> such charge, I would not think it necessary to make any requests of you, believing that for the prosecutor in such matters it is appropriate only to provide proof, and for the defendant to plead his case. But since this man corrupted the judges and because of this the <term>tripod</term> <gloss>a victory trophy for the chorus</gloss> was unjustly taken from the tribe, and I myself have received blows and have been treated with such outrage as I do not know if any other <note>chorus sponsor</note> has ever been outraged, I am now entering into this charge which the people made, having been angered and provoked on account of these things, and I will not hesitate to make a request. For if it is possible to say this, I am now the one on trial, if it is indeed a misfortune to have been outraged and to receive no justice. I therefore ask of all of you, O men of the jury, and I implore you, first of all...

D. I do not see how that could be in any way. 
M. What if the figure shows three straight, unequal lines? Can there be angles in such a figure? 
D. I do not see that at all. 
M. You speak correctly. Tell me, I pray, which figure seems better and more beautiful to you? The one made with equal lines, or the one with unequal lines? 
D. Who would doubt that the one in which equality prevails is better?

D. I do not know who would not. 
M. Now look at the figure which is completed with three equal angles. What is in it, three angles? That is, placed from the other side. Is it a line or an angle?

The earthy substance, which is incorporated into the sulfur, is clean and most subtle, suspended vapor-like throughout the whole substance of the radical moisture of that sulfur. From this, it remains that the heat of that sulfur is constitutional, departing in no way from a temperate state, and this is like the masculine virtue in the composition of gold. Similarly, its quicksilver has two substances purified to their extremes. The third part, which is in the heat itself, is subtilized, not only materially divided but also vapor-like; it is most subtle, having been often sublimated in the cavities of the earth by the life-giving heat of the sun and stars. Similarly, the watery part is also subtilized through the same mode of repeated subtilization, and since glass is thus present, it is subtilized finely. It is certain that when mixed together by the heat and the disposition of the place, which closes in and reflects the vapor upon itself, they are bound by the strongest bond of mixture. Sulfur does not enter the composition of gold only through quality but through substance, and since it is a subtle substance, it will penetrate everywhere through the quicksilver and, by coagulating, it colors it. And since both are brought by sublimation to the form of the superior elements, which in the nature of transparency always proceed with a perpetual body, both will possess the fullness of purity.
