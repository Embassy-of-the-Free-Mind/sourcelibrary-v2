## XII.

It is therefore established that the fanatical opinions of the Paracelsians, Weigelians, Mystics, Quakers, and others—who are accustomed to being called by the common name of "the illuminated"—conspire in this: that besides the body and the rational soul, there is in man a certain substantial part granted to him divinely and, as most do not hide, granted from the very divine essence itself. The same doctrine regarding the three parts of man is explained quite verbosely by Felgenhawer in *The Forecourt, in the Holy and Most Holy Place of the Temple of God* <note>original: "Vorhoff/ im heiligen und Allerheiligsten am Tempel GOttes"</note>: the individual readings of whose fanatical little book sufficiently declare the reasons for the individual parts and the dreams of the Fanatics concerning them. VII. Q. *Whether a Genus in general is predicated univocally of a Genus of real being and a Genus of being of reason?* I answer. These two Genera, according to their material parts alone, agree among themselves analogically; because Being can only be predicated analogically of real being and being of reason. But indeed, if they are taken in the being of a genus and as they are of logical consideration, since the logical artifice and concept of both are precisely the same, there is no doubt that they agree univocally among themselves.

VIII. Q. *Since the highest Genus is as distant from the lowest as the lowest Genus is from the Species; why is one lowest Genus a universal other than a species, and the highest Genus not something other than the lowest genus?* I answer. That diversity is not to be sought from the greater or lesser distance that universals have in the predicamental line, but from their quidditative concept. Since, therefore, both the highest Genus and the lowest Genus are predicated as a material part, but the species as the whole essence, there can be no doubt that a species differs more from the lowest Genus than this does from the highest.
