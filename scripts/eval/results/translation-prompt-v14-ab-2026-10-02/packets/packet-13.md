I heard them narrating that they had traveled a long journey, having been provided with assistance by the king of Ethiopia and having been commended to neighboring kings, from whom they had pressed on to further regions. They said, "We arrived at immense marshes, the exit of which neither the inhabitants knew, nor could anyone hope to discover. For the plants and the water are so intertwined that the waters can be negotiated neither by foot nor by boat, unless it is a small one, capable of holding only one person, which the muddy and overgrown swamp might bear." There, he says, we saw two rocks from which a massive force of river water poured out. Whether this is the head of the <term>Nilus</term> <gloss>the Nile river</gloss> or merely an addition to it, or whether it is born there or returns to the earth after having previously completed a course, who are you to believe that it rises from a great subterranean lake? For it must have moisture scattered in many places, and gathered in the depths, so that it can erupt with such force. 

Some judge that fire and earthquakes share the same cause, while others do not. Primarily <term>Anaxagoras</term> <gloss>an ancient Greek philosopher</gloss>, who thinks that the earth is shaken by almost the same cause as the air, when, in the lower region, the spirit strikes the thick air, which is gathered into clouds in the same way that clouds are accustomed to break apart among us. The fire emerges from this collision of clouds and the movement of the displaced air. He himself rushes into the obstacles, seeking an exit, and tears apart whatever stands in his way, until, through narrow passages, he either finds a path to exit into the sky, or creates one through force or destruction. Others do not judge that fire is the cause of this, but rather that it burns in many places where it encounters obstacles and consumes whatever is nearest. If these things fall once they have been eaten away, then the movement of those parts that are deprived of their underlying supports follows, as they slide until they collapse, with nothing appearing to take the burden, and then it is calmed, leaving a vast chasm.
