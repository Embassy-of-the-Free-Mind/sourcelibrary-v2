### The System of the Well-field is Prepared in the 'Yi Tong'

The surface of the fields in an <term>Yi Tong</term> <gloss>an administrative unit of one hundred square li</gloss> measures eighteen thousand feet square. Adding the sixty-four feet for the <term>hui</term> <gloss>large drainage ditch</gloss> and path, one hundred forty-four feet for the <term>xu</term> <gloss>secondary ditch</gloss> and road, seven hundred twenty feet for the <term>gou</term> <gloss>canal</gloss> and embankment, and eight hundred feet for the <term>sui</term> <gloss>small field ditch</gloss> and path, the total area measures seventeen hundred twenty-eight feet square. Dividing this by six yields thirty thousand two hundred eighty-eight <term>bu</term> <gloss>paces</gloss>. Squaring this result yields a total area of nine hundred seventeen million, three hundred sixty-two thousand, nine hundred forty-four <term>bu</term>. Dividing this by the <term>mu fa</term> <gloss>standard measurement of one hundred square paces per mu</gloss>, one arrives at nine million, one hundred seventy-three thousand, six hundred twenty-nine and four hundred forty-four thousandths <term>mu</term>. Within this, the sixty-four <term>cheng</term> <gloss>administrative divisions</gloss> occupy an area of five hundred million.

If the sun has a yellow straight pillar, the sovereign establishes his authority, and the ministers are loyal. If a blue-red pillar appears, a minister will declare independence.

If the straight pillar is red in the center and blue on the outside, a self-declared ruler will not succeed. If it is yellow-white, moist, and lustrous, the ruler will succeed.

When blue and red vapors appear in a shape like two straight lines intersecting above, below, or to the sides of the sun, this is called a **Cross-Intersection** <term>original: *Jiao* (交)</term>, which signifies licentiousness. This vapor signifies licentious conduct. If a cross-shaped red cloud appears beside the sun, there will be military conflict below it.

If two vapors appear on either side of the sun, intersecting, passing through each other, or facing away from each other, it signifies that a great military conflict will arise throughout the world. It also indicates internal turmoil and a lack of unity within the army. It is also said that if there is a cross-shaped vapor beside the sun, the side towards which the intersection points will be victorious.

If vapors arise in a shape like the character for "mouth" <term>original: *kou* (口)</term> at the edge of the sun, it is called a **Sun-Mouth** <term>original: *ri kou* (日口)</term>. If one or two "mouths" appear, it signifies treacherous and harmful people. If three, four, or five "mouths" appear, it indicates that feudal lords or princes are plotting to establish themselves as rulers.

If a yellow straight pillar appears by the sun, the minister will rebel against the sovereign. If a blue and red straight pillar appears, the minister will declare independence.

If the straight pillar is red in the center and blue on the outside, the one who declares independence will not succeed. If it is yellow and white and appears moist and lustrous, the one who declares independence will succeed.

When the appearance of blue and red vapors resembles two straight lines crossing each other, whether above, below, to the left, or to the right of the sun, this is called a **Crossed Vapor** <term>original: *Jiao* (交)</term>, which signifies lewd conduct. If these vapors are accompanied by a lewd atmosphere, it indicates licentious behavior. If there is a crossed red cloud beside the sun, there will be military conflict below it.

If there are two vapors on either side of the sun that cross, penetrate, or face back-to-back, all these signify that great military conflict will arise under heaven. In the time of the emperor Theophilus, Scholasticius the door-keeper was chamberlain, and in the time of Michael, son of Theophilus, the patrician Damian was chamberlain and after him, in the same reign, Basil, the Christ-loving emperor, was chamberlain. In the time of Basil, the Christ-loving sovereign, there was no chamberlain during all his reign. In the time of Leo, the Christ-loving sovereign, the patrician Samonas was chamberlain, and after him, in the time of the same emperor, the patrician Constantine. In the time of Alexander the emperor, the patrician Barbatus was chamberlain; and in the time of Constantine, the Christ-loving sovereign, the patrician Constantine, mentioned before in the time of the sovereign Leo, was chamberlain again; and in the time of the sovereign Romanus, the patrician Theophanes; and in the second reign of Constantine, the patrician Basil.

In the time of Leo, the Christ-loving and ever-memorable emperor, lived the late Ktenas, an aged cleric of great wealth, who was precentor of the New Church and was skilled in singing as was no other at that time. This same Ktenas besought the patrician Samonas, who was at that time chamberlain, to intercede for him with the emperor so that he might be made protospatharius and wear the shirt and go in procession to the Lausiacus and take his seat as protospatharius and receive a stipend of one pound, and in respect of this remuneration might give the emperor forty pounds. But the emperor could not bring himself to do this, saying that it was out of his power, and «to the great disgrace of my imperial majesty if a cleric becomes protospatharius». On hearing this from the patrician Samonas, this same Ktenas added to the forty pounds a pair of ear-rings valued at ten pounds, and a silver table with animals on it in gold relief, also valued at ten pounds. And the emperor, besought by the request of the patrician Samonas, the chamberlain, took the forty pounds of gold and the pair of ear-rings and the table with its gold on silver relief work, so that the total gift of the same Ktenas amounted to sixty pounds. Then the emperor made him protospatharius, and he received a stipend on that occasion of one pound. After being honoured with the rank of protospatharius this same Ktenas lived two years and then died; and he received a stipend of one pound for each of the two years.

When the arrow is held by the index finger near the thumb, with the bow gripped in the fist and the nock <note>the notch at the end of an arrow</note> aligned, this is called steady alignment. <note>verse 47</note>
In a steady alignment from a chariot, there should be downward aiming; therefore, from that same position, aiming upwards is considered upward alignment. <note>verse 48</note>
For a target located far above, a downward alignment is appropriate; for a low target, one aims upward; for a target at the same level, it should be level. <note>verse 49</note>
A circular target sixteen fingers in circumference is called a large target. A subtle one is two fingers, and one with a head is five fingers. <note>verse 50</note>
A target inferred by sound is called a remote target. Thus, the five types of targets should be demonstrated in their piercing. <note>verse 51</note>
Moving sideways, walking, turning, and jumping—these are the locations of such targets, whether in the sky, on the ground, or in the water. <note>verse 52</note>
Two hundred units is declared the best distance, one hundred and fifty is the medium, and one hundred and twenty is the lowest, for archery on the ground. <note>verse 53</note>
The king should strike the best distance with four arrows gradually, with straight blades, thick and thin, or leafless arrows, as appropriate. <note>verse 54</note>
One should show lightness of hand by striking one arrow at the fall, one in the hand, and five in the sky; thus, the King should practice his marksmanship. <note>verse 55</note>
