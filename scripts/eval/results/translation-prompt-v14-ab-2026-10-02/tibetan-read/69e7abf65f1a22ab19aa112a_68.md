"King, even the wild beasts of my hermitage are respected. Will you kill this deer that has come for refuge?" Thus the sage spoke to the king. Then the army arrived and asked, "Your Majesty, to whom is this man speaking so rudely?" The King replied, "To me." They said, "O Wise One, what is the punishment for one who speaks so harshly to the King?" He replied, "Your Majesty, the punishment is death." If that was the case, the sage was completely abandoned. As they prepared to kill him, he made a perverse prayer: "By this king's quarrelsome act of seeking to kill me, though I am innocent and have done no harm, wherever he is born, may I be the one to deprive him of life!" Then he thought, "These kings are very well-hidden, very well-guarded, and very well-protected. If I take birth elsewhere, I will never find an opportunity. Therefore, by this prayer, may I be born in the womb of his noble queen." Because of that perverse prayer, he was born in the womb of Gochen. On the very day he entered the womb, a rain of blood fell. Gochen developed a craving: "Oh, if only I could cut and eat the flesh from the King's back!" When the King was told of this, he summoned the diviners and asked them.

At that time, in Vaishali, there lived a Licchavi named Mingchen. In his pleasure grove was a grove of mango trees. There, a banana tree <note>or plantain</note> suddenly grew, which was seen by the keeper of the grove. Immediately flowers appeared, and being amazed, he told Mingchen. Mingchen summoned the diviners and asked them. They said, "Lord, please protect it. On the seventh day it will burst open, and a girl will emerge from within." When the householder Mingchen heard this, he was even more amazed. He stationed guards all around the grove and began counting the days. When seven days had passed, he cleared the stones, pebbles, and shards from the grove, sprinkled it with sandalwood water, prepared fragrant incense burners, hung many silk tassels, and scattered loose flowers. Surrounded by songs, instrumental music of many kinds, and friends and kinsmen, he went with his wife in great splendor.
