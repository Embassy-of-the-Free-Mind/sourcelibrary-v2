SIR,
I Have obſerved the two Inſects, which Mr. Ray ſaith, ſmell
of Musk, which indeed they do in an high degree. The
ſmall Bees are very frequent in the Wooles in Lincoln-ſhire, and
about the latter end of April are to be found in paſtures and
meadows, upon the early-blown flowers of a ſort of Ranuncu-

improper to ſay Bees feed on flowers: And likewiſe the ſame
Bees are no leſs frequent on the flowers of Dens Leonis, &c.
The ſweet Beetle, is a very large Inſect, and well known about
Cambridge. All the trials I have made to preſerve them with
their ſmell, have proved ineffectual: For, both ſorts of theſe
Inſects will of themſelves in a very few weeks become almoſt
quite ſent-leſs. To theſe I ſhall add another ſweet-ſmelling
Inſect, which is a Hexapode-worm feeding on Gallium lu-
teum.

ſlender Waſps after divers ſtrange ways are concerned.
