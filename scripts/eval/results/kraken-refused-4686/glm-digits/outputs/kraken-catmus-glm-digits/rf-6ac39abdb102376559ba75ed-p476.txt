March 5th, I carefully cut off one of the lobes, and the in¬
ward structure seemed like a cane or dried flag when transver¬
sly cut ; and, upon blowing in at the syphon, I fancied the
Air to come equally out at all the pores I had exposed to view.
Whereupon I fixed spittle in several places, and upon fresh
blowing found multitudes of bubles, made in the denudated
parts of the lobe. Immediately I made a deep transverse in¬
cision into that lobe, and blowing in at the syphon, I found
the Air to come so freely out at the larger ramifications ot the
bronchiæ, that I could not give the lobe a considerable rise with
a strong blast : Yet upon stopping with my fingers the larger
passages of the bronchiæ, which I had cut, I found that lobe,
upon a frefh blast, considerably to arise with unequal protu¬
berances (where the incision was made,) giving no small sus¬
