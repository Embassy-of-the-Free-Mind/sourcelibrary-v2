# Appendix — A catalogue of OCR errors on early printed books

*Source Library, 2026-10-01 · issue #5488 · generated from `scripts/eval/ocr-error-classes.py` and a by-eye check of every example.*

This appendix lists concrete instances of each class of OCR error. Each one comes from a page where we hold a human transcription of **the same edition**:
- **EEBO-TCP:** 73 English and Latin books, 1563–1700. CC0 keyed texts of the same EEBO microfilm (or the same edition), each page leaf-checked.
- **Wikisource:** 120 proofread pages of the same scan, in Latin, German and Greek.

Three readers are compared:
- **flash-lite**, production's engine, on the benchmark's generic transcription prompt.
- **flash** (gemini-3-flash-preview), on the same prompt.
- **served OCR**, the text Source Library actually stores for the page. It was read mostly by flash with the production prompt, and exists for EEBO-TCP only.

**How the examples were chosen and checked.** The classifier aligns each reader's words to the reference and sorts every difference into a class. For each class we took up to five instances, one per page, spread across readers. A reviewer then opened the page image and recorded what the page actually prints at that spot. The checks were made by a model, not a person (`by: model-eye`). The **verdict** column says who was right: *engine wrong* (the page agrees with the reference), ***reference wrong*** (the page agrees with the engine), *both wrong*, *convention* (the two differ only in transcription convention), or *not on page*. "Page prints" is the reviewer's reading of the image.

Shares in the summary are **word-weighted**: each error counts the length of the words it touches. They rank the classes; they are not character error rates (CERs).

## What the check found

Of 94 differences checked against the page image:
- the **engine was wrong** in 44;
- the **reference was wrong** in 16. These include the TCP keyers' own long-s slips (*ſit* keyed as "fit"), typos ("Deuchess"), and regularised spellings;
- 17 were **convention** differences;
- 11 were **not on the page**. These are artefacts of the reference window or the aligner, not reading errors;
- 5 were refusals of pages with plainly legible text.

The reference is not infallible ground truth at the level of single words. A CER computed against it counts some correct readings as errors.

## D. Errors of the instrument

Two kinds of error belong to the measuring, not the reading.
- **A tag stripper that deletes text.** Production OCR marks centred lines `->like this<-`. A stripper of the form `/<[^>]+>/` reads `<-` as the opening of a tag and deletes everything up to the next `>`. On Jonson's *Catiline* 1611 (https://sourcelibrary.org/book/6a08fd2e25e3a402b23b3bca?page=26), the first pass of this analysis therefore reported 100+ words "omitted" from a transcription that, by eye, is complete. The same pattern was live in production code (n-gram search, OCR loop guard); filed and fixed under #5564.
- **Classifier residue.** About a third of the first pass's "other misread" bucket was tag names left in the stored text ("printed", "English") and page numbers aligned against words. Examples were checked by eye for that reason; the class shares in the summary still contain some of this residue.
