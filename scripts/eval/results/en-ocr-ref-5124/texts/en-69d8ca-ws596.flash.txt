The varieties 1, 2 and 3, with the pileus acuminated, are most certainly of this description, and nearly proved fatal to a poor family in Piccadilly, London, who were so indiscreet as to stew a quantity (found in St. James’s Green Park) for breakfast. ->T A B. CCXLIX.<-

->AGARICUS PILIPES.<-

This Agaric grew on a whitish variety of *A. plumbeus* or *A. Listeri* mentioned *tab.* 245. It came to me in a phial of Hungary water, and seemed all of one colour, which it still retains.

->T A B. CCL.<-

BOLETUS RUBEOLARIUS. *Bull.* 100 and 490. *fig.* 1.
*With. ed.* 3. *v.* 4. 315.
——— LURIDUS. *Schæff.* 117.

Not very rare. I have found it at Hornsey-wood, and at Hainault forest, Essex. My friend, the Rev. Mr. Charles Abbot, sent me a specimen from Bedfordshire. The fine carmine, cinnabar, or vermillion-coloured powder or seed, is often so copiously shed as to stain every thing that touches it, and is so thick under the pores as almost to obscure them.
