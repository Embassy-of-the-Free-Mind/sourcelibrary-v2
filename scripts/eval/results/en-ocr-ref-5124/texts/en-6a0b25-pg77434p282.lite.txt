26. §. Some few of the Conjunctions therein fet down, may not be found actually exiftent in Nature. The abatement of which, will be much more than compenfated two ways. First, by the other six species of simple Tasts, which are also sometimes compounded. And by other more complex Conjunctions, as of many Quadruples, and perhaps some Quintuple or Sextuple ones. Thus far of the Simple Species, and Conjunctions of Tasts.

27. §. THE DEGREES of Tasts are also numerous; and each species, in every Conjunction, capable of Variation herein. For the more accurate obferving whereof, it will be beft, To take those Bodies, whose Tasts are, as near as may be, the fame in specie: and that those be firft tafted, which are lefs ftrong; whereby the true Degree will be more precisely taken.

28. §. The Tasts of Bodies will thus appear to be varied, in moft Species unto Five Degrees; and in fome of them, unto Ten. So the Root of Turmerick, is bitter in the First Degree; of Gentian, in the Tenth. The Root of Carduus Benedictus, is Hot in the First Degree; the Green Pods or Seed-Cases of Clematis peregrina, in the Tenth. So that, allowing fome to vary under Five; yet by a moderate eftimate, we may reckon every Species, one with another, to be varied by at leaft Five Degrees. Which being added to the feveral Species of Tasts, in all the Treble Conjunctions of the aforesaid Table, come to 1800 fenfible and defineable Variations of Taste. And these are the Diversities of Taste, with respect to the sensation it felf.

## Of the DURATION and several TERMES of Tasts.

For there are many Tasts, which have their Motions analogous to those of Diseases; and by those may be distinguished in the same manner. For as of Diseases, so of Tasts, there are Four Times, as Physicians call them, or Terms of Motion; sc. Principium, Augmentum, Status, & Declinatio.
