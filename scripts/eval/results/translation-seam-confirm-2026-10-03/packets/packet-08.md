### XII.
The subject of educative action is that very thing which gives being to the effect in the genus of the material cause, and supports the effect in this way; it matters little whether you call it the subject of education, of support, or of inhesion, except that education and support seem to imply only the causality of the material cause, whereas inhesion seems to imply even the very union of the form to the subject. From this union, the educative action itself also really differs, as a mode differs from a mode, although by natural necessity an educative action must have the union connected to the absolute form, and the educative action itself, insofar as it requires having it conjoined, can be said to be a union, <note>though</note> radically, not formally or entitatively. The subject of adherence for creative action is the created terminus itself, which cannot be the subject of inhesion or support, because creative action is not support or inhesion; for that creative action is not something by means of which something else, distinct from the created terminus to which it gives being as that by which it is, is caused or supported. However, an educative action is support and inhesion, because it is that by means of which something distinct from the subject is caused and supported or inheres. Wherefore, just as the created terminus does not cause itself or anything else through creative action, so neither can that action received in it be said to be the causality of that terminus as a subject. On the contrary, however, just as something distinct from the subject in which it is received, and to which that action itself does not give being, is caused and supported by educative action, so also that action can be said to be the causality of its subject toward an effect distinct from the subject. But when the created terminus is called the subject of adherence, it is not signified by this that the terminus is only extrinsically affected by the action (since it is truly affected by it intrinsically, no less than the subject of support is by educative action), but only that it is not intrinsically affected by it as an intrinsic cause is affected by causality, just as matter is intrinsically affected by the educative action by means of which the effect is generated.

### XIII.
Actions are diversified in species according to the specific diversity of both termini and principles, so that an action is not of the same species if it does not depend on principles and termini of the same species, although it is not denied by this that an essential distinction can also be introduced into action from elsewhere, and that a terminus of the same species can be produced by the same principle entirely through an action of a different species. Just as the union between subject and form might perhaps exist not only as distinct in number but also as different in species, and not only the same in number; unless, however, a sufficient reason for a specific or numerical distinction appears from elsewhere, the union between the same form and matter in number, or the action of the same adequate agent in number toward the same terminus, will not be said to differ in number or species without cause. Moreover, both action and union differ only in number whenever one or both of the extremes of the former, or the active or passive principle (if it is an educative action) or the terminus or both of the latter, differ only in number. But it seems necessary to admit in these things that one differs more or less in number from another than from a different one, or that they have more or fewer principles from which numerical distinction is taken. A numerically identical effect can be produced of itself by actions of different species, and a cause can produce effects of different species through actions of different species, for the reason that the same cause looks toward many effects of different species producible by it, and the effect is producible by different causes. And neither the effect nor the cause becomes essentially the actual exercise of the other, as this action is essentially the actual exercise of this cause into this effect in number. ### XXII.
For the consequence from the principle *that*—or the whole <term>suppositum</term> <gloss>individual substance</gloss>—to the principles *by which*—or parts of the <term>suppositum</term>—does by no means hold, as both the examples already brought forward and those yet to be presented render clearer than the noonday sun. Hence, it does not follow that: The human being is three yards long, therefore his body is three yards long, and his soul is three yards long. The camps of the Jesuits are in a fervor, and this point in particular stirs their sweat: If GOD is infinite, there is no evil, no degree of goodness. For the infinity of one thing destroys every opposite and every inequality of distances from Divine perfection.
