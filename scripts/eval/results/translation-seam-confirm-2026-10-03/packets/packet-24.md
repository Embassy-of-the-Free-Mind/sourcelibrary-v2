¶ IT IS FAR, therefore, it is far from us, Fathers and Brothers, that we should henceforth inquire too curiously how God is in the flesh, and in what kind of flesh, and if man was assumed, perfect and imperfect; from which questions, truly, that are not so much profitable as they are frivolous, the Apostle himself dissuades us, saying: Avoid foolish and undisciplined questions, knowing that they breed quarrels, since they are useless and vain. This he says, and (as with all things) truly. Since it is certain that we love each other less because of these new questions, at a great cost to harmony, while we busy ourselves more than enough to understand those things which we would far more rightly entrust to God, who prefers that unknown things be worshipped religiously rather than that inscrutable things be debated. For with what, I ask, shall we more healthfully praise those things which are rightly handed down and heard? Which even that great Apostle, however learned he was in the third heaven to which he was caught up, did not hesitate to do regarding this kind of business.

### XII.
The subject of an eductive action is that very thing which gives being to an effect in the genus of Material cause, and in this respect sustains the effect. Whether one speaks of eduction, or of sustenance, or of inherence of the subject, it matters little, unless one considers that eduction and sustenance seem to imply only the causality of the material cause, whereas inherence seems to imply even the very union of the form to the subject. From this union the eductive action itself also really differs, as a mode differs from a mode, although by natural necessity an eductive action has a union connected to an absolute form; and the eductive action itself, insofar as it requires having that union connected to it, can be called a union radically, not formally or entitatively. The subject of adherence of a creative action is the created terminus itself, which cannot be a subject of inherence or sustenance, because a creative action is not sustenance or inherence. For such a creative action is not something by means of which another thing, distinct from the created terminus, to which that action gives being as a "by-which" <note>original: "ut quo"</note>, is caused or sustained. An eductive action, however, is sustenance and inherence, because it is that by means of which something distinct from the subject is caused and sustained or inheres. Wherefore, just as the created terminus through a creative action causes neither itself nor anything else, so that action received in it cannot be said to be the causality of that terminus as a subject. Conversely, however, just as through an eductive action something distinct from the subject, in which that action is received and to which that action itself does not give being, is caused and sustained; so also that action can be said to be the causality of that subject toward an effect distinct from the subject. While a created terminus is called a subject of adherence, this does not signify that the terminus is only extrinsically affected by the action (since it is truly affected by it intrinsically, no less than the subject of sustenance is by an eductive action), but only that it is not affected by it intrinsically, as an intrinsic cause is affected by causality, just as matter is intrinsically affected by an eductive action, by means of which an effect is generated.
