### XXXVIII.

A demonstration of the *propter quid* <gloss>the reason why</gloss> can proceed from every kind of cause; principally, however, from the formal cause; and hence, that which is accustomed to be said by some, that the middle term of demonstration is most especially the definition of the subject itself, is to be understood as the formal definition.

### XXXIX.

Nor do I think it should be denied that it can be proven, and that demonstratively, that such a formal definition agrees with its subject; but not as its definition; nor even through a formal cause; but either from the material, or the end, or from the efficient cause itself; for of a formal definition, there is no other definition given in the same genus.

### XL.

But when a proper attribute is demonstrated of its subject through the formal cause of that attribute itself, then there is nothing to prevent the formal definition from also being proven demonstratively through the formal cause, that is, the definition of the subject.

### XLI.

Concluding therefore, we rightly establish as the most excellent middle term of demonstration neither the definition of the subject alone, nor the definition of the attribute alone, but both combined together, inasmuch as it contains in itself all the causes of the conclusion.

In the Spring and Autumn period, Gongzi Yishi died.

Hu Yuan <note>a scholar of the Northern Song period</note> said: The ruler's ministers and assistants are called his arms and legs. Therefore, inquiring about their illnesses, mourning their deaths, and providing funeral gifts must be done with generous attention to the kindness of seeing them to their final rest. This is the intent behind the *Spring and Autumn Annals* recording the deaths of grandees.

Zhong Sui died at Chui. On the day *renwu*, the *yi* <note>a post-sacrifice ritual</note> was still performed, and the *yue* <note>a type of flute used in music</note> was removed during the *wan* dance. <note>Year 8 of Duke Xuan</note>

Hu Anguo’s commentary says: According to the rites, when a grandee dies, if there is a sacrifice, one should not announce it until the funeral rites are finished. If one hears of the death, the *yi* ritual should not be performed. The reason one does not announce it is to show the utmost sincerity and respect toward the ancestral temple. The reason one does not perform the *yi* ritual is to fulfill the kindness between ruler and subject from beginning to end. Now, Zhong Sui was a high minister of the state; if the *yi* was still performed upon his death, then the rites for honoring a high minister were lost. Although the *Spring and Autumn Annals* elevates the ruler and suppresses the minister, if the decorum and appearance of respect are increased, then the distinction between high and low becomes even more revered.

# Commentary on the Meaning of the Treatise on the Hundred Dharmas

Because the four great elements <note>earth, water, fire, wind</note> are illusory forms, the originally present wisdom-light turns into deluded perception. By taking these illusory forms as the realm of what is perceived, deluded perception persists for a long time. It then seizes small parts of the four great elements to serve as a "self," and deluded perception relies upon these four elements to act as a "body." Thus, the four great elements are originally devoid of awareness; it is only because of the attachment of deluded perception that they appear to have awareness. The true mind is boundless, but now it is sealed and solidified by ignorance, infiltrating the four great elements to act as the mind. This is what is called "the combination of form and deluded thoughts acting as the body." Therefore, it is said: "Knowledge and perception are sentient beings." This refers to sentient beings of the five aggregates. Therefore, the inner five roots and outer six dusts all belong to the "object part" of the eight consciousnesses. Consequently, when practicing Zen, one must first internally discard body and mind, and externally renounce the world. This is precisely to erase these two parts—the "object part" and the "perceiving part"—in order to solely investigate the original substance of the ignorance of the eight consciousnesses. If body, mind, and world do not disappear, they are always obstacles to life and death. Regarding what are called the two attachments to self and dharmas, attachment to the body as "self" is the attachment to self, and attachment to the roots and dusts is the attachment to dharmas. When those of the two vehicles <note>Sravakas and Pratyekabuddhas</note> practice, they only break the view of self, thereby exiting fractional life and death. The attachment to dharmas, however, only begins to be broken from the initial stage of faith, through the three virtuous stages, and all the way until the first stage of a Bodhisattva. How could this be easy?

The twenty-four kinds of non-associated [dharmas] represent the positions or states of form and mind. They are established nominally based on the previous three categories of dharmas, such as acquisition. They are distinguished from mind-kings, mental factors, and forms, and are therefore called "non-associated." Because they do not associate with the mind-king, and because they cannot perform good or evil, they are not mental factors. They are merely distinctions of positions counted by the consciousness-only [school]. Because they are dharmas of attachment belonging to the self, they are listed among the conditioned dharmas. There are many interpretations of their significance, but they are not the urgent priority, so there is no need to hinder the correct practice.

---

The above ninety-four kinds are named "conditioned dharmas" because they are the dharmas of the life and death of sentient beings, and being calculated by deluded consciousness, they involve creation and action. Thus, they are called "conditioned" and "worldly dharmas." Regarding the sixteen unconditioned dharmas, there are six types: the unconditioned of space, the unconditioned of analytical extinction, the unconditioned of non-analytical extinction, the unconditioned of immovability, the unconditioned of the extinction of perception and feeling, and the unconditioned of thusness. These six types of dharmas are established as "unconditioned" to distinguish them from the conditioned. Although they are called "supramundane dharmas," they actually extend to the two vehicles; the "immovable" is the third fruit, the Anagamin, and the "extinction of perception and feeling" is the samadhi of extinction. The "unconditioned of space" takes its name from a metaphor, meaning that the substance of the unconditioned dharma is like empty space, without any creation or action. The following five unconditioned dharmas generally use this metaphor. However, this metaphor of empty space varies in magnitude. As the *Avatamsaka Sutra* says: "If one wishes to know the Buddha-realm, one should purify one's intent like empty space, distancing oneself from deluded thoughts and all grasping, so that wherever the mind is directed, there is no obstruction." It also says: "The pure Dharma-body is like empty space." This directly points to the emptiness of the nature of the dharma-realm. This is the great vehicle's true emptiness of dharma-nature, which is the absolute purity from defilement in the one mind, and is thus like empty space. The meaning of empty space here extends through both great and small vehicles, but primarily takes the emptiness and lack of creation as a metaphor for the following five unconditioned dharmas and the ultimate truth. The "unconditioned of analytical extinction" means to distinguish; it means to cut off and extinguish. It is the true principle revealed by using non-lethal wisdom to cut off all defilements and obstacles, hence this name is established. This concerns the partial severing and partial realization of Bodhisattvas in provisional teachings, and the Nirvana-emptiness realized by the two vehicles. Because it correctly belongs to extinction, it is called "realized extinction." High realization of the unconditioned is not truly for the two vehicles.

we have spoken regarding the office of the Angels in defending the pious.

2. When? Namely, the same night: for only one night intervenes between the imprisonment and the gathering of the council. They are therefore liberated on this very night: and they rest here most safely, with great security: soon to return to their duty, as the persona of the Angel serves for consolation: so also does the brevity of time. Adversities do not last long. Therefore, they must be endured with a brave heart.

<margin>Liberation increases the constancy in the Apostles to teach.</margin> 3. Why are they liberated? The word of the Angel declares it: "Go and standing speak to the people in the temple, all the words of this life." The intent of the Angel is that they must preach the Gospel more spiritedly from then on, because it is as if the end of the liberation, as if he were saying, You were not to be liberated unless you were to teach henceforth, even more constantly against the Pharisees. These are arguments for diligence: because they must go, as if to say, from the prison to the temple, from bonds to doctrine. Furthermore, because he commands them to do it standing, that is, intrepidly and spiritedly. Thirdly, because in the temple, not privately. Nor does the contamination of the temple stand in the way of them being sent there.

<margin>Periphrasis of the Gospel.</margin> Fourth, because to the people, that is, publicly, to all who gather for sacred rites. Fifth, what will they teach? "All the words of this life." It is a brief periphrasis of the Gospel, according to that in John 6: "You have the words of eternal life": and in the same place, "The words that I speak, they are life." It is called life because it contains the method of obtaining eternal life. Nor does it only contain the method, but it provides the power to attain it. Hence that in Romans 1: "It is the power of God unto salvation to everyone that believeth." Those are the words of this life. Nor is there any need here for any <term>hypallage</term> <gloss>reversal of terms</gloss>, because the relation ταύτης [this] indicates the present life, concerning which there was a question for them with the Pharisees. That, however, was not this external life: but that eternal one, and the whole sum of Evangelical doctrine.

4. There follows the obedience of the Apostles, who enter the temple, and that very early in the morning, so that you may know that the temple was revisited immediately after the prison, and that in the morning: because they gathered for sacred rites in that temple even before sunrise.

They teach, therefore, in a suitable place and time. Let us learn what befits those who have been liberated from dangers, namely to apply oneself to piety with greater diligence.

III. There follows the attempt of the enemies while the matter was not yet revealed to them. First, they convene the council. <margin>What the High Priest does with his cohorts when the Apostles are liberated.</margin> The author of this is the High Priest and a few others, that is, the Sadducees. Hence the <term>synedrium</term> <gloss>sanhedrin/council</gloss> and the γερουσία [senate/council of elders] of the sons of Israel, that is, the fullest gathering, the perfect Senate as in a grave matter. This senate was not accustomed to be gathered in trivial causes.

2. The investigation of the matter, and the truth discovered: they send ministers to the prison, who bring the captives. Returning, what do they report? We found the prison secured: the guards even standing outside: but upon entering we found no one. You see an unusual change of events.

Here, first of all, weigh why this miracle is not explained as such by the Angel or by the Apostles: but rather its narration is attributed to the enemies' ministers here. This is done for the greater emphasis of the matter, so that you may see that neither the guards perceived such a thing, nor did others suspect it: but the miracle was unforeseen and astonishing.

Then, why does this escape such great men? Surely the novelty of the matter should have struck them: and it would be more valid among them, as they should have learned it from no others than their own ministers.

But they are not moved by this. Learn, therefore, at least this, that the effect was that it might be evident how God eludes the counsels of His enemies: they think the prey is in their hands: but what? They are eluded, like gaping crows. Thus we previously saw Sapphira, who for three hours remained ignorant of her husband's fortune.

3. As to how they were affected, it is astonishment: διηπόρουν [they were perplexed], that is, they are of an ambiguous mind: they do not know what to do; this becomes also what is to be gathered by them from this, so they are confused. That they were obstinate, however, is certain, because they think only of the outcome, nothing of repentance, as to where it would end, as if to say, the power of the miracle did not escape them: but blinded by envy, they resist, greedy even for the outcome.

4. A fuller knowledge of the matter. While they stand astonished, another messenger arrives, who recounts the matter more fully. What kind of messenger this was matters little: it is possible that he was a simple and good man: more probably, however, some flatterer who wanted to hunt for some favor with the High Priests from this. As it happens, it seems to them that he is present by chance: when they had neither sent for him nor expected him by counsel. As for God, however, he is present, sent by certain counsel and providence. Thus many things happen to us by chance and accidentally in our actions: which, however, as far as God is concerned, happen by the counsel and reason of providence.

<margin>The Jews are hardened to the wonder of the liberation.</margin> IIII. One could have expected here from good minds some sign of their amendment: but he proceeds to recount how the matter always turns for the worse among the High Priests.

For first they are captured again, and that by the prefect of the temple, so that you may know that this was a decree of the council: for he is here by right. This is their amendment. But malice is provident: they do not inflict force, as before, but rather they invite them in a friendly manner to the assembly, as if to be heard freely.

This is the custom of hypocrites: they are accustomed to ensnare smoothly those whom they dare not openly overwhelm. And what is the reason for this act? Are they beginning to become wise? Not at all: but they fear stoning: Behold the fear and the cause of fear. You have the same in the cause of Christ in Matthew 21 and Luke 20. It is a chorus of evil men, yet an impotent attempt, and full of danger.

2. They set them again before the council. You may see spirits exasperated. They do not want to appear to have gathered in vain.
