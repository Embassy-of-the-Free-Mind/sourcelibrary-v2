### XXXVIII.

A demonstration of the *propter quid* <gloss>that which is on account of which</gloss> can proceed from every kind of cause; primarily, however, from the formal cause; and hence, what is commonly said by some, that the medium of demonstration is most especially the definition of the subject itself, should be understood as the formal definition.

### XXXIX.

Nor do I think it should be denied that it can be proven, and that demonstratively, that such a formal definition agrees with its subject; but not as its own definition; nor even through a formal cause; but rather from the matter, or from the end, or from its efficient cause; for of a formal definition, there is not given another definition in the same genus.

### XL.

But when a proper property is demonstrated of its subject through the formal cause of that property itself, then there is nothing that prohibits the formal definition from being proven by a demonstration in the same way through a formal cause, that is, the definition of the subject.

### XLI.

Concluding therefore, we justly establish as the most excellent medium of demonstration neither the definition of the subject alone, nor the definition of the property, but both joined together, as that which contains in itself all the causes of the conclusion.
