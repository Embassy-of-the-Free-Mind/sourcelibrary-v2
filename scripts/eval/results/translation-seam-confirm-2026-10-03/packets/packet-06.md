§ 13. The Vulgate translates this as *Annumeratus est* <gloss>he was numbered</gloss>. Erasmus translates it as *co-optatus* <gloss>co-opted</gloss>: a rendering which Laurence Valla also approves, and explains by the phrase *suffragiis electus est* <gloss>he was elected by vote</gloss>. Vatablus and Pagninus agree with these, rendering it as *suffragiis additus est ad numerum undecim Apostolorum* <gloss>he was added by vote to the number of the eleven Apostles</gloss>. Truly, a shorter interpretation by others is more pleasing and approaches closer to the truth, by which *συγκατεψηφίσθη* <gloss>he was numbered with</gloss> is taken to mean that everyone praised and approved the divine lot, and through that election, commended Matthias with a unanimous voice. For how could he be said to be elected by votes, whom the lot made an Apostle? Therefore, the particle *συν* <gloss>with</gloss> in the compound verb must be well noted: for here is described the outcome of the lots, which was confirmed by common agreement; as if to say that everyone voted for, and acquiesced in, the divine lot which fell upon Matthias and designated him as Apostle, because—as the learned Dr. Walther beautifully states in the cited passage—the Lord had chosen him, and through that casting of lots, only the declaration of that election took place. And so, the remaining Eleven willingly received Matthias into the number of the Apostles, and acknowledged and held him as a colleague.

§ 14. Thus far we have considered the election of Matthias. It is not useless, but rather necessary, to speak a few words about the man elected and his Apostleship. He shared this title with the rest, as one who had seen Christ in the flesh, had been called immediately by Him, and at the Feast of Pentecost had received the wondrous gifts of the Holy Spirit with the others. He was sent into the whole world, whence the Arabic interpreter elegantly calls him *אלתרס* <gloss>the sent one</gloss>, which others—though frivolously—wish to derive from the Hebrew and Syriac root *חור* <gloss>white</gloss>, as if the Apostles were dressed in white garments. It is better derived from the verb used by the Ethiopians for *πορευεσθαι* <gloss>to go, to depart</gloss>, because the duty of the Apostles entailed never fixing a residence, but traveling in every direction.

§ 15. As to the place where this Matthias of ours preached, there is little certainty from reliable and genuine authors. Since scholars posit a division of the Apostles into the regions of the world, Jerome writes in his *Catalogue* that he preached in Ethiopia, others in Judea

Eusebius Pamphili relates in his *Ecclesiastical History*, Bk. IV, ch. 15, fol. 86 (Latin ed., Basel 1544), that the Christians of Smyrna formerly held solemn and famous assemblies in the place where the burned bones of the Martyr Polycarp had been laid to rest. Although, it must be noted in this latter instance that this administration of Baptism was not undertaken with the same intention as it was in the Corinthian Church, namely, for the sake of proving the Resurrection of the Dead. For the writers of Sacred History would by no means have passed over that in silence. And they then eat one another; the one who wins eats the one who is defeated. And among other meats, human flesh is commonly their food. But of this thing you should be certain: that in these times, it has been seen that a father has eaten his son and his wife. And I have known one with whom I spoke myself, of whom they said that he has eaten more than three hundred human bodies. And I stood in one place for twenty-seven days, where I saw salted human flesh in the houses and hung up to dry, just as the custom is among us to hang up bacon and pork. Furthermore, I say that they marvel why we do not eat the flesh of our enemies and use it as our food, for they say that such is the most delicious and best meat. Their weapons are bows and arrows. And when they rush to battle, they cover their bodies in no parts for protection; in this piece, especially, they are like irrational animals. We exerted great effort as much as we could and were able to do, and advised them to desist from such evil ways. They promised us to desist from such things. The women, as I have said before, and although they walk about bare and naked, they nevertheless have bodies that are quite pretty, well-formed, and clean, and they are not as shameful as perhaps anyone might think. Because they are free and sufficiently full-bodied, their shame is never seen, as they are mostly covered by good limbs and the arrangement of the body. We were amazed that among them, no one was seen who had pendulous breasts, or who had borne children, such that the bellies of those would be shaped differently than those of virgins and those who had never borne children. And in other limbs and parts of the body, the same was noted, which I have left further unchanged for the sake of honor and decency, as they might have joined themselves to Christian people.
