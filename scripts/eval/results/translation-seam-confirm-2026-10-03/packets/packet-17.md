==== TRANSLATION (this page) ====
The Junoesque peacock unfurls its praised wings:
    And covers its body and head with feathers.
In the new spring, the lively stag changes its antlers:
    And restores the long seasons of its old age.
He who wishes to enjoy perennial praise on earth
    Must extend his virtue through excellent deeds.
Whoever desires to outlive the ages of the long-lived stag
    Often has need to cast off his bad habits.
Until, free from hardships and every crime,
    He may bear the long joys of eternal life.
Truly, your ancestors and great-grandfathers, the Barons,
    Earned their name and these shields by these means.
If you follow the praiseworthy examples of those men:
    You shall soon surpass the long ages of the aged stag.

->Jeremias Reusner<-
->Leorinus.<-

Participating in Heaven and doubling the Earth to rely upon the numbers does not refer to the standard numbers of Heaven and Earth. To "rely upon" <note>original: 倚</note> means to approximate <note>original: 擬</note>. The numbers are generated from the standard numbers of Heaven and Earth.

The numbers of the <term>Yi</term> <gloss>Book of Changes</gloss> exhaust the beginning and end of Heaven and Earth. Someone might ask, do Heaven and Earth also have a beginning and an end? I would reply that since there is waxing and waning, how could there not be a beginning and an end? Although Heaven and Earth are vast, they are still physical forms and are therefore dual entities.

The generative numbers of the <term>Yi</term> total 129,600, which is the sum of 4,320 multiplied by the great number of waxing and waning <note>30 years, as explained in the following clause</note>. Calculating the number of <term>chen</term> <gloss>two-hour periods</gloss> in 30 years yields this number. A year of 360 days contains 4,320 <term>chen</term>; multiplying this by 30 years gives the total. Generally, <term>Jia-Zi</term> and <term>Jia-Wu</term> are the leaders of the <term>Shi</term> <gloss>generation</gloss>. This is how the numbers of the <term>Jingshi</term> <gloss>World-Ordering</gloss> begin with the Day <term>Jia</term>, Month <term>Zi</term>, Star <term>Jia</term>, and Time <term>Zi</term>.

### To the Same.

Kindly Themis never watches with gentle eyes
    Those who, having barely tasted the sweetness of wisdom,
Run through the open fields of Astraea with premature effort,
Examining what Baldus has, or what Bartolus has; as if
It were sufficient, with the foundation utterly spurned,
To have built anything, even if it threatens ruin with its very front.
    Perhaps with this sagacious spirit, dear friend Dietschi,
You will ponder, while you touch more closely the face of divine
Minerva, while you learn to arrange the ways of men with their fellows,
The best foundations of civil life.
I am grateful for your proposal, and for the honors of the virtue you have seized.

## ANOTHER.

Therefore, studious cultivator of the nine Sisters,
    Do you ask to have a place among men?
Since you dare to contend for the Highest of Goods,
    And you do not hesitate to ascend the learned pulpits of the wise.
Great Aristotle, wise Plato, and Epicurus,
    Teach their graceful writings, whatever anyone may say.
Each preferred his own pleasure to the actions of the rest:
    I can report as many opinions as there are days in the world!
Another tends to glittering riches, finally a part
    Desires praises, and longs to possess luxury as a good.
You defend in your theses which opinion is true.
    You propose your themes to be discussed.
