### XXII.
The inference from a principle *that*—or the whole <term>suppositum</term> <gloss>individual substance</gloss>—to the principles *by which*—or parts of the <term>suppositum</term>—is by no means valid, as the examples already provided and those yet to be brought forward render clearer than the noonday sun. Thus, a human being is three cubits long, yet the soul is not three cubits long, but rather the body, even though both soul and body together constitute the human being. A human being is, clearly, the principle *that*; the body is the principle *by which*, or that according to which that length belongs to the human being. Whence it does not follow: "A human being is three cubits long; therefore, his body is three cubits long and his soul is three cubits long." Let it be permitted to add as a crowning example one taken from theology, which is likewise most clear in its own light. Christ is infinite as the principle *that*; that is, Christ is the whole <term>suppositum</term>, or that person who is infinite and is so denominated. The principle *by which*, however—or that nature according to which it belongs to Christ to be infinite—is not his human nature, but his divine nature. Whence it likewise does not follow: "Christ is infinite; therefore, his divine nature is infinite and his human nature is infinite." Such collections are constructed from this principle: "Whatever belongs to the principle *that*, or the <term>suppositum</term>, must also belong to all its parts"; the falsity of which has already appeared all too clearly.

### LVIII.

The air, according to the varying nature of its qualities, was once divided by the ancients into two parts, namely the lower and the upper, of which the latter was called hot, and the former cold. At this time, however, it is distributed by almost all philosophers, according to the common custom of the schools, and for the sake of greater convenience and ease, into three regions: the highest, the middle, and the lowest.

### LVIIII.

The highest region of the air, which is placed immediately beneath the sphere of fire, is hot and dry on account of the proximity of fire and the perpetual motion of the celestial bodies, with which it is also carried around from east to west by the swiftest and most rapid motion of the <term>primum mobile</term> <gloss>the first moved sphere</gloss>.

### LX.

The middle region is cold and moist, because it is not rarefied by celestial motion, as that motion languishes here, nor do reflected solar rays reach it. Instead, the heat surrounding it on all sides makes it exist as colder on account of the cold vapors enclosed within this region of the air; this coldness is sometimes even intensified by <term>antiperistasis</term> <gloss>opposition or resistance</gloss>, that is, by the containment or compression of a quality by a contrary quality surrounding it.

### LXI.

The lowest region of the air, being closest to the earth, is sometimes hot and sometimes cold, depending on whether the solar rays are more direct or oblique, due to the reflection of solar rays on the surface of the earth.
