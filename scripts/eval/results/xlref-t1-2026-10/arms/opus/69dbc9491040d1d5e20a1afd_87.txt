And so, foreign nations having been harassed and ruined, we saw Massilia <note>modern Marseille, a long-standing Greek ally of Rome, captured by Caesar in 49 BC</note> carried in a triumphal procession as an example of an empire lost, and a triumph celebrated over that city without which our commanders never triumphed from their wars beyond the Alps. I would recall many other crimes against our allies, if the sun had seen anything more shameful than this one. Justly, therefore, are we punished. For had we not let the crimes of many go unpunished, such great license would never have come to one man. From him the inheritance of his estate passed to a few, but the inheritance of his greed to many wicked men. Nor indeed will the seed and cause of civil wars ever be lacking, so long as abandoned men remember that bloody spear <note>the auction spear of Sulla's proscriptions, under which the property of the condemned was sold</note> and hope for it again. When Publius Sulla had brandished it under his kinsman the dictator, the same man did not shrink, in the thirty-sixth year afterward, from a still more criminal spear <note>Caesar's sales of confiscated property</note>. And another, who had been a clerk in that dictatorship, was city quaestor in this one. From this it ought to be understood that, with such rewards held out, civil wars will never be lacking. And so only the walls of the city stand and remain, and even they now fear the ultimate crimes; but the Republic we have lost entirely. And we have fallen into these disasters (for I must return to my subject) while we preferred to be feared rather than to be dear and loved.
