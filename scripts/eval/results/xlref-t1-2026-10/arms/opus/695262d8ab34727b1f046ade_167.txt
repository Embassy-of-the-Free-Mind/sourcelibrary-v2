# FOURTH

The method of that citrination <note>citrination: the yellowing of silver toward the color of gold</note> which is accomplished through <term>Vitriol</term> <gloss>a metallic sulfate</gloss> or <term>Copperas</term> <gloss>green vitriol, iron sulfate</gloss> is as follows. Take a definite quantity of each of them, and sublime that part of it which allows itself to be sublimed, until it is sublimed entirely, with full driving-off. After this, sublime again what has been sublimed, with the degree of fire proper to it, so that some part of it is fixed after another, until the greater part of it is fixed. Afterwards treat it with careful attention to the fire, so that a greater fire may be applied to it to bring it to perfection. After this, let it be dissolved into a most deeply red water, which has no equal. From there you must use your ingenuity to give it entry into the body of <term>Luna</term> <gloss>silver</gloss>; and these devices have been shown to you sufficiently, if you are a diligent searcher of the complete work which we have written.

And because we see that these substances cling in friendly fashion to the depths of silver, we have therefore concluded, and it is certain, that they come from its root, and that is why silver happens to be altered by them. These, then, are all the medicines of the first order that we have set forth. Yet their methods can be multiplied in many ways, provided the essence of the coloring substances <note>original: "pigmentalium rerum"</note> is preserved through the variety of methods.

Some, indeed, have devised more medicines; but one of two things must necessarily follow. Either they must create their medicine from these same substances, or from things having the same nature. Or else they compose a medicine which, with its alteration, is equal to what does not exist, and which benefits neither the world nor the parts of the world, until the mover in the lofty movable of nature has come to rest uncorrupted. <note>An obscure closing phrase. It seems to mean that such invented medicines will never be of any use, as if "until the end of the world," when the heavenly mover of nature ceases its motion.</note>

<summary>The author completes his account of yellowing silver, adding a method with iron and a step-by-step procedure using vitriol or copperas: repeated sublimation, gradual fixation, and dissolution into a deep red water that can enter silver. He closes the medicines of the first order, noting that methods may vary but that newly invented medicines either rely on the same substances or are useless.</summary>
