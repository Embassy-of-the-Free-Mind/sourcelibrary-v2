Look, and pity her. Even now the gangrene of bloody war creeps through all her joints. <note>France was then torn by the Wars of Religion.</note> Nor is it otherwise in the whole world. Think on these things, Lipsius; and by this fellowship of miseries lighten your own. And just as a slave used to be placed behind those who triumphed, to call out again and again in the very joy of the triumph, **You are a man** <note>original: "Homo es"; in a Roman triumph a slave reminded the victorious general of his mortality</note>: so let this monitor always stand beside you, **These things are human** <note>original: "Esse hæc humana"</note>. For as labor shared with many becomes lighter, so too does sorrow.

### CHAPTER XXVI.

->*The Conclusion of the whole discourse, and a brief admonition to repeat it and ruminate on it.*<-

I HAVE set out all my resources and my discourse, Lipsius: and you have what I judged should be said for Constancy against Sorrow. I wish that these things may be not only pleasing to you but healthful; not that they may delight, but rather that they may help! And they will help, if you let them down not only into your ears but into your mind; and if you do not suffer them, once heard, to lie and wither away, like seeds scattered on the surface of the soil.
