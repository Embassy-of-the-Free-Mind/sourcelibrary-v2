Pus, therefore, is blood corrupted in the part where the wound is, and the effect of a digestion corrupted in that same place. To have damaged the integrity, continuity and digestion of the parts, and finally to have turned blood into pus: this very thing is reckoned in the *Schools* <note>the university medicine of the Galenic tradition, which Van Helmont attacks throughout</note> as having headed off *Catarrhs* <note>catarrh: a supposed downflow of waste fluid from the head to other parts of the body</note>. Either it has drawn out through the hole of the cautery an excremental humor from the head (from which they derive all *Catarrhs* in the first place), a humor which otherwise threatened to settle upon a <term>noble part</term> <gloss>a vital organ</gloss>; or else, meanwhile, there is a sympathy between the head and the wounded part, or there is not. It is all the same to them, so long as the skin is kept wounded. Whether that excremental humor is blood, or becomes pus or <term>sanies</term> <gloss>a thin, bloody discharge</gloss>, it is all the same, so long as the world is taken in by the worn-out words catarrh, precaution, derivation, revulsion <note>derivation and revulsion: Galenic terms for drawing a humor away to a nearby part or to a distant part</note>, and <term>fontanelle</term> <gloss>an artificial ulcer kept open to drain humors</gloss>.

For I look upon an infant of this year, already teething, suffering fever, foam at the mouth, and ceaseless drooling; and at length gripes come, and stools of greenish yellow. Well, that tooth is part of the head: therefore the diarrhea will be a Catarrh of the head! But what sympathy has a tooth about to break through, or a swollen gum, with the intestine? Or what power has it to generate and send a Catarrh from the little infant's stomach to the head, and from there into the *Ileum* <note>the lower part of the small intestine</note>? By what right will a vapor distilled from the stomach become verdigris-green bile in the head? Has the workshop of bile, perhaps, already moved to the head at the very start of life? Could a cautery (if the infant could bear it) draw the leek-green diarrhea into itself, and make up for several pounds of leek-green bile with a few little drops of pus? Why does the infant's stomach fabricate a Catarrh because of the pain of a tooth? Why is it sent into the Intestine, and not to the aching tooth? Does the reader not yet see that diarrhea is not a Catarrh? Rather, the angered *Archeus* <note>Van Helmont's term, taken from Paracelsus, for the vital governing spirit that directs each bodily process</note>, anywhere it may be, stands ready in the Intestine to change the nourishing cream <note>original: "alimentarium cremorem"; the chyle, the milky product of digestion</note> into excrements, which the *Schools* take to be bile, phlegm, and so forth. If, then, diarrhea is not a Catarrh, and the angered

For they do not consider that in women and in fat people the fleshy membrane around the usual sites of a cautery is mere grease, at least two fingers thick; and yet these are the very persons to whom cauteries are urged most often, and as most beneficial. Hence the bottom of the fontanelle will scarcely reach halfway through the fat.

There is therefore no way by which the wicked, fictitious humor of *Catarrh*, banished from the brain or from between the skull and the skin, could rush down through the middle of the fat. But what, after all, is that solitary humor which, banished for its own sin by the part that sends it, descends unmixed through the substance of the fat and degenerates into pus? If it is an exhalation of vapors from the stomach, why will it not be more frequent in the young and in those with hot stomachs than in the weak, the old, and the cold? How will that water, distilled from vapor, take on the form of pus? How will it hurry through the brain, the <term>meninges</term> <gloss>the membranes around the brain</gloss>, and the skull to find the hole made by the cautery, so that it flows down and is purged only there? Why does the vapor not fly off into the air a hundred times before it reaches the place marked out for the cautery at the surgeon's pleasure? How will water climbing from the stomach now be blood, and the mother of pus? How will blood (the matter of pus, according to *Galen* <note>the Greek physician of the 2nd century AD whose teachings dominated the Schools</note>) be the matter of *Catarrh*? Why should blood be reduced to the rank of the bad humors, when, not yet contaminated, it is sent out by nature to the wounded place? Why, once the wound is made, will nature stop driving the condemned matter out through, and into, the places it is used to? When the skin is opened at the healer's whim, will nature forget all its other paths? <summary>Van Helmont argues that pus is simply blood spoiled at the site of the wound, and mocks the Schools' claim that cauteries drain a "catarrh" from the head. Using a teething infant with diarrhea and the thick fat of women and stout patients as examples, he shows that no humor could travel from the brain to the cautery, and that the Archeus can expel waste through the body's own outlets.</summary>
