Marcus Varro testifies that he saw them. <note>Marcus Terentius Varro (116 to 27 BC), the Roman scholar; the story of the dancing islands of Lydia is reported by Pliny the Elder.</note> And there are things more wondrous than these: for on the Attic shore the sea sounds like a lyre. <note>original: "citharam sonat"; the cithara was the concert lyre of the ancient Greeks.</note> At Megara a certain stone plays like a lyre at every stroke; so great is the power of Music. <note>Pausanias describes a stone at Megara that rang like a lyre string when struck; legend said Apollo had laid his lyre upon it.</note> Music itself soothes the soul, lifts up the mind, rouses warriors to battle, and eases the labors and weariness of every kind of work. It calls back the fallen and the despairing, and it comforts travelers. And the Arabs say that camels carrying loads are strengthened when their drivers sing. In the same way, those who carry heavy loads sing, and are made more able and lightened by it: for song produces delight and readiness. It calms the wrathful, gladdens the sad and anxious, makes peace among those in discord, tempers the rage of the frenzied, and scatters vain thoughts. Hence it is that Democritus and Theophrastus affirm that certain diseases of the body and of the mind can in this way be either cured or brought on. <note>Democritus, the Greek atomist philosopher, and Theophrastus, Aristotle's successor, were both cited in antiquity for the healing power of music; Theophrastus reportedly held that the flute could ease sciatica.</note> Thus we read that Terpander and Arion of Methymna <note>Terpander, a seventh-century BC poet-musician of Lesbos, and Arion of Methymna, the legendary singer said to have been saved from drowning by a dolphin.</note>
