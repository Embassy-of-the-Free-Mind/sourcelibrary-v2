of the citizens. The eldest, as I said, presides over the household. Wives serve their husbands, children their parents, and, in short, the younger serve their elders. Every city is divided into four equal parts. In the middle of each part is a market for all kinds of goods. The products of each household are brought there into certain buildings, and each kind of goods is stored separately in warehouses. From these, any <term>paterfamilias</term> <gloss>head of a household</gloss> asks for whatever he and his family need, and carries off whatever he has asked for without money and without any payment at all. For why should anything be refused? There is more than enough of everything, and there is no fear that anyone will want to demand more than he needs. For why would anyone be suspected of asking for more than he needs, when he is sure that he will never lack anything? <note>More describes a society without money, where goods are held in common and distributed freely. This was a pointed contrast with the poverty and greed he criticized in the England of his day.</note>

<summary>Hythloday describes the Utopian household, where the eldest rules and the young serve their elders, and explains how each city is divided into four quarters with central markets. Heads of households take whatever they need from public storehouses without money, because abundance removes any reason for greed.</summary>
