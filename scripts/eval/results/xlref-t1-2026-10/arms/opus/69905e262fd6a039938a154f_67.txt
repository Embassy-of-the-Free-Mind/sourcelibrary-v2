In the middle circle, write first the name of the hour in which you do the work; in the second place, the name of the Angel of the hour; third, the seal of the Angel of the hour; fourth, the name of the Angel who rules the day on which you do the work, and the names of his ministers; fifth, the name of the present season; sixth, the names of the Spirits reigning in that part of the season and presiding over it; seventh, the name of the head of the sign reigning in that part of the season in which you do the work; eighth, the name of the Earth according to that part of the season in which you do the work; ninth, and to complete the middle circle, write the names of the Sun and of the Moon according to the same reckoning of the season. For when the season changes, the names change too. <note>The Heptameron gives different names for the Earth, Sun and Moon in each of the four seasons, so the circle must be redrawn to suit the time of year.</note> In the upper circle <note>that is, the outermost of the three circles</note>, let there be placed in the four corners the names of the angels who preside over the air on the day on which you do the work: namely, of the King and his three ministers. Outside the circle, in the four corners, let there be pentagons <note>five-pointed figures, the pentagrams commonly used as protective signs in ritual magic</note>. In the lower circle <note>that is, the innermost circle</note> let four divine names be written, with crosses placed between them.
