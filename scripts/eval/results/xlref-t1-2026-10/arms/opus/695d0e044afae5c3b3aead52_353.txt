It only directs the spirits flowing from the heart through the brain into the muscles, and determines them to particular motions, since these spirits, of themselves, can be applied with equal ease to many different actions. <note>The "animal spirits" were, in Descartes' physiology, a very fine and fast-moving fluid made from the blood, which travelled through the nerves and inflated the muscles to produce movement.</note>

Most of the movements that take place in us, however, do not depend on the mind in any way. Such are the beating of the heart, the digestion of food, nourishment, the breathing of those who are asleep, and even, in those who are awake, walking, singing and similar actions, when they are done without the mind paying attention.

And when those who fall from a height throw their hands out toward the ground to protect their head, they certainly do not do this by any deliberate reasoning. They do it only because the sight of the coming fall reaches the brain and sends the <term>animal spirits</term> <gloss>original: "spiritus animales"</gloss> into the nerves in the way needed to produce this movement, even against the will of the mind, and just as it would be produced in a machine.

This supports his view that animal bodies can be explained as machines.</summary>
