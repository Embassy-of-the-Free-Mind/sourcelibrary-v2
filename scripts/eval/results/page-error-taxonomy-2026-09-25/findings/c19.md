# c19 — by-eye findings
Books read: 6 of 6. Images opened: 12 of 12.

Stratum note: 4 of the 6 books are English sources (Burton, Taylor's Proclus, Crowther's Yoruba grammar in English, Whinfield's Gulshan). For these the "translation" is English into English. How the pipeline handles that case (verbatim copy vs paraphrase vs footnote condensation) is itself a finding; see the Summary.

## Summary
Defects by class (pages affected):
- English→English paraphrase/modernisation of an English source: 6 (B1 p112, p113; B6 p37, p38; partial B2 p304, p305). Verbatim copy on B3 p29, p30.
- Footnote apparatus dropped or condensed in translation: 2 (B6 p37, p38)
- Paraphrase changes meaning (subject/qualifier/hedge): 4 (B1 p113, B2 p304, B2 p305, B6 p38)
- Textual-critical Greek footnote mistranslated: 1 (B2 p304)
- Block shift of tail paragraphs into the next page's translation: 2 (B4 p320, p321)
- Sentence broken at the page join: 1 (B2 break)
- Hieroglyphs described instead of transcribed / signs misread / glossed by invention: 2 (B5 p396, p397)
- Yoruba diacritics dropped: 2 (B3 p29, p30)
- Malformed markdown tables: 1 (B3 p29)
- Running head promoted to H1 or leaked into the body: 6 (B1 p112, B2 p304, p305, B3 p29, p30, B6 p38)
- Footnote anchors/notes converted to `<margin>`/`<note>`: 3 (B2 p305, B5 p396, p397)
- Invented italics / inline quotes turned into blockquotes: 2 (B1 p113, B2 p304)
- Script mislabel (Kurrent): 1 (B5 p397)

NEW classes proposed:
- English-source paraphrase: when source language = output language, the translator rewrites the author's prose instead of copying it, and changes titles and wording.
- Apparatus stripping: footnotes are dropped or condensed to a gloss, with attributions (e.g. "L.") removed. This occurs even when the OCR has them verbatim.
- Tail block shift: the last section(s) of page N are omitted from N's translation and prepended to N+1's. That is page misalignment of an otherwise complete text.
- Critical-apparatus mistranslation: emendation notes ("For X read Y") are rendered as meaningless English.
- Marginal inscription omission: a marginal column in a non-Latin script (hieroglyphs) is neither transcribed nor described.
- Running-head-as-heading: the running head is emitted as `#`/`##` in the translation, sometimes merged with the facing page's head.

Surprises:
- The OCR on 19th-century print is very good. The Greek in Taylor's and Mullach's footnotes, the Latin two-column reading order and most Yoruba diacritics are all right. Nearly all the reader-visible damage is in the translation lane.
- Worst of all is the English-source case: the pipeline "translates" English into modernised English and strips the scholarly notes.
- The Mullach block shift is the most insidious. Every word is translated somewhere, so completeness checks pass, but page N's translation is missing a section and page N+1's shows text that is not on its image.
- The Lepsius hieroglyph glosses ("itf [father]" ×2) are confident inventions layered on a misread sign.
