NEW classes proposed:
- repetition-loop hallucination: the OCR emits the same passage many times verbatim where the image has varied text.
- canonical-text substitution: for a well-known scripture, the OCR partly recites remembered canonical text instead of transcribing the leaf. Lines appear that are not on the image, and lines on the image go missing.
- fluent-over-garble: translation turns nonsense or garbled OCR into confident, fluent prose with no uncertainty marker.
- confident translation of `<unclear>` source: the OCR flags lines as unclear, but the translation renders them as definite text.
- token-gloss word salad: the translator glosses syllable by syllable ("Body, hundred, abandon, again, six") instead of translating or declining, and the result ships as the page translation.
- split-word tail replaced by ellipsis: the continuation half of a word at the page top is written "[...]", implying missing text.
- multi-leaf photo as page unit: one image holds several loose pecha leaves (all rectos, then all versos on the next image), so site page order is not reading order and page-level OCR/translation mixes texts.

Surprises:
- Book 1's Tibetan leaves got a wholly invented Sanskrit OCR in the wrong script (Devanagari). A free check (Unicode block of OCR vs book language) would catch it.
- The known lower-leaf first-line drop did NOT show on either Tibetan pair. In B1 nothing is transcribed at all; in B5 each leaf's opening survives while the interiors collapse.
- The most dangerous errors are in the Latin and English, which read fluently: "Brother Facius was healed", "Saint Rotoi", "in presence of the law", "Arjun Nanak", "Vishnu". Each is plausible to a reader and wrong on the image.
- On every Tibetan page here, and on Gurmukhi 505, "no translation yet" would serve the reader better than what ships.
