## Summary
Defects by class (pages affected):
- spread not split / image shared or not matching the transcribed half: 4 (Philadelphia 90, 91; Malvasia 135, 136)
- skipped lines in OCR, carried into translation: 2 (Malvasia 135, Boehme 37), both HIGH
- whole translation inside <meta> (hidden from reader): 1 (Agrippa 233), HIGH
- raw/broken markup (pseudo-HTML <a>/<b>, stray </margin>, split <insert>): 2 (Boehme 38, Philadelphia 90)
- misread word changing meaning (incl. long-s→f): 3 (Sejanus 26 "truly", Sejanus 27 "fitting", Agrippa 234)
- translation inverts or drops a qualifier: 2 (Agrippa 234 "lucis magnæ", Malvasia 136 praeteritio)
- catchword doubled or split fragment repeated at break: 3 (Philadelphia, Boehme, Campanella)
- margin note merged into body / dropped: 3 (Agrippa 233, Boehme 37, Boehme 38)
- reader's annotations transcribed as text or given meaning: 3 (Philadelphia 90 underlines, Boehme 38 symbols, Campanella 86 "fide")
- note-reference letters lost: 1 (Sejanus 26)
- convention flip between facing pages: 1 (Boehme)
- catchword not recorded / catchword translated: 3 (Sejanus 27; Malvasia 135; Campanella 85)
NEW classes proposed:
- translation-in-meta: the full translation body is wrapped in a <meta> continuity tag, so a correct translation exists but the reader is shown nothing (tr_len far below OCR length is the tell).
- underline-as-insertion: printed words a reader underlined in pencil or ink are tagged <insert>, and the translation then asserts they are later additions to the text.
- other-half bleed: on an unsplit spread, the translator's meta or summary describes the facing page that this record does not transcribe.
- doodle exegesis: a later reader's handwritten symbols are flagged significance="high" and the translation invents a doctrinal meaning for them.
- pseudo-HTML footnote markers: footnote keys emitted as <a>, <b>, <c>, which collide with real HTML tags.
Surprises: the unsplit spread is systematic. It appears in 3 of 12 books across both strata (Poliphile, Philadelphia, Malvasia), always with one half transcribed per record. Where it occurs, the translator sees the facing page and leaks it (Malvasia meta). The two HIGH omissions (Sappho attribution, Boehme's four elements) produce fluent English that no text-only judge would flag. The English-language books (Philadelphia, Sejanus) are "translated" into paraphrase with interpretive drift, which is itself a small fidelity cost on sources that needed only transcription.
