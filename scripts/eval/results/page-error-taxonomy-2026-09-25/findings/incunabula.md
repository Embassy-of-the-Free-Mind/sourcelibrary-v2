# incunabula — by-eye findings
Books read: 6 of 6. Images opened: 12 of 12 (none skipped, no crops; the Avenzoar and Suetonius pages are dense, so claims there are limited to what was legible at full-page resolution).

Stratum note: Book 1 is a MANUSCRIPT (Middle English, Anglicana/textura hand), not an incunable. Book 2's catalogue metadata is wrong: it is titled "Prognosticon for Leipzig, 1490 [German]", but the leaves are a Latin Suetonius with the Sabellicus/Beroaldus commentary. OCR handling of abbreviations is inconsistent even within one book: some pages are diplomatic (macrons and ꝯ/ꝑ kept or dropped raw), and the next page of the same book is silently normalised (full expansion, u→v).

NEW classes proposed:
- neighbour-leaf bleed-in as marginalia: line-ends of the facing page, visible in the photograph, are transcribed as this page's margin and then "translated".
- sinne→liue class: a systematic minim-cluster misread in textura that consistently flips a key word throughout a page.
- homeoteleuton skip: the OCR jumps between two identical words and drops the clause between them.
- OCR truncated / translation overruns: the stored OCR stops early, but the translation continues (from memory or later pages) to 5× the source length.
- OCR–translation divergence: the translation contains a passage absent from the stored OCR (the two were produced from different reads).
- book incipit dropped: a rubricated book/tractate division is lost, so the structure is invisible.
- fabricated marginal summary: the model invents a Latin side-note summarising the page.
- within-book convention flip: diplomatic vs normalised transcription alternates page to page, unflagged.
- whole-page-as-note: the entire translation is wrapped in <note>, so it renders as apparatus.
- recipe-quantity/ingredient misreads (℞ as Item, ʒ ij as iij, parum as panis/panuim): high-stakes in pharmacological texts.
