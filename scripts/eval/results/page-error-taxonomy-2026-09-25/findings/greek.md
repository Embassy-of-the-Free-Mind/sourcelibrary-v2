NEW classes proposed:
- catalogue-says-print-but-manuscript: metadata date/format contradicts the images and the OCR's own script tag.
- whole-page-hallucinated-ocr: fluent text in the right language with no correspondence to the leaf (tell: anachronistic vocabulary such as Modern Greek καλοκαιρίῳ, the running title changed).
- spread-with-one-page-transcribed: a two-page image where the OCR picks one page, or calls the pair "2 columns", and loses the other.
- ocr-verb-invention: the OCR inserts a plausible word not on the page ("ᾄδει"), and the translation builds on it.
- translation-of-the-crib: in a Greek/Latin or Greek/English parallel edition, the "Greek" translation is really the printed crib, which smooths over the editor's lacunae.
- parallel-text-translated-twice: both columns of a facing-text edition are translated in full.
- english-crib-paraphrased-as-translation: an English source column is "translated" into a degraded paraphrase of itself.
- translation-not-grounded-in-ocr: the translation contains text missing from the OCR, so the two panels disagree.
- ocr-skips-middle-of-column: the OCR keeps the head and tail of a column and drops a block in the middle.
- footnote-anchor-as-margin-with-duplication: a superscript note number at a line-end hyphen is wrapped in `<margin>` and the split word is doubled.
- speculative-translator-note: a `<note>`/`<gloss>` explains an OCR mis-token with confident but invented scholarship.
- fabricated-bracket-bridge: the translator inserts a bracketed verb to close a sentence whose real verb is on the next page.
- watermark-as-catchword: "Digitized by Google" or a neighbouring fragment is recorded as the catchword.

Anything that surprised me:
- The model handled 1545 ligature Greek reasonably at the word level, then failed exactly on the ligatures (ην, ευ, ος, ᾶν) at the points where grammar hinges. The translations of those points are fluent and wrong, and nothing flags them.
- Two of six "printed Greek" books are manuscripts, and for the microfilmed spread the OCR simply invented a treatise.
- In the parallel-text editions the translator clearly ignored the Greek and followed the crib. For Didot this silently erases the editor's printed lacunae, which is a fidelity claim the reader cannot see.
- The Loeb Greek OCR was essentially perfect, including breathings and iota subscripts. The defects there are all in the translation step (paraphrasing Perrin).
