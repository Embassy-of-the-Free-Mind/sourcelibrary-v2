- **hindu-sanskrit** · show_with_caveat · [Sharada Tilaka Tantra (Laxmanacharya)](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/69d7e10be9e5e67989c373f9) — This is a printed Devanagari text of the Śāradātilaka, Lakṣmaṇadeśika's Tantric ritual manual, and the transcription is of that text, leaf by leaf. The English follows the verses in order and is usable for ritual sequence. Where the OCR misreads a word it does not flag it: mantra encodings and the effects of rites are sometimes wrong (on p.135 'brings him under control' becomes 'makes the invisible visible'), so check any technical verse against the Devanagari.
- **hindu-sanskrit** · show_with_caveat · [Tantraloka Vol. 1 with Jayaratha Commentary](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/6953e2ff77f38f6761beb2fa) — This is the 1918 Kashmir Series edition of Tantrāloka Āhnika 1 with Jayaratha's commentary, transcribed accurately, footnoted variant readings included. The English is a faithful, readable rendering of verse and commentary and can be trusted for the argument. It is wrapped in model-written preface and summary boxes, which are not part of the text. The last ~40 leaves restart the printed pagination and should not be cited until that section is identified.
- **hindu-sanskrit** · do_not_show · [Paramesvaratantra](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/6991d8a08c1030b12444c14c) — The images are the Cambridge palm-leaf manuscript of the Parameśvaratantra, an early Śaiva tantra in an early Nepalese hand. The transcription is not a reliable reading of it: a few words match the leaf, and much of the rest is invented or repeated. The English should not be trusted at all; it turns misread letters into mantras, stories and notes.
- **hindu-sanskrit** · show_with_caveat · [Rig-Veda-Sanhita: Sacred Hymns of the Brahmans (Vol. 3)](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/699209a9bed8f4b5ff5b1fd0) — This is Max Müller's printed Ṛgveda with Sāyaṇa's commentary (Vol. III). The transcription is the right text and accurate to the line, with accents converted to Unicode marks. The English translates hymn, word-by-word analysis and commentary, and is generally faithful. It occasionally inverts or substitutes a gloss, so check the hymn line against the Sanskrit before quoting it.
