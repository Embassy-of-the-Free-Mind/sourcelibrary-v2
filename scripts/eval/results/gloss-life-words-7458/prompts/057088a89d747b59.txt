# The Connection is with the Possessor of Breath, according to Scripture (2.4.15)
<note>2.4.15</note>

Even though there are presiding deities <term>devatā; celestial beings who govern natural and bodily functions</term> over the vital breaths, it is understood from the scriptures that these breaths are connected to the embodied soul <term>śārīra; the individual self dwelling in the body</term>, who is the master of the aggregate of the body and its causes and effects. This is true; however, in the Smriti <term>traditional texts like the Bhagavad Gita or Manu Smriti</term>, the mind is included as a sense-organ along with the ear and others in the count of eleven. But the Chief Breath is never famously known as a "sense" <note>original: "indriya"</note> in either the primary scriptures (Shruti) or traditional law (Smriti). This difference in designation makes sense only if there is a difference in reality. If they were the same reality, it would be contradictory for the same Breath to be called a "sense" and not be called a "sense" at the same time. Therefore, the others are distinct realities from the Chief.

And why else are they distinct realities?
