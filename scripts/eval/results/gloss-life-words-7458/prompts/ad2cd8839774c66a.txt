<note>399. This is the gist of the verse: Through the <term>Māyā-śakti</term> of the Supreme Lord, who is the essence of Light, the universe—which is actually His own self—is made to appear as separate. Therefore, that same consciousness is "knowledge" <term>jñāna</term> when it is direct perception. Memory, intention, and determination of that same separate appearance are also just consciousness, even when taking the form of the activities of the mind and intellect.</note>
<note>400. This shows the grammatical resolution of the term "freedom within <term>Māyā</term>."</note>
<note>401. Regarding "from the knower": The Self is indeed the all-pervading Supreme Lord. However, through the <term>Māyā-śakti</term>, He becomes a limited subject <term>grāhaka</term> <gloss>grasper</gloss> due to contraction. Similarly, the power of reflection also becomes contracted by the influence of objects that have been limited by the <term>Māyā-śakti</term>. In this state, it is called "knowledge," "memory," and so on. But the point is that these are not separate principles other than the power of reflection itself; they are merely different names.</note>
<note>402. This explains the phrase "separate objects of consciousness" by adopting the view of repeated manifestation.</note>
