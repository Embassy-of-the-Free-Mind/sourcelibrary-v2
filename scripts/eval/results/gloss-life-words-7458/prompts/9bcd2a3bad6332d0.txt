### Vital heat is nourished by moisture: When it is lacking, dissolution occurs; when it exceeds, suffocation occurs. Chapter 2.

Life, indeed, consists in natural heat, just like a light <note>Ficino uses the metaphor of a lamp flame to represent the "vital heat" of the body.</note>. The fuel for this heat is an airy and fatty moisture, much like oil. If, therefore, such moisture happens to fail, or if it entirely exceeds or becomes tainted, the heat is immediately weakened, and at last, having been weakened, it is extinguished. If the heat is weakened and perishes through a deficiency of moisture, death occurs by **resolution** <note>original: "resolutione" — a process of drying out or "thinning" where the body's substance dissolves away.</note>.
