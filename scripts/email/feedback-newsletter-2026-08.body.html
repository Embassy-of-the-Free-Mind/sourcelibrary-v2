Hello Source Library friends,

Can I get your feedback?

I’ve had the most fun time building the Source Library, but I haven’t had the time to read all of the 20,000+ books in there. If you come across something that could be improved, no matter how small, please let me know. It might be in the UI or it might be the content of the translation or maybe some of our book data is wrong.

I want you to know that I personally read the feedback on the Source Library every day. You are welcome to email me!

I also want to make sure everyone knows how to leave feedback in the site. You see, down on the bottom of every page there is a button to leave feedback.

And then, further down, here… and even here, on the footer.

That way, I know what page or service you are leaving the feedback on.

And if it is the translation you want to flag, there is a quieter one right under the English text while you are reading. That one already knows which book and which page you are on.

I really want to engage the reading community. Not everyone is interested in reading texts that are hundreds of years old. Yet. I’d love to make the reading experience as pleasant and trustworthy as possible.

Can you trust it? Well, my attitude is “Don’t trust any translation.” But by providing AI translations next to the original source, it means that if you don’t trust the translation, you can do it again yourself!

Which works far better with help. If you read Latin, or German, or Arabic, or Chinese, or anything else we hold, I would love a second pair of eyes on a text you already care about. Tell me how good the translation really is. Rate it, argue with it, mark the places where it drifts away from the original. The same goes for the blog posts, which I write quickly and which surely contain things only a specialist would catch.

You do not need credentials for this, just the language and the interest. Tick “I’d like to help” in that feedback box, or go to sourcelibrary.org/welcome and tell me what you read.

And one last thing, which is the part I am most curious about. What are you actually researching? I would love to know what brought you here, what you have been looking for and could not find, and what you would read tomorrow if somebody put it into English.

Just hit reply and tell me. I read those too, and it genuinely changes what we go and get next.
