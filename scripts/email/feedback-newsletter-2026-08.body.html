Every page in this library was read by a machine before it was read by a person. There are about six million scanned pages here, and close to five million of them have been put into English by an AI model. Hardly any have been checked by a human. At that scale, hardly any ever will be.

If you open a page and read it properly, there is a good chance nobody has done that before, and that you are the only person who will notice if something is off.

Two from July. A reader sent us fourteen words: “I’m seeing chapter 4 on the image page and chapter 5 in the text.” We went and looked, and 261 books turned out to be showing every scan one leaf behind its own text. Around 105,000 pages, like that for four months. Another reader wrote four words on an encyclopedia entry: “What is this page about? Real?” They were right to ask. We had been citing page numbers we could not back up, roughly four out of every five of them.

We had not spotted either one, and we would not have.

Three ways to tell us

1. At the bottom of any page. Click it, write whatever you noticed, send. It records which page you were on, so there is no need to explain where you are.

2. Underneath a translation, while you are reading it. There is a faint line below the English: Notice a translation issue? Let us know. That one carries the book and the page number along with it, so “wrong word in line three” is enough to work from.

3. Or ask us for something. A book we do not have, a translation that does not exist yet. Forty-nine pages are readable in English today because somebody clicked “Request one” on a book that had none.

None of it has to be careful. Two of the most useful notes we have ever had, in full:

“Not the original edition.”
“This image doesn’t correspond with this text.”

Both were fixed. We have had 336 messages so far and have dealt with 298 of them. If you leave an email address, you will hear back when yours is done.

If something looks wrong, it probably is. Please tell us.
